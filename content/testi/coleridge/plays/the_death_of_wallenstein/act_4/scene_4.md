| *(didascalia)* | SCENE — A Gothic Apartment at the DUCHESS FRIEDLAND'S. THEKLA on a seat, pale, her eyes closed. The DUCHESS and LADY NEUBRUNN busied about her. WALLENSTEIN and the COUNTESS in conversation. |
| WALLENSTEIN | How knew she it so soon? |
| COUNTESS | She seems to have<br>Foreboded some misfortune. The report<br>Of an engagement, in the which had fallen<br>A colonel of the Imperial army, frighten'd her.<br>I saw it instantly. She flew to meet<br>The Swedish Courier, and with sudden questioning,<br>Soon wrested from him the disastrous secret.<br>Too late we missed her, hastened after her,<br>We found her lying in his arms, all pale<br>And in a swoon. |
| WALLENSTEIN | A heavy, heavy blow!<br>And she so unprepared! Poor [[Child\|child]]! How is it? |
| *(didascalia)* | [Turning to the DUCHESS. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Is she coming to herself? |
| DUCHESS | Her eyes are opening. |
| COUNTESS | She lives. |
| THEKLA | (looking around her). Where am I? |
| WALLENSTEIN | (steps to her, raising her up in his arms). Come,<br>cheerly, Thekla! be my own brave girl!<br>See, there's thy loving mother. Thou art in<br>Thy father's arms. |
| THEKLA | (standing up). Where is he? Is he gone? |
| DUCHESS | Who gone, my daughter? |
| THEKLA | He — the man who uttered<br>That word of misery. |
| DUCHESS | O! think not of it,<br>My Thekla! |
| WALLENSTEIN | Give her sorrow leave to talk!<br>Let her complain — mingle your [[Tears\|tears]] with hers,<br>For she hath suffered a deep anguish; but<br>She'll rise superior to it, for my Thekla<br>Hath all her father's unsubdued heart. |
| THEKLA | I am not ill. See, I have power to stand.<br>Why does my mother weep? Have I alarmed her?<br>It is gone by — I recollect myself — |
| *(didascalia)* | [She casts her eyes round the room, as seeking some one. |
| THEKLA | Where is he? Please you, do not hide him from me.<br>You see I have strength enough: now I will hear him. |
| DUCHESS | No, never shall this messenger of evil<br>Enter again into thy presence, Thekla! |
| THEKLA | My father — |
| WALLENSTEIN | Dearest daughter! |
| THEKLA | I'm not weak —<br>Shortly I shall be quite myself again.<br>You'll grant me one request? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Name it, my daughter. |
| THEKLA | Permit the stranger to be called to me,<br>And grant me leave, that by myself I may<br>Hear his report and question him. |
| DUCHESS | No, never! |
| COUNTESS | 'Tis not advisable — assent not to it. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Hush! Wherefore would'st thou speak with him, my daughter? |
| THEKLA | Knowing the whole, I shall be more collected;<br>I will not be deceived. My mother wishes<br>Only to spare me. I will not be spared.<br>The worst is said already: I can hear<br>Nothing of deeper anguish! |
| COUNTESS AND DUCHESS | Do it not. |
| THEKLA | The horror overpowered me by surprise.<br>My heart betrayed me in the stranger's presence;<br>He was a witness of my weakness, yea,<br>I sank into his arms; and that has shamed me.<br>I must replace myself in his esteem,<br>And I must speak with him, perforce, that he,<br>The stranger, may not think ungently of me. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I see she is in the right, and am inclined<br>To grant her this request of hers. Go, call him. |
| *(didascalia)* | [LADY NEUBRUNN goes to call him. |
| DUCHESS | But I, thy mother, will be present — |
| THEKLA | 'Twere<br>More pleasing to me, if [[Alone\|alone]] I saw him:<br>Trust me, I shall behave myself the more<br>Collectedly. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Permit her her own will.<br>Leave her [[Alone\|alone]] with him: for there are sorrows,<br>Where of necessity the [[Soul\|soul]] must be<br>Its own support. A strong heart will rely<br>On its own strength [[Alone\|alone]]. In her own bosom,<br>Not in her mother's arms, must she collect<br>The strength to rise superior to this blow.<br>It is mine own brave girl. I'll have her treated<br>Not as the woman, but the heroine. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Going. |
| COUNTESS | (detaining him). Where art thou going? I heard Tertsky say<br>That 'tis thy purpose to depart from hence<br>To-morrow early, but to leave us here. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Yes, ye stay here, placed under the protection<br>Of gallant men. |
| COUNTESS | O take us with you, brother.<br>Leave us not in this gloomy [[Solitude\|solitude]]<br>To brood o'er anxious thoughts. The mists of [[Doubt\|doubt]]<br>Magnify evils to a shape of horror. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Who speaks of evil? I entreat you, sister,<br>Use words of better omen. |
| COUNTESS | Then take us with you.<br>O leave us not behind you in a place<br>That forces us to such sad omens. Heavy<br>And sick within me is my heart ——<br>These walls breathe on me, like a church-yard vault.<br>I cannot tell you, brother, how this place<br>Doth go against my [[Nature\|nature]]. Take us with you.<br>Come, sister, join you your entreaty! — Niece,<br>Yours too. We all entreat you, take us with you! |
| WALLENSTEIN | The place's evil omens will I change,<br>Making it that which shields and shelters for me<br>My best beloved. |
| LADY NEUBRUNN | (returning). The Swedish officer. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Leave her [[Alone\|alone]] with him. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit. |
| DUCHESS | (to Thekla who starts and shivers). There — pale as<br>[[Death\|death]]! — [[Child]], 'tis impossible<br>That thou should'st speak with him. Follow thy mother. |
| THEKLA | The Lady Neubrunn then may stay with me. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exeunt DUCHESS and COUNTESS. |
| *(didascalia)* | SCENE — A Gothic and gloomy, &c. 1800, 1828, 1829. |
