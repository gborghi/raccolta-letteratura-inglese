| *(didascalia)* | To these enter the COUNTESS TERTSKY, pale and disordered. Her utterance<br>is slow and feeble, and unimpassioned. |
| OCTAVIO | O Countess Tertsky! These are the results<br>Of luckless unblest deeds. |
| COUNTESS | They are the fruits<br>Of your contrivances. The Duke is dead,<br>My husband too is dead, the Duchess struggles<br>In the pangs of [[Death\|death]], my niece has disappeared.<br>This [[house]] of splendour, and of princely glory,<br>Doth now stand desolated: the affrighted servants<br>Rush forth through all its doors. I am the last<br>Therein; I shut it up, and here deliver<br>The keys. |
| OCTAVIO | O Countess! my [[house]] too is desolate. |
| COUNTESS | Who next is to be murdered? Who is next<br>To be maltreated? Lo! The Duke is dead.<br>The Emperor's vengeance may be pacified!<br>Spare the old servants; let not their fidelity<br>Be imputed to the faithful as a crime —<br>The evil destiny surprised my brother<br>Too suddenly; he could not think on them. |
| OCTAVIO | Speak not of vengeance! Speak not of maltreatment!<br>The Emperor is appeased; the heavy fault<br>Hath heavily been expiated — nothing<br>Descended from the father to the daughter,<br>Except his glory and his services.<br>The Empress honours your adversity,<br>Takes part in your afflictions, opens to you<br>Her motherly arms! Therefore no farther fears!<br>Yield yourself up in [[Hope\|hope]] and confidence<br>To the Imperial Grace! |
| COUNTESS | To the grace and [[Mercy\|mercy]] of a greater Master<br>Do I yield up myself. Where shall the [[Body\|body]]<br>Of the Duke have its place of final rest?<br>In the Chartreuse, which he himself did found,<br>At Gitschin rests the Countess Wallenstein;<br>And by her side, to whom he was indebted<br>For his first fortunes, gratefully he wished<br>He might sometime repose in [[Death\|death]]! O let him<br>Be buried there. And likewise, for my husband's<br>Remains, I ask the like grace. The Emperor<br>Is now proprietor of all our castles.<br>This sure may [[Well\|well]] be granted us — one sepulchre<br>Beside the sepulchres of our forefathers! |
| OCTAVIO | Countess, you tremble, you turn pale! |
| COUNTESS | You think<br>More worthily of me, than to believe<br>I would survive the downfall of my [[house]].<br>We did not hold ourselves too mean to grasp<br>After a monarch's [[Crown\|crown]] — the [[Crown\|crown]] did fate<br>Deny, but not the feeling and the spirit<br>That to the [[Crown\|crown]] belong! We deem a<br>Courageous [[Death\|death]] more worthy of our free station<br>Than a dishonoured life. — I have taken [[Poison\|poison]]. |
| OCTAVIO | Help! Help! Support her! |
| COUNTESS | Nay, it is too late.<br>In a few moments is my fate accomplished. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit COUNTESS.] |
| GORDON | O [[house]] of [[Death\|death]] and horrors! |
| *(didascalia)* | [An officer enters, and brings a letter with the great seal.] |
| GORDON | What is this?<br>It is the Imperial Seal. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He reads the Address, and delivers the letter to OCTAVIO<br>with a look of reproach, and with an emphasis on the word.] |
| GORDON | To the Prince Piccolomini. |
| *(didascalia)* | [OCTAVIO, with his whole frame expressive of sudden<br>anguish, raises his eyes to heaven.] |
| *(didascalia)* | (The curtain drops.) |
