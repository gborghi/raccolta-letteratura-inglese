| *(didascalia)* | To these enter SENI. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Is not that Seni? and beside himself,<br>If one may trust his looks! What brings thee hither<br>At this late hour, [[Baptista]]? |
| SENI | Terror, Duke!<br>On thy account. |
| WALLENSTEIN | What now? |
| SENI | Flee ere the day-break!<br>Trust not thy person to the Swedes! |
| WALLENSTEIN | What now<br>Is in thy thoughts? |
| SENI | Trust not thy person to these Swedes. |
| WALLENSTEIN | What is it then? |
| SENI | O wait not the arrival of these<br>Swedes!<br>An evil near at hand is threatening thee<br>From false friends. All the signs stand full of horror!<br>Near, near at hand the net-work of perdition —<br>Yea, even now 'tis being cast around thee! |
| WALLENSTEIN | [[Baptista]], thou art dreaming! — [[Fear]] befools thee. |
| SENI | Believe not that an empty [[Fear\|fear]] deludes me.<br>Come, read it in the planetary aspects;<br>Read it thyself, that ruin threatens thee<br>From false friends! |
| WALLENSTEIN | From the falseness of my friends<br>Has risen the whole of my unprosperous fortunes.<br>The warning should have come before! At present<br>I need no revelation from the [[Stars\|stars]]<br>To know that. |
| SENI | Come and see! trust thine own eyes!<br>A fearful sign stands in the [[house]] of life;<br>An enemy, a fiend lurks close behind<br>The radiance of thy planet — O be warned!<br>Deliver not thyself up to these heathens<br>To wage a war against our holy church. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The oracle rails that way! Yes,<br>yes! Now<br>I recollect. This junction with the Swedes<br>Did never please thee — lay thyself to [[Sleep\|sleep]],<br>[[Baptista]]! Signs like these I do not [[Fear\|fear]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Gordon (who during the whole of this dialogue has shewn marks of extreme agitation, and now turns to Wallenstein). |
| GORDON | My Duke and<br>General! May I dare presume? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Speak freely. |
| GORDON | What if 'twere no mere creation<br>Of [[Fear\|fear]], if God's high [[Providence\|providence]] vouchsaf'd<br>To interpose its aid for your deliverance,<br>And made that mouth its organ. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Ye're both feverish!<br>How can mishap come to me from the Swedes?<br>They sought this junction with me—'tis their interest. |
| *(didascalia)* | Gordon (with difficulty suppressing his emotion). |
| GORDON | But what if the<br>arrival of these Swedes —<br>What if this were the very thing that winged<br>The ruin that is flying to your temples? |
| *(didascalia)* | [Flings himself at his feet. |
| GORDON | There is yet [[Time\|time]], my Prince. |
| SENI | O hear him! hear him! |
| GORDON | The Rhinegrave's still far off. Give but the<br>orders,<br>This citadel shall close its gates upon him.<br>If then he will besiege us, let him try it.<br>But this I say; he'll find his own destruction<br>With his whole force before these ramparts, sooner<br>Than weary down the valour of our spirit.<br>He shall experience what a band of heroes,<br>Inspirited by an heroic leader,<br>Is able to perform. And if indeed<br>It be thy serious wish to make amends<br>For that which thou hast done amiss, — this, this<br>Will touch and reconcile the Emperor,<br>Who gladly turns his heart to thoughts of [[Mercy\|mercy]],<br>And Friedland, who returns repentant to him,<br>Will stand yet higher in his Emperor's favour,<br>Than e'er he stood when he had never fallen. |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (contemplates him with surprise, remains silent awhile, betraying strong emotion). |
| WALLENSTEIN | Gordon — your zeal and<br>fervour lead you far.<br>[[Well]], [[Well\|well]] — an old friend has a privilege.<br>[[Blood]], Gordon, has been flowing. Never, never<br>Can the Emperor pardon me: and if he could,<br>Yet I — I ne'er could let myself be pardoned.<br>Had I foreknown what now has taken place,<br>That he, my dearest friend, would fall for me,<br>My first [[Death\|death]]-offering: and had the heart<br>Spoken to me, as now it has done — Gordon,<br>It may be, I might have bethought myself.<br>It may be too, I might not. Might or might not,<br>Is now an idle question. All too seriously<br>Has it begun to end in nothing, Gordon!<br>Let it then have its course. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Stepping to the [[Window\|window]]. |
| WALLENSTEIN | All dark and silent — at the [[Castle\|castle]] too<br>All is now hushed — Light me, Chamberlain! |
| *(didascalia)* | [The Groom of the Chamber, who had entered during the last dialogue, and had been standing at a distance and listening to it with visible expressions of the deepest interest, advances in extreme agitation, and throws himself at the DUKE'S feet. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And thou too! But I know why thou dost wish<br>My reconcilement with the Emperor.<br>Poor man! he hath a small estate in Cärnthen,<br>And fears it will be forfeited because<br>He's in my service. Am I then so poor,<br>That I no longer can indemnify<br>My servants? [[Well]]! To no one I employ<br>Means of compulsion. If 'tis thy belief<br>That fortune has fled from me, go! Forsake me.<br>This [[Night\|night]] for the last [[Time\|time]] mayst thou unrobe me,<br>And then go over to thy Emperor.<br>Gordon, good [[Night\|night]]! I think to make a long<br>[[Sleep]] of it: for the struggle and the turmoil<br>Of this last day or two were great. May't please you!<br>Take care that they awake me not too early. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit WALLENSTEIN, the Groom of the Chamber lighting him. SENI follows. GORDON remains on the darkened stage, following the DUKE with his eye, till he disappears at the farther end of the gallery: then by his gestures the old man expresses the depth of his anguish, and stands leaning against a pillar. |
