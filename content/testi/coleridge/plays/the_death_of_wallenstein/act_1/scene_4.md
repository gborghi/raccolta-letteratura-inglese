| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, ILLO, COUNTESS, DUCHESS, THEKLA. |
| WALLENSTEIN | All quiet in the camp? |
| ILLO | It is all quiet. |
| WALLENSTEIN | In a few hours may couriers come from Prague<br>With tidings, that this capital is ours.<br>Then we may drop the mask, and to the troops<br>Assembled in this town make known the measure<br>And its result together. In such cases<br>Example does the whole. Whoever is foremost<br>Still leads the herd. An imitative creature<br>Is man. The troops at Prague conceive no other,<br>Than that the Pilsen army has gone through<br>The forms of homage to us; and in Pilsen<br>They shall swear fealty to us, because<br>The example has been given them by Prague.<br>Butler, you tell me, has declared himself. |
| ILLO | At his own bidding, unsolicited,<br>He came to offer you himself and regiment. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I find we must not give implicit credence<br>To every warning voice that makes itself<br>Be listened to in the heart. To hold us back,<br>Oft does the lying spirit counterfeit<br>The voice of [[Truth]] and inward Revelation,<br>Scattering false oracles. And thus have I<br>To intreat [[Forgiveness\|forgiveness]], for that secretly<br>I've wrong'd this honourable gallant man,<br>This Butler: for a feeling, of the which<br>I am not master ([[Fear\|fear]] I would not call it),<br>Creeps o'er me instantly, with sense of shuddering,<br>At his approach, and stops [[Love\|love]]'s joyous motion.<br>And this same man, against whom I am warned,<br>This honest man is he, who reaches to me<br>The first pledge of my fortune. |
| ILLO | And [[Doubt\|doubt]] not<br>That his example will win over to you<br>The best men in the army. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Go and send<br>Isolani hither. Send him immediately.<br>He is under recent obligations to me.<br>With him will I commence the trial. Go. |
| *(didascalia)* | [ILLO exit. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Lo, there the mother with the darling daughter!<br>For once we'll have an interval of rest —<br>Come! my heart yearns to live a cloudless hour<br>In the beloved circle of my family. |
| COUNTESS | 'Tis long since we've been thus together, brother. |
| WALLENSTEIN *(aside to Countess)* | Can she sustain the news? Is she prepared? |
| COUNTESS | Not yet. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Come here, my sweet girl! Seat thee by me,<br>For there is a good spirit on thy lips.<br>Thy mother praised to me thy ready skill:<br>She says a voice of melody dwells in thee,<br>Which doth enchant the [[Soul\|soul]]. Now such a voice<br>Will drive away from me the evil demon<br>That beats his black wings close above my head. |
| DUCHESS | Where is thy lute, my daughter? Let thy father<br>Hear some small trial of thy skill. |
| THEKLA | My mother!<br>I — |
| DUCHESS | Trembling? Come, collect thyself. Go, cheer<br>Thy father. |
| THEKLA | O my mother! I — I cannot. |
| COUNTESS | How, what is that, niece? |
| THEKLA *(to the Countess)* | O spare me — sing — now — in this sore anxiety,<br>Of the o'erburthen'd [[Soul\|soul]] — to sing to him,<br>Who is thrusting, even now, my mother headlong<br>Into her [[Grave\|grave]]! |
| DUCHESS | How, Thekla? Humoursome?<br>What! shall thy father have expressed a wish<br>In vain? |
| COUNTESS | Here is the lute. |
| THEKLA | My God! how can I — |
| *(didascalia)* | [The orchestra plays. During the ritornello THEKLA<br>expresses in her gestures and countenance the<br>struggle of her feelings: and at the moment<br>that she should begin to sing, contracts<br>herself together, as one shuddering, throws<br>the instrument down, and retires abruptly. |
| DUCHESS | My [[Child\|child]]! O she is ill — |
| WALLENSTEIN | What ails the maiden?<br>Say, is she often so? |
| COUNTESS | Since then herself<br>Has now betrayed it, I too must no longer<br>Conceal it. |
| WALLENSTEIN | What? |
| COUNTESS | She loves him! |
| WALLENSTEIN | Loves him! Whom? |
| COUNTESS | Max does she [[Love\|love]]! Max Piccolomini.<br>Hast thou ne'er noticed it? Nor yet my sister? |
| DUCHESS | Was it this that lay so heavy on her heart?<br>God's blessing on thee, my sweet [[Child\|child]]! Thou needest<br>Never take shame upon thee for thy choice. |
| COUNTESS | This [[Journey\|journey]], if 'twere not thy aim, ascribe it<br>To thine own [[Self\|self]]. Thou shouldest have chosen another<br>To have attended her. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And does he know it? |
| COUNTESS | Yes, and he hopes to win her. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Hopes to win her!<br>Is the boy mad? |
| COUNTESS | [[Well]] — hear it from themselves. |
| WALLENSTEIN | He thinks to carry off Duke Friedland's daughter!<br>Aye? — The thought pleases me.<br>The young man has no grovelling spirit. |
| COUNTESS | Since<br>Such and such constant favour you have shewn him — |
| WALLENSTEIN | He chooses finally to be my heir.<br>And true it is, I [[Love\|love]] the youth; yea, [[Honour\|honour]] him.<br>But must he therefore be my daughter's husband!<br>Is it daughters only? Is it only children<br>That we must shew our favour by? |
| DUCHESS | His noble disposition and his manners — |
| WALLENSTEIN | Win him my heart, but not my daughter. |
| DUCHESS | Then<br>His rank, his ancestors — |
| WALLENSTEIN | Ancestors! What?<br>He is a subject, and my son-in-law<br>I will seek out upon the thrones of Europe. |
| DUCHESS | O dearest Albrecht! Climb we not too high.<br>Lest we should fall too low. |
| WALLENSTEIN | What? have I paid<br>A price so heavy to ascend this eminence,<br>And jut out high above the common herd,<br>Only to close the mighty part I play<br>In Life's great drama, with a common kinsman?<br>Have I for this — [pause.] She is the only thing<br>That will remain behind of me on earth;<br>And I will see a [[Crown\|crown]] around her head,<br>Or die in the attempt to place it there.<br>I hazard all — all! and for this [[Alone\|alone]],<br>To lift her into [[Greatness\|greatness]] —<br>Yea, in this moment, in the which we are speaking — [pause.<br>And I must now, like a soft-hearted father,<br>Couple together in good peasant fashion<br>The pair, that chance to suit each other's liking —<br>And I must do it now, even now, when I<br>Am stretching out the wreath that is to twine<br>My full accomplished work — no! she is the [[Jewel\|jewel]],<br>Which I have treasured long, my last, my noblest,<br>And 'tis my purpose not to let her from me<br>For less than a [[King\|king]]'s sceptre. |
| DUCHESS | O my husband!<br>You're ever building, building to the clouds,<br>Still building higher, and still higher building,<br>And ne'er reflect, that the poor narrow basis<br>Cannot sustain the giddy tottering column. |
| WALLENSTEIN *(to the Countess)* | Have you announced the place of residence<br>Which I have destined for her? |
| COUNTESS | No! not yet.<br>'Twere better you yourself disclosed it to her. |
| DUCHESS | How? Do we not return to Karn then? |
| WALLENSTEIN | No. |
| DUCHESS | And to no other of your lands or seats? |
| WALLENSTEIN | You would not be secure there. |
| DUCHESS | Not secure<br>In the Emperor's realms, beneath the Emperor's<br>Protection? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Friedland's wife may be permitted<br>No longer to [[Hope\|hope]] that. |
| DUCHESS | O God in heaven!<br>And have you brought it even to this? |
| WALLENSTEIN | In Holland<br>You'll find protection. |
| DUCHESS | In a Lutheran country?<br>What? And you send us into Lutheran countries? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Duke Franz of Lauenburg conducts you thither. |
| DUCHESS | Duke Franz of Lauenburg?<br>The ally of Sweden, the Emperor's enemy. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The Emperor's enemies are mine no longer. |
| DUCHESS *(casting a look of terror on the Duke and the Countess)* | Is it then true? It is. You are degraded?<br>Deposed from the command? O God in heaven! |
| COUNTESS *(aside to the Duke)* | Leave her in this belief. Thou seest she cannot<br>Support the real [[Truth\|truth]]. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Have I for this — |
| *(didascalia)* | [Stops suddenly, repressing himself. |
