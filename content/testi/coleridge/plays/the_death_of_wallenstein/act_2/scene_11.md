| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | To these enter WALLENSTEIN, accompanied by ILLO and BUTLER. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Tertsky! |
| TERTSKY | My General? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Let our regiments hold themselves<br>In readiness to march; for we shall leave<br>Pilsen ere evening. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit TERTSKY. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Butler! |
| BUTLER | Yes, my General. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The Governor at Egra is your friend<br>And countryman. Write to him instantly<br>By a Post Courier. He must be advised,<br>That we are with him early on the morrow.<br>You follow us yourself, your regiment with you. |
| BUTLER | It shall be done, my General! |
| WALLENSTEIN | Part! |
| MAX | O God! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Cuirassiers enter with drawn swords, and assemble in the back-ground. At the same [[Time\|time]] there are heard from below some spirited passages out of the Pappenheim March, which seem to address MAX. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Here he is, he is at liberty: I keep him<br>No longer. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He turns away, and stands so that MAX cannot pass by him nor approach the PRINCESS. |
| MAX | Thou know'st that I have not yet learnt to live<br>Without thee! I go forth into a [[Desert\|desert]],<br>Leaving my all behind me. O do not turn<br>Thine eyes away from me! O once more shew me<br>Thy ever dear and honoured countenance. |
| *(didascalia)* | [MAX attempts to take his hand, but is repelled; he turns to the COUNTESS. |
| MAX | Is there no eye that has a look of pity for me? |
| *(didascalia)* | [The COUNTESS turns away from him; he turns to the DUCHESS. |
| MAX | My mother! |
| DUCHESS | Go where duty calls you. Haply<br>The [[Time\|time]] may come, when you may prove to us<br>A true friend, a good angel at the throne<br>Of the Emperor. |
| MAX | You give me [[Hope\|hope]]; you would not<br>Suffer me wholly to [[Despair\|despair]]. No! No!<br>Mine is a certain misery — Thanks to heaven<br>That offers me a means of ending it. |
| *(didascalia)* | [The military music begins again. The stage fills more and more with armed men. MAX sees BUTLER, and addresses him. |
| MAX | And you here, Colonel Butler — and will you<br>Not follow me? [[Well]], then! remain more faithful<br>To your new lord, than you have proved yourself<br>To the Emperor. Come, Butler! promise me,<br>Give me your hand upon it, that you'll be<br>The guardian of his life, its shield, its watchman.<br>He is attainted, and his princely head<br>Fair booty for each slave that trades in murder.<br>Now he doth need the faithful eye of [[Friendship\|friendship]],<br>And those whom here I see — |
| *(didascalia)* | [Casting suspicious looks on ILLO and BUTLER. |
| ILLO | Go — seek for traitors<br>In Galas', in your father's quarters. Here<br>Is only one. Away! away! and free us<br>From his detested sight! Away! |
| *(didascalia)* | [MAX attempts once more to approach THEKLA. WALLENSTEIN prevents him. MAX stands irresolute, and in apparent anguish. In the mean [[Time\|time]] the stage fills more and more; and the horns sound from below louder and louder, and each [[Time\|time]] after a shorter interval. |
| MAX | Blow, blow! O were it but the Swedish Trumpets,<br>And all the naked swords, which I see here,<br>Were plunged into my breast! What purpose you?<br>You come to tear me from this place! Beware,<br>Ye drive me not in desperation. — Do it not!<br>Ye may repent it! |
| *(didascalia)* | [The stage is entirely filled with armed men. |
| MAX | Yet more! weight upon weight to drag me down!<br>Think what ye're doing. It is not [[Well\|well]] done<br>To choose a man despairing for your leader;<br>You tear me from my happiness. [[Well]], then,<br>I dedicate your souls to vengeance. Mark!<br>For your own ruin you have chosen me:<br>Who goes with me, must be prepared to perish. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He turns to the background, there ensues a sudden and violent movement among the Cuirassiers; they surround him, and carry him off in wild tumult. WALLENSTEIN remains immovable. THEKLA sinks into her mother's arms. The curtain falls. The music becomes loud and overpowering, and passes into a complete war-march — the orchestra joins it — and continues during the interval between the second and third Act. |
