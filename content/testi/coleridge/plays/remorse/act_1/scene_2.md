| *(didascalia)* | Enter TERESA and VALDEZ. |
| TERESA | I hold Ordonio dear; he is your son<br>And Alvar's brother. |
| VALDEZ | [[Love]] him for himself,<br>Nor make the living wretched for the dead. |
| TERESA | I mourn that you should plead in vain, Lord Valdez,<br>But heaven hath heard my vow, and I remain<br>Faithful to Alvar, be he dead or living. |
| VALDEZ | Heaven knows with what delight I saw your loves,<br>And could my heart's [[Blood\|blood]] give him back to thee<br>I would die smiling. But these are idle thoughts!<br>Thy dying father comes upon my [[Soul\|soul]]<br>With that same look, with which he gave thee to me;<br>I held thee in my arms a powerless babe,<br>While thy poor mother with a mute entreaty<br>Fixed her faint eyes on mine. Ah not for this,<br>That I should let thee feed thy [[Soul\|soul]] with gloom,<br>And with slow anguish wear away thy life,<br>The victim of a useless constancy.<br>I must not see thee wretched. |
| TERESA | There are woes<br>Ill bartered for the garishness of joy!<br>If it be wretched with an untired eye<br>To watch those skiey tints, and this green ocean;<br>Or in the sultry hour beneath some rock,<br>My hair dishevelled by the pleasant sea breeze,<br>To shape sweet visions, and live o'er again<br>All past hours of delight! If it be wretched<br>To watch some bark, and fancy Alvar there,<br>To go through each minutest circumstance<br>Of the blest meeting, and to frame adventures<br>Most terrible and strange, and hear him tell them;<br>(As once I knew a crazy Moorish maid<br>Who drest her in her buried [[Lover\|lover]]'s clothes,<br>And o'er the smooth [[spring]] in the mountain cleft<br>Hung with her lute, and played the selfsame tune<br>He used to play, and listened to the [[Shadow\|shadow]]<br>Herself had made) — if this be wretchedness,<br>And if indeed it be a wretched thing<br>To trick out mine own [[Death\|death]]-bed, and imagine<br>That I had died, died just ere his return!<br>Then see him listening to my constancy,<br>Or hover round, as he at midnight oft<br>Sits on my [[Grave\|grave]] and gazes at the [[Moon\|moon]];<br>Or haply in some more fantastic mood,<br>To be in Paradise, and with choice [[Flowers\|flowers]]<br>Build up a bower where he and I might dwell,<br>And there to wait his coming! O my sire!<br>My Alvar's sire! if this be wretchedness<br>That eats away the life, what were it, think you,<br>If in a most assured reality<br>He should return, and see a brother's infant<br>Smile at him from my arms?<br>Oh what a thought! |
| VALDEZ | A thought? even so! mere thought! an empty thought.<br>The very week he promised his return —— |
| TERESA | Was it not then a busy joy? to see him,<br>After those three years' travels! we had no fears —<br>The frequent tidings, the ne'er failing letter.<br>Almost endeared his absence! Yet the gladness,<br>The tumult of our joy! What then if now —— |
| VALDEZ | O power of youth to feed on pleasant thoughts,<br>Spite of conviction! I am old and heartless!<br>Yes, I am old — I have no pleasant fancies —<br>Hectic and unrefreshed with rest — |
| TERESA | My father! |
| VALDEZ | The sober [[Truth\|truth]] is all too much for me!<br>I see no sail which brings not to my mind<br>The [[Home\|home]]-bound bark in which my son was captured<br>By the Algerine — to perish with his captors! |
| TERESA | Oh no! he did not! |
| VALDEZ | Captured in sight of land!<br>From yon [[Hill\|hill]] point, nay, from our [[Castle\|castle]] watch-tower<br>We might have seen —— |
| TERESA | His capture, not his [[Death\|death]]. |
| VALDEZ | Alas! how aptly thou forget'st a tale<br>Thou ne'er didst wish to learn! my brave Ordonio<br>Saw both the pirate and his prize go down,<br>In the same [[Storm\|storm]] that baffled his own valour,<br>And thus twice snatched a brother from his hopes:<br>Gallant Ordonio! O beloved Teresa,<br>Would'st thou best prove thy [[Faith\|faith]] to generous Alvar,<br>And most delight his spirit, go, make thou<br>His brother happy, make his aged father<br>Sink to the [[Grave\|grave]] in joy. |
| TERESA | For [[Mercy\|mercy]]'s sake<br>Press me no more! I have no power to [[Love\|love]] him.<br>His proud forbidding eye, and his dark brow,<br>Chill me like dew-damps of the unwholesome [[Night\|night]]:<br>My [[Love\|love]], a timorous and tender flower,<br>Closes beneath his touch. |
| VALDEZ | You wrong him, maiden!<br>You wrong him, by my [[Soul\|soul]]! Nor was it [[Well\|well]]<br>To character by such unkindly phrases<br>The stir and workings of that [[Love\|love]] for you<br>Which he has toiled to smother. 'Twas not [[Well\|well]],<br>Nor is it grateful in you to forget<br>His wounds and perilous voyages, and how<br>With an heroic fearlessness of danger<br>He roam'd the coast of Afric for your Alvar.<br>It was not [[Well\|well]] — You have moved me even to [[Tears\|tears]]. |
| TERESA | Oh pardon me, Lord Valdez! pardon me!<br>It was a foolish and ungrateful speech,<br>A most ungrateful speech! But I am hurried<br>Beyond myself, if I but hear of one<br>Who aims to rival Alvar. Were we not<br>Born in one day, like twins of the same parent?<br>Nursed in one cradle? Pardon me, my father!<br>A six years' absence is a heavy thing,<br>Yet still the [[Hope\|hope]] survives —— |
| VALDEZ | Hush! 'tis Monviedro. |
| *(didascalia)* | Valdez looking forward. |
| TERESA | The Inquisitor! on what new scent of [[Blood\|blood]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter MONVIEDRO with ALHADRA. |
| MONVIEDRO | Peace and the [[Truth\|truth]] be with you! Good my Lord,<br>My present need is with your son.<br>We have hit the [[Time\|time]]. Here comes he! Yes, 'tis he. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter from the opposite side DON ORDONIO. |
| MONVIEDRO | My Lord Ordonio, this Moresco woman<br>(Alhadra is her name) asks audience of you. |
| ORDONIO | Hail, reverend father! what may be the business? |
| MONVIEDRO | My lord, on strong suspicion of relapse<br>To his false creed, so recently abjured,<br>The secret servants of the [[Inquisition]]<br>Have seized her husband, and at my command<br>To the supreme tribunal would have led him,<br>But that he made appeal to you, my lord,<br>As surety for his soundness in the [[Faith\|faith]].<br>Though lessoned by experience what small trust<br>The asseverations of these Moors deserve,<br>Yet still the deference to Ordonio's name,<br>Nor less the wish to prove, with what high [[Honour\|honour]]<br>The Holy Church regards her faithful soldiers,<br>Thus far prevailed with me that —— |
| ORDONIO | Reverend father,<br>I am much beholden to your high opinion,<br>Which so o'erprizes my light services. |
| *(didascalia)* | Then to ALHADRA. |
| ORDONIO | I would that I could serve you; but in [[Truth\|truth]]<br>Your face is new to me. |
| MONVIEDRO | My mind foretold me<br>That such would be the event. In [[Truth\|truth]], Lord Valdez,<br>'Twas little probable, that Don Ordonio,<br>That your illustrious son, who fought so bravely<br>Some four years since to quell these [[Rebel\|rebel]] Moors,<br>Should prove the patron of this infidel!<br>The warranter of a Moresco's [[Faith\|faith]]!<br>Now I return. |
| ALHADRA | My Lord, my husband's name<br>Is Isidore. (ORDONIO starts.) You may remember it:<br>Three years ago, three years this very week,<br>You left him at Almeria. |
| MONVIEDRO | Palpably false!<br>This very week, three years ago, my lord,<br>(You needs must recollect it by your wound)<br>You were at sea, and there engaged the pirates,<br>The murderers doubtless of your brother Alvar!<br>What, is he ill, my Lord? how strange he looks! |
| VALDEZ | You pressed upon him too abruptly, father!<br>The fate of one, on whom, you know, he doted. |
| ORDONIO | O Heavens! I? — I doted?<br>Yes! I doted on him. |
| *(didascalia)* | ORDONIO walks to the end of the stage, VALDEZ follows. |
| TERESA | I do not, can not, [[Love\|love]] him. Is my heart hard?<br>Is my heart hard? that even now the thought<br>Should force itself upon me? — Yet I feel it! |
| MONVIEDRO | The drops did start and stand upon his forehead!<br>I will return. In very [[Truth\|truth]], I grieve<br>To have been the occasion. Ho! attend me, woman! |
| ALHADRA | O gentle lady! make the father stay,<br>Until my lord recover. I am sure,<br>That he will say he is my husband's friend. |
| TERESA | Stay, father! stay! my lord will soon recover. |
| ORDONIO | Strange, that this Monviedro<br>Should have the power so to distemper me! |
| *(didascalia)* | As they return, to Valdez. |
| VALDEZ | Nay, 'twas an amiable weakness, son! |
| MONVIEDRO | My lord, I truly grieve —— |
| ORDONIO | Tut! name it not.<br>A sudden seizure, father! think not of it.<br>As to this woman's husband, I do know him.<br>I know him [[Well\|well]], and that he is a Christian. |
| MONVIEDRO | I [[Hope\|hope]], my lord, your merely human pity<br>Doth not prevail —— |
| ORDONIO | 'Tis certain that he was a catholic;<br>What changes may have happened in three years,<br>I can not say; but grant me this, good father:<br>Myself I'll sift him: if I find him sound,<br>You'll grant me your authority and name<br>To liberate his [[house]]. |
| MONVIEDRO | Your zeal, my lord,<br>And your late merits in this holy warfare<br>Would authorize an ampler trust — you have it. |
| ORDONIO | I will attend you [[Home\|home]] within an hour. |
| VALDEZ | Meantime return with us and take refreshment. |
| ALHADRA | Not till my husband's free! I may not do it.<br>I will stay here. |
| TERESA | Who is this Isidore? |
| *(didascalia)* | Aside. |
| VALDEZ | Daughter! |
| TERESA | With your permission, my dear lord,<br>I'll loiter yet awhile t' enjoy the sea breeze. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt VALDEZ, MONVIEDRO and ORDONIO. |
| ALHADRA | Hah! there he goes! a bitter curse go with him,<br>A scathing curse!<br>You hate him, don't you, lady? |
| TERESA | Oh [[Fear\|fear]] not me! my heart is sad for you. |
| ALHADRA | These fell inquisitors! these sons of [[Blood\|blood]]!<br>As I came on, his face so maddened me,<br>That ever and anon I clutched my dagger<br>And half unsheathed it —— |
| TERESA | Be more calm, I pray you. |
| ALHADRA | And as he walked along the narrow path<br>Close by the mountain's edge, my [[Soul\|soul]] grew eager;<br>'Twas with hard toil I made myself remember<br>That his Familiars held my babes and husband.<br>To have leapt upon him with a tiger's plunge,<br>And hurl'd him down the rugged precipice,<br>O, it had been most sweet! |
| TERESA | Hush! hush for shame!<br>Where is your woman's heart? |
| ALHADRA | O gentle lady!<br>You have no skill to guess my many wrongs,<br>Many and strange! Besides, I am a Christian,<br>And Christians never pardon—'tis their [[Faith\|faith]]! |
| TERESA | Shame fall on those who so have shewn it to thee! |
| ALHADRA | I know that man; 'tis [[Well\|well]] he knows not me.<br>Five years ago (and he was the prime agent),<br>Five years ago the holy brethren seized me. |
| TERESA | What might your crime be? |
| ALHADRA | I was a Moresco!<br>They cast me, then a young and nursing mother,<br>Into a dungeon of their [[Prison\|prison]] [[house]],<br>Where was no bed, no [[Fire\|fire]], no ray of light,<br>No touch, no sound of comfort! The black air,<br>It was a toil to breathe it! when the door,<br>Slow opening at the appointed hour, disclosed<br>One human countenance, the lamp's red flame<br>Cowered as it entered, and at once sank down.<br>Oh miserable! by that lamp to see<br>My infant quarrelling with the coarse hard bread<br>Brought daily; for the little wretch was sickly —<br>My rage had dried away its natural food.<br>In darkness I remained — the dull bell counting,<br>Which haply told me, that the all-cheering sun<br>Was rising on our [[Garden\|garden]]. When I dozed,<br>My infant's moanings mingled with my slumbers<br>And waked me. — If you were a mother, lady,<br>I should scarce dare to tell you, that its noises<br>And peevish cries so fretted on my brain<br>That I have struck the [[Innocent\|innocent]] babe in anger. |
| TERESA | O Heaven! it is too horrible to hear. |
| ALHADRA | What was it then to suffer? 'Tis most right<br>That such as you should hear it. — Know you not,<br>What [[Nature\|nature]] makes you mourn, she bids you heal?<br>Great evils ask great passions to redress them,<br>And whirlwinds fitliest scatter pestilence. |
| TERESA | You were at length released? |
| ALHADRA | Yes, at length<br>I saw the blessed arch of the whole heaven!<br>'Twas the first [[Time\|time]] my infant smiled. No more —<br>For if I dwell upon that moment, Lady,<br>A trance comes on which makes me o'er again<br>All I then was — my knees hang loose and drag,<br>And my lip falls with such an idiot laugh,<br>That you would start and shudder! |
| TERESA | But your husband — |
| ALHADRA | A month's [[Imprisonment\|imprisonment]] would kill him, Lady.<br>He hath a [[Lion\|lion]]'s [[Courage\|courage]],<br>Fearless in act, but feeble in [[Endurance\|endurance]];<br>Unfit for boisterous times, with gentle heart<br>He worships [[Nature\|nature]] in the [[Hill\|hill]] and valley,<br>Not knowing what he loves, but loves it all — |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter ALVAR disguised as a Moresco, and in Moorish garments. |
| TERESA | Know you that stately Moor? |
| ALHADRA | I know him not:<br>But [[Doubt\|doubt]] not he is some Moresco chieftain,<br>Who hides himself among the Alpujarras. |
| TERESA | The Alpujarras? Does he know his danger,<br>So near this seat? |
| ALHADRA | He wears the Moorish robes too,<br>As in defiance of the royal edict. |
| *(didascalia)* | ALHADRA advances to ALVAR, who has walked to the back of the stage, near the rocks. TERESA drops her veil. |
| ALHADRA | Gallant Moresco! An inquisitor,<br>Monviedro, of known hatred to our race —— |
| ALVAR | You have mistaken me. I am a Christian. |
| ALHADRA | He deems, that we are plotting to ensnare him:<br>Speak to him, Lady — none can hear you speak,<br>And not believe you [[Innocent\|innocent]] of guile. |
| TERESA | If aught enforce you to concealment, Sir — |
| ALHADRA | He trembles strangely. |
| *(didascalia)* | ALVAR sinks down and hides his face in his robe. |
| TERESA | See, we have disturbed him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Approaches nearer to him. |
| TERESA | I pray you, think us friends — uncowl your face,<br>For you seem faint, and the [[Night\|night]]-breeze blows healing.<br>I pray you, think us friends! |
| ALVAR | Calm, very calm!<br>'Tis all too tranquil for reality!<br>And she spoke to me with her [[Innocent\|innocent]] voice,<br>That voice, that [[Innocent\|innocent]] voice! She is no traitress! |
| TERESA | Let us retire |
| *(didascalia)* | Haughtily to Alhadra. |
| ALHADRA | He is indeed a Christian. |
| ALVAR | She deems me dead, yet wears no mourning garment!<br>Why should my brother's — wife — wear mourning garments? |
| *(didascalia)* | Aside. Then to TERESA. |
| ALVAR | Your pardon, noble dame! that I disturbed you:<br>I had just started from a frightful dream. |
| TERESA | [[Dreams]] tell but of the past, and yet, 'tis said,<br>They prophesy — |
| ALVAR | The Past lives o'er again<br>In its effects, and to the guilty spirit<br>The ever-frowning Present is its image. |
| TERESA | Traitress! |
| *(didascalia)* | Then aside. |
| TERESA | What sudden spell o'ermasters me?<br>Why seeks he me, shunning the Moorish woman? |
| ALVAR | I dreamt I had a friend, on whom I leant<br>With blindest trust, and a betrothéd maid,<br>Whom I was wont to call not mine, but me:<br>For mine own [[Self\|self]] seem'd nothing, lacking her.<br>This maid so idolized, that trusted friend<br>Dishonoured in my absence, [[Soul\|soul]] and [[Body\|body]]!<br>[[Fear]], following [[Guilt\|guilt]], tempted to blacker [[Guilt\|guilt]],<br>And murderers were suborned against my life.<br>But by my looks, and most impassioned words,<br>I roused the virtues that are dead in no man,<br>Even in the assassins' hearts! they made their terms,<br>And thanked me for redeeming them from murder. |
| ALHADRA | You are lost in thought: hear him no more, sweet Lady! |
| TERESA | From morn to [[Night\|night]] I am myself a dreamer,<br>And slight things bring on me the idle mood!<br>[[Well]] sir, what happened then? |
| ALVAR | On a rude rock,<br>A rock, methought, fast by a grove of firs,<br>Whose thready [[Leaves\|leaves]] to the low-breathing gale<br>Made a soft sound most like the distant ocean,<br>I stayed, as though the hour of [[Death\|death]] were passed,<br>And I were sitting in the world of spirits —<br>For all things seemed unreal! There I sate —<br>The dews fell clammy, and the [[Night\|night]] descended,<br>Black, sultry, close! and ere the midnight hour<br>A [[Storm\|storm]] came on, mingling all sounds of [[Fear\|fear]],<br>That woods, and sky, and [[Mountains\|mountains]], seemed one havock.<br>The second flash of [[Lightning\|lightning]] shewed a tree<br>Hard by me, newly scathed. I [[Rose\|rose]] tumultuous:<br>My [[Soul\|soul]] worked high, I bared my head to the [[Storm\|storm]],<br>And with loud voice and clamorous agony,<br>Kneeling I prayed to the great Spirit that made me,<br>Prayed, that Remorse might fasten on their hearts,<br>And cling with poisonous tooth, inextricable<br>As the gored [[Lion\|lion]]'s bite! |
| TERESA | A fearful curse! |
| ALHADRA | But dreamt you not that you returned and killed them?<br>Dreamt you of no [[Revenge\|revenge]]? |
| ALVAR | She would have died<br>Died in her [[Guilt\|guilt]] — perchance by her own hands!<br>And bending o'er her [[Self\|self]]-inflicted wounds,<br>I might have met the evil glance of frenzy,<br>And leapt myself into an unblest [[Grave\|grave]]!<br>I prayed for the punishment that cleanses hearts:<br>For still I loved her! |
| ALHADRA | And you dreamt all this? |
| TERESA | My [[Soul\|soul]] is full of visions all as wild! |
| ALHADRA | There is no room in this heart for puling [[Love\|love]]-tales. |
| *(didascalia)* | Teresa lifts up her veil, and advances to Alvar. |
| TERESA | Stranger, farewell! I guess not who you are,<br>Nor why you so addressed your tale to me.<br>Your mien is noble, and, I own, perplexed me,<br>With obscure [[Memory\|memory]] of something past,<br>Which still escaped my efforts, or presented<br>Tricks of a fancy pampered with long wishing.<br>If, as it sometimes happens, our rude startling,<br>Whilst your full heart was shaping out its dream,<br>Drove you to this, your not ungentle, wildness —<br>You have my sympathy, and so farewell!<br>But if some undiscovered wrongs oppress you,<br>And you need strength to drag them into light,<br>The generous Valdez, and my Lord Ordonio,<br>Have arm and will to aid a noble sufferer,<br>Nor shall you want my favourable pleading. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt TERESA and ALHADRA. |
| ALVAR | 'Tis strange! It cannot be! my Lord Ordonio!<br>Her Lord Ordonio! Nay, I will not do it!<br>I cursed him once — and one curse is enough!<br>How sad she looked, and pale! but not like [[Guilt\|guilt]] —<br>And her calm tones — sweet as a [[Song\|song]] of [[Mercy\|mercy]]!<br>If the bad spirit retain'd his angel's voice,<br>Hell scarce were Hell. And why not [[Innocent\|innocent]]?<br>Who meant to murder me, might [[Well\|well]] cheat her?<br>But ere she married him, he had stained her [[Honour\|honour]];<br>Ah! there I am hampered. What if this were a lie<br>Framed by the assassin? Who should tell it him,<br>If it were [[Truth\|truth]]? Ordonio would not tell him.<br>Yet why one lie? all else, I know, was [[Truth\|truth]].<br>No start, no [[Jealousy\|jealousy]] of stirring [[Conscience\|conscience]]!<br>And she referred to me — fondly, methought!<br>Could she walk here if she had been a traitress?<br>Here where we played together in our [[Childhood\|childhood]]?<br>Here where we plighted vows? where her cold cheek<br>Received my last kiss, when with suppressed feelings<br>She had fainted in my arms? It cannot be!<br>'Tis not in [[Nature\|nature]]! I will die believing,<br>That I shall meet her where no evil is,<br>No treachery, no cup dashed from the lips.<br>I'll haunt this scene no more! live she in peace!<br>Her husband — aye her husband! May this angel<br>New mould his canker'd heart! Assist me, heaven,<br>That I may pray for my poor guilty brother! |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Alone\|alone]]. Exit. |
