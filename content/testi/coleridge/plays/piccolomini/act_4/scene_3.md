| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | To them enters ILLO. |
| ILLO | Has he heard it? |
| TERTSKY | He has heard it. |
| ILLO | Thinkest thou still<br>To make thy peace with the Emperor, to regain<br>His confidence? — E'en were it now thy wish<br>To abandon all thy plans, yet still they know<br>What thou hast wished; then forwards thou must press;<br>Retreat is now no longer in thy power. |
| TERTSKY | They have documents against us, and in hands,<br>Which shew beyond all power of contradiction — |
| WALLENSTEIN | Of my hand-writing — no iota. Thee<br>I punish for thy lies. |
| ILLO | And thou believest,<br>That what this man, that what thy sister's husband,<br>Did in thy name, will not stand on thy reck'ning?<br>His word must pass for thy word with the Swede,<br>And not with those that hate thee at Vienna. |
| TERTSKY | In writing thou gav'st nothing — But bethink thee,<br>How far thou ventured'st by word of mouth<br>With this Sesina? And will he be silent?<br>If he can save himself by yielding up<br>Thy secret purposes, will he retain them? |
| ILLO | Thyself dost not conceive it possible;<br>And since they now have evidence authentic<br>How far thou hast already gone, speak! — tell us,<br>What art thou waiting for? thou canst no longer<br>Keep thy command; and beyond [[Hope\|hope]] of rescue<br>Thou'rt lost, if thou resign'st it. |
| WALLENSTEIN | In the army<br>Lies my security. The army will not<br>Abandon me. Whatever they may know,<br>The power is mine, and they must gulp it down —<br>And substitute I caution for my fealty,<br>They must be satisfied, at least appear so. |
| ILLO | The army, Duke, is thine now — for this moment —<br>'Tis thine: but think with terror on the slow,<br>The quiet power of [[Time\|time]]. From open violence<br>The attachment of thy soldiery secures thee<br>To-day — to-morrow; but grant'st thou them a respite,<br>Unheard, unseen, they'll undermine that [[Love\|love]]<br>On which thou now dost feel so firm a footing,<br>With wily theft will draw away from thee<br>One after the other —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | 'Tis a curséd accident! |
| ILLO | O, I will call it a most blessed one,<br>If it work on thee as it ought to do,<br>Hurry thee on to action — to decision.<br>The Swedish General —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | He's arrived! Know'st thou<br>What his commission is —— |
| ILLO | To thee [[Alone\|alone]]<br>Will he entrust the purpose of his coming. |
| WALLENSTEIN | A curséd, curséd accident! Yes, yes,<br>Sesina knows too much, and won't be silent. |
| TERTSKY | He's a Bohemian fugitive and [[Rebel\|rebel]],<br>His neck is forfeit. Can he save himself<br>At thy cost, think you he will scruple it?<br>And if they put him to the torture, will he,<br>Will he, that dastardling, have strength enough —— |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (lost in thought). |
| WALLENSTEIN | Their confidence is lost — irreparably!<br>And I may act what way I will, I shall<br>Be and remain for ever in their thought<br>A traitor to my country. How sincerely<br>Soever I return back to my duty,<br>It will no longer help me —— |
| ILLO | Ruin thee,<br>That it will do! Not thy fidelity,<br>Thy weakness will be deemed the sole occasion —— |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (pacing up and down in extreme agitation). |
| WALLENSTEIN | What! I must realize it now in earnest,<br>Because I toy'd too freely with the thought?<br>Accurséd he who dallies with a devil!<br>And must I — I must realize it now —<br>Now, while I have the power, it must take place? |
| ILLO | Now — now — ere they can ward and parry it! |
| WALLENSTEIN | I have the<br>Generals' word — a written promise!<br>Max Piccolomini stands not here — how's that? |
| TERTSKY | It was —— he fancied —— |
| ILLO | Mere [[Self\|self]]-willedness.<br>There needed no such thing 'twixt him and you. |
| WALLENSTEIN | He is quite right — there needeth no such thing.<br>The regiments, too, deny to march for Flanders —<br>Have sent me in a paper of remonstrance,<br>And openly resist the Imperial orders.<br>The first step to revolt's already taken. |
| ILLO | Believe me, thou wilt find it far more easy<br>To lead them over to the enemy<br>Than to the Spaniard. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I will hear, however,<br>What the Swede has to say to me. |
| ILLO | Go, call him!<br>He stands without the door in waiting. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Stay!<br>Stay yet a little. It hath taken me<br>All by surprise, — it came too quick upon me;<br>'Tis wholly [[Novel\|novel]], that an accident,<br>With its dark lordship, and blind agency,<br>Should force me on with it. |
| ILLO | First hear him only,<br>And after weigh it. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exeunt TERTSKY and ILLO.] |
