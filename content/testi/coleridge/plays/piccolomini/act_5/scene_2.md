| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, OCTAVIO PICCOLOMINI. |
| WALLENSTEIN | He sends me word from Linz, that he lies sick;<br>But I have sure intelligence, that he<br>Secretes himself at Frauenberg with Galas.<br>Secure them both, and send them to me hither.<br>Remember, thou tak'st on thee the command<br>Of those same Spanish regiments, — constantly<br>Make preparation, and be never ready;<br>And if they urge thee to draw out against me,<br>Still answer yes, and stand as thou wert fettered.<br>I know, that it is doing thee a service<br>To keep thee out of action in this business.<br>Thou lovest to linger on in fair appearances;<br>Steps of extremity are not thy province,<br>Therefore have I sought out this part for thee.<br>Thou wilt this [[Time\|time]] be of most service to me<br>By thy inertness. The mean [[Time\|time]], if fortune<br>Declare itself on my side, thou wilt know<br>What is to do. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter MAX PICCOLOMINI. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Now go, Octavio.<br>This [[Night\|night]] must thou be off, take my own horses:<br>Him here I keep with me — make short farewell —<br>Trust me, I think we all shall meet again<br>In joy and thriving fortunes. |
| OCTAVIO | *(to his son.)* I shall see you<br>Yet ere I go. |
