| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, TERTSKY. |
| TERTSKY | Max Piccolomini just left you? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Where is Wrangel? |
| TERTSKY | He is already gone. |
| WALLENSTEIN | In such a hurry? |
| TERTSKY | It is as if the earth had swallowed him.<br>He had scarce left thee, when I went to seek him.<br>I wished some words with him — but he was gone.<br>How, when, and where, could no one tell me. Nay,<br>I half believe it was the devil himself;<br>A human creature could not so at once<br>Have vanished. |
| *(didascalia)* | Illo (enters). |
| ILLO | Is it true that thou wilt send<br>Octavio? |
| TERTSKY | How, Octavio! Whither send him? |
| WALLENSTEIN | He goes to Frauenberg, and will lead hither<br>The Spanish and Italian regiments. |
| ILLO | No!<br>Nay, Heaven forbid! |
| WALLENSTEIN | And why should Heaven forbid? |
| ILLO | Him! — that deceiver! Would'st thou trust to him<br>The soldiery? Him wilt thou let slip from thee,<br>Now, in the very instant that decides us —— |
| TERTSKY | Thou wilt not do this! — No! I pray thee, no! |
| WALLENSTEIN | Ye are whimsical. |
| ILLO | O but for this [[Time\|time]], Duke,<br>Yield to our warning! Let him not depart. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And why should I not trust him only this [[Time\|time]],<br>Who have always trusted him? What, then, has happened,<br>That I should lose my good opinion of him?<br>In complaisance to your whims, not my own,<br>I must, forsooth, give up a rooted judgment.<br>Think not I am a woman. Having trusted him<br>E'en till to-day, to-day too will I trust him. |
| TERTSKY | Must it be he — he only? Send another. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It must be he, whom I myself have chosen;<br>He is [[Well\|well]] fitted for the business. Therefore<br>I gave it him. |
| ILLO | Because he's an Italian —<br>Therefore is he [[Well\|well]] fitted for the business. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I know you [[Love\|love]] them not — nor sire nor son —<br>Because that I esteem them, [[Love\|love]] them — visibly<br>Esteem them, [[Love\|love]] them more than you and others,<br>E'en as they merit. Therefore are they eye-blights,<br>Thorns in your foot-path. But your jealousies,<br>In what affect they me or my concerns?<br>Are they the worse to me because you hate them?<br>[[Love]] or hate one another as you will,<br>I leave to each man his own moods and likings;<br>Yet know the worth of each of you to me. |
| ILLO | Von Questenberg, while he was here, was always<br>Lurking about with this Octavio. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It happened with my knowledge and permission. |
| ILLO | I know that secret messengers came to him<br>From Galas —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | That's not true. |
| ILLO | O thou art blind<br>With thy deep-seeing eyes. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Thou wilt not shake<br>My [[Faith\|faith]] for me — my [[Faith\|faith]], which founds itself<br>On the profoundest science. If 'tis false,<br>Then the whole science of the [[Stars\|stars]] is false.<br>For know, I have a pledge from fate itself,<br>That he is the most faithful of my friends. |
| ILLO | Hast thou a pledge, that this pledge is not false? |
| WALLENSTEIN | There exist moments in the life of man,<br>When he is nearer the great [[Soul\|soul]] of the world<br>Than is man's custom, and possesses freely<br>The power of questioning his destiny:<br>And such a moment 'twas, when in the [[Night\|night]]<br>Before the action in the plains of Lützen,<br>Leaning against a tree, thoughts crowding thoughts,<br>I looked out far upon the ominous plain.<br>My whole life, past and future, in this moment<br>Before my mind's eye glided in procession,<br>And to the destiny of the next morning<br>The spirit, filled with anxious presentiment,<br>Did knit the most removed futurity.<br>Then said I also to myself, 'So many<br>Dost thou command. They follow all thy [[Stars\|stars]],<br>And as on some great number set their All<br>Upon thy single head, and only man<br>The vessel of thy fortune. Yet a day<br>Will come, when destiny shall once more scatter<br>All these in many a several direction:<br>Few be they who will stand out faithful to thee.'<br>I yearn'd to know which one was faithfullest<br>Of all, this camp included. Great Destiny,<br>Give me a sign! And he shall be the man,<br>Who, on the approaching morning, comes the first<br>To meet me with a token of his [[Love\|love]]:<br>And thinking this, I fell into a slumber.<br>Then midmost in the battle was I led<br>In spirit. Great the pressure and the tumult!<br>Then was my [[Horse\|horse]] killed under me: I sank:<br>And over me away, all unconcernedly,<br>Drove [[Horse\|horse]] and rider — and thus trod to pieces<br>I lay, and panted like a dying man.<br>Then seized me suddenly a saviour arm;<br>It was Octavio's — I awoke at once,<br>'Twas broad day, and Octavio stood before me.<br>'My brother,' said he,'do not ride to-day<br>The dapple, as you're wont; but mount the [[Horse\|horse]]<br>Which I have chosen for thee. Do it, brother!<br>In [[Love\|love]] to me. A strong dream warned me so.'<br>It was the swiftness of this [[Horse\|horse]] that snatched me<br>From the hot pursuit of Bannier's dragoons.<br>My cousin rode the dapple on that day.<br>And never more saw I or [[Horse\|horse]] or rider. |
| ILLO | That was a chance. |
| WALLENSTEIN | There's no such thing as chance.<br>In brief, 'tis signed and sealed that this Octavio<br>Is my good angel — and now no word more. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He is retiring.] |
| TERTSKY | This is my comfort — Max remains our hostage. |
| ILLO | And he shall never stir from here alive. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Are ye not like the women, who for ever<br>Only recur to their first word, although<br>One had been talking reason by the hour?<br>Know, that the human being's thoughts and deeds<br>Are not, like ocean billows, blindly moved.<br>The inner world, his microcosmus, is<br>The deep shaft, out of which they [[spring]] eternally.<br>They grow by certain laws, like the tree's fruit —<br>No juggling chance can metamorphose them.<br>Have I the human kernel first examined?<br>Then I know, too, the future will and action. |
