| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | To them enter BUTLER from the second table. |
| BUTLER | Don't disturb yourselves.<br>Field Marshal, I have understood you perfectly.<br>Good luck be to the scheme; and as to me,<br>You may depend upon me. |
| ILLO | May we, Butler? |
| BUTLER | With or without the clause, all one to me!<br>You understand me? My fidelity<br>The Duke may put to any proof — I'm with him!<br>Tell him so! I'm the Emperor's officer,<br>As long as 'tis his pleasure to remain<br>The Emperor's general! and Friedland's servant,<br>As soon as it shall please him to become<br>His own lord. |
| TERTSKY | You would make a good exchange.<br>No stern economist, no [[Ferdinand]],<br>Is he to whom you plight your services. |
| BUTLER | I do not put up my fidelity<br>To sale, Count Tertsky! Half a year ago<br>I would not have advised you to have made me<br>An overture to that, to which I now<br>Offer myself of my own free accord. —<br>But that is past! and to the Duke, Field Marshal,<br>I bring myself together with my regiment.<br>And mark you, 'tis my [[Humour\|humour]] to believe,<br>The example which I give will not remain<br>Without an influence. |
| ILLO | Who is ignorant,<br>That the whole army look to Colonel Butler,<br>As to a light that moves before them? |
| BUTLER | Ey?<br>Then I repent me not of that fidelity<br>Which for the length of forty years I held,<br>If in my sixtieth year my old good name<br>Can purchase for me a [[Revenge\|revenge]] so full.<br>Start not at what I say, sir Generals!<br>My real motives — they concern not you.<br>And you yourselves, I trust, could not expect<br>That this your game had crooked my judgment — or<br>That fickleness, quick [[Blood\|blood]], or such light cause,<br>Had driven the old man from the track of [[Honour\|honour]],<br>Which he so long had trodden. — Come, my friends!<br>I'm not thereto determined with less firmness,<br>Because I know and have looked steadily<br>At that on which I have determined. |
| ILLO | Say,<br>And speak roundly, what are we to deem you? |
| BUTLER | A friend! I give you here my hand! I'm yours<br>With all I have. Not only men, but money<br>Will the Duke want. —— Go, tell him, sirs!<br>I've earned and laid up somewhat in his service,<br>I lend it him; and is he my survivor,<br>It has been already long ago bequeathed him.<br>He is my heir. For me, I stand [[Alone\|alone]],<br>Here in the world; nought know I of the feeling<br>That binds the husband to a wife and children.<br>My name dies with me, my existence ends. |
| ILLO | 'Tis not your money that he needs — a heart<br>Like yours weighs tons of gold down, weighs down millions! |
| BUTLER | I came a simple soldier's boy from Ireland<br>To Prague — and with a master, whom I buried.<br>From lowest stable-duty I climbed up,<br>Such was the fate of war, to this high rank,<br>The plaything of a whimsical good fortune.<br>And Wallenstein too is a [[Child\|child]] of luck,<br>I [[Love\|love]] a fortune that is like my own. |
| ILLO | All powerful souls have kindred with each other. |
| BUTLER | This is an awful moment! to the brave,<br>To the determined, an auspicious moment.<br>The Prince of Weimar arms, upon the Maine<br>To found a mighty dukedom. He of Halberstadt,<br>That Mansfeld, wanted but a longer life<br>To have marked out with his good [[Sword\|sword]] a lordship<br>That should reward his [[Courage\|courage]]. Who of these<br>Equals our Friedland? there is nothing, nothing<br>So high, but he may set the ladder to it! |
| TERTSKY | That's spoken like a man! |
| BUTLER | Do you secure the Spaniard and Italian —<br>I'll be your warrant for the Scotchman Lesly.<br>Come! to the company! |
| TERTSKY | Where is the master of the cellar? Ho!<br>Let the best wines come up. Ho! cheerly, boy!<br>Luck comes to-day, so give her hearty welcome. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exeunt, each to his table. |
