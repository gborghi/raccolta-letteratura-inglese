| *(didascalia)* | To them enters the COUNTESS TERTSKY. |
| COUNTESS | Come!<br>My husband sends me for you — It is now<br>The latest moment.<br>Part you! |
| THEKLA | O, not yet!<br>It has been scarce a moment. |
| COUNTESS | Aye! Then [[Time\|time]]<br>Flies swiftly with your Highness, Princess niece! |
| MAX | There is no hurry, aunt. |
| COUNTESS | Away! Away!<br>The folks begin to miss you. Twice already<br>His father has asked for him. |
| THEKLA | Ha! his father? |
| COUNTESS | You understand that, niece! |
| THEKLA | Why needs he<br>To go at all to that society?<br>'Tis not his proper company. They may<br>Be worthy men, but he's too young for them.<br>In brief, he suits not such society. |
| COUNTESS | You mean, you'd rather keep him wholly here? |
| THEKLA | Yes! you have hit it, aunt! That is my meaning.<br>Leave him here wholly! Tell the company — |
| COUNTESS | What? have you lost your senses, niece? —<br>Count, you remember the conditions. Come! |
| MAX | Lady, I must obey. Farewell, dear lady! |
| *(didascalia)* | [THEKLA turns away from him with a quick motion. |
| MAX | What say you then, dear lady? |
| THEKLA | Nothing. Go! |
| MAX | Can I, when you are angry —— |
| *(didascalia)* | [He draws up to her, their eyes meet, she stands silent<br>a moment, then throws herself into his arms; he<br>presses her fast to his heart. |
| COUNTESS | Off! Heavens! if any one should come!<br>Hark! What's that noise? It comes this way. —— Off! |
| *(didascalia)* | [MAX [[Tears\|tears]] himself away out of her arms, and goes. The<br>COUNTESS accompanies him. THEKLA follows him with<br>her eyes at first, walks restlessly across the<br>room, then stops, and remains standing, lost in<br>thought. A guitar lies on the table, she seizes it<br>as by a sudden emotion, and after she has played a<br>while an irregular and [[Melancholy\|melancholy]] symphony, she<br>falls gradually into the music and sings. |
| THEKLA (plays and sings) | The cloud doth gather, the greenwood roar,<br>The [[Damsel\|damsel]] paces along the shore;<br>The billows they tumble with might, with might;<br>And she flings out her voice to the darksome [[Night\|night]];<br>Her bosom is swelling with sorrow;<br>The world it is empty, the heart will die,<br>There's nothing to wish for beneath the sky:<br>Thou Holy One, call thy [[Child\|child]] away!<br>I've lived and loved, and that was to-day —<br>Make ready my [[Grave\|grave]]-clothes to-morrow. |
| *(didascalia)* | LITERAL [[Translation\|TRANSLATION]]. |
| THEKLA (plays and sings) | The oak-forest bellows, the clouds gather, the [[Damsel\|damsel]] walks to and fro<br>on the green of the shore; the wave breaks with might, with might, and<br>she sings out into the dark [[Night\|night]], her eye discoloured with weeping: the<br>heart is dead, the world is empty, and further gives it nothing more to<br>the wish. Thou Holy One, call thy [[Child\|child]] [[Home\|home]]. I have enjoyed the<br>happiness of this world, I have lived and have loved. |
| *(didascalia)* | I cannot but add here an [[Imitation\|imitation]] of this [[Song\|song]], with which the author<br>of The Tale of Rosamond Gray and Blind Margaret has favoured me, and<br>which appears to me to have caught the happiest manner of our old<br>ballads.<br>The clouds are black'ning, the storms threat'ning,<br>The cavern doth mutter, the greenwood moan;<br>Billows are breaking, the [[Damsel\|damsel]]'s heart aching,<br>Thus in the dark [[Night\|night]] she singeth [[Alone\|alone]],<br>Her eye upward roving:<br>The world is empty, the heart is dead surely,<br>In this world plainly all seemeth amiss;<br>To thy heaven, Holy One, take [[Home\|home]] thy little one,<br>I have partaken of all earth's bliss,<br>Both living and loving. |
| *(didascalia)* | The text of [[Lamb]]'s version as printed in Works, 1818, i. 42 is as<br>follows:<br>BALLAD.<br>FROM THE GERMAN.<br>The clouds are blackening, the storms threatening,<br>And ever the forest maketh a moan:<br>Billows are breaking, the [[Damsel\|damsel]]'s heart aching,<br>Thus by herself she singeth [[Alone\|alone]],<br>Weeping right plenteously.<br>The world is empty, the heart is dead surely,<br>In this world plainly all seemeth amiss:<br>To thy breast, holy one, take now thy little one,<br>I have had earnest of all earth's bliss<br>Living most lovingly. |
