| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, COUNT TERTSKY. |
| WALLENSTEIN | She hath seen all things as they are — It is so<br>And squares completely with my other notices.<br>They have determined finally in Vienna,<br>Have given me my successor already;<br>It is the [[King\|king]] of Hungary, [[Ferdinand]],<br>The Emperor's delicate son! he's now their saviour,<br>He's the new star that's rising now! Of us<br>They think themselves already fairly rid,<br>And as we were deceased, the heir already<br>Is entering on possession — Therefore — dispatch! |
| *(didascalia)* | [As he turns round he observes TERTSKY, and gives him a letter. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Count Altringer will have himself excused,<br>And Galas too — I like not this! |
| TERTSKY | And if<br>Thou loiterest longer, all will fall away,<br>One following the other. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Altringer<br>Is master of the Tyrole passes. I must forthwith<br>Send some one to him, that he let not in<br>The Spaniards on me from the Milanese.<br>—— [[Well]], and the old Sesin, that ancient trader<br>In contraband negotiations, he<br>Has shewn himself again of late. What brings he<br>From the Count Thur? |
| TERTSKY | The Count communicates,<br>He has found out the Swedish chancellor<br>At Halberstadt, where the convention's held,<br>Who says, you've tired him out, and that he'll have<br>No further dealings with you. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And why so? |
| TERTSKY | speeches,<br>That you decoy the Swedes — to make fools of them,<br>Will league yourself with Saxony against them,<br>And at last make yourself a riddance of them<br>With a paltry sum of money. |
| WALLENSTEIN | So then, doubtless,<br>Yes, doubtless, this same modest Swede expects<br>That I shall yield him some fair German tract<br>For his prey and booty, that ourselves at last<br>On our own soil and native territory,<br>May be no longer our own lords and masters!<br>An excellent scheme! No, no! They must be off,<br>Off, off! away! we want no such neighbours. |
| TERTSKY | Nay, yield them up that dot, that speck of land —<br>It goes not from your portion. If you win<br>The game what matters it to you who pays it? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Off with them, off! Thou understand'st not this.<br>Never shall it be said of me, I parcelled<br>My native land away, dismembered [[Germany]],<br>Betrayed it to a foreigner, in order<br>To come with stealthy tread, and filch away<br>My own share of the plunder — Never! never! —<br>No foreign power shall strike root in the empire,<br>And least of all, these Goths! these hunger-wolves!<br>Who send such envious, hot and greedy glances<br>T'wards the rich blessings of our German lands!<br>I'll have their aid to cast and draw my nets,<br>But not a single fish of all the draught<br>Shall they come in for. |
| TERTSKY | You will deal, however,<br>More fairly with the Saxons? They lose patience<br>While you shift ground and make so many curves.<br>Say, to what purpose all these masks? Your friends<br>Are plunged in doubts, baffled, and led astray in you.<br>There's Oxenstirn, there's Arnheim — neither knows<br>What he should think of your procrastinations.<br>And in the end I prove the liar: all<br>Passes through me. I have not even your hand-writing. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I never give my handwriting; thou knowest it. |
| TERTSKY | But how can it be known that you're in earnest,<br>If the act follows not upon the word?<br>You must yourself acknowledge, that in all<br>Your intercourses hitherto with the enemy<br>You might have done with safety all you have done,<br>Had you meant nothing further than to gull him<br>For the Emperor's service. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And from whence dost thou know<br>That I'm not gulling him for the Emperor's service?<br>Whence knowest thou that I'm not gulling all of you?<br>Dost thou know me so [[Well\|well]]? When made I thee<br>The intendant of my secret purposes?<br>I am not conscious that I ever open'd<br>My inmost thoughts to thee. The Emperor, it is true,<br>Hath dealt with me amiss; and if I would,<br>I could repay him with usurious interest<br>For the evil he hath done me. It delights me<br>To know my power; but whether I shall use it,<br>Of that, I should have thought that thou could'st speak<br>No wiselier than thy fellows. |
| TERTSKY | So hast thou always played thy game with us. |
