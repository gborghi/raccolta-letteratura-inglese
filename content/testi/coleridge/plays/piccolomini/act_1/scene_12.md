| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, TERTSKY, ILLO. — To them enter QUESTENBERG, OCTAVIO, and MAX PICCOLOMINI, BUTLER, ISOLANI, MARADAS, and three other Generals. WALLENSTEIN motions QUESTENBERG, who in consequence takes the Chair directly opposite to him; the others follow, arranging themselves according to their rank. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I have understood, 'tis true, the sum and import<br>Of your instructions, Questenberg, have weighed them,<br>And formed my final, absolute resolve;<br>Yet it seems fitting, that the Generals<br>Should hear the will of the Emperor from your mouth.<br>May't please you then to open your commission<br>Before these noble Chieftains. |
| QUESTENBERG | I am ready<br>To obey you; but will first entreat your Highness,<br>And all these noble Chieftains, to consider,<br>The Imperial dignity and sovereign right<br>Speaks from my mouth, and not my own presumption. |
| WALLENSTEIN | We excuse all preface. |
| QUESTENBERG | When his Majesty<br>The Emperor to his courageous armies<br>Presented in the person of Duke Friedland<br>A most experienced and renowned commander,<br>He did it in glad [[Hope\|hope]] and confidence<br>To give thereby to the fortune of the war<br>A rapid and auspicious change. The onset<br>Was favourable to his royal wishes.<br>Bohemia was delivered from the Saxons,<br>The Swede's career of conquest checked! These lands<br>Began to draw breath freely, as Duke Friedland<br>From all the streams of [[Germany]] forced hither<br>The scattered armies of the enemy,<br>Hither invoked as round one [[Magic\|magic]] circle<br>The Rhinegrave, Bernhard, Banner, Oxenstirn,<br>Yea, and that never-conquered [[King]] himself;<br>Here finally, before the eye of Nürnberg,<br>The fearful game of battle to decide. |
| WALLENSTEIN | May't please you to the point. |
| QUESTENBERG | In Nürnberg's camp the Swedish monarch left<br>His fame — in Lützen's plains his life. But who<br>Stood not astounded, when victorious Friedland<br>After this day of triumph, this proud day,<br>Marched toward Bohemia with the [[Speed\|speed]] of flight,<br>And vanished from the theatre of war;<br>While the young Weimar [[Hero\|hero]] forced his way<br>Into Franconia, to the Danube, like<br>Some delving [[Winter\|winter]]-stream, which, where it rushes,<br>Makes its own channel; with such sudden [[Speed\|speed]]<br>He marched, and now at once 'fore Regenspurg<br>Stood to the affright of all good Catholic Christians.<br>Then did Bavaria's [[Well\|well]]-deserving Prince<br>Entreat swift aidance in his extreme need;<br>The Emperor sends seven horsemen to Duke Friedland,<br>Seven horsemen couriers sends he with the entreaty:<br>He superadds his own, and supplicates<br>Where as the sovereign lord he can command.<br>In vain his supplication! At this moment<br>The Duke hears only his old hate and grudge,<br>Barters the general good to gratify<br>Private [[Revenge\|revenge]] — and so falls Regenspurg. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Max, to what period of the war alludes he?<br>My recollection fails me here. |
| MAX | He means<br>When we were in Silesia. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Ay! Is it so!<br>But what had we to do there? |
| MAX | To beat out<br>The Swedes and Saxons from the province. |
| WALLENSTEIN | True.<br>In that description which the Minister gave<br>I seemed to have forgotten the whole war. |
| *(didascalia)* | [To QUESTENBERG. |
| WALLENSTEIN | [[Well]], but proceed a little. |
| QUESTENBERG | Yes! at length<br>Beside the [[River\|river]] Oder did the Duke<br>Assert his ancient fame. Upon the fields<br>Of Steinau did the Swedes lay down their arms,<br>Subdued without a blow. And here, with others,<br>The righteousness of Heaven to his [[Avenger\|avenger]]<br>Delivered that long-practised stirrer-up<br>Of insurrection, that curse-laden torch<br>And kindler of this war, Matthias Thur.<br>But he had fallen into magnanimous hands;<br>Instead of punishment he found reward,<br>And with rich presents did the Duke dismiss<br>The arch-foe of his Emperor. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I know,<br>I know you had already in Vienna<br>Your windows and balconies all forestalled<br>To see him on the executioner's cart.<br>I might have lost the battle, lost it too<br>With infamy, and still retained your graces —<br>But, to have cheated them of a spectacle,<br>Oh! that the good folks of Vienna never,<br>No, never can forgive me. |
| QUESTENBERG | So Silesia<br>Was freed, and all things loudly called the Duke<br>Into Bavaria, now pressed hard on all sides.<br>And he did put his troops in motion: slowly,<br>Quite at his ease, and by the longest [[Road\|road]]<br>He traverses Bohemia; but ere ever<br>He hath once seen the enemy, faces round,<br>Breaks up the march, and takes to [[Winter\|winter]] quarters. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The troops were pitiably destitute<br>Of every necessary, every comfort.<br>The [[Winter\|winter]] came. What thinks his Majesty<br>His troops are made of? Arn't we men? subjected<br>Like other men to wet, and cold, and all<br>The circumstances of necessity?<br>O miserable lot of the poor soldier!<br>Wherever he comes in, all flee before him,<br>And when he goes away, the general curse<br>Follows him on his route. All must be seized,<br>Nothing is given him. And compelled to seize<br>From every man, he's every man's abhorrence.<br>Behold, here stand my Generals. Karaffa!<br>Count Deodate! Butler! Tell this man<br>How long the soldiers' pay is in arrears. |
| BUTLER | Already a full year. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And 'tis the hire<br>That constitutes the hireling's name and duties,<br>The soldier's pay is the soldier's covenant. |
| QUESTENBERG | Ah! this is a far other tone from that<br>In which the Duke spoke eight, nine years ago. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Yes! 'tis my fault, I know it: I myself<br>Have spoilt the Emperor by indulging him.<br>Nine years ago, during the Danish war,<br>I raised him up a force, a mighty force,<br>Forty or fifty thousand men, that cost him<br>Of his own purse no doit. Through Saxony<br>The fury goddess of the war marched on,<br>E'en to the surf-rocks of the Baltic, bearing<br>The terrors of his name. That was a [[Time\|time]]!<br>In the whole Imperial realm no name like mine<br>Honoured with festival and celebration —<br>And Albrecht Wallenstein, it was the title<br>Of the third [[Jewel\|jewel]] in his [[Crown\|crown]]!<br>But at the Diet, when the Princes met<br>At Regenspurg, there, there the whole broke out,<br>There 'twas laid open, there it was made known,<br>Out of what money-bag I had paid the host.<br>And what was now my thank, what had I now,<br>That I, a faithful servant of the Sovereign,<br>Had loaded on myself the people's curses,<br>And let the Princes of the empire pay<br>The expenses of this war, that aggrandizes<br>The Emperor [[Alone\|alone]] — What thanks had I!<br>What? I was offered up to their complaints,<br>Dismissed, degraded! |
| QUESTENBERG | But your Highness knows<br>What little [[Freedom\|freedom]] he possessed of action<br>In that disastrous diet. |
| WALLENSTEIN | [[Death]] and hell!<br>I had that which could have procured him [[Freedom\|freedom]].<br>No! Since 'twas proved so inauspicious to me<br>To serve the Emperor at the empire's cost,<br>I have been taught far other trains of thinking<br>Of the empire, and the diet of the empire.<br>From the Emperor, doubtless, I received this staff,<br>But now I hold it as the empire's general —<br>For the common weal, the universal interest,<br>And no more for that one man's aggrandizement!<br>But to the point. What is it that's desired of me? |
| QUESTENBERG | First, his imperial Majesty hath willed<br>That without pretexts of delay the army<br>Evacuate Bohemia. |
| WALLENSTEIN | In this season?<br>And to what quarter wills the Emperor<br>That we direct our course? |
| QUESTENBERG | To the enemy.<br>His Majesty resolves, that Regenspurg<br>Be purified from the enemy, ere Easter,<br>That Lutheranism may be no longer preached<br>In that [[Cathedral\|cathedral]], nor heretical<br>Defilement desecrate the celebration<br>Of that pure festival. |
| WALLENSTEIN | My generals,<br>Can this be realized? |
| ILLO | 'Tis not possible. |
| BUTLER | It can't be realized. |
| QUESTENBERG | The Emperor<br>Already hath commanded Colonel Suys<br>To advance toward Bavaria! |
| WALLENSTEIN | What did Suys? |
| QUESTENBERG | That which his duty prompted. He advanced! |
| WALLENSTEIN | What? he advanced? And I, his general,<br>Had given him orders, peremptory orders,<br>Not to [[Desert\|desert]] his station! Stands it thus<br>With my authority? Is this the obedience<br>Due to my office, which being thrown aside<br>No war can be conducted? Chieftains, speak!<br>You be the judges, generals! What deserves<br>That officer, who of his oath neglectful<br>Is guilty of contempt of orders? |
| ILLO | [[Death]]. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Count Piccolomini! what has he deserved? |
| MAX PICCOLOMINI | According to the letter of the law,<br>[[Death]]. |
| ISOLANI | [[Death]]. |
| BUTLER | [[Death]], by the laws of war. |
| *(didascalia)* | [QUESTENBERG rises from his seat, WALLENSTEIN follows; all the rest rise. |
| WALLENSTEIN | To this the law condemns him, and not I.<br>And if I shew him favour, 'twill arise<br>From the reverence that I owe my Emperor. |
| QUESTENBERG | If so, I can say nothing further — here! |
| WALLENSTEIN | I accepted the command but on conditions!<br>And this the first, that to the diminution<br>Of my authority no human being,<br>Not even the Emperor's [[Self\|self]], should be entitled<br>To do aught, or to say aught, with the army.<br>If I stand warranter of the event,<br>Placing my [[Honour\|honour]] and my head in pledge,<br>Needs must I have full mastery in all<br>The means thereto. What rendered this Gustavus<br>Resistless, and unconquered upon earth?<br>This — that he was the monarch in his army!<br>A monarch, one who is indeed a monarch,<br>Was never yet subdued but by his equal.<br>But to the point! The best is yet to come.<br>Attend now, generals! |
| QUESTENBERG | The prince Cardinal<br>Begins his route at the approach of [[spring]]<br>From the Milanese; and leads a Spanish army<br>Through [[Germany]] into the Netherlands.<br>That he may march secure and unimpeded,<br>'Tis the Emperor's will you grant him a detachment<br>Of eight [[Horse\|horse]]-regiments from the army here. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Yes, yes! I understand! — Eight regiments! [[Well]],<br>Right [[Well\|well]] concerted, father Lamormain!<br>Eight thousand [[Horse\|horse]]! Yes, yes! 'Tis as it should be!<br>I see it coming! |
| QUESTENBERG | There is nothing coming.<br>All stands in front: the counsel of state-prudence,<br>The dictate of necessity! —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | What then?<br>What, my Lord Envoy? May I not be suffered<br>To understand, that folks are tired of seeing<br>The [[Sword\|sword]]'s hilt in my grasp: and that your [[Court\|court]]<br>Snatch eagerly at this pretence, and use<br>The Spanish title, to drain off my forces,<br>To lead into the empire a new army<br>Unsubjected to my control. To throw me<br>Plumply aside, — I am still too powerful for you<br>To venture that. My stipulation runs,<br>That all the Imperial forces shall obey me<br>Where'er the German is the native language.<br>Of Spanish troops and of Prince Cardinals<br>That take their route, as visitors, through the empire,<br>There stands no syllable in my stipulation.<br>No syllable! And so the politic [[Court\|court]]<br>Steals in a-tiptoe, and creeps round behind it;<br>First makes me weaker, then to be dispensed with,<br>Till it dares strike at length a bolder blow<br>And make short work with me.<br>What need of all these crooked ways, Lord Envoy?<br>Straight-forward man! His compact with me pinches<br>The Emperor. He would that I moved off! —<br>[[Well]]! — I will gratify him! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Here there commences an agitation among the Generals which increases continually. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It grieves me for my noble officers' sakes!<br>I see not yet, by what means they will come at<br>The moneys they have advanced, or how obtain<br>The recompense their services demand.<br>Still a new leader brings new claimants forward,<br>And prior merit superannuates quickly.<br>There serve here many foreigners in the army,<br>And were the man in all else brave and gallant,<br>I was not wont to make nice scrutiny<br>After his pedigree or catechism.<br>This will be otherwise, i'the [[Time\|time]] to come.<br>[[Well]] — me no longer it concerns. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He seats himself. |
| MAX PICCOLOMINI | Forbid it. Heaven, that it should come to this!<br>Our troops will swell in dreadful fermentation —<br>The Emperor is abused — it cannot be. |
| ISOLANI | It cannot be; all goes to instant wreck. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Thou hast said truly, faithful Isolani!<br>What we with toil and foresight have built up,<br>Will go to wreck — all go to instant wreck.<br>What then? another chieftain is soon found,<br>Another army likewise (who dares [[Doubt\|doubt]] it?)<br>Will flock from all sides to the Emperor<br>At the first beat of his recruiting drum. |
| *(didascalia)* | [During this speech, ISOLANI, TERTSKY, ILLO and MARADAS talk confusedly with great agitation. |
| MAX PICCOLOMINI | Hear, my commander! Hear me, generals!<br>Let me conjure you, Duke! Determine nothing,<br>Till we have met and represented to you<br>Our joint remonstrances. — Nay, calmer! Friends!<br>I [[Hope\|hope]] all may be yet set right again. |
| TERTSKY | Away! let us away! in the antechamber<br>Find we the others. |
| *(didascalia)* | [They go. |
| BUTLER | If good counsel gain<br>Due audience from your wisdom, my Lord Envoy!<br>You will be cautious how you shew yourself<br>In public for some hours to come — or hardly<br>Will that gold key protect you from maltreatment. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Commotions heard from without. |
| WALLENSTEIN | A salutary counsel —— Thou, Octavio!<br>Wilt answer for the safety of our guest.<br>Farewell, Von Questenberg! |
| *(didascalia)* | [QUESTENBERG is about to speak. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Nay, not a word.<br>Not one word more of that detested subject!<br>You have performed your duty — We know how<br>To separate the office from the man. |
| *(didascalia)* | [As QUESTENBERG is going off with OCTAVIO, GOETZ, TIEFENBACH, KOLATTO, press in; several other Generals following them. |
| GOETZ | Where's he who means to rob us of our general? |
| TIEFENBACH | What are we forced to hear?<br>That thou wilt leave us? |
| KOLATTO | We will live with thee, we will die with thee. |
| WALLENSTEIN | There! the Field-Marshal knows our will. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit. |
