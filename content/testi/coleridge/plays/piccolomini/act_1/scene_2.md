| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter OCTAVIO PICCOLOMINI and QUESTENBERG. |
| OCTAVIO | Ay, ay! more still! Still more new visitors!<br>Acknowledge, friend! that never was a camp,<br>Which held at once so many heads of heroes.<br>Welcome, Count Isolani! |
| ISOLANI | My noble brother,<br>Even now am I arrived; it had been else my duty — |
| OCTAVIO | And Colonel Butler — trust me, I rejoice<br>Thus to renew acquaintance with a man<br>Whose worth and services I know and [[Honour\|honour]].<br>See, see, my friend!<br>There might we place at once before our eyes<br>The sum of war's whole trade and mystery — |
| *(didascalia)* | [To QUESTENBERG, presenting BUTLER and ISOLANI at the same [[Time\|time]] to him. |
| OCTAVIO | These two the total sum — Strength and Dispatch. |
| QUESTENBERG | (to Octavio). And lo! betwixt them both experienced<br>Prudence! |
| OCTAVIO | (presenting Questenberg to Butler and Isolani). The<br>Chamberlain and War-commissioner Questenberg,<br>The bearer of the Emperor's behests,<br>The long-tried friend and patron of all soldiers,<br>We [[Honour\|honour]] in this noble visitor. |
| ILLO | 'Tis not the first [[Time\|time]], noble Minister,<br>You have shewn our camp this [[Honour\|honour]]. |
| QUESTENBERG | Once before<br>I stood before these colours. |
| ILLO | Perchance too you remember where that was.<br>It was at Znäim in Moravia, where<br>You did present yourself upon the part<br>Of the Emperor, to supplicate our Duke<br>That he would straight assume the chief command. |
| QUESTENBERG | To supplicate? Nay, noble General!<br>So far extended neither my commission<br>(At least to my own knowledge) nor my zeal. |
| ILLO | [[Well]], [[Well\|well]], then — to compel him, if you choose.<br>I can remember me right [[Well\|well]], Count Tilly<br>Had suffered total rout upon the Lech.<br>Bavaria lay all open to the enemy,<br>Whom there was nothing to delay from pressing<br>Onwards into the very heart of Austria.<br>At that [[Time\|time]] you and Werdenberg appeared<br>Before our General, storming him with prayers,<br>And menacing the Emperor's displeasure,<br>Unless he took compassion on this wretchedness. |
| ISOLANI | Yes, yes, 'tis comprehensible enough,<br>Wherefore with your commission of to-day<br>You were not all too willing to remember<br>Your former one. |
| QUESTENBERG | Why not, Count Isolan?<br>No contradiction sure exists between them.<br>It was the urgent business of that [[Time\|time]]<br>To snatch Bavaria from her enemy's hand;<br>And my commission of to-day instructs me<br>To free her from her good friends and protectors. |
| ILLO | A worthy office! After with our [[Blood\|blood]]<br>We have wrested this Bohemia from the Saxon,<br>To be swept out of it is all our thanks,<br>The sole reward of all our hard-won victories. |
| QUESTENBERG | Unless that wretched land be doomed to suffer<br>Only a change of evils, it must be<br>Freed from the scourge alike of friend and foe. |
| ILLO | What? 'Twas a favourable year; the Boors<br>Can answer fresh demands already. |
| QUESTENBERG | Nay,<br>If you discourse of herds and meadow-grounds — |
| ISOLANI | The war maintains the war. Are the Boors ruined,<br>The Emperor gains so many more new soldiers. |
| QUESTENBERG | And is the poorer by even so many subjects. |
| ISOLANI | Poh! We are all his subjects. |
| QUESTENBERG | Yet with a difference, General! The one fill<br>With profitable industry the purse,<br>The others are [[Well\|well]] skilled to empty it.<br>The [[Sword\|sword]] has made the Emperor poor; the plough<br>Must reinvigorate his resources. |
| ISOLANI | Sure!<br>Times are not yet so bad. Methinks I see |
| *(didascalia)* | [Examining with his eye the dress and ornaments of QUESTENBERG. |
| ISOLANI | Good store of gold that still remains uncoined. |
| QUESTENBERG | Thank Heaven! that means have been found out to hide<br>Some little from the fingers of the Croats. |
| ILLO | There! The Stawata and the Martinitz,<br>On whom the Emperor heaps his gifts and graces,<br>To the heart-burning of all good Bohemians —<br>Those minions of [[Court\|court]] favour, those [[Court\|court]] harpies,<br>Who fatten on the wrecks of citizens<br>Driven from their [[house]] and [[Home\|home]] — who reap no harvests<br>Save in the general calamity —<br>Who now, with kingly pomp, insult and mock<br>The desolation of their country — these,<br>Let these, and such as these, support the war,<br>The fatal war, which they [[Alone\|alone]] enkindled! |
| BUTLER | And those state-parasites, who have their feet<br>So constantly beneath the Emperor's table,<br>Who cannot let a benefice fall, but they<br>Snap at it with dog's hunger — they, forsooth,<br>Would pare the soldier's bread, and [[Cross\|cross]] his reckoning! |
| ISOLANI | My life long will it anger me to think,<br>How when I went to [[Court\|court]] seven years ago,<br>To see about new horses for our regiment,<br>How from one antechamber to another<br>They dragged me on, and left me by the hour<br>To kick my heels among a crowd of simpering<br>Feast-fattened slaves, as if I had come thither<br>A mendicant suitor for the crumbs of favour<br>That fall beneath their tables. And, at last,<br>Whom should they send me but a Capuchin!<br>Straight I began to muster up my sins<br>For absolution — but no such luck for me!<br>This was the man, this Capuchin, with whom<br>I was to treat concerning the army horses:<br>And I was forced at last to quit the field,<br>The business unaccomplished. Afterwards<br>The Duke procured me in three days, what I<br>Could not obtain in thirty at Vienna. |
| QUESTENBERG | Yes, yes! your travelling bills soon found their way to us:<br>Too [[Well\|well]] I know we have still accounts to settle. |
| ILLO | War is a violent trade; one cannot always<br>Finish one's work by soft means; every trifle<br>Must not be blackened into sacrilege.<br>If we should wait till you, in solemn council,<br>With due deliberation had selected<br>The smallest out of four-and-twenty evils,<br>I'[[Faith\|faith]], we should wait long. —<br>'Dash! and through with it!' — That's the better watch-word.<br>Then after come what may come. 'Tis man's [[Nature\|nature]]<br>To make the best of a bad thing once past.<br>A bitter and perplexed 'what shall I do?'<br>Is worse to man than worst necessity. |
| QUESTENBERG | Ay, doubtless, it is true: the Duke does spare us<br>The troublesome task of choosing. |
| BUTLER | Yes, the Duke<br>Cares with a father's feelings for his troops;<br>But how the Emperor feels for us, we see. |
| QUESTENBERG | His cares and feelings all ranks share alike,<br>Nor will he offer one up to another. |
| ISOLANI | And therefore thrusts he us into the deserts<br>As beasts of prey, that so he may preserve<br>His dear sheep fattening in his fields at [[Home\|home]]. |
| QUESTENBERG | Count, this comparison you make, not I. |
| BUTLER | Why, were we all the [[Court]] supposes us,<br>'Twere dangerous, sure, to give us liberty. |
| QUESTENBERG | You have taken liberty — it was not given you.<br>And therefore it becomes an urgent duty<br>To rein it in with curbs. |
| OCTAVIO | My noble friend,<br>This is no more than a remembrancing<br>That you are now in camp, and among warriors.<br>The soldier's boldness constitutes his [[Freedom\|freedom]].<br>Could he act daringly, unless he dared<br>Talk even so? One runs into the other.<br>The boldness of this worthy officer, |
| *(didascalia)* | [pointing to BUTLER. |
| OCTAVIO | Which now has but mistaken in its mark,<br>Preserved, when nought but boldness could preserve it,<br>To the Emperor his capital [[City\|city]], Prague,<br>In a most formidable mutiny<br>Of the whole garrison. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Military music at a distance. |
| OCTAVIO | Hah! here they come! |
| ILLO | The sentries are saluting them: this signal<br>Announces the arrival of the Duchess. |
| OCTAVIO | Then my son Max too has returned. 'Twas he<br>Fetched and attended them from Carnthen hither. |
| ISOLANI | (to Illo). Shall we not go in company to greet them? |
| ILLO | [[Well]], let us go. — Ho! Colonel Butler, come. |
| *(didascalia)* | [To OCTAVIO. |
| ILLO | You'll not forget, that yet ere [[Noon\|noon]] we meet<br>The noble Envoy at the General's palace. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exeunt all but QUESTENBERG and OCTAVIO. |
