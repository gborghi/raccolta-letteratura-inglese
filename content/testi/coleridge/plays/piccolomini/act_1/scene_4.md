| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | MAX PICCOLOMINI, OCTAVIO PICCOLOMINI, QUESTENBERG. |
| MAX | Ha! there he is himself. Welcome, my father!<br>You are engaged, I see. I'll not disturb you. |
| OCTAVIO | How, Max? Look closer at this visitor;<br>Attention, Max, an old friend merits — Reverence<br>Belongs of right to the envoy of your sovereign. |
| MAX | Von Questenberg! — Welcome — if you bring with you<br>Aught good to our head quarters. |
| QUESTENBERG | Nay, draw not<br>Your hand away, Count Piccolomini!<br>Not on mine own account [[Alone\|alone]] I seized it,<br>And nothing common will I say therewith. |
| *(didascalia)* | Taking the hands of both. |
| QUESTENBERG | Octavio — Max Piccolomini!<br>O saviour names, and full of happy omen!<br>Ne'er will her prosperous genius turn from Austria,<br>While two such [[Stars\|stars]], with blessed influences<br>Beaming protection, shine above her hosts. |
| MAX | Heh! — Noble minister! You miss your part.<br>You came not here to act a panegyric.<br>You're sent, I know, to find fault and to scold us —<br>I must not be beforehand with my comrades. |
| OCTAVIO | He comes from [[Court\|court]], where people are not quite<br>So [[Well\|well]] contented with the duke, as here. |
| MAX | What now have they contrived to find out in him?<br>That he [[Alone\|alone]] determines for himself<br>What he himself [[Alone\|alone]] doth understand?<br>[[Well]], therein he does right, and will persist in 't.<br>Heaven never meant him for that passive thing<br>That can be struck and hammered out to suit<br>Another's taste and fancy. He'll not [[Dance\|dance]]<br>To every tune of every minister.<br>It goes against his [[Nature\|nature]] — he can't do it.<br>He is possessed by a commanding spirit,<br>And his too is the station of command.<br>And [[Well\|well]] for us it is so! There exist<br>Few fit to rule themselves, but few that use<br>Their intellects intelligently. — Then<br>[[Well]] for the whole, if there be found a man,<br>Who makes himself what [[Nature\|nature]] destined him,<br>The pause, the central point to thousand thousands —<br>Stands fixed and stately, like a firm-built column,<br>Where all may press with joy and confidence.<br>Now such a man is Wallenstein; and if<br>Another better suits the [[Court\|court]] — no other<br>But such a one as he can serve the army. |
| QUESTENBERG | The army? Doubtless! |
| OCTAVIO | *(aside)* Hush! suppress it, friend!<br>Unless some end were answered by the utterance. —<br>Of him there you'll make nothing. |
| MAX | In their distress<br>They call a spirit up, and when he comes,<br>Straight their flesh creeps and quivers, and they dread him<br>More than the ills for which they called him up.<br>The uncommon, the [[Sublime\|sublime]], must seem and be<br>Like things of every day. — But in the field,<br>Aye, there the Present Being makes itself felt.<br>The personal must command, the actual eye<br>Examine. If to be the chieftain asks<br>All that is great in [[Nature\|nature]], let it be<br>Likewise his privilege to move and act<br>In all the correspondencies of [[Greatness\|greatness]].<br>The oracle within him, that which lives,<br>He must invoke and question — not dead books,<br>Not ordinances, not mould-rotted papers. |
| OCTAVIO | My son! of those old narrow ordinances<br>Let us not hold too lightly. They are weights<br>Of priceless value, which oppressed mankind<br>Tied to the volatile will of their oppressors.<br>For always formidable was the league<br>And partnership of free power with free will.<br>The way of ancient ordinance, though it winds,<br>Is yet no devious way. Straight forward goes<br>The [[Lightning\|lightning]]'s path, and straight the fearful path<br>Of the cannon-ball. Direct it flies and rapid,<br>Shattering that it may reach, and shattering what it reaches.<br>My son! the [[Road\|road]] the human being travels,<br>That on which blessing comes and goes, doth follow<br>The [[River\|river]]'s course, the valley's playful windings,<br>Curves round the corn-field and the [[Hill\|hill]] of vines,<br>Honouring the holy bounds of property!<br>And thus secure, though late, leads to its end. |
| QUESTENBERG | O hear your father, noble youth! hear him,<br>Who is at once the [[Hero\|hero]] and the man. |
| OCTAVIO | My son, the nursling of the camp spoke in thee!<br>A war of fifteen years<br>Hath been thy [[Education\|education]] and thy school.<br>Peace hast thou never witnessed! There exists<br>A higher than the warrior's excellence.<br>In war itself war is no ultimate purpose.<br>The vast and sudden deeds of violence,<br>Adventures wild, and wonders of the moment,<br>These are not they, my son, that generate<br>The calm, the blissful, and the enduring mighty!<br>Lo there! the soldier, rapid architect!<br>Builds his light town of canvas, and at once<br>The whole scene moves and bustles momently,<br>With arms, and neighing steeds, and mirth and quarrel<br>The motley market fills; the roads, the streams<br>Are crowded with new freights, trade stirs and hurries!<br>But on some morrow morn, all suddenly,<br>The tents drop down, the horde renews its march.<br>Dreary, and solitary as a church-yard<br>The meadow and down-trodden seed-plot lie,<br>And the year's harvest is gone utterly. |
| MAX | O let the Emperor make peace, my father!<br>Most gladly would I give the [[Blood\|blood]]-stained laurel<br>For the first violet of the leafless [[spring]],<br>Plucked in those quiet fields where I have journeyed! |
| OCTAVIO | What ails thee? What so moves thee all at once? |
| MAX | Peace have I ne'er beheld? I have beheld it.<br>From thence am I come hither: O! that sight,<br>It glimmers still before me, like some landscape<br>Left in the distance, — some delicious landscape!<br>My [[Road\|road]] conducted me through countries where<br>The war has not yet reached. Life, life, my father —<br>My venerable father, life has charms<br>Which we have ne'er experienced. We have been<br>But voyaging along its barren coasts,<br>Like some poor ever-roaming horde of pirates,<br>That, crowded in the rank and narrow [[Ship\|ship]],<br>[[house\|House]] on the wild sea with wild usages,<br>Nor know aught of the main land, but the bays<br>Where safeliest they may venture a thieves' landing.<br>Whate'er in the inland dales the land conceals<br>Of fair and exquisite, O! nothing, nothing,<br>Do we behold of that in our rude voyage. |
| OCTAVIO | And so your [[Journey\|journey]] has revealed this to you? |
| MAX | 'Twas the first leisure of my life. O tell me,<br>What is the meed and purpose of the toil,<br>The painful toil, which robbed me of my youth,<br>Left me a heart unsoul'd and solitary,<br>A spirit uninformed, unornamented.<br>For the camp's stir and crowd and ceaseless larum,<br>The neighing war-[[Horse\|horse]], the air-shattering trumpet,<br>The unvaried, still-returning hour of duty,<br>Word of command, and exercise of arms —<br>There's nothing here, there's nothing in all this<br>To satisfy the heart, the gasping heart!<br>Mere bustling nothingness, where the [[Soul\|soul]] is not —<br>This cannot be the sole felicity,<br>These cannot be man's best and only pleasures. |
| OCTAVIO | Much hast thou learnt, my son, in this short [[Journey\|journey]]. |
| MAX | O! day thrice lovely! when at length the soldier<br>Returns [[Home\|home]] into life; when he becomes<br>A fellow-man among his fellow-men.<br>The colours are unfurled, the cavalcade<br>Marshals, and now the buzz is hushed, and hark!<br>Now the soft peace-march beats, [[Home\|home]], brothers, [[Home\|home]]!<br>The caps and helmets are all garlanded<br>With green boughs, the last plundering of the fields.<br>The [[City\|city]] gates fly open of themselves,<br>They need no longer the petard to tear them.<br>The ramparts are all filled with men and women,<br>With peaceful men and women, that send onwards<br>Kisses and welcomings upon the air,<br>Which they make breezy with affectionate gestures.<br>From all the towers rings out the merry peal,<br>The joyous vespers of a bloody day.<br>O happy man, O fortunate! for whom<br>The [[Well\|well]]-known door, the faithful arms are open,<br>The faithful tender arms with mute embracing. |
| QUESTENBERG | O! that you should speak<br>Of such a distant, distant [[Time\|time]], and not<br>Of the to-morrow, not of this to-day. |
| MAX | Where lies the fault but on you in Vienna?<br>I will deal openly with you, Questenberg.<br>Just now, as first I saw you standing here,<br>(I'll own it to you freely) indignation<br>Crowded and pressed my inmost [[Soul\|soul]] together.<br>'Tis ye that hinder peace, ye! — and the warrior,<br>It is the warrior that must force it from you.<br>Ye fret the General's life out, blacken him,<br>Hold him up as a [[Rebel\|rebel]], and Heaven knows<br>What else still worse, because he spares the Saxons,<br>And tries to awaken confidence in the enemy;<br>Which yet 's the only way to peace: for if<br>War intermit not during war, how then<br>And whence can peace come? — Your own plagues fall on you!<br>Even as I [[Love\|love]] what's virtuous, hate I you.<br>And here make I this vow, here pledge myself;<br>My [[Blood\|blood]] shall spurt out for this Wallenstein,<br>And my heart drain off, drop by drop, ere ye<br>Shall revel and [[Dance\|dance]] jubilee o'er his ruin. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit. |
