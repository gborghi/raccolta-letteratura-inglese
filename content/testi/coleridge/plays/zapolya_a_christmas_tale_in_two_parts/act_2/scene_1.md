| *(didascalia)* | A Mountainous Country. BATHORY’S Dwelling at the end of the Stage. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter LADY SAROLTA and GLYCINE. |
| GLYCINE | [[Well]] then! our round of [[Charity|charity]] is finished.<br>Rest, Madam! You breathe quick. |
| SAROLTA | What, tired, Glycine?<br>No delicate [[Court|court]]-dame, but a mountaineer<br>By choice no less than birth, I gladly use<br>The good strength [[Nature]] gave me. |
| GLYCINE | That last cottage<br>Is built as if an eagle or a raven<br>Had chosen it for her nest. |
| SAROLTA | So many are<br>The sufferings which no human aid can reach,<br>It needs must be a duty doubly sweet<br>To heal the few we can. [[Well]]! let us rest. |
| GLYCINE | There?   [Pointing to BATHORY’S dwelling. |
| SAROLTA | Here! For on this spot Lord Casimir<br>Took his last leave. On yonder mountain-ridge<br>I lost the misty image which so long<br>Lingered, or seemed at least to linger on it. |
| GLYCINE | And what if even now, on that same ridge,<br>A speck should rise, and still enlarging, lengthening,<br>As it clomb downwards, shape itself at last<br>To a numerous cavalcade, and spurring foremost,<br>Who but Sarolta’s own dear lord returned<br>From his high embassy? |
| SAROLTA | Thou hast hit my thought!<br>All the long day, from yester-morn to evening,<br>The restless [[Hope|hope]] fluttered about my heart.<br>Oh we are querulous creatures! Little less<br>Than all things can suffice to make us happy;<br>And little more than nothing is enough<br>To discontent us. — Were he come, then should I<br>Repine he had not arrived just one day earlier<br>To keep his birth-day here, in his own birth-place. |
| GLYCINE | But our best sports belike, and gay processions<br>Would to my lord have seemed but work-day sights<br>Compared with those the royal [[Court|court]] affords. |
| SAROLTA | I have small wish to see them. A [[spring]] morning<br>With its wild gladsome minstrelsy of [[Birds|birds]]<br>And its bright jewelry of [[Flowers|flowers]] and dew-drops<br>(Each orbéd drop an orb of glory in it)<br>Would put them all in eclipse. This sweet retirement<br>Lord Casimir’s wish [[Alone|alone]] would have made sacred:<br>But, in good [[Truth|truth]], his loving [[Jealousy|jealousy]]<br>Did but command, what I had else entreated. |
| GLYCINE | And yet had I been born Lady Sarolta,<br>Been wedded to the noblest of the realm,<br>So beautiful besides, and yet so stately —— |
| SAROLTA | Hush! [[Innocent]] flatterer! |
| GLYCINE | Nay! to my poor fancy<br>The royal [[Court|court]] would seem an earthly heaven,<br>Made for such [[Stars|stars]] to shine in, and be gracious. |
| SAROLTA | So doth the ignorant distance still delude us!<br>Thy fancied heaven, dear girl, like that above thee,<br>In its mere [[Self|self]] cold, drear, colourless void,<br>Seen from below and in the large, becomes<br>The bright blue ether, and the seat of gods!<br>[[Well]]! but this broil that scared you from the [[Dance|dance]]?<br>And was not Laska there: he, your betrothed? |
| GLYCINE | Yes, madam! he was there. So was the maypole,<br>For we danced round it. |
| SAROLTA | Ah, Glycine! why,<br>Why did you then betroth yourself? |
| GLYCINE | Because<br>My own dear lady wished it! ‘twas you asked me! |
| SAROLTA | Yes, at my lord’s request, but never wished,<br>My poor affectionate girl, to see thee wretched.<br>Thou knowest not yet the duties of a wife. |
| GLYCINE | Oh, yes! It is a wife’s chief duty, madam!<br>To stand in awe of her husband, and obey him,<br>And, I am sure, I never shall see Laska<br>But I shall tremble. |
| SAROLTA | Not with [[Fear|fear]], I think,<br>For you still mock him. Bring a seat from the cottage.<br>[Exit GLYCINE into the cottage, SAROLTA continues her speech looking after her.<br>Something above thy rank there hangs about thee,<br>And in thy countenance, thy voice, and motion,<br>Yea, e’en in thy simplicity, Glycine,<br>A fine and feminine grace, that makes me feel<br>More as a mother than a mistress to thee!<br>Thou art a soldier’s [[Orphan|orphan]]! that — the [[Courage|courage]],<br>Which rising in thine eye, seems oft to give<br>A new [[Soul|soul]] to its gentleness, doth prove thee!<br>Thou art sprung too of no ignoble [[Blood|blood]],<br>Or there’s no [[Faith|faith]] in instinct!<br>[Angry voices and clamour within. |
| *(didascalia)* | Re-enter GLYCINE. |
| GLYCINE | Oh, madam! there’s a party of your servants,<br>And my lord’s steward, Laska, at their head,<br>Have come to search for old Bathory’s son,<br>Bethlen, that brave young man! ‘twas he, my lady,<br>That took our parts, and beat off the intruders,<br>And in mere spite and malice, now they charge him<br>With bad words of Lord Casimir and the [[King|king]].<br>Pray don’t believe them, madam! This way! This way!<br>Lady Sarolta’s here. —      [Calling without. |
| SAROLTA | Be calm, Glycine. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter LASKA and Servants with OLD BATHORY. |
| LASKA | (to Bathory). We have no concern with you! What needs your presence? |
| OLD BATHORY | What! Do you think I’ll suffer my brave boy<br>To be slandered by a set of coward-ruffians,<br>And leave it to their malice, — yes, mere malice! —<br>To tell its own tale?<br>[LASKA and Servants bow to Lady SAROLTA. |
| SAROLTA | Laska! What may this mean? |
| LASKA | Madam! and may it please your ladyship!<br>This old man’s son, by name Bethlen Bathory,<br>Stands charged, on weighty evidence, that he,<br>On yester-eve, being his lordship’s birth-day,<br>Did traitorously defame Lord Casimir:<br>The lord high steward of the realm, moreover —— |
| SAROLTA | Be brief! We know his titles! |
| LASKA | And moreover<br>Raved like a traitor at our liege [[King]] Emerick.<br>And furthermore, said witnesses make oath,<br>Led on the assault upon his lordship’s servants;<br>Yea, insolently tore, from this, your huntsman,<br>His badge of livery of your noble [[house]],<br>And trampled it in scorn. |
| SAROLTA | (to the Servants who offer to speak). You have had your spokesman!<br>Where is the young man thus accused? |
| OLD BATHORY | I know not:<br>But if no ill betide him on the [[Mountains|mountains]],<br>He will not long be absent! |
| SAROLTA | Thou art his father? |
| OLD BATHORY | None ever with more reason prized a son;<br>Yet I hate falsehood more than I [[Love|love]] him.<br>But more than one, now in my lady’s presence,<br>Witnessed the affray, besides these men of malice;<br>And if I swerve from [[Truth|truth]] —— |
| GLYCINE | Yes! good old man!<br>My lady! pray believe him! |
| SAROLTA | Hush, Glycine<br>Be silent, I command you.     [Then to BATHORY.<br>Speak! we hear you! |
| OLD BATHORY | My tale is brief. During our festive [[Dance|dance]],<br>Your servants, the accusers of my son,<br>Offered gross insults, in unmanly sort,<br>To our village maidens. He (could he do less?)<br>[[Rose]] in defence of outraged modesty,<br>And so persuasive did his cudgel prove,<br>(Your hectoring sparks so over-brave to women<br>Are always cowards) that they soon took flight,<br>And now in mere [[Revenge|revenge]], like baffled boasters,<br>Have framed this tale, out of some hasty words<br>Which their own threats provoked. |
| SAROLTA | Old man! you talk<br>Too bluntly! Did your son owe no respect<br>To the livery of our [[house]]? |
| OLD BATHORY | Even such respect<br>As the sheep’s skin should gain for the hot wolf<br>That hath begun to worry the poor lambs! |
| LASKA | Old insolent ruffian! |
| GLYCINE | Pardon! pardon, madam!<br>I saw the whole affray. The good old man<br>Means no offence, sweet lady! — You, yourself,<br>Laska! know [[Well|well]], that these men were the ruffians!<br>Shame on you! |
| SAROLTA | What! Glycine? Go, retire!       [Exit GLYCINE.<br>Be it then that these men faulted. Yet yourself,<br>Or better still belike the maidens’ parents,<br>Might have complained to us. Was ever access<br>Denied you? Or free audience? Or are we<br>Weak and unfit to punish our own servants? |
| OLD BATHORY | So then! So then! Heaven grant an old man patience!<br>And must the gardener leave his seedling plants,<br>Leave his young roses to the rooting swine<br>While he goes ask their master, if perchance<br>His leisure serve to scourge them from their ravage? |
| LASKA | Ho! Take the rude clown from your lady’s presence!<br>I will report her further will! |
| SAROLTA | Wait then,<br>Till thou hast learnt it! Fervent good old man!<br>Forgive me that, to try thee, I put on<br>A face of sternness, alien to my meaning!<br>[Then speaks to the Servants.<br>Hence! leave my presence! and you, Laska! mark me!<br>Those rioters are no longer of my household!<br>If we but shake a dewdrop from a [[Rose|rose]]<br>In vain would we replace it, and as vainly<br>Restore the tear of wounded modesty<br>To a maiden’s eye familiarized to licence. —<br>But these men, Laska — |
| LASKA | (aside). Yes, now ‘tis coming. |
| SAROLTA | Brutal aggressors first, then baffled dastards,<br>That they have sought to piece out their [[Revenge|revenge]]<br>With a tale of words lured from the lips of anger<br>Stamps them most dangerous; and till I want<br>Fit means for wicked ends, we shall not need<br>Their services. Discharge them! You, Bathory!<br>Are henceforth of my household! I shall place you<br>Near my own person. When your son returns,<br>Present him to us! |
| OLD BATHORY | Ha! what strangers here!Your goodness, lady — and it came so sudden —<br>I can not — must not — let you be deceived.<br>I have yet another tale, but — [Then to SAROLTA aside.<br>not for all ears! |
| SAROLTA | I oft have passed your cottage, and still praised<br>Its [[Beauty|beauty]], and that trim orchard-plot, whose blossoms<br>The gusts of April showered aslant its thatch.<br>Come, you shall show it me! And, while you bid it<br>Farewell, be not ashamed that I should witness<br>The oil of gladness glittering on the [[Water|water]]<br>Of an ebbing grief.      [BATHORY shows her into his cottage. |
| LASKA | ([[Alone|alone]]).    Vexation! baffled! school’d!<br>Ho! Laska! wake! why? what can all this mean?<br>She sent away that cockatrice in anger!<br>Oh the false [[Witch|witch]]! It is too plain, she loves him.<br>And now, the old man near my lady’s person,<br>She’ll see this Bethlen hourly!<br>[LASKA flings himself into the seat. GLYCINE peeps in. |
| GLYCINE | Laska! Laska!<br>Is my lady gone? |
| LASKA | Gone. |
| GLYCINE | Have you yet seen him?<br>Is he returned?      [LASKA starts up.<br>Has the seat stung you, Laska? |
| LASKA | No, serpent! no; ‘tis you that sting me; you!<br>What! you would cling to him again? |
| GLYCINE | Whom? |
| LASKA | Bethlen! Bethlen!<br>Yes; gaze as if your very eyes embraced him!<br>Ha! you forget the scene of yesterday!<br>Mute ere he came, but then — Out on your screams,<br>And your pretended fears! |
| GLYCINE | Your fears, at least,<br>Were real, Laska! or your trembling limbs<br>And white cheeks played the hypocrites most vilely! |
| LASKA | I [[Fear|fear]]! whom? what? |
| GLYCINE | I know what I should [[Fear|fear]],<br>Were I in Laska’s place. |
| LASKA | What? |
| GLYCINE | My own [[Conscience|conscience]],<br>For having fed my [[Jealousy|jealousy]] and envy<br>With a plot, made out of other men’s revenges,<br>Against a brave and [[Innocent|innocent]] young man’s life!<br>Yet, yet, pray tell me! |
| LASKA | You will know too soon. |
| GLYCINE | Would I could find my lady! though she chid me —<br>Yet this suspense —     [Going. |
| LASKA | Stop! stop! one question only —<br>I am quite calm — |
| GLYCINE | Ay, as the old [[Song|song]] says,<br>Calm as a tiger, valiant as a dove.<br>Nay now, I have marred the verse: [[Well|well]]! this one question — |
| LASKA | Are you not bound to me by your own promise?<br>And is it not as plain — |
| GLYCINE | Halt! that’s two questions. |
| LASKA | Pshaw! Is it not as plain as impudence,<br>That you’re in [[Love|love]] with this young swaggering beggar,<br>Bethlen Bathory? When he was accused,<br>Why pressed you forward? Why did you defend him? |
| GLYCINE | Question meet question: that’s a woman’s privilege,<br>Why, Laska, did you urge Lord Casimir<br>To make my lady force that promise from me? |
| LASKA | So then, you say, Lady Sarolta, forced you? |
| GLYCINE | Could I look up to her dear countenance,<br>And say her nay? As far back as I wot of<br>All her commands were gracious, sweet requests.<br>How could it be then, but that her requests<br>Must needs have sounded to me as commands?<br>And as for [[Love|love]], had I a score of loves,<br>I’d keep them all for my dear, kind, good mistress. |
| LASKA | Not one for Bethlen? |
| GLYCINE | Oh! that’s a different thing.<br>To be sure he’s brave, and handsome, and so pious<br>To his good old father. But for loving him —<br>Nay, there, indeed you are mistaken, Laska!<br>Poor youth! I rather think I grieve for him;<br>For I sigh so deeply when I think of him!<br>And if I see him, the [[Tears|tears]] come in my eyes,<br>And my heart beats; and all because I dreamt<br>That the war-wolf had gored him as he hunted<br>In the haunted forest! |
| LASKA | You dare own all this?<br>Your lady will not warrant promise-breach.<br>Mine, pampered Miss! you shall be; and I’ll make you<br>Grieve for him with a vengeance. Odd’s, my fingers<br>Tingle already!       [Makes threatening signs. |
| GLYCINE | (aside). Ha! Bethlen coming this way!<br>[GLYCINE then cries out.<br>Oh, save me! save me! Pray don’t kill me, Laska! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter BETHLEN in a Hunting Dress. |
| BETHLEN | What, beat a woman! |
| LASKA | (to Glycine).     O you cockatrice! |
| BETHLEN | Unmanly dastard, hold! |
| LASKA | Do you chance to know<br>Who — I — am, Sir? — (‘Sdeath! how black he looks!) |
| BETHLEN | I have started many strange beasts in my [[Time|time]],<br>But none less like a man, than this before me<br>That lifts his hand against a timid female. |
| LASKA | Bold youth! she’s mine. |
| GLYCINE | No, not my master yet,<br>But only is to be; and all, because<br>Two years ago my lady asked me, and<br>I promised her, not him; and if she’ll let me,<br>I’ll hate you, my lord’s steward. |
| BETHLEN | Hush, Glycine! |
| GLYCINE | Yes, I do, Bethlen; for he just now brought<br>False witnesses to swear away your life:<br>Your life, and old Bathory’s too. |
| BETHLEN | Bathory’s!<br>Where is my father? Answer, or —— Ha! gone!<br>[LASKA during this [[Time|time]] retires from the Stage. |
| GLYCINE | Oh, heed not him! I saw you pressing onward,<br>And did but feign alarm. Dear gallant youth,<br>It is your life they seek! |
| BETHLEN | My life? |
| GLYCINE | Alas,<br>Lady Sarolta even — |
| BETHLEN | She does not know me! |
| GLYCINE | Oh that she did! she could not then have spoken<br>With such stern countenance. But though she spurn me,<br>I will kneel, Bethlen — |
| BETHLEN | Not for me, Glycine!<br>What have I done? or whom have I offended? |
| GLYCINE | Rash words, ‘tis said, and treasonous of the [[King|king]].<br>[BETHLEN mutters to himself. |
| GLYCINE | (aside). So looks the statue, in our hall, o’ the god,<br>The shaft just flown that killed the serpent! |
| BETHLEN | [[King]]! |
| GLYCINE | Ah, often have I wished you were a [[King|king]].<br>You would protect the helpless every where,<br>As you did us. And I, too, should not then<br>Grieve for you, Bethlen, as I do; nor have<br>The [[Tears|tears]] come in my eyes; nor dream bad [[Dreams|dreams]]<br>That you were killed in the forest; and then Laska<br>Would have no right to rail at me, nor say<br>(Yes, the base man, he says,) that I — I [[Love|love]] you. |
| BETHLEN | Pretty Glycine! wert thou not betrothed —<br>But in good [[Truth|truth]] I know not what I speak.<br>This luckless morning I have been so haunted<br>With my own fancies, starting up like omens,<br>That I feel like one, who waking from [[A Dream|a dream]]<br>Both asks and answers wildly. — But Bathory? |
| GLYCINE | Hist! ‘tis my lady’s step! She must not see you!<br>[BETHLEN retires. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter from the Cottage SAROLTA and BATHORY. |
| SAROLTA | Go, seek your son! I need not add, be speedy —<br>You here, Glycine?      [Exit BATHORY. |
| GLYCINE | Pardon, pardon, Madam!<br>If you but saw the old man’s son, you would not,<br>You could not have him harmed. |
| SAROLTA | Be calm, Glycine! |
| GLYCINE | No, I shall break my heart. |
| SAROLTA | Ha! is it so?<br>O strange and hidden power of sympathy,<br>That of — like fates, though all unknown to each,<br>Dost make blind instincts, [[Orphan|orphan]]’s heart to [[Orphan|orphan]]’s<br>Drawing by dim disquiet! |
| GLYCINE | Old Bathory — |
| SAROLTA | Seeks his brave son. Come, wipe away thy [[Tears|tears]].<br>Yes, in good [[Truth|truth]], Glycine, this same Bethlen<br>Seems a most noble and deserving youth. |
| GLYCINE | My lady does not mock me? |
| SAROLTA | Where is Laska?<br>Has he not told thee? |
| GLYCINE | Nothing. In his [[Fear|fear]] —<br>Anger, I mean — stole off — I am so fluttered —<br>Left me abruptly — |
| SAROLTA | His shame excuses him!<br>He is somewhat hardly tasked; and in discharging<br>His own tools, cons a lesson for himself.<br>Bathory and the youth henceforward live<br>Safe in my lord’s protection. |
| GLYCINE | The saints bless you!<br>Shame on my graceless heart! How dared I [[Fear|fear]],<br>Lady Sarolta could be cruel? |
| SAROLTA | Come,<br>Be yourself, girl! |
| GLYCINE | O, ‘tis so full here!<br>And now it can not harm him if I tell you,<br>That the old man’s son — |
| SAROLTA | Is not that old man’s son!<br>A destiny, not unlike thine own, is his.<br>For all I know of thee is, that thou art<br>A soldier’s [[Orphan|orphan]]: left when rage intestine<br>Shook and engulphed the pillars of Illyria.<br>This other [[Fragment|fragment]], thrown back by that same earthquake,<br>This, so mysteriously inscribed by [[Nature|nature]],<br>Perchance may piece out and interpret thine.<br>Command thyself! Be secret! His true father ——<br>Hear’st thou? |
| GLYCINE | O tell — |
| BETHLEN | (rushing out). Yes, tell me, Shape from heaven!<br>Who is my father? |
| SAROLTA | (gazing with surprise). Thine? Thy father? Rise! |
| GLYCINE | Alas! He hath alarmed you, my dear lady! |
| SAROLTA | His countenance, not his act! |
| GLYCINE | Rise, Bethlen! Rise! |
| BETHLEN | No; kneel thou too! and with thy [[Orphan|orphan]]’s tongue<br>Plead for me! I am rooted to the earth<br>And have no power to rise! Give me a father!<br>There is a [[prayer]] in those uplifted eyes<br>That seeks high Heaven! But I will overtake it,<br>And bring it back, and make it plead for me<br>In thine own heart! Speak! Speak! Restore to me<br>A name in the world! |
| SAROLTA | By that blest Heaven I gazed at,<br>I know not who thou art. And if I knew,<br>Dared I — But rise! |
| BETHLEN | Blest spirits of my parents,<br>Ye hover o’er me now! Ye shine upon me!<br>And like a flower that coils forth from a ruin,<br>I feel and seek the light I can not see! |
| SAROLTA | Thou see’st yon dim spot on the mountain’s ridge,<br>But what it is thou know’st not. Even such<br>Is all I know of thee — haply, brave youth,<br>Is all Fate makes it safe for thee to know! |
| BETHLEN | Safe? Safe? O let me then inherit danger,<br>And it shall be my birth-right! |
| SAROLTA | (aside).     That look again! —<br>The wood which first incloses, and then skirts<br>The highest track that leads across the [[Mountains|mountains]] —<br>Thou know’st it, Bethlen? |
| BETHLEN | Lady, ‘twas my wont<br>To roam there in my [[Childhood|childhood]] oft [[Alone|alone]]<br>And mutter to myself the name of father.<br>For still Bathory (why, till now I guessed not)<br>Would never hear it from my lips, but sighing<br>Gazed upward. Yet of late an idle terror —— |
| GLYCINE | Madam, that wood is haunted by the war-wolves,<br>Vampires, and monstrous —— |
| SAROLTA | [[Moon]]-calves, credulous girl!<br>Haply some o’ergrown savage of the forest<br>Hath his lair there, and [[Fear|fear]] hath framed the rest.<br>After that last great battle, (O young man!<br>Thou wakest anew my life’s sole anguish) that<br>Which fixed Lord Emerick on his throne, Bathory<br>Led by a cry, far inward from the track,<br>In the hollow of an oak, as in a nest,<br>Did find thee, Bethlen, then a helpless babe.<br>The robe that wrapt thee was a widow’s mantle. |
| BETHLEN | An infant’s weakness doth relax my frame.<br>O say — I [[Fear|fear]] to ask —— |
| SAROLTA | And I to tell thee. |
| BETHLEN | Strike! O strike quickly! See, I do not shrink.<br>I am stone, cold stone. |
| SAROLTA | Hid in a brake hard by,<br>Scarce by both palms supported from the earth,<br>A wounded lady lay, whose life fast waning<br>Seemed to survive itself in her fixt eyes,<br>That strained towards the babe. At length one arm<br>Painfully from her own weight disengaging,<br>She pointed first to heaven, then from her bosom<br>Drew forth a golden casket. Thus entreated<br>Thy foster-father took thee in his arms,<br>And kneeling spake: ‘If aught of this world’s comfort<br>Can reach thy heart, receive a poor man’s troth,<br>That at my life’s risk I will save thy [[Child|child]]!’<br>Her countenance worked, as one that seemed preparing<br>A loud voice, but it died upon her lips<br>In a faint whisper, ‘Fly! Save him! Hide — hide all!’ |
| BETHLEN | And did he leave her? What! had I a mother?<br>And left her bleeding, dying? Bought I vile life<br>With the desertion of a dying mother?<br>Oh agony! |
| GLYCINE | Alas! thou art bewildered,<br>And dost forget thou wert a helpless infant! |
| BETHLEN | What else can I remember, but a mother<br>Mangled and left to perish? |
| SAROLTA | Hush, Glycine!<br>It is the ground-swell of a teeming instinct:<br>Let it but lift itself to air and sunshine,<br>And it will find a [[Mirror|mirror]] in the waters<br>It now makes boil above it. Check him not! |
| BETHLEN | O that I were diffused among the waters<br>That pierce into the secret depths of earth,<br>And find their way in darkness! Would that I<br>Could spread myself upon the homeless winds!<br>And I would seek her! for she is not dead!<br>She can not die! O pardon, gracious lady!<br>You were about to say, that he returned — |
| SAROLTA | Deep [[Love]], the godlike in us, still believes<br>Its objects as immortal as itself! |
| BETHLEN | And found her still — |
| SAROLTA | Alas! he did return,<br>He left no spot unsearched in all the forest,<br>But she (I trust me by some friendly hand)<br>Had been borne off. |
| BETHLEN | O whither? |
| GLYCINE | Dearest Bethlen!<br>I would that you could weep like me! O do not<br>Gaze so upon the air! |
| SAROLTA | While he was absent,<br>A friendly troop, ‘tis certain, scoured the wood,<br>Hotly pursued indeed by Emerick. |
| BETHLEN | Emerick.<br>Oh hell! |
| GLYCINE | Bethlen! |
| BETHLEN | Hist! I’ll curse him in a whisper!<br>This gracious lady must hear blessings only.<br>She hath not yet the glory round her head,<br>Nor those strong eagle wings, which make swift way<br>To that appointed place, which I must seek;<br>Or else she were my mother! |
| SAROLTA | Noble youth!<br>From me [[Fear|fear]] nothing!
