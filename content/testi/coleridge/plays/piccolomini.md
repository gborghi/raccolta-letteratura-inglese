Coleridge's blank-verse translation of Schiller's Wallenstein I, set in the Thirty Years' War: the general Wallenstein's growing rebellion against his Emperor, with intrigue, ambition, loyalty and fate; a historical verse tragedy.

| BUTLER | Both wife and daughter does the Duke call hither?<br>He crowds in visitants from all sides. |
| ISOLANI | Hm!<br>So much the better! I had framed my mind<br>To hear of nought but warlike circumstance,<br>Of marches, and attacks, and batteries:<br>And lo! the Duke provides, that something too<br>Of gentler sort, and lovely, should be present<br>To feast our eyes. |
| ILLO *(aside to Butler)* | And how came you to know<br>That the Count Galas joins us not? |
| BUTLER | Because<br>He importuned me to remain behind. |
| ILLO | And you? — You hold out firmly?<br>Noble Butler! |
| BUTLER | After the obligation which the Duke<br>Had laid so newly on me —— |
| ILLO | I had forgotten<br>A pleasant duty — Major-General,<br>I wish you joy! |
| ISOLANI | What, you mean, of his regiment?<br>I hear, too, that to make the gift still sweeter,<br>The Duke has given him the very same<br>In which he first saw service, and since then,<br>Worked himself, step by step, through each preferment,<br>From the ranks upwards. | BUTLER | I am perplexed and doubtful, whether or no<br>I dare accept this your congratulation.<br>The Emperor has not yet confirmed the appointment. |
| ISOLANI | Seize it, friend! Seize it! The hand which in that post<br>Placed you, is strong enough to keep you there,<br>Spite of the Emperor and his Ministers! |
| ILLO | Ay, if we would but so consider it! —<br>If we would all of us consider it so!<br>The Emperor gives us nothing; from the Duke<br>Comes all — whate'er we [[concepts/hope\|hope]], whate'er we have. |
| ISOLANI *(to Illo)* | My noble brother! did I tell you how<br>The Duke will satisfy my creditors?<br>Will be himself my banker for the future,<br>Make me once more a creditable man! —<br>And this is now the third [[motifs/time\|time]], think of that!<br>This kingly-minded man has rescued me<br>From absolute ruin, and restored my [[concepts/honour\|honour]]. |
| ILLO | O that his power but kept pace with his wishes!<br>Why, friend! he'd give the whole world to his soldiers.<br>But at Vienna, brother! here's the grievance! —<br>What politic schemes do they not lay to shorten<br>His arm, and, where they can, to clip his pinions.<br>Then these new dainty requisitions! these,<br>Which this same Questenberg brings hither! — |
| BUTLER | Ay,<br>These requisitions of the Emperor, —<br>I too have heard about them; but I [[concepts/hope\|hope]]<br>The Duke will not draw back a single inch! |
| ILLO | Not from his right most surely, unless first<br>— From office! |
| BUTLER | Know you aught then? You alarm me. |
| ISOLANI *(at the same [[motifs/time\|time]] with Butler, and in a hurrying voice)* | We should be ruined, every one of us! |
| ILLO | No more!<br>Yonder I see our worthy friend approaching<br>With the Lieutenant-General, Piccolomini. |
| BUTLER | I [[concepts/fear\|fear]] we shall not go hence as we came. |

| QUESTENBERG | How shall we hold footing<br>Beneath this tempest, which collects itself<br>And threats us from all quarters? | OCTAVIO | Nay, nay, friend! let us not [[concepts/despair\|despair]] too soon,<br>Men's words are ever bolder than their deeds:<br>And many a resolute, who now appears<br>Made up to all extremes, will, on a sudden<br>Find in his breast a heart he knew not of,<br>Let but a single honest man speak out<br>The true name of his crime! | QUESTENBERG | 'Tis quite<br>Incomprehensible, that he detects not<br>The foe so near! |
