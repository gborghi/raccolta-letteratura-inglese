| *(didascalia)* | SCENE THE FIRST. — A wild and mountainous country. OSORIO and [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]] are discovered at a little distance from a [[house]], which stands under the brow of a slate rock, the rock covered with vines. [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]] and OSORIO. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | Thrice you have sav’d my life. Once in the battle<br>You gave it me, next rescued me from [[suicide]],<br>When for my follies I was made to wander<br>With mouths to feed, and not a morsel for them.<br>Now, but for you, a dungeon’s slimy stones<br>Had pillow’d my snapt joints. |
| OSORIO | Good [[Ferdinand]]!<br>Why this to me? It is enough you know it. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | A common trick of gratitude, my lord!<br>Seeking to ease her own full heart. |
| OSORIO | Enough.<br>A debt repay’d ceases to be a debt.<br>You have it in your power to serve me greatly. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | As how, my lord? I pray you name the thing!<br>I would climb up an ice-glaz’d precipice<br>To pluck a weed you fancied. |
| OSORIO | *(with embarrassment and hesitation.)* Why — that — lady — |
| [[Ferdinand]] | ‘Tis now three years, my lord! since last I saw you.<br>Have you a son, my lord? |
| OSORIO | O miserable! *(Aside.)*<br>[[Ferdinand]]! you are a man, and know this world.<br>I told you what I wish’d — now for the [[Truth|truth]]!<br>She lov’d the man you kill’d! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | *(looking as suddenly alarmed.)* You jest, my lord? |
| OSORIO | And till his [[Death|death]] is proved, she will not wed me. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | You sport with me, my lord? |
| OSORIO | Come, come, this foolery<br>Lives only in thy looks — thy heart disowns it. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | I can bear this, and anything more grievous<br>From you, my lord! — but how can I serve you here? |
| OSORIO | Why, you can mouth set speeches solemnly,<br>Wear a quaint garment, make mysterious antics. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | I am dull, my lord! I do not comprehend you. |
| OSORIO | In blunt terms] you can play the sorcerer.<br>She has no [[Faith|faith]] in Holy Church, ‘tis true.<br>Her [[Lover|lover]] school’d her in some newer nonsense:<br>Yet still a tale of spirits works on her.<br>She is a lone enthusiast, sensitive,<br>Shivers, and cannot keep the [[Tears|tears]] in her eye.<br>Such ones do [[Love|love]] the marvellous too [[Well|well]]<br>Not to believe it. We will [[Wind|wind]] her up<br>With a strange music, that she knows not of,<br>With fumes of frankincense, and mummery —<br>Then leave, as one sure token of his [[Death|death]],<br>That portrait, which from off the dead man’s neck<br>I bade thee take, the trophy of thy conquest. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | *(with hesitation.)* Just now I should have cursed the man who told me<br>You could ask aught, my lord! and I refuse.<br>But this I cannot do. |
| OSORIO | Where lies your scruple? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | That shark Francesco. |
| OSORIO | O! an o’ersiz’d gudgeon!<br>I baited, sir, my hook with a painted mitre,<br>And now I play with him at the end of the line.<br>[[Well]] — and what next? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | *(stammering.)* Next, next — my lord!<br>You know you told me that the lady loved you,<br>Had loved you with incautious tenderness.<br>That if the young man, her betrothéd husband,<br>Return’d, yourself, and she, and an [[unborn]] babe,<br>Must perish. Now, my lord! to be a man! |
| OSORIO | *(aloud, though to express his contempt he speaks in the third person.)* This fellow is a man! he kill’d for hire<br>One whom he knew not — yet has tender scruples. *(Then turning to [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]].)*<br>Thy hums and ha’s, thy whine and stammering.<br>Pish — [[Fool|fool]]! thou blunder’st through the devil’s book,<br>Spelling thy villany! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | My lord — my lord!<br>I can bear much, yes, very much from you.<br>But there’s a point where sufferance is meanness!<br>I am no [[Villain|villain]], never kill’d for hire.<br>My gratitude —— |
| OSORIO | O! aye, your gratitude!<br>‘Twas a [[Well|well]]-sounding word — what have you done with it? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | Who proffers his past favours for my virtue<br>Tries to o’erreach me, is a very sharper,<br>And should not speak of gratitude, my lord!<br>I knew not ‘twas your brother! |
| OSORIO | *(evidently alarmed.)* And who told you? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | He himself told me. |
| OSORIO | Ha! you talk’d with him?<br>And those, the two Morescoes, that went with you? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | Both fell in a [[Night|night]]-brawl at Malaga. |
| OSORIO | *(in a low voice.)* My brother! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | Yes, my lord! I could not tell you:<br>I thrust away the thought, it drove me wild.<br>But listen to me now. I pray you, listen! |
| OSORIO | [[Villain]]! no more! I’ll hear no more of it. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | My lord! it much imports your future safety<br>That you should hear it. |
| OSORIO | *(turning off from [[Ferdinand]].)* Am I not a man?<br>‘Tis as it should be! Tut — the deed itself<br>Was idle — and these after-pangs still idler! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | We met him in the very place you mention’d,<br>Hard by a grove of firs. |
| OSORIO | Enough! enough! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | He fought us valiantly, and wounded all;<br>In fine, compell’d a parley! |
| OSORIO | *(sighing as if lost in thought.)* Albert! Brother! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | He offer’d me his purse. |
| OSORIO | Yes? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | Yes! I spurn’d it.<br>He promis’d us I know not what — in vain!<br>Then with a look and voice which overaw’d me,<br>He said — What mean you, friends? My life is dear.<br>I have a brother and a promised wife<br>Who make life dear to me, and if I fall<br>That brother will roam earth and hell for vengeance.<br>There was a likeness in his face to yours.<br>I ask’d his brother’s name; he said, Osorio,<br>Son of Lord Velez! I had [[Well|well]]-nigh fainted!<br>At length I said (if that indeed I said it,<br>And that no spirit made my tongue his organ),<br>That woman is now pregnant by that brother,<br>And he the man who sent us to destroy you,<br>He drove a thrust at me in rage. I told him,<br>He wore her portrait round his neck — he look’d<br>As he had been made of the rock that propp’d him back;<br>Ay, just as you look now — only less ghastly!<br>At last recovering from his trance, he threw<br>His [[Sword|sword]] away, and bade us take his life —<br>It was not worth his keeping. |
| OSORIO | And you kill’d him?<br>O [[Blood|blood]]-hounds! may eternal wrath flame round you!<br>He was the image of the Deity. *(A pause.)*<br>It seizes me — by Hell! I will go on!<br>What? would’st thou stop, man? thy pale looks won’t save thee! *(Then suddenly pressing his forehead.)*<br>Oh! cold, cold, cold — shot thro’ with icy cold! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | *(aside.)* Were he alive, he had return’d ere now.<br>The consequence the same, dead thro’ his plotting! |
| OSORIO | O this unutterable dying away here,<br>This sickness of the heart! *(A pause.)*<br>What if I went<br>And liv’d in a hollow tomb, and fed on weeds?<br>Ay! that’s the [[Road|road]] to heaven! O [[Fool|fool]]! [[Fool|fool]]! [[Fool|fool]]! *(A pause.)*<br>What have I done but that which [[Nature|nature]] destin’d<br>Or the blind elements stirr’d up within me?<br>If good were meant, why were we made these beings?<br>And if not meant —— |
| [[Ferdinand]] | How feel you now, my lord? |
| *(didascalia)* | *(OSORIO starts, looks at him wildly, then, after a pause, during which his features are forced into a smile.)* |
| OSORIO | A gust of the [[Soul|soul]]! i’[[Faith|faith]], it overset me.<br>O ‘twas all folly — all! idle as laughter!<br>Now, [[Ferdinand]], I swear that thou shalt aid me. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | *(in a low voice.)* I’ll perish first! Shame on my coward heart,<br>That I must slink away from wickedness<br>Like a cow’d dog! |
| OSORIO | What dost thou mutter of? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | Some of your servants know me, I am certain. |
| OSORIO | There’s some sense in that scruple; but we’ll mask you. |
| [[Ferdinand]] | They’ll know my gait. But stay! of late I have watch’d<br>A stranger that lives nigh, still picking weeds,<br>Now in the swamp, now on the walls of the ruin,<br>Now clamb’ring, like a runaway lunatic,<br>Up to the summit of our highest mount.<br>I have watch’d him at it morning-tide and [[Noon|noon]],<br>Once in the moonlight. Then I stood so near,<br>I heard him mutt’ring o’er the plant. A wizard!<br>Some gaunt slave, prowling out for dark employments. |
| OSORIO | What may his name be? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | That I cannot tell you.<br>Only Francesco bade an officer<br>Speak in your name, as lord of this domain.<br>So he was question’d, who and what he was.<br>This was his answer: Say to the Lord Osorio,<br>‘He that can bring the dead to life again.’ |
| OSORIO | A strange reply! |
| [[Ferdinand]] | Aye — all of him is strange.<br>He call’d himself a Christian — yet he wears<br>The Moorish robe, as if he courted [[Death|death]]. |
| OSORIO | Where does this wizard live? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | *(pointing to a distance.)* You see that brooklet?<br>Trace its course backward thro’ a narrow opening<br>It leads you to the place. |
| OSORIO | How shall I know it? |
| [[Ferdinand]] | You can’t mistake. It is a small green dale<br>Built all around with high off-sloping hills,<br>And from its shape our peasants aptly call it<br>The Giant’s Cradle. There’s a lake in the midst,<br>And round its banks tall wood, that branches over<br>And makes a kind of faery forest grow<br>Down in the [[Water|water]]. At the further end<br>A puny cataract falls on the lake;<br>And there (a curious sight) you see its [[Shadow|shadow]]<br>For ever curling, like a wreath of smoke,<br>Up through the foliage of those faery trees.<br>His cot stands opposite — you cannot miss it.<br>Some three yards up the [[Hill|hill]] a mountain ash<br>Stretches its lower boughs and scarlet clusters<br>O’er the new thatch. |
| OSORIO | I shall not fail to find it. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Exit OSORIO. [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]] goes into his [[house]]. Scene changes.)* |
