| *(didascalia)* | The inside of a cottage, around which [[Flowers|flowers]] and plants of various kinds are seen. ALBERT and MAURICE. |
| ALBERT | He doth believe himself an iron [[Soul|soul]],<br>And therefore puts he on an iron outward<br>And those same mock habiliments of strength<br>Hide his own weakness from himself. |
| MAURICE | His weakness!<br>Come, come, speak out! Your brother is a [[Villain|villain]]!<br>Yet all the wealth, power, influence, which is yours<br>You suffer him to hold! |
| ALBERT | Maurice! dear Maurice!<br>That my return involved Osorio’s [[Death|death]]<br>I trust would give me an unmingl’d pang —<br>Yet bearable. But when I see my father<br>Strewing his scant grey hairs even on the ground<br>Which soon must be his [[Grave|grave]]; and my [[Maria]],<br>Her husband proved a [[Monster|monster]], and her infants<br>His infants — poor [[Maria]]! — all would perish,<br>All perish — all! — and I (nay bear with me!)<br>Could not survive the complicated ruin! |
| MAURICE | *(much affected.)* Nay, now, if I have distress’d you — you [[Well|well]] know,<br>I ne’er will quit your fortunes! true, ‘tis tiresome.<br>You are a painter — one of many fancies —<br>You can call up past deeds, and make them live<br>On the blank canvas, and each little herb,<br>That grows on mountain bleak, or tangled forest,<br>You’ve learnt to name — but I —— |
| ALBERT | [[Well]], to the Netherlands<br>We will return, the heroic Prince of Orange<br>Will grant us an asylum, in remembrance<br>Of our past service. |
| MAURICE | Heard you not some steps? |
| ALBERT | What if it were my brother coming onward!<br>Not very wisely (but his creature teiz’d me)<br>I sent a most mysterious message to him. |
| MAURICE | Would he not know you? |
| ALBERT | I unfearingly<br>Trust this disguise. Besides, he thinks me dead;<br>And what the mind believes impossible,<br>The bodily sense is slow to recognize.<br>Add too my youth, when last we saw each other;<br>Manhood has swell’d my chest, and taught my voice<br>A hoarser note. |
| MAURICE | Most true! And Alva’s Duke<br>Did not improve it by the unwholesome viands<br>He gave so scantily in that foul dungeon,<br>During our long [[Imprisonment|imprisonment]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter OSORIO. |
| ALBERT | It is he! |
| MAURICE | Make yourself talk; you’ll feel the less. Come, speak.<br>How do you find yourself? Speak to me, Albert. |
| ALBERT | *(placing his hand on his heart.)* A little fluttering here; but more of sorrow! |
| OSORIO | You know my name, perhaps, better than me.<br>I am Osorio, son of the Lord Velez. |
| ALBERT | *(groaning aloud.)* The son of Velez! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(OSORIO walks leisurely round the room, and looks attentively at the plants.)* |
| MAURICE | Why, what ails you now? |
| *(didascalia)* | *(ALBERT grasps MAURICE’S hand in agitation.)* |
| MAURICE | How your hand trembles, Albert! Speak! what wish you? |
| ALBERT | To fall upon his neck and weep in anguish! |
| OSORIO | *(returning.)* All very curious! from a ruin’d abbey<br>Pluck’d in the moonlight. There’s a strange power in weeds<br>When a few odd prayers have been mutter’d o’er them.<br>Then they work miracles! I warrant you,<br>There’s not a leaf, but underneath it lurks<br>Some serviceable imp. There’s one of you,<br>Who sent me a strange message. |
| ALBERT | I am he! |
| OSORIO | I will speak with you, and by yourself. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Exit MAURICE.)* |
| OSORIO | ‘He that can bring the dead to life again.’<br>Such was your message, Sir! You are no dullard,<br>But one that strips the outward rind of things! |
| ALBERT | ‘Tis fabled there are fruits with tempting rinds<br>That are all [[Dust|dust]] and rottenness within.<br>Would’st thou I should strip such? |
| OSORIO | Thou quibbling [[Fool|fool]],<br>What dost thou mean? Think’st thou I [[Journey|journey]]’d hither<br>To sport with thee? |
| ALBERT | No, no! my lord! to sport<br>Best fits the gaiety of [[innocence]]! |
| OSORIO | *(draws back as if stung and embarrassed, then folding his arms.)* O what a thing is Man! the wisest heart<br>A [[Fool|fool]] — a [[Fool|fool]], that laughs at its own folly,<br>Yet still a [[Fool|fool]]! *(Looks round the cottage.)*<br>It strikes me you are poor! |
| ALBERT | What follows thence? |
| OSORIO | That you would fain be richer.<br>Besides, you do not [[Love|love]] the rack, perhaps,<br>Nor a black dungeon, nor a [[Fire|fire]] of faggots.<br>The [[Inquisition]] — hey? You understand me,<br>And you are poor. Now I have wealth and power,<br>Can quench the flames, and cure your [[Poverty|poverty]].<br>And for this service, all I ask you is<br>That you should serve me — once — for a few hours. |
| ALBERT | *(solemnly.)* Thou art the son of Velez! Would to Heaven<br>That I could truly and for ever serve thee! |
| OSORIO | The canting scoundrel softens. *(Aside.)*<br>You are my friend!<br>‘He that can bring the dead to life again.’<br>Nay, no defence to me. The holy brethren<br>Believe these calumnies. I know thee better. *(Then with great bitterness.)*<br>Thou art a man, and as a man I’ll trust thee! |
| ALBERT | Alas, this hollow mirth! Declare your business! |
| OSORIO | I [[Love|love]] a lady, and she would [[Love|love]] me<br>But for an idle and fantastic scruple.<br>Have you no servants round the [[house]]? no listeners? |
| *(didascalia)* | *(OSORIO steps to the door.)* |
| ALBERT | What! faithless too? false to his angel wife?<br>To such a wife? [[Well]] might’st thou look so wan,<br>Ill-starr’d [[Maria]]! Wretch! my softer [[Soul|soul]]<br>Is pass’d away! and I will probe his [[Conscience|conscience]]. |
| OSORIO | *(returned.)* In [[Truth|truth]] this lady loved another man,<br>But he has perish’d. |
| ALBERT | What? you kill’d him? hey? |
| OSORIO | I’ll dash thee to the earth, if thou but think’st it,<br>Thou slave! thou galley-slave! thou mountebank!<br>I leave thee to the hangman! |
| ALBERT | Fare you [[Well|well]]!<br>I pity you, Osorio! even to anguish! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(ALBERT retires off the stage.)* |
| OSORIO | *(recovering himself.)* ‘Twas ideotcy! I’ll tie myself to an aspen,<br>And wear a [[Fool]]’s Cap. Ho! *(Calling after ALBERT.)* |
| ALBERT | *(returning.)* Be brief, what wish you? |
| OSORIO | You are deep at bartering — you charge yourself<br>At a round sum. Come, come, I spake unwisely. |
| ALBERT | I listen to you. |
| OSORIO | In a sudden tempest<br>Did Albert perish — he, I mean, the [[Lover|lover]] —<br>The fellow —— |
| ALBERT | Nay, speak out, ‘twill ease your heart<br>To call him [[Villain|villain]]! Why stand’st thou aghast?<br>Men think it natural to hate their rivals! |
| OSORIO | *(hesitating and half doubting whether he should proceed.)* Now till she knows him dead she will not wed me! |
| ALBERT | *(with eager vehemence.)* Are you not wedded, then?<br>Merciful God!<br>Not wedded to [[Maria]]? |
| OSORIO | Why, what ails thee?<br>Art mad or drunk? Why look’st thou upward so?<br>Dost pray to [[Lucifer]], prince of the air? |
| ALBERT | Proceed. I shall be silent. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(ALBERT sits, and leaning on the table hides his face.)* |
| OSORIO | To [[Maria]]!<br>Politic wizard! ere you sent that message,<br>You had conn’d your lesson, made yourself proficient<br>In all my fortunes! Hah! you prophesied<br>A golden crop! — [[Well|well]], you have not mistaken —<br>Be faithful to me, and I’ll pay thee nobly. |
| ALBERT | *(lifting up his head.)* [[Well]] — and this lady! |
| OSORIO | If we could make her certain of his [[Death|death]],<br>She needs must wed me. Ere her [[Lover|lover]] left her,<br>She tied a little portrait round his neck<br>Entreating him to wear it. |
| ALBERT | *(sighing.)* Yes! he did so! |
| OSORIO | Why, no! he was afraid of accidents,<br>Of robberies and shipwrecks, and the like.<br>In secrecy he gave it me to keep<br>Till his return. |
| ALBERT | What, he was your friend then? |
| OSORIO | *(wounded and embarrassed.)* I was his friend. *(A pause.)*<br>Now that he gave it me<br>This lady knows not. You are a mighty wizard —<br>Can call this dead man up — he will not come —<br>He is in heaven then! — there you have no influence —<br>Still there are tokens; and your imps may bring you<br>Something he wore about him when he died.<br>And when the smoke of the incense on the altar<br>Is pass’d, your spirits will have left this picture.<br>What say you now? |
| ALBERT | *(after a long pause.)* Osorio, I will do it. |
| OSORIO | Delays are dangerous. It shall be to-morrow<br>In the early evening. Ask for the Lord Velez.<br>I will prepare him. Music, too, and incense,<br>All shall be ready. Here is this same picture —<br>And here what you will value more, a purse.<br>Before the dusk —— |
| ALBERT | I will not fail to meet you. |
| OSORIO | Till next we meet, farewell! |
| ALBERT | *([[Alone|alone]], gazes passionately at the portrait.)* And I did curse thee?<br>At midnight? on my knees? And I believed<br>Thee perjured, thee polluted, thee a murderess?<br>O blind and credulous [[Fool|fool]]! O [[Guilt|guilt]] of folly!<br>Should not thy inarticulate fondnesses,<br>Thy infant loves — should not thy maiden vows,<br>Have come upon my heart? And this sweet image<br>Tied round my neck with many a chaste endearment<br>And thrilling hands, that made me weep and tremble.<br>Ah, coward dupe! to yield it to the miscreant<br>Who spake pollutions of thee!<br>I am unworthy of thy [[Love|love]], [[Maria]]!<br>Of that unearthly smile upon those lips,<br>Which ever smil’d on me! Yet do not scorn me.<br>I lisp’d thy name ere I had learnt my mother’s! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter MAURICE. |
| ALBERT | Maurice! that picture, which I painted for thee,<br>Of my assassination. |
| MAURICE | I’ll go fetch it. |
| ALBERT | Haste! for I yearn to tell thee what has pass’d. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(MAURICE goes out.)* |
| ALBERT | *(gazing at the portrait.)* Dear image! rescued from a traitor’s keeping,<br>I will not now prophane thee, holy image!<br>To a dark trick! That worst bad man shall find<br>A picture which shall wake the hell within him,<br>And rouse a fiery whirlwind in his [[Conscience|conscience]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | END OF ACT THE SECOND. |
