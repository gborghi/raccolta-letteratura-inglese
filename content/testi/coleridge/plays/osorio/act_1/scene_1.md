| *(didascalia)* | SCENE — The sea shore on the coast of Granada. VELEZ, [[Maria|MARIA]]. |
| [[Maria]] | I hold Osorio dear: he is your son,<br>And Albert’s brother. |
| VELEZ | [[Love]] him for himself,<br>Nor make the living wretched for the dead. |
| [[Maria]] | I mourn that you should plead in vain, Lord Velez!<br>But Heaven hath heard my vow, and I remain<br>Faithful to Albert, be he dead or living. |
| VELEZ | Heaven knows with what delight I saw your loves;<br>And could my heart’s [[Blood|blood]] give him back to thee<br>I would die smiling. But these are idle thoughts!<br>Thy dying father comes upon my [[Soul|soul]]<br>With that same look, with which he gave thee to me:<br>I held thee in mine arms, a powerless babe,<br>While thy poor mother with a mute entreaty<br>Fix’d her faint eyes on mine: ah, not for this,<br>That I should let thee feed thy [[Soul|soul]] with gloom,<br>And with slow anguish wear away thy life,<br>The victim of a useless constancy.<br>I must not see thee wretched. |
| [[Maria]] | There are woes<br>Ill-barter’d for the garishness of joy!<br>If it be wretched with an untired eye<br>To watch those skiey tints, and this green ocean;<br>Or in the sultry hour beneath some rock,<br>My hair dishevell’d by the pleasant sea-breeze,<br>To shape sweet visions, and live o’er again<br>All past hours of delight; if it be wretched<br>To watch some bark, and fancy Albert there;<br>To go through each minutest circumstance<br>Of the bless’d meeting, and to frame adventures<br>Most terrible and strange, and hear him tell them:<br>(As once I knew a crazy Moorish maid,<br>Who dress’d her in her buried [[Lover|lover]]’s cloaths,<br>And o’er the smooth [[spring]] in the mountain cleft<br>Hung with her lute, and play’d the selfsame tune<br>He used to play, and listen’d to the [[Shadow|shadow]]<br>Herself had made); if this be wretchedness,<br>And if indeed it be a wretched thing<br>To trick out mine own [[Death|death]]-bed, and imagine<br>That I had died — died, just ere his return;<br>Then see him listening to my constancy;<br>And hover round, as he at midnight ever<br>Sits on my [[Grave|grave]] and gazes at the [[Moon|moon]];<br>Or haply in some more fantastic mood<br>To be in Paradise, and with choice [[Flowers|flowers]]<br>Build up a bower where he and I might dwell,<br>And there to wait his coming! O my sire!<br>My Albert’s sire! if this be wretchedness<br>That eats away the life, what were it, think you,<br>If in a most assur’d reality<br>He should return, and see a brother’s infant<br>Smile at him from my arms? *(Clasping her forehead.)*<br>O what a thought!<br>‘Twas horrible! it pass’d my brain like [[Lightning|lightning]]. |
| VELEZ | ‘Twere horrible, if but one [[Doubt|doubt]] remain’d<br>The very week he promised his return. |
| [[Maria]] | Ah, what a busy joy was ours — to see him<br>After his three years’ travels! tho’ that absence<br>His still-expected, never-failing letters<br>Almost endear’d to me! Even then what tumult! |
| VELEZ | O power of youth to feed on pleasant thoughts<br>Spite of conviction! I am old and heartless!<br>Yes, I am old — I have no pleasant [[Dreams|dreams]] —<br>Hectic and unrefresh’d with rest. |
| [[Maria]] | *(with great tenderness.)* My father! |
| VELEZ | Aye, ‘twas the morning thou didst try to cheer me<br>With a fond gaiety. My heart was bursting,<br>And yet I could not tell me, how my [[Sleep|sleep]]<br>Was throng’d with swarthy faces, and I saw<br>The merchant-[[Ship|ship]] in which my son was captured —<br>[[Well]], [[Well|well]], enough — captured in sight of land —<br>We might almost have seen it from our [[house]]-top! |
| [[Maria]] | *(abruptly.)* He did not perish there! |
| VELEZ | *(impatiently.)* Nay, nay — how aptly thou forgett’st a tale<br>Thou ne’er didst wish to learn — my brave Osorio<br>Saw them both founder in the [[Storm|storm]] that parted<br>Him and the pirate: both the vessels founder’d.<br>Gallant Osorio! *(Pauses, then tenderly.)*<br>O belov’d [[Maria]],<br>Would’st thou best prove thy [[Faith|faith]] to generous Albert<br>And most delight his spirit, go and make<br>His brother happy, make his agéd father<br>Sink to the [[Grave|grave]] with joy! |
| [[Maria]] | For [[Mercy|mercy]]’s sake<br>Press me no more. I have no power to [[Love|love]] him!<br>His proud forbidding eye, and his dark brow<br>Chill me, like dew-damps of the unwholesome [[Night|night]].<br>My [[Love|love]], a timorous and tender flower,<br>Closes beneath his touch. |
| VELEZ | You wrong him, maiden.<br>You wrong him, by my [[Soul|soul]]! Nor was it [[Well|well]]<br>To character by such unkindly phrases<br>The stir and workings of that [[Love|love]] for you<br>Which he has toil’d to smother. ‘Twas not [[Well|well]] —<br>Nor is it grateful in you to forget<br>His wounds and perilous voyages, and how<br>With an heroic fearlessness of danger<br>He roamed the coast of Afric for your Albert.<br>It was not [[Well|well]] — you have moved me even to [[Tears|tears]]. |
| [[Maria]] | O pardon me, my father! pardon me.<br>It was a foolish and ungrateful speech,<br>A most ungrateful speech! But I am hurried<br>Beyond myself, if I but dream of one<br>Who aims to rival Albert. Were we not<br>Born on one day, like twins of the same parent?<br>Nursed in one cradle? Pardon me, my father!<br>A six years’ absence is an heavy thing;<br>Yet still the [[Hope|hope]] survives —— |
| VELEZ | *(looking forwards.)* Hush — hush! [[Maria]]. |
| [[Maria]] | It is Francesco, our Inquisitor;<br>That busy man, gross, ignorant, and cruel! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter FRANCESCO and ALHADRA. |
| FRANCESCO | *(to Velez.)* Where is your son, my lord? Oh! here he comes. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter OSORIO. |
| FRANCESCO | My Lord Osorio! this Moresco woman<br>(Alhadra is her name) asks audience of you. |
| OSORIO | Hail, reverend father! What may be the business? |
| FRANCESCO | O the old business — a Mohammedan!<br>The officers are in her husband’s [[house]],<br>And would have taken him, but that he mention’d<br>Your name, asserting that you were his friend,<br>Aye, and would warrant him a Catholic.<br>But I know [[Well|well]] these children of perdition,<br>And all their idle fals[e]hoods to gain [[Time|time]];<br>So should have made the officers proceed,<br>But that this woman with most passionate outcries,<br>(Kneeling and holding forth her infants to me)<br>So work’d upon me, who (you know, my lord!)<br>Have human frailties, and am tender-hearted,<br>That I came with her. |
| OSORIO | You are merciful. *(Looking at ALHADRA.)*<br>I would that I could serve you; but in [[Truth|truth]]<br>Your face is new to me. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(ALHADRA is about to speak, but is interrupted by Francesco.)* |
| FRANCESCO | Aye, aye — I thought so;<br>And so I said to one of the familiars.<br>A likely story, said I, that Osorio,<br>The gallant nobleman, who fought so bravely<br>Some four years past against these [[Rebel|rebel]] Moors;<br>Working so hard from out the [[Garden|garden]] of [[Faith|faith]]<br>To eradicate these weeds detestable;<br>That he should countenance this vile Moresco,<br>Nay, be his friend — and warrant him, forsooth!<br>[[Well]], [[Well|well]], my lord! it is a warning to me;<br>Now I return. |
| ALHADRA | My lord, my husband’s name<br>Is [[Ferdinand]]: you may remember it.<br>Three years ago — three years this very week —<br>You left him at Almeria. |
| FRANCESCO | *(triumphantly.)* Palpably false!<br>This very week, three years ago, my lord!<br>(You needs must recollect it by your wound)<br>You were at sea, and fought the Moorish fiends<br>Who took and murder’d your poor brother Albert. |
| *(didascalia)* | *([[Maria|MARIA]] looks at FRANCESCO with disgust and horror. OSORIO’S appearance to be collected from the speech that follows.)* |
| FRANCESCO | *(to Velez and pointing to Osorio.)* What? is he ill, my lord? How strange he looks! |
| VELEZ | *(angrily.)* You started on him too abruptly, father!<br>The fate of one, on whom you know he doted. |
| OSORIO | *(starting as in a sudden agitation.)* O heavens! I doted! *(Then, as if recovering himself.)* Yes! I DOTED on him! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(OSORIO walks to the end of the stage. VELEZ follows soothing him.)* |
| [[Maria]] | *(her eye following them.)* I do not, cannot [[Love|love]] him. Is my heart hard?<br>Is my heart hard? that even now the thought<br>Should force itself upon me — yet I feel it! |
| FRANCESCO | The drops did start and stand upon his forehead!<br>I will return — in very [[Truth|truth]] I grieve<br>To have been the occasion. Ho! attend me, woman! |
| ALHADRA | *(to [[Maria]].)* O gentle lady, make the father stay<br>Till that my lord recover. I am sure<br>That he will say he is my husband’s friend. |
| [[Maria]] | Stay, father, stay — my lord will soon recover. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(OSORIO and VELEZ returning.)* |
| OSORIO | *(to Velez as they return.)* Strange! that this Francesco<br>Should have the power so to distemper me. |
| VELEZ | Nay, ‘twas an amiable weakness, son! |
| FRANCESCO | *(to Osorio.)* My lord, I truly grieve —— |
| OSORIO | Tut! name it not.<br>A sudden seizure, father! think not of it.<br>As to this woman’s husband, I do know him:<br>I know him [[Well|well]], and that he is a Christian. |
| FRANCESCO | I [[Hope|hope]], my lord, your sensibility<br>Doth not prevail. |
| OSORIO | Nay, nay — you know me better.<br>You hear what I have said. But ‘tis a trifle.<br>I had something here of more importance. *(Touching his forehead as if in the act of recollection.)*<br>Hah!<br>The Count Mondejar, our great general,<br>Writes, that the bishop we were talking of<br>Has sicken’d dangerously. |
| FRANCESCO | Even so. |
| OSORIO | I must return my answer. |
| FRANCESCO | When, my lord? |
| OSORIO | To-morrow morning, and shall not forget<br>How bright and strong your zeal for the Catholic [[Faith|faith]]. |
| FRANCESCO | You are too kind, my lord! You overwhelm me. |
| OSORIO | Nay, say not so. As for this [[Ferdinand]],<br>‘Tis certain that he was a Catholic.<br>What changes may have happen’d in three years,<br>I cannot say, but grant me this, good father!<br>I’ll go and sift him: if I find him sound,<br>You’ll grant me your authority and name<br>To liberate his [[house]]. |
| FRANCESCO | My lord you have it. |
| OSORIO | *(to Alhadra.)* I will attend you [[Home|home]] within an hour.<br>Meantime return with us, and take refreshment. |
| ALHADRA | Not till my husband’s free, I may not do it.<br>I will stay here. |
| [[Maria]] | *(aside.)* Who is this [[Ferdinand]]? |
| VELEZ | Daughter! |
| [[Maria]] | With your permission, my dear lord,<br>I’ll loiter a few minutes, and then join you. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Exeunt VELEZ, FRANCESCO, and OSORIO.)* |
| ALHADRA | Hah! there he goes. A bitter curse go with him.<br>A scathing curse! *(ALHADRA had been betrayed by the warmth of her feelings into an imprudence. She checks herself, yet recollecting [[Maria|MARIA]]’S manner towards FRANCESCO, says in a shy and distrustful manner)*<br>You hate him, don’t you, lady! |
| [[Maria]] | Nay, [[Fear|fear]] me not! my heart is sad for you. |
| ALHADRA | These fell Inquisitors, these sons of [[Blood|blood]]!<br>As I came on, his face so madden’d me<br>That ever and anon I clutch’d my dagger<br>And half unsheathed it. |
| [[Maria]] | Be more calm, I pray you. |
| ALHADRA | And as he stalk’d along the narrow path<br>Close on the mountain’s edge, my [[Soul|soul]] grew eager.<br>‘Twas with hard toil I made myself remember<br>That his foul officers held my babes and husband.<br>To have leapt upon him with a Tyger’s plunge<br>And hurl’d him down the ragged precipice,<br>O — it had been most sweet! |
| [[Maria]] | Hush, hush! for shame.<br>Where is your woman’s heart? |
| ALHADRA | O gentle lady!<br>You have no skill to guess my many wrongs,<br>Many and strange. Besides I am a Christian,<br>And they do never pardon, ‘tis their [[Faith|faith]]! |
| [[Maria]] | Shame fall on those who so have shown it to thee! |
| ALHADRA | I know that man; ‘tis [[Well|well]] he knows not me!<br>Five years ago, and he was the prime agent.<br>Five years ago the Holy Brethren seized me. |
| [[Maria]] | What might your crime be? |
| ALHADRA | Solely my complexion.<br>They cast me, then a young and nursing mother,<br>Into a dungeon of their [[Prison|prison]] [[house]].<br>There was no bed, no [[Fire|fire]], no ray of light,<br>No touch, no sound of comfort! The black air,<br>It was a toil to breathe it! I have seen<br>The gaoler’s lamp, the moment that he enter’d,<br>How the flame sunk at once down to the socket.<br>O miserable, by that lamp to see<br>My infant quarrelling with the coarse hard bread<br>Brought daily: for the little wretch was sickly —<br>My rage had dry’d away its natural food!<br>In darkness I remain’d, counting the clocks<br>Which haply told me that the blessed sun<br>Was rising on my [[Garden|garden]]. When I dozed,<br>My infant’s moanings mingled with my [[Dreams|dreams]]<br>And wak’d me. If you were a mother, Lady,<br>I should scarce dare to tell you, that its noises<br>And peevish cries so fretted on my brain<br>That I have struck the [[Innocent|innocent]] babe in anger! |
| [[Maria]] | O God! it is too horrible to hear! |
| ALHADRA | What was it then to suffer? ‘Tis most right<br>That such as you should hear it. Know you not<br>What [[Nature]] makes you mourn, she bids you heal?<br>Great evils ask great passions to redress them,<br>And whirlwinds fitliest scatter pestilence. |
| [[Maria]] | You were at length deliver’d? |
| ALHADRA | Yes, at length<br>I saw the blessed arch of the whole heaven.<br>‘Twas the first [[Time|time]] my infant smiled! No more.<br>For if I dwell upon that moment, lady,<br>A fit comes on, which makes me o’er again<br>All I then was, my knees hang loose and drag,<br>And my lip falls with such an ideot laugh<br>That you would start and shudder! |
| [[Maria]] | But your husband? |
| ALHADRA | A month’s [[Imprisonment|imprisonment]] would kill him, lady! |
| [[Maria]] | Alas, poor man! |
| ALHADRA | He hath a [[Lion|lion]]’s [[Courage|courage]],<br>But is not stern enough for fortitude.<br>Unfit for boisterous times, with gentle heart<br>He worships [[Nature]] in the [[Hill|hill]] and valley,<br>Not knowing what he loves, but loves it all! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Enter ALBERT disguised as a Moresco, and in Moorish garments.)* |
| ALBERT | *(not observing [[Maria]] and Alhadra.)* Three weeks have I been loitering here, nor ever<br>Have summon’d up my heart to ask one question,<br>Or stop one peasant passing on this way. |
| [[Maria]] | Know you that man? |
| ALHADRA | His person, not his name.<br>I [[Doubt|doubt]] not, he is some Moresco chieftain<br>Who hides himself among the Alpuxarras.<br>A week has scarcely pass’d since first I saw him;<br>He has new-roof’d the desolate old cottage<br>Where Zagri lived — who dared avow the [[Prophet|prophet]]<br>And died like one of the faithful! There he lives,<br>And a friend with him. |
| [[Maria]] | Does he know his danger<br>So near this seat? |
| ALHADRA | He wears the Moorish robes too,<br>As in defiance of the royal edict. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(ALHADRA advances to ALBERT, who has walked to the back of the stage near the rocks. [[Maria|MARIA]] drops her veil.)* |
| ALHADRA | Gallant Moresco! you are near the [[Castle|castle]]<br>Of the Lord Velez, and hard by does dwell<br>A priest, the creature of the [[Inquisition]]. |
| ALBERT | *(retiring.)* You have mistaken me — I am a Christian. |
| ALHADRA | *(to [[Maria]].)* He deems that we are plotting to ensnare him.<br>Speak to him, lady! none can hear you speak<br>And not believe you [[Innocent|innocent]] of guile. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(ALBERT, on hearing this, pauses and turns round.)* |
| [[Maria]] | If aught enforce you to concealment, sir! |
| ALHADRA | He trembles strangely. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(ALBERT sinks down and hides his face in his garment [robe Remorse].)* |
| [[Maria]] | See — we have disturb’d him. *(Approaches nearer to him.)*<br>I pray you, think us friends — uncowl your face,<br>For you seem faint, and the [[Night|night]]-breeze blows healing.<br>I pray you, think us friends! |
| ALBERT | *(raising his head.)* Calm — very calm;<br>‘Tis all too tranquil for reality!<br>And she spoke to me with her [[Innocent|innocent]] voice.<br>That voice! that [[Innocent|innocent]] voice! She is no traitress!<br>It was [[A Dream|a dream]], a [[Phantom|phantom]] of my [[Sleep|sleep]],<br>A lying dream. *(He starts up, and abruptly addresses her.)*<br>[[Maria]]! you are not wedded? |
| [[Maria]] | *(haughtily to Alhadra.)* Let us retire. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(They advance to the front of the stage.)* |
| ALHADRA | He is indeed a Christian.<br>Some stray Sir Knight, that falls in [[Love|love]] of a sudden. |
| [[Maria]] | What can this mean? How should he know my name?<br>It seems all shadowy. |
| ALHADRA | Here he comes again. |
| ALBERT | *(aside.)* She deems me dead, and yet no mourning garment!<br>Why should my brother’s wife wear mourning garments?<br>God of all [[Mercy|mercy]], make me, make me quiet! *(To [[Maria|MARIA]].)*<br>Your pardon, gentle maid! that I disturb’d you.<br>I had just started from a frightful dream. |
| ALHADRA | These renegado Moors — how soon they learn<br>The crimes and follies of their Christian tyrants! |
| ALBERT | I dreamt I had a friend, on whom I lean’d<br>With blindest trust, and a betrothéd maid<br>Whom I was wont to call not mine, but me,<br>For mine own [[Self|self]] seem’d nothing, lacking her!<br>This maid so idoliz’d, that trusted friend,<br>Polluted in my absence [[Soul|soul]] and [[Body|body]]!<br>And she with him and he with her conspired<br>To have me murder’d in a wood of the [[Mountains|mountains]]:<br>But by my looks and most impassion’d words<br>I roused the virtues, that are dead in no man,<br>Even in the assassins’ hearts. They made their terms,<br>And thank’d me for redeeming them from murder. |
| ALHADRA | *(to [[Maria]].)* You are lost in thought. Hear him no more, sweet lady! |
| [[Maria]] | From morn to [[Night|night]] I am myself a dreamer,<br>And slight things bring on me the idle mood.<br>[[Well]], sir, what happen’d then? |
| ALBERT | On a rude rock,<br>A rock, methought, fast by a grove of firs<br>Whose threaddy [[Leaves|leaves]] to the low breathing gale<br>Made a soft sound most like the distant ocean,<br>I stay’d as tho’ the hour of [[Death|death]] were past,<br>And I were sitting in the world of spirits,<br>For all things seem’d unreal! There I sate.<br>The dews fell clammy, and the [[Night|night]] descended,<br>Black, sultry, close! and ere the midnight hour<br>A [[Storm|storm]] came on, mingling all sounds of [[Fear|fear]]<br>That woods and sky and [[Mountains|mountains]] seem’d one havock!<br>The second flash of [[Lightning|lightning]] show’d a tree<br>Hard by me, newly-scathed. I [[Rose|rose]] tumultuous:<br>My [[Soul|soul]] work’d high: I bared my head to the [[Storm|storm]],<br>And with loud voice and clamorous agony<br>Kneeling I pray’d to the great Spirit that made me,<br>Pray’d that Remorse might fasten on their hearts,<br>And cling, with poisonous tooth, inextricable<br>As the gored [[Lion|lion]]’s bite! |
| [[Maria]] | A fearful curse! |
| ALHADRA | But dreamt you not that you return’d and kill’d him?<br>Dreamt you of no [[Revenge|revenge]]? |
| ALBERT | *(his voice trembling, and in tones of deep distress.)* She would have died,<br>Died in her sins — perchance, by her own hands!<br>And bending o’er her [[Self|self]]-inflicted wounds<br>I might have met the evil glance of frenzy<br>And leapt myself into an unblest [[Grave|grave]]!<br>I pray’d for the punishment that cleanses hearts,<br>For still I loved her! |
| ALHADRA | And you dreamt all this? |
| [[Maria]] | My [[Soul|soul]] is full of visions, all is wild! |
| ALHADRA | There is no room in this heart for puling [[Love|love]]-tales.<br>Lady! your servants there seem seeking us. |
| [[Maria]] | *(lifts up her veil and advances to Albert.)* Stranger, farewell! I guess not who you are,<br>Nor why you so address’d your tale to me.<br>Your mien is noble, and, I own, perplex’d me<br>With obscure [[Memory|memory]] of something past,<br>Which still escap’d my efforts, or presented<br>Tricks of a fancy pamper’d with long-wishing.<br>If (as it sometimes happens) our rude startling,<br>While your full heart was shaping out its dream,<br>Drove you to this, your not ungentle wildness,<br>You have my sympathy, and so farewell!<br>But if some undiscover’d wrongs oppress you,<br>And you need strength to drag them into light,<br>The generous Velez, and my Lord Osorio<br>Have arm and will to aid a noble sufferer,<br>Nor shall you want my favourable pleading. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Exeunt [[Maria|MARIA]] and ALHADRA.)* |
| ALBERT | *([[Alone|alone]].)* ‘Tis strange! it cannot be! my Lord Osorio!<br>Her Lord Osorio! Nay, I will not do it.<br>I curs’d him once, and one curse is enough.<br>How sad she look’d and pale! but not like [[Guilt|guilt]],<br>And her calm tones — sweet as a [[Song|song]] of [[Mercy|mercy]]!<br>If the bad spirit retain’d his angel’s voice,<br>Hell scarce were hell. And why not [[Innocent|innocent]]?<br>Who meant to murder me might [[Well|well]] cheat her.<br>But ere she married him, he had stain’d her [[Honour|honour]].<br>Ah! there I am hamper’d. What if this were a lie<br>Fram’d by the assassin? who should tell it him<br>If it were [[Truth|truth]]? Osorio would not tell him.<br>Yet why one lie? All else, I know, was [[Truth|truth]].<br>No start! no [[Jealousy|jealousy]] of stirring [[Conscience|conscience]]!<br>And she referr’d to me — fondly, methought!<br>Could she walk here, if that she were a traitress?<br>Here where we play’d together in our [[Childhood|childhood]]?<br>Here where we plighted vows? Where her cold cheek<br>Received my last kiss, when with suppress’d feelings<br>She had fainted in my arms? It cannot be!<br>‘Tis not in [[Nature|nature]]! I will die, believing<br>That I shall meet her where no evil is,<br>No treachery, no cup dash’d from the lips!<br>I’ll haunt this scene no more — live she in peace!<br>Her husband — ay, her husband! May this Angel<br>New-mould his canker’d heart! Assist me, Heaven!<br>That I may pray for my poor guilty brother! |
| *(didascalia)* | END OF ACT THE FIRST. |
