| *(didascalia)* | [[Maria|MARIA]] and her FOSTER-MOTHER. |
| [[Maria]] | And when I heard that you desired to see me,<br>I thought your business was to tell me of him. |
| FOSTER-MOTHER | I never saw the Moor, whom you describe. |
| [[Maria]] | ‘Tis strange! he spake of you familiarly<br>As mine and Albert’s common foster-mother. |
| FOSTER-MOTHER | Now blessings on the man, whoe’er he be,<br>That join’d your names with mine! O my sweet lady,<br>As often as I think of those dear times<br>When you two little ones would stand at eve,<br>On each side of my chair, and make me learn<br>All you had learnt in the day; and how to talk<br>In gentle phrase, then bid me sing to you,<br>‘Tis more like heaven to come, that what has been! |
| [[Maria]] | O my dear mother! this strange man has left me<br>Wilder’d with wilder fancies than yon [[Moon|moon]]<br>Breeds in the [[Love|love]]-sick maid — who gazes at it<br>Till lost in inward vision, with wet eye<br>She gazes idly! But that entrance, mother! |
| FOSTER-MOTHER | Can no one hear? It is a perilous tale! |
| [[Maria]] | No one. |
| FOSTER-MOTHER | My husband’s father told it me,<br>Poor old Leoni. Angels rest his [[Soul|soul]]!<br>He was a woodman, and could fell and saw<br>With lusty arm. You know that huge round beam<br>Which props the hanging wall of the old chapel?<br>Beneath that tree, while yet it was a tree,<br>He found a baby wrapt in mosses, lined<br>With thistle-beards, and such small locks of wool<br>As hang on brambles. [[Well]], he brought him [[Home|home]],<br>And rear’d him at the then Lord Velez’ cost.<br>And so the babe grew up a pretty boy.<br>A pretty boy, but most unteachable —<br>And never learnt a [[prayer]], nor told a bead,<br>But knew the names of [[Birds|birds]], and mock’d their notes,<br>And whistled, as he were a bird himself.<br>And all the [[Autumn|autumn]] ‘twas his only play<br>To get the seeds of wild [[Flowers|flowers]], and to plant them<br>With earth and [[Water|water]] on the stumps of trees.<br>A friar who gather’d simples in the wood,<br>A grey-hair’d man — he loved this little boy,<br>The boy loved him — and, when the friar taught him,<br>He soon could write with the pen; and from that [[Time|time]]<br>Lived chiefly at the convent or the [[Castle|castle]].<br>So he became a very learned youth.<br>But O! poor wretch — he read, and read, and read,<br>Till his brain turn’d — and ere his twentieth year,<br>He had unlawful thoughts of many things.<br>And though he pray’d, he never loved to pray<br>With holy men, nor in a holy place.<br>But yet his speech, it was so soft and sweet,<br>The late Lord Velez ne’er was wearied with him,<br>And once as by the north side of the chapel<br>They stood together, chain’d in deep discourse,<br>The earth heav’d under them with such a groan,<br>That the wall totter’d, and had [[Well|well]]-nigh fall’n<br>Right on their heads. My lord was sorely frighten’d;<br>A fever seiz’d him; and he made confession<br>Of all the heretical and lawless talk<br>Which brought this judgment: so the youth was seiz’d<br>And cast into that hole. My husband’s father<br>Sobb’d like a [[Child|child]] — it almost broke his heart.<br>And once as he was working in the cellar,<br>He heard a voice distinctly; ‘twas the youth’s,<br>Who sung a doleful [[Song|song]] about green fields,<br>How sweet it were on lake or wild savannah<br>To hunt for food, and be a naked man,<br>And wander up and down at liberty.<br>He always doted on the youth, and now<br>His [[Love|love]] grew desperate; and defying [[Death|death]],<br>He made that cunning entrance I described:<br>And the young man escaped. |
| [[Maria]] | ‘Tis a sweet tale:<br>Such as would lull a list’ning [[Child|child]] to [[Sleep|sleep]],<br>His rosy face besoil’d with unwiped [[Tears|tears]].<br>And what became of him? |
| FOSTER-MOTHER | He went on shipboard<br>With those bold voyagers, who made discovery<br>Of golden lands; Leoni’s younger brother<br>Went likewise, and when he return’d to Spain,<br>He told Leoni that the poor mad youth,<br>Soon after they arrived in that new world,<br>In spite of his dissuasion seized a boat,<br>And all [[Alone|alone]] set sail by silent moonlight,<br>Up a great [[River|river]], great as any sea,<br>And ne’er was heard of more; but ‘tis supposed<br>He liv’d and died among the savage men. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter VELEZ. |
| VELEZ | Still sad, [[Maria]]? This same wizard haunts you. |
| [[Maria]] | O [[Christ]]! the tortures that hang o’er his head,<br>If ye betray him to these holy brethren! |
| VELEZ | *(with a kind of sneer.)* A portly man, and eloquent, and tender!<br>In [[Truth|truth]], I shall not [[Wonder|wonder]] if you mourn<br>That their rude grasp should seize on such a victim. |
| [[Maria]] | The horror of their ghastly punishments<br>Doth so o’ertop the height of sympathy,<br>That I should feel too little for mine enemy —<br>Ah! far too little — if ‘twere possible,<br>I could feel more, even tho’ my [[Child|child]] or husband<br>Were doom’d to suffer them! That such things are —— |
| VELEZ | Hush! thoughtless woman! |
| [[Maria]] | Nay — it wakes within me<br>More than a woman’s spirit. |
| VELEZ | *(angrily.)* No more of this —<br>I can endure no more. |
| FOSTER-MOTHER | My [[Honour|honour]]’d master!<br>Lord Albert used to talk so. |
| [[Maria]] | Yes! my mother!<br>These are my Albert’s lessons, and I con them<br>With more delight than, in my fondest hour,<br>I bend me o’er his portrait. |
| VELEZ | *(to the Foster-Mother.)* My good woman,<br>You may retire. *(Exit the FOSTER-MOTHER.)* |
| VELEZ | We have mourn’d for Albert.<br>Have I no living son? |
| [[Maria]] | Speak not of him!<br>That low imposture — my heart sickens at it,<br>If it be [[Madness|madness]], must I wed a madman?<br>And if not [[Madness|madness]], there is mystery,<br>And [[Guilt|guilt]] doth lurk behind it! |
| VELEZ | Is this [[Well|well]]? |
| [[Maria]] | Yes! it is [[Truth|truth]]. Saw you his countenance?<br>How rage, remorse, and scorn, and stupid [[Fear|fear]],<br>Displac’d each other with swift interchanges?<br>If this were all assumed, as you believe,<br>He must needs be a most consummate actor;<br>And hath so vast a power to deceive me,<br>I never could be safe. And why assume<br>The semblance of such execrable feelings? |
| VELEZ | Ungrateful woman! I have tried to stifle<br>An old man’s passion! Was it not enough<br>That thou hast made my son a restless man,<br>Banish’d his health and half-unhinged his reason,<br>But that thou wilt insult him with suspicion,<br>And toil to blast his [[Honour|honour]]? I am old —<br>A comfortless old man! Thou shalt not stay<br>Beneath my roof! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(FRANCESCO enters and stands listening.)* |
| VELEZ | Repent and marry him —<br>Or to the convent. |
| FRANCESCO | *(muttering.)* Good! good! very good! |
| [[Maria]] | Nay, grant me some small pittance of my fortune,<br>And I will live a solitary woman,<br>Or my poor foster-mother and her grandsons<br>May be my household. |
| FRANCESCO | *(advancing.)* I abhor a listener;<br>But you spoke so, I could not choose but hear you.<br>I pray, my lord! will you embolden me<br>To ask you why this lady doth prefer<br>To live in lonely sort, without a friend<br>Or fit companion? |
| VELEZ | Bid her answer you. |
| [[Maria]] | [[Nature]] will be my friend and fit companion. *(Turns off from them.)*<br>O Albert! Albert! that they could return,<br>Those blessed days, that imitated heaven!<br>When we two wont to walk at evening-tide;<br>When we saw nought but [[Beauty|beauty]]; when we heard<br>The voice of that Almighty One, who lov’d us,<br>In every gale that breath’d, and wave that murmur’d!<br>O we have listen’d, even till high-wrought pleasure<br>Hath half-assumed the countenance of grief,<br>And the deep sigh seem’d to heave up a weight<br>Of bliss, that press’d too heavy on the heart. |
| FRANCESCO | But in the convent, lady, you would have<br>Such aids as might preserve you from perdition.<br>There you might dwell. |
| [[Maria]] | With tame and credulous [[Faith|faith]],<br>Mad [[Melancholy|melancholy]], antic merriment,<br>Leanness, disquietude, and secret pangs!<br>O God! it is a horrid thing to know<br>That each pale wretch, who sits and drops her beads<br>Had once a mind, which might have given her wings<br>Such as the angels wear! |
| FRANCESCO | *(stifling his rage.)* Where is your son, my lord? |
| VELEZ | I have not seen him, father, since he left you. |
| FRANCESCO | His lordship’s generous [[Nature|nature]] hath deceiv’d him!<br>That [[Ferdinand]] (or if not he his wife)<br>I have fresh evidence — are infidels.<br>We are not safe until they are rooted out. |
| [[Maria]] | Thou man, who call’st thyself the minister<br>Of Him whose law was [[Love|love]] unutterable!<br>Why is thy [[Soul|soul]] so parch’d with cruelty,<br>That still thou thirstest for thy brother’s [[Blood|blood]]? |
| VELEZ | *(rapidly.)* Father! I have long suspected it — her brain —<br>Heed it not, father! |
| FRANCESCO | Nay — but I must heed it. |
| [[Maria]] | Thou miserable man! I [[Fear|fear]] thee not,<br>Nor prize a life which soon may weary me.<br>Bear witness, Heav’n! I neither scorn nor hate him —<br>But O! ‘tis wearisome to mourn for evils,<br>Still mourn, and have no power to remedy! *(Exit [[Maria|MARIA]].)* |
| FRANCESCO | My lord! I shall presume to wait on you<br>To-morrow early. |
| VELEZ | Be it so, good father! *(Exit FRANCESCO.)* |
| VELEZ | *([[Alone|alone]].)* I do want solace, but not such as thine!<br>The [[Moon|moon]] is high in heaven, and my eyes ache,<br>But not with [[Sleep|sleep]]. [[Well]] — it is ever so.<br>A [[Child|child]], a [[Child|child]] is born! and the fond heart<br>Dances! and yet the childless are most happy. |
| *(didascalia)* | SCENE changes to the [[Mountains|mountains]] by moonlight. |
