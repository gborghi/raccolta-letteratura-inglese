| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | Caesar is fall'n! The baneful tree of Java,<br>Whose [[Death\|death]]-distilling boughs dropt poisonous dew,<br>Is rooted from its base. This worse than Cromwell,<br>The austere, the [[Self\|self]]-denying Robespierre,<br>Even in this hall, where once with terror mute<br>We listen'd to the hypocrite's harangues,<br>Has heard his doom. |
| BILLAUD VARENNES | Yet must we not suppose<br>The [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] will fall tamely. His sworn hireling<br>Henriot, the daring desperate Henriot,<br>Commands the force of [[Paris]]. I denounce him. |
| FRERON | I denounce Fleuriot too, the mayor of [[Paris]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter DUBOIS CRANCÉ. |
| DUBOIS CRANCÉ | Robespierre is rescued. Henriot at the head<br>Of the arm'd force has rescued the fierce [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]. |
| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | Ring the tocsin — call all the citizens<br>To save their country — never yet has [[Paris]]<br>Forsook the representatives of France. |
| TALLIEN | It is the hour of danger. I propose<br>This sitting be made permanent. [Loud applauses. |
| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | The National Convention shall remain<br>Firm at its post. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger. |
| MESSENGER | Robespierre has reach'd the Commune. They espouse<br>The [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]'s cause. St. Just is up in arms!<br>St. Just — the young ambitious bold St. Just<br>Harangues the mob. The sanguinary Couthon<br>Thirsts for your [[Blood\|blood]]. [Tocsin rings. |
| TALLIEN | These tyrants are in arms against the law:<br>Outlaw the rebels. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter MERLIN OF DOUAY. |
| MERLIN | Health to the representatives of France!<br>I past this moment through the arméd force —<br>They ask'd my name — and when they heard a delegate,<br>Swore I was not the friend of France. |
| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | The tyrants threaten us as when they turn'd<br>The cannon's mouth on Brissot. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger. |
| SECOND MESSENGER | Vivier harangues the Jacobins — the Club<br>Espouse the cause of Robespierre. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger. |
| THIRD MESSENGER | All's lost — the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] triumphs. Henriot leads<br>The soldiers to his aid. — Already I hear<br>The rattling cannon destined to surround<br>This sacred hall. |
| TALLIEN | Why, we will die like men then.<br>The representatives of France dare [[Death\|death]],<br>When duty steels their bosoms. [Loud applauses. |
| TALLIEN | (addressing the galleries.) Citizens!<br>France is insulted in her delegates —<br>The majesty of the Republic is insulted —<br>Tyrants are up in arms. An arméd force<br>Threats the Convention. The Convention swears<br>To die, or save the country! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Violent applauses from the galleries. |
| CITIZEN | (from above.) We too swear<br>To die, or save the country. Follow me. |
| *(didascalia)* | [All the men quit the galleries. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger. |
| FOURTH MESSENGER | Henriot is taken! [Loud applauses.<br>Three of your brave soldiers<br>Swore they would seize the [[Rebel\|rebel]] slave of tyrants,<br>Or perish in the attempt. As he patroll'd<br>The streets of [[Paris]], stirring up the mob,<br>They seiz'd him. [Applauses. |
| BILLAUD VARENNES | Let the names of these brave men<br>Live to the future day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter BOURDON L'OISE, [[Sword\|sword]] in hand. |
| BOURDON L'OISE | I have clear'd the Commune.<br>[Applauses.<br>Through the throng I rush'd,<br>Brandishing my good [[Sword\|sword]] to drench its blade<br>Deep in the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]'s heart. The timid rebels<br>Gave way. I met the soldiery — I spake<br>Of the dictator's crimes — of patriots chain'd<br>In dark deep dungeons by his lawless rage —<br>Of knaves secure beneath his fostering power.<br>I spake of Liberty. Their honest hearts<br>Caught the warm flame. The general shout burst forth,<br>'Live the Convention — Down with Robespierre!' [Applauses. |
| *(didascalia)* | (Shouts from without — Down with the [[Tyrant]]!) |
| TALLIEN | I hear, I hear the [[Soul\|soul]]-inspiring sounds,<br>France shall be saved! her generous sons attached<br>To principles, not persons, spurn the idol<br>They worshipp'd once. Yes, Robespierre shall fall<br>As Capet fell! Oh! never let us deem<br>That France shall crouch beneath a [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]'s throne,<br>That the almighty people who have broke<br>On their oppressors' heads the oppressive chain,<br>Will [[Court\|court]] again their fetters! easier were it<br>To hurl the cloud-capt mountain from its base,<br>Than force the bonds of [[Slavery\|slavery]] upon men<br>Determined to be free! [Applauses. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter LEGENDRE — a [[Pistol\|pistol]] in one hand, keys in the other. |
| LEGENDRE | (flinging down the keys.) So — let the mutinous Jacobins meet now<br>In the open air. [Loud applauses.<br>A factious turbulent party<br>Lording it o'er the state since Danton died,<br>And with him the Cordeliers. — A hireling band<br>Of loud-tongued orators controull'd the Club,<br>And bade them bow the knee to Robespierre.<br>Vivier has 'scaped me. Curse his coward heart —<br>This fate-fraught tube of Justice in my hand,<br>I rush'd into the hall. He mark'd mine eye<br>That beam'd its patriot anger, and flash'd full<br>With [[Death\|death]]-denouncing meaning. 'Mid the throng<br>He mingled. I pursued — but stay'd my hand,<br>Lest haply I might shed the [[Innocent\|innocent]] [[Blood\|blood]]. [Applauses. |
| FRERON | They took from me my ticket of admission —<br>Expell'd me from their sittings. — Now, forsooth,<br>Humbled and trembling re-insert my name.<br>But Freron enters not the Club again<br>'Till it be purged of [[Guilt\|guilt]]:—'till, purified<br>Of tyrants and of traitors, honest men<br>May breathe the air in safety. [Shouts from without. |
| BARRERE | What means this uproar! if the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] band<br>Should gain the people once again to rise —<br>We are as dead! |
| TALLIEN | And wherefore [[Fear\|fear]] we [[Death\|death]]?<br>Did [[Brutus]] [[Fear\|fear]] it? or the Grecian friends<br>Who buried in Hipparchus' breast the [[Sword\|sword]],<br>And died triumphant? Caesar should [[Fear\|fear]] [[Death\|death]],<br>[[Brutus]] must scorn the bugbear. |
| *(didascalia)* | (Shouts from without — Live the Convention! — Down with the Tyrants!) |
| TALLIEN | Hark! again<br>The sounds of honest [[Freedom]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Deputies from the Sections. |
| CITIZEN | Citizens! representatives of France!<br>Hold on your steady course. The men of [[Paris]]<br>Espouse your cause. The men of [[Paris]] swear<br>They will defend the delegates of [[Freedom]]. |
| TALLIEN | Hear ye this, Colleagues? hear ye this, my brethren?<br>And does no thrill of joy pervade your breasts?<br>My bosom bounds to rapture. I have seen<br>The sons of France shake off the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] yoke;<br>I have, as much as lies in mine own arm,<br>Hurl'd down the [[Usurper\|usurper]]. — Come [[Death\|death]] when it will,<br>I have lived long enough. [Shouts without. |
| BARRERE | Hark! how the noise increases! through the gloom<br>Of the still evening — harbinger of [[Death\|death]],<br>Rings the tocsin! the dreadful generale<br>Thunders through [[Paris]] — |
| *(didascalia)* | [Cry without — Down with the [[Tyrant]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter LECOINTRE. |
| LECOINTRE | So may eternal justice blast the foes<br>Of France! so perish all the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] brood,<br>As Robespierre has perish'd! Citizens,<br>Caesar is taken. [Loud and repeated applauses.<br>I marvel not that with such fearless front<br>He braved our vengeance, and with angry eye<br>Scowled round the hall defiance. He relied<br>On Henriot's aid — the Commune's [[Villain\|villain]] [[Friendship\|friendship]],<br>And Henriot's boughten succours. Ye have heard<br>How Henriot rescued him — how with open arms<br>The Commune welcom'd in the [[Rebel\|rebel]] [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] —<br>How Fleuriot aided, and seditious Vivier<br>Stirr'd up the Jacobins. All had been lost —<br>The representatives of France had perish'd —<br>[[Freedom]] had sunk beneath the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] arm<br>Of this foul parricide, but that her spirit<br>Inspir'd the men of [[Paris]]. Henriot call'd<br>'To arms' in vain, whilst Bourdon's patriot voice<br>Breathed eloquence, and o'er the Jacobins<br>Legendre frown'd dismay. The tyrants fled —<br>They reach'd the Hôtel. We gather'd round — we call'd<br>For vengeance! Long [[Time\|time]], obstinate in [[Despair\|despair]],<br>With knives they hack'd around them. 'Till foreboding<br>The sentence of the law, the clamorous cry<br>Of joyful thousands hailing their destruction,<br>Each sought by [[suicide]] to escape the dread<br>Of [[Death\|death]]. Lebas succeeded. From the [[Window\|window]]<br>Leapt the younger Robespierre, but his fractur'd limb<br>Forbade to escape. The [[Self\|self]]-will'd dictator<br>Plunged often the keen knife in his dark breast,<br>Yet impotent to die. He lives all mangled<br>By his own tremulous hand! All gash'd and gored<br>He lives to taste the bitterness of [[Death\|death]].<br>Even now they meet their doom. The bloody Couthon,<br>The fierce St. Just, even now attend their [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]<br>To fall beneath the axe. I saw the torches<br>Flash on their visages a dreadful light —<br>I saw them whilst the black [[Blood\|blood]] roll'd adown<br>Each stern face, even then with dauntless eye<br>Scowl round contemptuous, dying as they lived,<br>Fearless of fate! [Loud and repeated applauses. |
| BARRERE | (mounts the Tribune.) For ever hallowed be this glorious day,<br>When [[Freedom]], bursting her oppressive chain,<br>Tramples on the oppressor. When the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]<br>Hurl'd from his [[Blood\|blood]]-cemented throne, by the arm<br>Of the almighty people, meets the [[Death\|death]]<br>He plann'd for thousands. Oh! my sickening heart<br>Has sunk within me, when the various woes<br>Of my brave country crowded o'er my brain<br>In ghastly numbers — when assembled hordes,<br>Dragg'd from their hovels by despotic power,<br>Rush'd o'er her frontiers, plunder'd her fair hamlets,<br>And sack'd her populous towns, and drench'd with [[Blood\|blood]]<br>The reeking fields of Flanders. — When within,<br>Upon her vitals prey'd the rankling tooth<br>Of treason; and oppression, giant form,<br>Trampling on [[Freedom\|freedom]], left the alternative<br>Of [[Slavery\|slavery]], or of [[Death\|death]]. Even from that day,<br>When, on the guilty Capet, I pronounced<br>The doom of injured France, has faction reared<br>Her hated head amongst us. Roland preach'd<br>Of [[Mercy\|mercy]] — the uxorious dotard Roland,<br>The woman-govern'd Roland durst aspire<br>To govern France; and Petion talk'd of virtue,<br>And Vergniaud's eloquence, like the honeyed tongue<br>Of some soft Syren wooed us to destruction.<br>We triumphed over these. On the same scaffold<br>Where the last Louis pour'd his guilty [[Blood\|blood]],<br>Fell Brissot's head, the womb of darksome treasons,<br>And Orleans, [[Villain\|villain]] kinsman of the Capet,<br>And Hébert's atheist crew, whose maddening hand<br>Hurl'd down the altars of the living God,<br>With all the infidel's intolerance.<br>The last worst traitor triumphed — triumph'd long,<br>Secur'd by matchless villainy — by turns<br>Defending and deserting each accomplice<br>As interest prompted. In the goodly soil<br>Of [[Freedom]], the foul tree of treason struck<br>Its deep-fix'd roots, and dropt the dews of [[Death\|death]]<br>On all who slumber'd in its specious shade.<br>He wove the web of treachery. He caught<br>The listening crowd by his wild eloquence,<br>His cool ferocity that persuaded murder,<br>Even whilst it spake of [[Mercy\|mercy]]! — never, never<br>Shall this regenerated country wear<br>The despot yoke. Though myriads round assail,<br>And with worse fury urge this new crusade<br>Than savages have known; though the leagued despots<br>Depopulate all Europe, so to pour<br>The accumulated mass upon our coasts,<br>[[Sublime]] amid the [[Storm\|storm]] shall France arise,<br>And like the rock amid surrounding waves<br>Repel the rushing ocean. — She shall wield<br>The thunder-bolt of vengeance — she shall blast<br>The despot's [[Pride\|pride]], and liberate the world! |
