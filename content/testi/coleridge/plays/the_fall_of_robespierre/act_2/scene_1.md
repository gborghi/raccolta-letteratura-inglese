| *(didascalia)* | Robespierre mounts the Tribune. |
| ROBESPIERRE | Once more befits it that the voice<br>of [[Truth]],<br>Fearless in [[innocence]], though leaguered round<br>By Envy and her hateful brood of hell,<br>Be heard amid this hall; once more befits<br>The patriot, whose prophetic eye so oft<br>Has pierced thro' faction's veil, to flash on crimes<br>Of deadliest import. Mouldering in the [[Grave\|grave]]<br>Sleeps Capet's caitiff corse; my daring hand<br>Levelled to earth his [[Blood\|blood]]-cemented throne,<br>My voice declared his [[Guilt\|guilt]], and stirred up France<br>To call for vengeance. I too dug the [[Grave\|grave]]<br>Where [[Sleep\|sleep]] the Girondists, detested band!<br>Long with the shew of [[Freedom\|freedom]] they abused<br>Her ardent sons. Long [[Time\|time]] the [[Well\|well]]-turn'd phrase,<br>The high-fraught sentence and the lofty tone<br>Of declamation, thunder'd in this hall,<br>Till reason midst a labyrinth of words<br>Perplex'd, in [[Silence\|silence]] seem'd to yield assent.<br>I durst oppose. [[Soul]] of my honoured friend,<br>Spirit of Marat, upon thee I call —<br>Thou know'st me faithful, know'st with what warm zeal<br>I urg'd the cause of justice, stripp'd the mask<br>From faction's deadly visage, and destroy'd<br>Her traitor brood. Whose patriot arm hurl'd down<br>Hébert and Rousin, and the [[Villain\|villain]] friends<br>Of Danton, foul apostate! those, who long<br>Mask'd treason's form in liberty's fair garb,<br>Long deluged France with [[Blood\|blood]], and durst defy<br>Omnipotence! but I it seems am false!<br>I am a traitor too! I — Robespierre!<br>I — at whose name the dastard despot brood<br>Look pale with [[Fear\|fear]], and call on saints to help them!<br>Who dares accuse me? who shall dare belie<br>My spotless name? Speak, ye accomplice band,<br>Of what am I accus'd? of what strange crime<br>Is Maximilian Robespierre accus'd,<br>That through this hall the buz of discontent<br>Should murmur? who shall speak? |
| BILLAUD VARENNES | O patriot tongue<br>Belying the foul heart! Who was it urg'd<br>Friendly to tyrants that accurst decree,<br>Whose influence brooding o'er this hallowed hall,<br>Has chill'd each tongue to [[Silence\|silence]]? Who destroyed<br>The [[Freedom\|freedom]] of [[debate]], and carried through<br>The fatal law, that doom'd the delegates,<br>Unheard before their equals, to the bar<br>Where cruelty sat throned, and murder reign'd<br>With her Dumas coequal? Say — thou man<br>Of mighty eloquence, whose law was that? |
| COUTHON | That law was mine. I urged it — I propos'd —<br>The voice of France assembled in her sons<br>Assented, though the tame and timid voice<br>Of traitors murmur'd. I advis'd that law —<br>I justify it. It was wise and good. |
| BARRERE | Oh, wonderous wise and most convenient too!<br>I have long mark'd thee, Robespierre — and now<br>Proclaim thee traitor [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Loud applauses. |
| ROBESPIERRE | It is [[Well\|well]].<br>I am a traitor! oh, that I had fallen<br>When Regnault lifted high the murderous knife,<br>Regnault the instrument belike of those<br>Who now themselves would fain assassinate,<br>And legalise their murders. I stand here<br>An isolated patriot — hemmed around<br>By faction's noisy pack; beset and bay'd<br>By the foul hell-hounds who know no escape<br>From Justice' outstretch'd arm, but by the force<br>That pierces through her breast. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Murmurs, and shouts of — Down with the [[Tyrant]]! |
| ROBESPIERRE | Nay, but I will be heard. There was a [[Time\|time]]<br>When Robespierre began, the loud applauses<br>Of honest patriots drown'd the honest sound.<br>But times are chang'd, and villainy prevails. |
| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | No — villainy shall fall. France could not brook<br>A monarch's sway — sounds the dictator's name<br>More soothing to her ear? |
| BOURDON L'OISE | Rattle her [[Chains\|chains]]<br>More musically now than when the hand<br>Of Brissot forged her fetters; or the crew<br>Of Hébert thundered out their blasphemies,<br>And Danton talk'd of virtue? |
| ROBESPIERRE | Oh, that Brissot<br>Were here again to thunder in this hall,<br>That Hébert lived, and Danton's giant form<br>Scowl'd once again defiance! so my [[Soul\|soul]]<br>Might cope with worthy foes.<br>People of France,<br>Hear me! Beneath the vengeance of the law<br>Traitors have perish'd countless; more survive:<br>The hydra-headed faction lifts anew<br>Her daring front, and fruitful from her wounds,<br>Cautious from past defects, contrives new wiles<br>Against the sons of [[Freedom]]. |
| TALLIEN | [[Freedom]] lives!<br>Oppression falls — for France has felt her [[Chains\|chains]],<br>Has burst them too. Who traitor-like stept forth<br>Amid the hall of Jacobins to save<br>Camille Desmoulins, and the venal wretch<br>D'Eglantine? |
| ROBESPIERRE | I did — for I thought them honest.<br>And Heaven forefend that Vengeance e'er should strike,<br>Ere justice doom'd the blow. |
| BARRERE | Traitor, thou didst.<br>Yes, the accomplice of their dark designs,<br>Awhile didst thou defend them, when the [[Storm\|storm]]<br>Lower'd at safe distance. When the clouds frown'd darker,<br>[[Fear]]'d for yourself and left them to their fate.<br>Oh, I have mark'd thee long, and through the veil<br>Seen thy foul projects. Yes, ambitious man,<br>[[Self]]-will'd dictator o'er the realm of France,<br>The vengeance thou hast plann'd for patriots<br>Falls on thy head. Look how thy brother's deeds<br>Dishonour thine! He the firm patriot,<br>Thou the foul parricide of Liberty! |
| ROBESPIERRE JUNIOR | Barrere — attempt not meanly to divide<br>Me from my brother. I partake his [[Guilt\|guilt]],<br>For I partake his virtue. |
| ROBESPIERRE | Brother, by my [[Soul\|soul]],<br>More dear I hold thee to my heart, that thus<br>With me thou dar'st to tread the dangerous path<br>Of virtue, than that [[Nature]] twined her cords<br>Of kindred round us. |
| BARRERE | Yes, allied in [[Guilt\|guilt]],<br>Even as in [[Blood\|blood]] ye are. O, thou worst wretch,<br>Thou worse than Sylla! hast thou not proscrib'd,<br>Yea, in most foul anticipation slaughter'd<br>Each patriot representative of France? |
| BOURDON L'OISE | Was not the younger Caesar too to reign<br>O'er all our valiant armies in the south,<br>And still continue there his merchant wiles? |
| ROBESPIERRE JUNIOR | His merchant wiles! Oh, grant me patience, heaven!<br>Was it by merchant wiles I gain'd you back<br>Toulon, when proudly on her captive towers<br>Wav'd high the English [[Flag\|flag]]? or fought I then<br>With merchant wiles, when [[Sword\|sword]] in hand I led<br>Your troops to conquest? fought I merchant-like,<br>Or barter'd I for victory, when [[Death\|death]]<br>Strode o'er the reeking streets with giant stride,<br>And shook his ebon plumes, and sternly smil'd<br>Amid the bloody banquet? when appall'd<br>The hireling sons of England spread the sail<br>Of safety, fought I like a merchant then?<br>Oh, patience! patience! |
| BOURDON L'OISE | How this younger [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]<br>Mouths out defiance to us! even so<br>He had led on the armies of the south,<br>Till once again the plains of France were drench'd<br>With her best [[Blood\|blood]]. |
| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | Till once again display'd<br>Lyons' sad [[Tragedy\|tragedy]] had call'd me forth<br>The minister of wrath, whilst slaughter by<br>Had bathed in human [[Blood\|blood]]. |
| DUBOIS CRANCÉ | No [[Wonder\|wonder]], friend,<br>That we are traitors — that our heads must fall<br>Beneath the axe of [[Death\|death]]! when Caesar-like<br>Reigns Robespierre, 'tis wisely done to doom<br>The fall of [[Brutus]]. Tell me, bloody man,<br>Hast thou not parcell'd out deluded France,<br>As it had been some province won in fight,<br>Between your curst triumvirate? You, Couthon,<br>Go with my brother to the southern plains;<br>St. Just, be yours the army of the north;<br>Meantime I rule at [[Paris]]. |
| ROBESPIERRE | Matchless knave!<br>What — not one blush of [[Conscience\|conscience]] on thy cheek —<br>Not one poor blush of [[Truth\|truth]]! most likely tale!<br>That I who ruined Brissot's towering hopes,<br>I who discover'd Hébert's impious wiles,<br>And sharp'd for Danton's recreant neck the axe,<br>Should now be traitor! had I been so minded,<br>Think ye I had destroyed the very men<br>Whose plots resembled mine? bring forth your proofs<br>Of this deep treason. Tell me in whose breast<br>Found ye the fatal scroll? or tell me rather<br>Who forg'd the shameless falsehood? |
| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | Ask you proofs?<br>Robespierre, what proofs were ask'd when Brissot died? |
| LEGENDRE | What proofs adduced you when the Danton died?<br>When at the imminent peril of my life<br>I [[Rose\|rose]], and fearless of thy frowning brow,<br>Proclaim'd him guiltless? |
| ROBESPIERRE | I remember [[Well\|well]]<br>The fatal day. I do repent me much<br>That I kill'd Caesar and spar'd Antony.<br>But I have been too lenient. I have spared<br>The stream of [[Blood\|blood]], and now my own must flow<br>To fill the current. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Loud applauses. |
| ROBESPIERRE | Triumph not too soon,<br>Justice may yet be victor. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter ST. JUST, and mounts the Tribune. |
| ST. JUST | I come from the Committee — charged to speak<br>Of matters of high import. I omit<br>Their orders. Representatives of France,<br>Boldly in his own person speaks St. Just<br>What his own heart shall dictate. |
| TALLIEN | Hear ye this,<br>Insulted delegates of France? St. Just<br>From your Committee comes — comes charg'd to speak<br>Of matters of high import, yet omits<br>Their orders! Representatives of France,<br>That bold man I denounce, who disobeys<br>The nation's orders. — I denounce St. Just. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Loud applauses. |
| ST. JUST | Hear me! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Violent murmurs. |
| ROBESPIERRE | He shall be heard! |
| BOURDON L'OISE | Must we contaminate this sacred hall<br>With the foul breath of treason? |
| COLLOT D'HERBOIS | Drag him away!<br>Hence with him to the bar. |
| COUTHON | Oh, just proceedings!<br>Robespierre prevented liberty of speech —<br>And Robespierre is a [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]! Tallien reigns,<br>He dreads to hear the voice of [[innocence]] —<br>And St. Just must be silent! |
| LEGENDRE | Heed we [[Well\|well]]<br>That justice guide our actions. No light import<br>Attends this day. I move St. Just be heard. |
| FRERON | Inviolate be the sacred right of man.<br>The [[Freedom\|freedom]] of [[debate]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Violent applauses. |
| ST. JUST | I may be heard then! much the times are chang'd,<br>When St. Just thanks this hall for hearing him.<br>Robespierre is call'd a [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]. Men of France,<br>Judge not too soon. By popular discontent<br>Was Aristides driven into [[Exile\|exile]],<br>Was Phocion murder'd. Ere ye dare pronounce<br>Robespierre is guilty, it befits ye [[Well\|well]],<br>Consider who accuse him. Tallien,<br>Bourdon of Oise — the very men denounced,<br>For that their dark intrigues disturb'd the plan<br>Of [[government]]. Legendre the sworn friend<br>Of Danton, fall'n apostate. Dubois Crancé,<br>He who at Lyons spared the royalists —<br>Collot d'Herbois — |
| BOURDON L'OISE | What — shall the traitor rear<br>His head amid our tribune — and blaspheme<br>Each patriot? shall the hireling slave of faction — |
| ST. JUST | I am of no one faction. I contend<br>Against all factions. |
| TALLIEN | I espouse the cause<br>Of [[Truth\|truth]]. Robespierre on yester morn pronounced<br>Upon his own authority a report.<br>To-day St. Just comes down. St. Just neglects<br>What the Committee orders, and harangues<br>From his own will. O citizens of France<br>I weep for you — I weep for my poor country —<br>I tremble for the cause of Liberty,<br>When individuals shall assume the sway,<br>And with more insolence than kingly [[Pride\|pride]]<br>Rule the Republic. |
| BILLAUD VARENNES | Shudder, ye representatives of France,<br>Shudder with horror. Henriot commands<br>The marshall'd force of [[Paris]]. Henriot,<br>Foul parricide — the sworn ally of Hébert,<br>Denounced by all — upheld by Robespierre.<br>Who spar'd La Valette? who promoted him,<br>Stain'd with the deep dye of nobility?<br>Who to an ex-peer gave the high command?<br>Who screen'd from justice the rapacious thief?<br>Who cast in [[Chains\|chains]] the friends of Liberty?<br>Robespierre, the [[Self\|self]]-stil'd patriot Robespierre —<br>Robespierre, allied with [[Villain\|villain]] Daubigné —<br>Robespierre, the foul arch-[[Tyrant\|tyrant]] Robespierre. |
| BOURDON L'OISE | He talks of virtue — of [[morality]] —<br>Consistent patriot! he Daubigné's friend!<br>Henriot's supporter virtuous! preach of virtue,<br>Yet league with villains, for with Robespierre<br>Villains [[Alone\|alone]] ally. Thou art a [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]!<br>I stile thee [[Tyrant\|tyrant]], Robespierre! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Loud applauses. |
| ROBESPIERRE | Take back the name. Ye citizens of France — |
| *(didascalia)* | [Violent clamour. Cries of — Down with the [[Tyrant]]! |
| TALLIEN | Oppression falls. The traitor stands appall'd —<br>[[Guilt]]'s iron fangs engrasp his shrinking [[Soul\|soul]] —<br>He hears assembled France denounce his crimes!<br>He sees the mask torn from his secret sins —<br>He trembles on the precipice of fate.<br>Fall'n guilty [[Tyrant\|tyrant]]! murder'd by thy rage<br>How many an [[Innocent\|innocent]] victim's [[Blood\|blood]] has stain'd<br>Fair [[Freedom\|freedom]]'s altar! Sylla-like thy hand<br>Mark'd down the virtues, that, thy foes removed,<br>Perpetual Dictator thou might'st reign,<br>And tyrannize o'er France, and call it [[Freedom\|freedom]]!<br>Long [[Time\|time]] in timid [[Guilt\|guilt]] the traitor plann'd<br>His fearful wiles — [[success]] emboldened sin —<br>And his stretch'd arm had grasp'd the diadem<br>Ere now, but that the coward's heart recoil'd,<br>Lest France awak'd should rouse her from her dream,<br>And call aloud for vengeance. He, like Caesar,<br>With rapid step urged on his bold career,<br>Even to the summit of ambitious power,<br>And deem'd the name of [[King]] [[Alone\|alone]] was wanting.<br>Was it for this we hurl'd proud Capet down?<br>Is it for this we wage eternal war<br>Against the [[Tyrant\|tyrant]] horde of murderers,<br>The crownéd cockatrices whose foul venom<br>Infects all Europe? was it then for this<br>We swore to guard our liberty with life,<br>That Robespierre should reign? the spirit of [[Freedom\|freedom]]<br>Is not yet sunk so low. The glowing flame<br>That animates each honest Frenchman's heart<br>Not yet extinguish'd. I invoke thy shade,<br>Immortal [[Brutus]]! I too wear a dagger;<br>And if the representatives of France,<br>Through [[Fear\|fear]] or favour, should delay the [[Sword\|sword]]<br>Of justice, Tallien emulates thy virtues;<br>Tallien, like [[Brutus]], lifts the avenging arm;<br>Tallien shall save his country. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Violent applauses. |
| BILLAUD VARENNES | I demand<br>The arrest of all the traitors. Memorable<br>Will be this day for France. |
| ROBESPIERRE | Yes! Memorable<br>This day will be for France — for villains triumph. |
| LEBAS | I will not share in this day's damning [[Guilt\|guilt]].<br>Condemn me too. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Great cry — Down with the Tyrants! |
| *(didascalia)* | (The two ROBESPIERRES, COUTHON, ST. JUST, and LEBAS are led off.) |
