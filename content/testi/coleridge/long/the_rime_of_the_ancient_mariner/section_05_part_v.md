PART V  
Oh [[Sleep|sleep]]! it is a gentle thing,  
Beloved from pole to pole!  
To Mary [[Queen]] the praise be given!  
She sent the gentle [[Sleep|sleep]] from Heaven,   295  
That slid into my [[Soul|soul]].  
[Sidenote: By grace of the holy Mother, the [[Ancient Mariner|ancient Mariner]] is refreshed  
with rain.]  
The silly buckets on the deck,  
That had so long remained,  
I dreamt that they were filled with dew;  
And when I awoke, it rained.   300  
My lips were wet, my throat was cold,  
My garments all were dank;  
Sure I had drunken in my [[Dreams|dreams]],  
And still my [[Body|body]] drank.  
I moved, and could not feel my limbs:   305  
I was so light — almost  
I thought that I had died in [[Sleep|sleep]],  
And was a blesséd [[Ghost|ghost]].  
[Sidenote: He heareth sounds and seeth strange sights and commotions in  
the sky and the element.]  
And soon I heard a roaring [[Wind|wind]]:  
It did not come anear;   310  
But with its sound it shook the sails,  
That were so thin and sere.  
The upper air burst into life!  
And a hundred [[Fire|fire]]-flags sheen,  
To and fro they were hurried about!   315  
And to and fro, and in and out,  
The wan [[Stars|stars]] danced between.  
And the coming [[Wind|wind]] did roar more loud,  
And the sails did sigh like sedge;  
And the rain poured down from one black cloud;   320  
The [[Moon]] was at its edge.  
The thick black cloud was cleft, and still  
The [[Moon]] was at its side:  
Like waters shot from some high crag,  
The [[Lightning|lightning]] fell with never a jag,   325  
A [[River|river]] steep and wide.  
[Sidenote: The bodies of the [[Ship|ship]]’s crew are inspired [inspirited, S.  
L.] and the [[Ship|ship]] moves on;]  
The loud [[Wind|wind]] never reached the [[Ship|ship]],  
Yet now the [[Ship|ship]] moved on!  
Beneath the [[Lightning|lightning]] and the [[Moon]]  
The dead men gave a groan.   330  
They groaned, they stirred, they all uprose,  
Nor spake, nor moved their eyes;  
It had been strange, even in [[A Dream|a dream]],  
To have seen those dead men rise.  
The helmsman steered, the [[Ship|ship]] moved on;   335  
Yet never a breeze up-blew;  
The mariners all ‘gan work the ropes,  
Where they were wont to do;  
They raised their limbs like lifeless tools —  
We were a ghastly crew.   340  
The [[Body|body]] of my brother’s son  
Stood by me, knee to knee:  
The [[Body|body]] and I pulled at one rope,  
But he said nought to me.  
[Sidenote: But not by the souls of the men, nor by dæmons of earth or  
middle air, but by a blessed troop of angelic spirits, sent down by the  
invocation of the guardian saint.]  
‘I [[Fear|fear]] thee, [[Ancient Mariner|ancient Mariner]]!’   345  
Be calm, thou Wedding-Guest!  
‘Twas not those souls that fled in [[Pain|pain]],  
Which to their corses came again,  
But a troop of spirits blest:  
For when it dawned — they dropped their arms,  
And clustered round the mast;   351  
Sweet sounds [[Rose|rose]] slowly through their mouths,  
And from their bodies passed.  
Around, around, flew each sweet sound,  
Then darted to the Sun;   355  
Slowly the sounds came back again,  
Now mixed, now one by one.  
Sometimes a-dropping from the sky  
I heard the sky-lark sing;  
Sometimes all little [[Birds|birds]] that are,   360  
How they seemed to fill the sea and air  
With their sweet jargoning!  
And now ‘twas like all instruments,  
Now like a lonely flute;  
And now it is an angel’s [[Song|song]],   365  
That makes the heavens be mute.  
It ceased; yet still the sails made on  
A pleasant noise till [[Noon|noon]],  
A noise like of a hidden brook  
In the leafy month of June,   370  
That to the sleeping woods all [[Night|night]]  
Singeth a quiet tune.  
Till [[Noon|noon]] we quietly sailed on,  
Yet never a breeze did breathe:  
Slowly and smoothly went the [[Ship|ship]],    375  
Moved onward from beneath.  
[Sidenote: The lonesome Spirit from the south-pole carries on the [[Ship|ship]]  
as far as the Line, in obedience to the angelic troop, but still  
requireth vengeance.]  
Under the keel nine fathom deep,  
From the land of mist and [[Snow|snow]],  
The spirit slid: and it was he  
That made the [[Ship|ship]] to go.    380  
The sails at [[Noon|noon]] left off their tune,  
And the [[Ship|ship]] stood still also.  
The Sun, right up above the mast,  
Had fixed her to the ocean:  
But in a minute she ‘gan stir,   385  
With a short uneasy motion —  
Backwards and forwards half her length  
With a short uneasy motion.  
Then like a pawing [[Horse|horse]] let go,  
She made a sudden bound:   390  
It flung the [[Blood|blood]] into my head,  
And I fell down in a swound.  
[Sidenote: The Polar Spirit’s fellow-dæmons, the invisible inhabitants  
of the element, take part in his wrong; and two of them relate, one to  
the other, that penance long and heavy for the [[Ancient Mariner|ancient Mariner]] hath been  
accorded to the Polar Spirit, who returneth southward.]  
How long in that same fit I lay,  
I have not to declare;  
But ere my living life returned,   395  
I heard and in my [[Soul|soul]] discerned  
Two voices in the air.  
‘Is it he?’ quoth one, ‘Is this the man?  
By him who died on [[Cross|cross]],  
With his cruel bow he laid full low   400  
The harmless Albatross.  
The spirit who bideth by himself  
In the land of mist and [[Snow|snow]],  
He loved the bird that loved the man  
Who shot him with his bow.’   405  
The other was a softer voice,  
As soft as honey-dew:  
Quoth he, ‘The man hath penance done,  
And penance more will do.’
