<span class="atom-split" data-atom="012_monody_on_the_death_of_chatterton" data-title="MONODY ON THE DEATH OF CHATTERTON" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


FIRST VERSION, IN [[characters/christ|CHRIST]]’S HOSPITAL BOOK — 1790  
Cold penury repress’d his noble rage,  
And froze the genial current of his [[motifs/soul|soul]].  
Now prompts the Muse poetic lays,  
And high my bosom beats with [[motifs/love|love]] of Praise!  
But, Chatterton! methinks I hear thy name,  
For cold my Fancy grows, and dead each [[concepts/hope|Hope]] of Fame.  
When Want and cold Neglect had chill’d thy [[motifs/soul|soul]],   5  
Athirst for [[motifs/death|Death]] I see thee drench the bowl!  
Thy corpse of many a livid hue  
On the bare ground I view,  
Whilst various passions all my mind engage;  
Now is my breast distended with a sigh,   10  
And now a flash of Rage  
Darts through the tear, that glistens in my eye.  
Is this the land of liberal Hearts!  
Is this the land, where Genius ne’er in vain  
Pour’d forth her [[motifs/soul|soul]]-enchanting strain?   15  
Ah me! yet Butler ‘gainst the bigot foe  
[[motifs/well|Well]]-skill’d to aim keen [[works/humour-(chesterton)|Humour]]’s dart,  
Yet Butler felt Want’s poignant sting;  
And Otway, Master of the Tragic art,  
Whom Pity’s [[concepts/self|self]] had taught to sing,   20  
Sank beneath a load of Woe;  
This ever can the generous Briton hear,  
And starts not in his eye th’ indignant Tear?  
Elate of Heart and confident of Fame,  
From vales where Avon sports, the Minstrel came,   25  
Gay as the Poet hastes along  
He meditates the future song,  
How Ælla battled with his country’s foes,  
And whilst Fancy in the air  
Paints him many a vision fair   30  
His eyes [[motifs/dance|dance]] rapture and his bosom glows.  
With generous joy he views th’ ideal gold:  
He listens to many a Widow’s prayers,  
And many an [[archetypes/orphan|Orphan]]’s thanks he hears;  
He soothes to peace the care-worn breast,   35  
He bids the Debtor’s eyes know rest,  
And Liberty and Bliss behold:  
And now he punishes the heart of steel,  
And her own iron rod he makes Oppression feel.  
Fated to heave sad Disappointment’s sigh,   40  
To feel the [[concepts/hope|Hope]] now rais’d, and now deprest,  
To feel the burnings of an injur’d breast,  
From all thy Fate’s deep sorrow keen  
In vain, O Youth, I turn th’ affrighted eye;  
For powerful Fancy evernigh   45  
The hateful picture forces on my sight.  
There, [[motifs/death|Death]] of every dear delight,  
Frowns [[concepts/poverty|Poverty]] of Giant mien!  
In vain I seek the charms of youthful grace,  
Thy sunken eye, thy haggard cheeks it shews,   50  
The quick emotions struggling in the Face  
Faint index of thy mental Throes,  
When each strong Passion spurn’d controll,  
And not a Friend was nigh to calm thy stormy [[motifs/soul|soul]].  
Such was the sad and gloomy hour   55  
When anguish’d Care of sullen brow  
Prepared the [[motifs/poison|Poison]]’s [[motifs/death|death]]-cold power.  
Already to thy lips was rais’d the bowl,  
When filial Pity stood thee by,  
Thy fixéd eyes she bade thee roll   60  
On scenes that [[motifs/well|well]] might melt thy [[motifs/soul|soul]] —  
Thy native cot she held to view,  
Thy native cot, where Peace ere long  
Had listen’d to thy evening song;  
Thy sister’s shrieks she bade thee hear,   65  
And mark thy mother’s thrilling tear,  
She made thee feel her deep-drawn sigh,  
And all her silent agony of Woe.  
And from thy Fate shall such distress ensue?  
Ah! dash the [[motifs/poison|poison]]’d chalice from thy hand!   70  
And thou had’st dash’d it at her soft command;  
But that [[concepts/despair|Despair]] and Indignation [[motifs/rose|rose]],  
And told again the story of thy Woes,  
Told the keen insult of th’ unfeeling Heart,  
The dread dependence on the low-born mind,   75  
Told every Woe, for which thy breast might smart,  
Neglect and grinning scorn and Want combin’d —  
Recoiling back, thou sent’st the friend of [[motifs/pain|Pain]]  
To roll a tide of [[motifs/death|Death]] thro’ every freezing vein.  
O Spirit blest!    80  
Whether th’ eternal Throne around,  
Amidst the blaze of Cherubim,  
Thou pourest forth the grateful [[forms/hymn|hymn]],  
Or, soaring through the blest Domain,  
Enraptur’st Angels with thy strain, — 85  
Grant me, like thee, the lyre to sound,  
Like thee, with [[motifs/fire|fire]] divine to glow —  
But ah! when rage the Waves of Woe,  
Grant me with firmer breast t’oppose their hate,  
And soar beyond the storms with upright eye elate!   90  
1790

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="016_pain" data-title="PAIN" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


Once could the Morn’s first beams, the healthful breeze,  
All [[concepts/nature|Nature]] charm, and gay was every hour: —  
But ah! not Music’s [[concepts/self|self]], nor fragrant bower  
Can glad the trembling sense of wan Disease.  
Now that the frequent pangs my frame assail,   5  
Now that my sleepless eyes are sunk and dim,  
And seas of [[motifs/pain|Pain]] seem waving through each limb —  
Ah what can all Life’s gilded scenes avail?  
I view the crowd, whom Youth and Health inspire,  
Hear the loud laugh, and catch the sportive lay,   10  
Then sigh and think — I too could laugh and play  
And gaily sport it on the Muse’s lyre,  
Ere [[archetypes/tyrant|Tyrant]] [[motifs/pain|Pain]] had chas’d away delight,  
Ere the wild pulse throbb’d anguish thro’ the [[motifs/night|night]]!  
? 1790.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="020_on_receiving_an_account_that_his_only_sisters_death_was_inevitable" data-title="ON RECEIVING AN ACCOUNT THAT HIS ONLY SISTER’S DEATH WAS INEVITABLE" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


The tear which mourn’d a brother’s fate scarce dry —  
[[motifs/pain|Pain]] after [[motifs/pain|pain]], and woe succeeding woe —  
Is my heart destin’d for another blow?  
O my sweet sister! and must thou too die?  
Ah! how has Disappointment pour’d the tear   5  
O’er infant [[concepts/hope|Hope]] destroy’d by early [[motifs/frost|frost]]!  
How are ye gone, whom most my [[motifs/soul|soul]] held dear!  
Scarce had I lov’d you ere I mourn’d you lost;  
Say, is this hollow eye, this heartless [[motifs/pain|pain]],  
Fated to rove thro’ Life’s wide cheerless plain — 10  
Nor father, brother, sister meet its ken —  
My woes, my joys unshared! Ah! long ere then  
On me thy icy dart, stern [[motifs/death|Death]], be prov’d; —  
Better to die, than live and not be lov’d!  
1791.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="021_on_seeing_a_youth_affectionately_welcomed_by_a_sister" data-title="ON SEEING A YOUTH AFFECTIONATELY WELCOMED BY A SISTER" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


I too a sister had! too cruel [[motifs/death|Death]]!  
How sad Remembrance bids my bosom heave!  
Tranquil her [[motifs/soul|soul]], as sleeping Infant’s breath;  
Meek were her manners as a vernal Eve.  
Knowledge, that frequent lifts the bloated mind,   5  
Gave her the treasure of a lowly breast,  
And Wit to venom’d Malice oft assign’d,  
Dwelt in her bosom in a Turtle’s nest.  
Cease, busy [[motifs/memory|Memory]]! cease to urge the dart;  
Nor on my [[motifs/soul|soul]] her [[motifs/love|love]] to me impress!   10  
For oh I mourn in anguish — and my heart  
Feels the keen pang, th’ unutterable distress.  
Yet wherefore grieve I that her sorrows cease,  
For Life was misery, and the [[motifs/grave|Grave]] is Peace!  
1791.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="045_the_gentle_look" data-title="THE GENTLE LOOK" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


Thou gentle Look, that didst my [[motifs/soul|soul]] beguile,  
Why hast thou left me? Still in some fond dream  
Revisit my sad heart, auspicious Smile!  
As falls on closing [[motifs/flowers|flowers]] the lunar beam:  
What [[motifs/time|time]], in sickly mood, at parting day   5  
I lay me down and think of happier years;  
Of joys, that glimmer’d in [[concepts/hope|Hope]]’s twilight ray,  
Then left me darkling in a vale of [[motifs/tears|tears]].  
O pleasant days of [[concepts/hope|Hope]] — for ever gone!  
Could I recall you! — But that thought is vain.   10  
Availeth not [[works/persuasion-(austen)|Persuasion]]’s sweetest tone  
To lure the fleet-wing’d Travellers back again:  
Yet fair, though faint, their images shall gleam  
Like the bright Rainbow on a willowy stream.  
? 1793.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="070_elegy_imitated_from_one_of_akensides_blank-verse_inscriptions" data-title="ELEGY IMITATED FROM ONE OF AKENSIDE’S BLANK-VERSE INSCRIPTIONS" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


Near the lone pile with ivy overspread,  
Fast by the rivulet’s [[motifs/sleep|sleep]]-persuading sound,  
Where ‘sleeps the moonlight’ on yon verdant bed —  
O humbly press that consecrated ground!  
For there does [[characters/edmund|Edmund]] rest, the learnéd swain!   5  
And there his spirit most delights to rove:  
Young [[characters/edmund|Edmund]]! fam’d for each harmonious strain,  
And the sore wounds of ill-requited [[motifs/love|Love]].  
Like some tall tree that spreads its branches wide,  
And loads the West-[[motifs/wind|wind]] with its soft perfume,   10  
His manhood blossom’d; till the faithless [[concepts/pride|pride]]  
Of fair Matilda sank him to the tomb.  
But soon did righteous Heaven her [[concepts/guilt|Guilt]] pursue!  
Where’er with wilder’d step she wander’d pale,  
Still [[characters/edmund|Edmund]]’s image [[motifs/rose|rose]] to blast her view,   15  
Still [[characters/edmund|Edmund]]’s voice accus’d her in each gale.  
With keen regret, and conscious [[concepts/guilt|Guilt]]’s alarms,  
Amid the pomp of Affluence she pined;  
Nor all that lur’d her [[concepts/faith|faith]] from [[characters/edmund|Edmund]]’s arms  
Could lull the wakeful horror of her mind.   20  
Go, Traveller! tell the tale with sorrow fraught:  
Some tearful Maid perchance, or blooming Youth,  
May hold it in remembrance; and be taught  
That Riches cannot pay for [[motifs/love|Love]] or [[concepts/truth|Truth]].  
? 1794.

Pale Roamer through the [[motifs/night|night]]! thou poor Forlorn!  
Remorse that man on his [[motifs/death|death]]-bed possess,  
Who in the credulous hour of tenderness  
Betrayed, then cast thee forth to Want and Scorn!  
The world is pitiless: the chaste one’s [[concepts/pride|pride]]   5  
Mimic of Virtue scowls on thy distress:  
Thy Loves and they that envied thee deride:  
And Vice alone will shelter Wretchedness!  
O! I could weep to think that there should be  
Cold-bosom’d lewd ones, who endure to place   10  
Foul offerings on the shrine of Misery,  
And force from Famine the caress of [[motifs/love|Love]];  
May He shed healing on the sore disgrace,  
He, the great Comforter that rules above!  
? 1794.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="078_lines_on_a_friend_who_died_of_a_frenzy_fever_induced_by_calumnious_reports" data-title="LINES ON A FRIEND WHO DIED OF A FRENZY FEVER INDUCED BY CALUMNIOUS REPORTS" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


[[characters/edmund|Edmund]]! thy [[motifs/grave|grave]] with aching eye I scan,  
And inly groan for Heaven’s poor outcast — Man!  
‘Tis tempest all or gloom: in early youth  
If gifted with th’ Ithuriel lance of [[concepts/truth|Truth]]  
We force to start amid her feign’d caress   5  
Vice, siren-hag! in native ugliness;  
A Brother’s fate will haply rouse the tear,  
And on we go in heaviness and [[concepts/fear|fear]]!  
But if our fond hearts call to Pleasure’s bower  
Some pigmy Folly in a careless hour,   10  
The faithless guest shall stamp the enchanted ground,  
And mingled forms of Misery rise around:  
Heart-fretting [[concepts/fear|Fear]], with pallid look aghast,  
That courts the future woe to hide the past;  
Remorse, the [[motifs/poison|poison]]’d arrow in his side,   15  
And loud lewd Mirth, to Anguish close allied:  
Till Frenzy, fierce-eyed [[archetypes/child|child]] of moping [[motifs/pain|Pain]],  
Darts her hot [[motifs/lightning|lightning]]-flash athwart the brain.  
Rest, injur’d shade! Shall Slander squatting near  
Spit her cold venom in a dead man’s ear?   20  
‘Twas thine to feel the sympathetic glow  
In Merit’s joy, and [[concepts/poverty|Poverty]]’s meek woe;  
Thine all, that cheer the moment as it flies,  
The zoneless Cares, and smiling Courtesies.  
Nurs’d in thy heart the firmer Virtues grew,   25  
And in thy heart they wither’d! Such chill dew  
Wan [[concepts/indolence|Indolence]] on each young blossom shed;  
And [[concepts/vanity|Vanity]] her filmy net-work spread,  
With eye that roll’d around in asking gaze,  
And tongue that traffick’d in the trade of praise.   30  
Thy follies such! the hard world mark’d them [[motifs/well|well]]!  
Were they more wise, the Proud who never fell?  
Rest, injur’d shade! the poor man’s grateful [[motifs/prayer|prayer]]  
On heaven-ward wing thy wounded [[motifs/soul|soul]] shall bear.  
As oft at twilight gloom thy [[motifs/grave|grave]] I pass,   35  
And sit me down upon its recent grass,  
With introverted eye I contemplate  
Similitude of [[motifs/soul|soul]], perhaps of — Fate!  
To me hath Heaven with bounteous hand assign’d  
Energic Reason and a shaping mind,   40  
The daring ken of [[concepts/truth|Truth]], the Patriot’s part,  
And Pity’s sigh, that breathes the gentle heart —  
Sloth-jaundic’d all! and from my graspless hand  
Drop [[concepts/friendship|Friendship]]’s precious pearls, like hour-glass sand.  
I weep, yet stoop not! the faint anguish flows,   45  
A dreamy pang in Morning’s feverous doze.  
Is this piled earth our Being’s passless mound?  
Tell me, cold [[motifs/grave|grave]]! is [[motifs/death|Death]] with poppies [[motifs/crown|crown]]’d?  
Tired Sentinel! mid fitful starts I nod,  
And fain would [[motifs/sleep|sleep]], though pillowed on a clod!   50  
1794.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="084_priestley" data-title="PRIESTLEY" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


Though rous’d by that dark Vizir Riot rude  
Have driven our PRIESTLEY o’er the Ocean swell;  
Though Superstition and her wolfish brood  
Bay his mild radiance, impotent and fell;  
Calm in his halls of brightness he shall dwell!   5  
For lo! RELIGION at his strong behest  
Starts with mild anger from the Papal spell,  
And flings to Earth her tinsel-glittering vest,  
Her mitred State and cumbrous Pomp unholy;  
And JUSTICE wakes to bid th’ Oppressor wail   10  
Insulting aye the wrongs of patient Folly;  
And from her dark retreat by Wisdom won  
Meek [[concepts/nature|NATURE]] slowly lifts her matron veil  
To smile with fondness on her gazing Son!  
December 11, 1794.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="088_to_the_rev_w_l_bowles" data-title="TO THE REV. W. L. BOWLES" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


FIRST VERSION, PRINTED IN ‘MORNING CHRONICLE’, DECEMBER 26, 1794  
My heart has thank’d thee, BOWLES! for those soft strains,  
That, on the still air floating, tremblingly  
Wak’d in me Fancy, [[motifs/love|Love]], and Sympathy!  
For hence, not callous to a Brother’s pains  
Thro’ Youth’s gay prime and thornless paths I went;   5  
And, when the darker day of life began,  
And I did roam, a thought-bewilder’d man!  
Thy kindred Lays an healing solace lent,  
Each lonely pang with dreamy joys combin’d,  
And stole from vain REGRET her scorpion stings;   10  
While shadowy PLEASURE, with mysterious wings,  
Brooded the wavy and tumultuous mind,  
Like that great Spirit, who with plastic sweep  
Mov’d on the darkness of the formless Deep!  
VIII

[TO CHARLES LLOYD]  
The piteous sobs that choke the Virgin’s breath  
For him, the fair betrothéd Youth, who lies  
Cold in the narrow dwelling, or the cries  
With which a Mother wails her darling’s [[motifs/death|death]],  
These from our [[concepts/nature|nature]]’s common impulse [[motifs/spring|spring]],   5  
Unblam’d, unprais’d; but o’er the piléd earth  
Which hides the sheeted corse of grey-hair’d Worth,  
If droops the soaring Youth with slacken’d wing;  
If he recall in saddest minstrelsy  
Each tenderness bestow’d, each [[concepts/truth|truth]] imprest,   10  
Such grief is Reason, Virtue, Piety!  
And from the Almighty Father shall descend  
Comforts on his late evening, whose young breast  
Mourns with no transient [[motifs/love|love]] the Agéd Friend.  
1796.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="131_to_the_rev_george_coleridge_of_ottery_st_mary_devon" data-title="TO THE REV. GEORGE COLERIDGE OF OTTERY ST. MARY, DEVON" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


With some [[works/poems-(chesterton)|Poems]]  
Notus in fratres animi paterni.  
HOR. Carm. lib. II. 2.  
A blesséd lot hath he, who having passed  
His youth and early manhood in the stir  
And turmoil of the world, retreats at length,  
With cares that move, not agitate the heart,  
To the same dwelling where his father dwelt;   5  
And haply views his tottering little ones  
Embrace those agéd knees and climb that lap,  
On which first kneeling his own infancy  
Lisp’d its brief [[motifs/prayer|prayer]]. Such, O my earliest Friend!  
Thy lot, and such thy brothers too enjoy.   10  
At distance did ye climb Life’s upland [[motifs/road|road]],  
Yet cheer’d and cheering: now fraternal [[motifs/love|love]]  
Hath drawn you to one centre. Be your days  
Holy, and blest and blessing may ye live!  
To me the Eternal Wisdom hath dispens’d   15  
A different fortune and more different mind —  
Me from the spot where first I sprang to light  
Too soon transplanted, ere my [[motifs/soul|soul]] had fix’d  
Its first domestic loves; and hence through life  
Chasing chance-started friendships. A brief while   20  
Some have preserv’d me from life’s pelting ills;  
But, like a tree with [[motifs/leaves|leaves]] of feeble stem,  
If the clouds lasted, and a sudden breeze  
Ruffled the boughs, they on my head at once  
Dropped the collected shower; and some most false,   25  
False and fair-foliag’d as the Manchineel,  
Have tempted me to slumber in their shade  
E’en mid the [[motifs/storm|storm]]; then breathing subtlest damps,  
Mix’d their own venom with the rain from Heaven,  
That I woke [[motifs/poison|poison]]’d! But, all praise to Him   30  
Who gives us all things, more have yielded me  
Permanent shelter; and beside one Friend,  
Beneath the impervious covert of one oak,  
I’ve rais’d a lowly shed, and know the names  
Of Husband and of Father; not unhearing   35  
Of that divine and nightly-whispering Voice,  
Which from my [[motifs/childhood|childhood]] to maturer years  
Spake to me of predestinated wreaths,  
Bright with no fading colours!  
Yet at times  
My [[motifs/soul|soul]] is sad, that I have roam’d through life   40  
Still most a stranger, most with naked heart  
At mine own [[motifs/home|home]] and birth-place: chiefly then,  
When I remember thee, my earliest Friend!  
Thee, who didst watch my boyhood and my youth;  
Didst trace my wanderings with a father’s eye;   45  
And boding evil yet still hoping good,  
Rebuk’d each fault, and over all my woes  
Sorrow’d in [[motifs/silence|silence]]! He who counts alone  
The beatings of the solitary heart,  
That Being knows, how I have lov’d thee ever,   50  
Lov’d as a brother, as a son rever’d thee!  
Oh! ‘tis to me an ever new delight,  
To talk of thee and thine: or when the blast  
Of the shrill [[motifs/winter|winter]], rattling our rude sash,  
Endears the cleanly hearth and social bowl;   55  
Or when, as now, on some delicious eve,  
We in our sweet sequester’d orchard-plot  
Sit on the tree crook’d earth-ward; whose old boughs,  
That hang above us in an arborous roof,  
Stirr’d by the faint gale of departing May,   60  
Send their loose blossoms slanting o’er our heads!  
Nor dost not thou sometimes recall those hours,  
When with the joy of [[concepts/hope|hope]] thou gavest thine ear  
To my wild firstling-lays. Since then my song  
Hath sounded deeper notes, such as beseem   65  
Or that sad wisdom folly [[motifs/leaves|leaves]] behind,  
Or such as, tuned to these tumultuous times,
