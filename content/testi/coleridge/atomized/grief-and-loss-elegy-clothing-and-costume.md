FIRST VERSION, IN [[Christ|CHRIST]]’S HOSPITAL BOOK — 1790  
Cold penury repress’d his noble rage,  
And froze the genial current of his [[Soul|soul]].  
Now prompts the Muse poetic lays,  
And high my bosom beats with [[Love|love]] of Praise!  
But, Chatterton! methinks I hear thy name,  
For cold my Fancy grows, and dead each [[Hope]] of Fame.  
When Want and cold Neglect had chill’d thy [[Soul|soul]],   5  
Athirst for [[Death]] I see thee drench the bowl!  
Thy corpse of many a livid hue  
On the bare ground I view,  
Whilst various passions all my mind engage;  
Now is my breast distended with a sigh,   10  
And now a flash of Rage  
Darts through the tear, that glistens in my eye.  
Is this the land of liberal Hearts!  
Is this the land, where Genius ne’er in vain  
Pour’d forth her [[Soul|soul]]-enchanting strain?   15  
Ah me! yet Butler ‘gainst the bigot foe  
[[Well]]-skill’d to aim keen [[Humour]]’s dart,  
Yet Butler felt Want’s poignant sting;  
And Otway, Master of the Tragic art,  
Whom Pity’s [[Self|self]] had taught to sing,   20  
Sank beneath a load of Woe;  
This ever can the generous Briton hear,  
And starts not in his eye th’ indignant Tear?  
Elate of Heart and confident of Fame,  
From vales where Avon sports, the Minstrel came,   25  
Gay as the Poet hastes along  
He meditates the future [[Song|song]],  
How Ælla battled with his country’s foes,  
And whilst Fancy in the air  
Paints him many a vision fair   30  
His eyes [[Dance|dance]] rapture and his bosom glows.  
With generous joy he views th’ ideal gold:  
He listens to many a Widow’s prayers,  
And many an [[Orphan]]’s thanks he hears;  
He soothes to peace the care-worn breast,   35  
He bids the Debtor’s eyes know rest,  
And Liberty and Bliss behold:  
And now he punishes the heart of steel,  
And her own iron rod he makes Oppression feel.  
Fated to heave sad Disappointment’s sigh,   40  
To feel the [[Hope]] now rais’d, and now deprest,  
To feel the burnings of an injur’d breast,  
From all thy Fate’s deep sorrow keen  
In vain, O Youth, I turn th’ affrighted eye;  
For powerful Fancy evernigh   45  
The hateful picture forces on my sight.  
There, [[Death]] of every dear delight,  
Frowns [[Poverty]] of Giant mien!  
In vain I seek the charms of youthful grace,  
Thy sunken eye, thy haggard cheeks it shews,   50  
The quick emotions struggling in the Face  
Faint index of thy mental Throes,  
When each strong Passion spurn’d controll,  
And not a Friend was nigh to calm thy stormy [[Soul|soul]].  
Such was the sad and gloomy hour   55  
When anguish’d Care of sullen brow  
Prepared the [[Poison]]’s [[Death|death]]-cold power.  
Already to thy lips was rais’d the bowl,  
When filial Pity stood thee by,  
Thy fixéd eyes she bade thee roll   60  
On scenes that [[Well|well]] might melt thy [[Soul|soul]] —  
Thy native cot she held to view,  
Thy native cot, where Peace ere long  
Had listen’d to thy evening [[Song|song]];  
Thy sister’s shrieks she bade thee hear,   65  
And mark thy mother’s thrilling tear,  
She made thee feel her deep-drawn sigh,  
And all her silent agony of Woe.  
And from thy Fate shall such distress ensue?  
Ah! dash the [[Poison|poison]]’d chalice from thy hand!   70  
And thou had’st dash’d it at her soft command;  
But that [[Despair]] and Indignation [[Rose|rose]],  
And told again the story of thy Woes,  
Told the keen insult of th’ unfeeling Heart,  
The dread dependence on the low-born mind,   75  
Told every Woe, for which thy breast might smart,  
Neglect and grinning scorn and Want combin’d —  
Recoiling back, thou sent’st the friend of [[Pain]]  
To roll a tide of [[Death]] thro’ every freezing vein.  
O Spirit blest!    80  
Whether th’ eternal Throne around,  
Amidst the blaze of Cherubim,  
Thou pourest forth the grateful [[Hymn|hymn]],  
Or, soaring through the blest Domain,  
Enraptur’st Angels with thy strain, — 85  
Grant me, like thee, the lyre to sound,  
Like thee, with [[Fire|fire]] divine to glow —  
But ah! when rage the Waves of Woe,  
Grant me with firmer breast t’oppose their hate,  
And soar beyond the storms with upright eye elate!   90  
1790

PRIMA VERSIONE, NEL LIBRO DEL [[Christ|CHRIST]]’S HOSPITAL — 1790  
La fredda penuria represse la sua nobile ira,  
E gelò la genial corrente della sua [[Soul|anima]].  
Or la Musa m’ispira poetici canti,  
E alto il petto mi batte d’[[Love|amore]] di Lode!  
Ma, Chatterton! parmi d’udire il tuo nome,  
Ché fredda si fa la Fantasia, e morta ogni [[Hope|Speranza]] di Fama.  
Quando Bisogno e freddo Abbandono t’ebbero agghiacciata l’[[Soul|anima]],   5  
Assetato di [[Death|Morte]] ti vedo tracannare la coppa!  
Il tuo cadavere di tante livide tinte  
Sulla nuda terra io miro,  
Mentre varie passioni tutta m’occupan la mente;  
Ora il mio petto si gonfia d’un sospiro,   10  
Ed ora un lampo d’Ira  
Guizza attraverso la lacrima che luccica nel mio occhio.  
È questa la terra dei Cuori liberali!  
È questa la terra dove il Genio non mai invano  
Effuse il suo canto che incanta l’[[Soul|anima]]?   15  
Ahimè! pur Butler contro il bigotto nemico  
[[Well|Ben]] esperto a vibrar l’acuto dardo dell’[[Humour|Umorismo]],  
Pur Butler sentì il pungolo acerbo del Bisogno;  
E Otway, Maestro dell’arte Tragica,  
Cui la Pietà [[Self|stessa]] aveva insegnato a cantare,   20  
Cadde sotto un carico di Dolore;  
Questo può mai udire il generoso Britanno,  
E non gli balza nell’occhio la Lacrima indignata?  
Esultante di Cuore e fidente di Fama,  
Dalle valli dove l’Avon scherza venne il Menestrello,   25  
Lieto mentre il Poeta s’affretta oltre  
Medita il futuro [[Song|canto]],  
Come Ælla combatté i nemici del suo paese,  
E mentre la Fantasia nell’aria  
Gli dipinge molte belle visioni   30  
Gli occhi gli [[Dance|danzano]] rapimento e il petto gli avvampa.  
Con generosa gioia contempla l’oro ideale:  
Ascolta le preghiere di molte Vedove,  
E molti ringraziamenti d’[[Orphan|Orfano]] egli ode;  
Acqueta in pace il petto logoro d’affanni,   35  
Comanda agli occhi del Debitore di conoscer riposo,  
E Libertà e Beatitudine mirare:  
Ed ora punisce il cuore d’acciaio,  
E la sua stessa verga di ferro fa sentire all’Oppressione.  
Destinato a trarre il sospiro della triste Delusione,   40  
A sentir la [[Hope|Speranza]] or levata, or depressa,  
A sentir gli ardori d’un petto offeso,  
Da tutto l’acuto dolore profondo del tuo Fato  
Invano, o Giovane, volgo l’occhio atterrito;  
Ché la Fantasia possente sempre vicina   45  
Forza l’odiosa pittura sulla mia vista.  
Là, [[Death|Morte]] d’ogni caro diletto,  
Aggrotta la [[Poverty|Povertà]] dal ciglio di Gigante!  
Invano cerco le grazie della giovanile leggiadria,  
Il tuo occhio infossato, le smunte guance essa mostra,   50  
Le rapide emozioni che lottano nel Volto  
Fioco indice dei tuoi Travagli mentali,  
Quando ogni forte Passione sdegnò il freno,  
E non un Amico era presso a calmar la tua tempestosa [[Soul|anima]].  
Tale fu l’ora triste e cupa   55  
Quando l’angosciato Affanno dal ciglio torvo  
Preparò del [[Poison|Veleno]] la potenza fredda di [[Death|morte]].  
Già alle tue labbra era levata la coppa,  
Quando la filiale Pietà ti stette accanto,  
Ti comandò di volgere gli occhi fissi   60  
Su scene che [[Well|bene]] potean struggerti l’[[Soul|anima]] —  
La tua casetta natia ti tenne dinanzi,  
La tua casetta natia, dove la Pace non da molto  
Aveva ascoltato il tuo [[Song|canto]] della sera;  
Ti comandò d’udire gli strilli di tua sorella,   65  
E di notar la lacrima tremante di tua madre,  
Ti fece sentire il suo sospiro profondo,  
E tutta la sua silente agonia di Dolore.  
E dal tuo Fato tale angoscia dovrà seguire?  
Ah! scaglia dalla tua mano il calice [[Poison|avvelenato]]!   70  
E l’avresti scagliato al suo dolce comando;  
Se non che [[Despair|Disperazione]] e Sdegno [[Rose|sorsero]],  
E ridissero la storia dei tuoi Dolori,  
Dissero l’acuto insulto del Cuore insensibile,  
La tremenda dipendenza dalla mente di nascita vile,   75  
Dissero ogni Dolore per cui il tuo petto potesse soffrire,  
Abbandono e ghignante scherno e Bisogno uniti —  
Rinculando, mandasti l’amico del [[Pain|Dolore]]  
A far rotolare un’onda di [[Death|Morte]] per ogni vena gelata.  
O Spirito beato!    80  
Sia che intorno all’eterno Trono,  
Fra il fulgore dei Cherubini,  
Tu effonda l’[[Hymn|inno]] della gratitudine,  
O, librandoti per il beato Regno,  
Rapisca gli Angeli col tuo canto, — 85  
Concedimi, come a te, di far sonare la lira,  
Come te, di risplendere di [[Fire|fuoco]] divino —  
Ma ahi! quando infuriano l’Onde del Dolore,  
Concedimi d’opporre con petto più fermo il loro odio,  
E di librarmi oltre le tempeste con occhio eretto ed esultante!   90  
1790



<span class="atom-split" data-atom="016_pain" data-title="PAIN" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/nature,motif/music_and_song,concept/melancholy,concept/mortality,concept/transience,form/sonnet,cluster/grief_and_loss,cluster/elegy,cluster/clothing_and_costume"></span>


Once could the Morn’s first beams, the healthful breeze,  
All [[Nature]] charm, and gay was every hour: —  
But ah! not Music’s [[Self|self]], nor fragrant bower  
Can glad the trembling sense of wan Disease.  
Now that the frequent pangs my frame assail,   5  
Now that my sleepless eyes are sunk and dim,  
And seas of [[Pain]] seem waving through each limb —  
Ah what can all Life’s gilded scenes avail?  
I view the crowd, whom Youth and Health inspire,  
Hear the loud laugh, and catch the sportive lay,   10  
Then sigh and think — I too could laugh and play  
And gaily sport it on the Muse’s lyre,  
Ere [[Tyrant]] [[Pain]] had chas’d away delight,  
Ere the wild pulse throbb’d anguish thro’ the [[Night|night]]!  
? 1790.  


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

Un tempo i primi raggi del Mattino, la brezza salubre,  
Tutta la [[Nature|Natura]] potean incantare, e lieta era ogni ora: —  
Ma ahi! né la Musica [[Self|stessa]], né il pergolato fragrante  
Può rallegrare il senso tremante della pallida Malattia.  
Ora che le fitte frequenti m’assalgono le membra,   5  
Ora che i miei occhi insonni son infossati e fiochi,  
E mari di [[Pain|Dolore]] par ondeggino per ogni membro —  
Ah, che valgono tutte le dorate scene della Vita?  
Miro la folla, cui Giovinezza e Salute ispirano,  
Odo il riso sonoro, e colgo il canto festoso,   10  
Poi sospiro e penso — anch’io potrei ridere e giocare  
E scherzare lietamente sulla lira della Musa,  
Prima che il [[Tyrant|Tiranno]] [[Pain|Dolore]] cacciasse via il diletto,  
Prima che il polso selvaggio pulsasse angoscia per la [[Night|notte]]!  
? 1790.



<span class="atom-split" data-atom="020_on_receiving_an_account_that_his_only_sisters_death_was_inevitable" data-title="ON RECEIVING AN ACCOUNT THAT HIS ONLY SISTER’S DEATH WAS INEVITABLE" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/death,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/mortality,concept/alienation,concept/melancholy,form/sonnet,cluster/grief_and_loss,cluster/elegy,cluster/clothing_and_costume"></span>


The tear which mourn’d a brother’s fate scarce dry —  
[[Pain]] after [[Pain|pain]], and woe succeeding woe —  
Is my heart destin’d for another blow?  
O my sweet sister! and must thou too die?  
Ah! how has Disappointment pour’d the tear   5  
O’er infant [[Hope]] destroy’d by early [[Frost|frost]]!  
How are ye gone, whom most my [[Soul|soul]] held dear!  
Scarce had I lov’d you ere I mourn’d you lost;  
Say, is this hollow eye, this heartless [[Pain|pain]],  
Fated to rove thro’ Life’s wide cheerless plain — 10  
Nor father, brother, sister meet its ken —  
My woes, my joys unshared! Ah! long ere then  
On me thy icy dart, stern [[Death]], be prov’d; —  
Better to die, than live and not be lov’d!  
1791.  


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

Appena asciutta la lacrima che pianse il fato d’un fratello —  
[[Pain|Pena]] dopo [[Pain|pena]], e dolore che segue dolore —  
È il mio cuore destinato a un altro colpo?  
O mia dolce sorella! e devi morire anche tu?  
Ah! come ha versato la lacrima la Delusione   5  
Sull’infante [[Hope|Speranza]] distrutta da precoce [[Frost|gelo]]!  
Come ve n’andaste, voi che più cari l’[[Soul|anima]] mia tenne!  
Appena v’avevo amati che vi piansi perduti;  
Di’, è quest’occhio incavato, questa [[Pain|pena]] senza cuore,  
Destinata a errare per l’ampia squallida pianura della Vita — 10  
Né padre, né fratello, né sorella incontrar la sua vista —  
I miei dolori, le mie gioie non divise! Ah! molto prima d’allora  
Su me il tuo dardo di ghiaccio, severa [[Death|Morte]], sia provato; —  
Meglio morire, che vivere e non essere amato!  
1791.



<span class="atom-split" data-atom="021_on_seeing_a_youth_affectionately_welcomed_by_a_sister" data-title="ON SEEING A YOUTH AFFECTIONATELY WELCOMED BY A SISTER" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/death,motif/memory,motif/the_grave,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/melancholy,form/sonnet,cluster/grief_and_loss,cluster/elegy,cluster/clothing_and_costume"></span>


I too a sister had! too cruel [[Death]]!  
How sad Remembrance bids my bosom heave!  
Tranquil her [[Soul|soul]], as sleeping Infant’s breath;  
Meek were her manners as a vernal Eve.  
Knowledge, that frequent lifts the bloated mind,   5  
Gave her the treasure of a lowly breast,  
And Wit to venom’d Malice oft assign’d,  
Dwelt in her bosom in a Turtle’s nest.  
Cease, busy [[Memory]]! cease to urge the dart;  
Nor on my [[Soul|soul]] her [[Love|love]] to me impress!   10  
For oh I mourn in anguish — and my heart  
Feels the keen pang, th’ unutterable distress.  
Yet wherefore grieve I that her sorrows cease,  
For Life was misery, and the [[Grave]] is Peace!  
1791.  


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

Anch’io una sorella ebbi! troppo crudele [[Death|Morte]]!  
Come il triste Ricordo fa sollevare il mio petto!  
Tranquilla l’[[Soul|anima]] sua, come il respiro d’Infante che dorme;  
Miti erano i suoi modi come Sera di primavera.  
Il Sapere, che sovente gonfia e leva la mente,   5  
Le diede il tesoro d’un petto umile,  
E l’Ingegno, spesso assegnato alla Malizia avvelenata,  
Dimorò nel suo seno come in nido di Tortora.  
Cessa, [[Memory|Memoria]] affannosa! cessa d’incalzare il dardo;  
Né sull’[[Soul|anima]] mia imprimere il suo [[Love|amore]] per me!   10  
Ché oh io piango in angoscia — e il mio cuore  
Sente la fitta acuta, l’indicibile pena.  
Pur perché m’affliggo che i suoi dolori cessino,  
Ché la Vita era miseria, e la [[Grave|Tomba]] è Pace!  
1791.



<span class="atom-split" data-atom="045_the_gentle_look" data-title="THE GENTLE LOOK" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/dreams,motif/flowers,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/melancholy,concept/nostalgia,concept/hope,concept/transience,form/sonnet,cluster/grief_and_loss,cluster/elegy,cluster/clothing_and_costume"></span>


Thou gentle Look, that didst my [[Soul|soul]] beguile,  
Why hast thou left me? Still in some fond dream  
Revisit my sad heart, auspicious Smile!  
As falls on closing [[Flowers|flowers]] the lunar beam:  
What [[Time|time]], in sickly mood, at parting day   5  
I lay me down and think of happier years;  
Of joys, that glimmer’d in [[Hope]]’s twilight ray,  
Then left me darkling in a vale of [[Tears|tears]].  
O pleasant days of [[Hope]] — for ever gone!  
Could I recall you! — But that thought is vain.   10  
Availeth not [[Persuasion]]’s sweetest tone  
To lure the fleet-wing’d Travellers back again:  
Yet fair, though faint, their images shall gleam  
Like the bright Rainbow on a willowy stream.  
? 1793.  


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

Tu gentile Sguardo, che l’[[Soul|anima]] mia incantasti,  
Perché m’hai lasciato? Ancora in qualche caro sogno  
Rivisita il mio triste cuore, Sorriso propizio!  
Come cade sui [[Flowers|fiori]] che si chiudono il raggio lunare:  
Nell’[[Time|ora]] in cui, d’animo infermo, al morir del giorno   5  
Mi corico e penso ad anni più felici;  
A gioie che brillarono nel raggio crepuscolare della [[Hope|Speranza]],  
Poi mi lasciarono al buio in una valle di [[Tears|lacrime]].  
O giorni soavi di [[Hope|Speranza]] — per sempre andati!  
Potessi richiamarvi! — Ma quel pensiero è vano.   10  
Non vale il tono più dolce della [[Persuasion|Persuasione]]  
Ad allettare indietro i Viaggiatori dall’ala veloce:  
Pur belle, benché fioche, le loro immagini brilleranno  
Come il vivido Arcobaleno su un ruscello di salici.  
? 1793.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="070_elegy_imitated_from_one_of_akensides_blank-verse_inscriptions" data-title="ELEGY IMITATED FROM ONE OF AKENSIDE’S BLANK-VERSE INSCRIPTIONS" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/the_grave,motif/death,concept/unrequited_love,concept/guilt,concept/conscience,concept/grief_and_loss,form/elegy,cluster/grief_and_loss,cluster/elegy,cluster/clothing_and_costume"></span>


Near the lone pile with ivy overspread,  
Fast by the rivulet’s [[Sleep|sleep]]-persuading sound,  
Where ‘sleeps the moonlight’ on yon verdant bed —  
O humbly press that consecrated ground!  
For there does [[Edmund]] rest, the learnéd swain!   5  
And there his spirit most delights to rove:  
Young [[Edmund]]! fam’d for each harmonious strain,  
And the sore wounds of ill-requited [[Love]].  
Like some tall tree that spreads its branches wide,  
And loads the West-[[Wind|wind]] with its soft perfume,   10  
His manhood blossom’d; till the faithless [[Pride|pride]]  
Of fair Matilda sank him to the tomb.  
But soon did righteous Heaven her [[Guilt]] pursue!  
Where’er with wilder’d step she wander’d pale,  
Still [[Edmund]]’s image [[Rose|rose]] to blast her view,   15  
Still [[Edmund]]’s voice accus’d her in each gale.  
With keen regret, and conscious [[Guilt]]’s alarms,  
Amid the pomp of Affluence she pined;  
Nor all that lur’d her [[Faith|faith]] from [[Edmund]]’s arms  
Could lull the wakeful horror of her mind.   20  
Go, Traveller! tell the tale with sorrow fraught:  
Some tearful Maid perchance, or blooming Youth,  
May hold it in remembrance; and be taught  
That Riches cannot pay for [[Love]] or [[Truth]].  
? 1794.  


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

Presso la mole solitaria coperta d’edera,  
Vicino al suono del ruscello che persuade il [[Sleep|sonno]],  
Dove ‘dorme il chiar di luna’ su quel verde letto —  
Oh calca umilmente quel suolo consacrato!  
Ché là riposa [[Edmund]], il dotto pastore!   5  
E là il suo spirito più si diletta d’errare:  
Giovane [[Edmund]]! famoso per ogni canto armonioso,  
E per le piaghe crudeli d’[[Love|Amore]] mal ricambiato.  
Come alto albero che stende larghe le sue braccia,  
E carica il [[Wind|vento]] d’Occidente del suo dolce profumo,   10  
Fiorì la sua virilità; finché l’infido [[Pride|orgoglio]]  
Della bella Matilde lo sprofondò nella tomba.  
Ma presto il giusto Cielo perseguì la sua [[Guilt|Colpa]]!  
Ovunque con passo smarrito ella errasse pallida,  
Sempre l’immagine d’[[Edmund]] [[Rose|sorgeva]] a devastarle la vista,   15  
Sempre la voce d’[[Edmund]] l’accusava in ogni brezza.  
Con acuto rimorso, e gli allarmi della [[Guilt|Colpa]] consapevole,  
Fra la pompa dell’Opulenza ella si consumò;  
Né tutto ciò che sviò la sua [[Faith|fede]] dalle braccia d’[[Edmund]]  
Poté sopire il desto orrore della sua mente.   20  
Va’, Viaggiatore! narra la storia carica di dolore:  
Qualche Fanciulla in lacrime forse, o Giovane in fiore,  
La terrà a memoria; e sarà ammaestrato

A WANDERER’S FAREWELL  
To know, to esteem, to [[Love|love]], — and then to part —  
Makes up life’s tale to many a feeling heart;  
Alas for some abiding-place of [[Love|love]],  
O’er which my spirit, like the mother dove,  
Might brood with warming wings!  
O fair! O kind!   5  
Sisters in [[Blood|blood]], yet each with each intwined  
More close by sisterhood of heart and mind!  
Me disinherited in form and face  
By [[Nature|nature]], and mishap of outward grace;  
Who, [[Soul|soul]] and [[Body|body]], through one guiltless fault   10  
Waste daily with the [[Poison|poison]] of sad thought,  
Me did you soothe, when solace hoped I none!  
And as on unthaw’d ice the [[Winter|winter]] sun,  
Though stern the [[Frost|frost]], though brief the genial day,  
You bless my heart with many a cheerful ray;   15  
For gratitude suspends the heart’s [[Despair|despair]],  
Reflecting bright though cold your image there.  
Nay more! its music by some sweeter strain  
Makes us live o’er our happiest hours again,  
[[Hope]] re-appearing dim in [[Memory|memory]]’s guise — 20  
Even thus did you call up before mine eyes  
Two dear, dear Sisters, prized all price above,  
Sisters, like you, with more than sisters’ [[Love|love]];  
So like you they, and so in you were seen  
Their relative statures, tempers, looks, and mien,   25  
That oft, dear ladies! you have been to me  
At once a vision and reality.  
Sight seem’d a sort of [[Memory|memory]], and amaze  
Mingled a trouble with affection’s gaze.  
Oft to my eager [[Soul|soul]] I whisper blame,   30  
A Stranger bid it feel the Stranger’s shame —  
My eager [[Soul|soul]], impatient of the name,  
No strangeness owns, no Stranger’s form descries:  
The chidden heart spreads trembling on the eyes.  
First-seen I gazed, as I would look you thro’!   35  
My best-beloved regain’d their youth in you, —  
And still I ask, though now familiar grown,  
Are you for their sakes dear, or for your own?  
O doubly dear! may Quiet with you dwell!  
In Grief I [[Love|love]] you, yet I [[Love|love]] you [[Well|well]]!   40  
[[Hope]] long is dead to me! an [[Orphan|orphan]]’s tear  
[[Love]] wept despairing o’er his [[Nurse|nurse]]’s bier.  
Yet still she flutters o’er her [[Grave|grave]]’s green slope:

Act I, Scene 1.
O my Tibullus,
Let us not blame him; for against such chances
The heartiest strife of virtue is not proof.
We may read constancy and fortitude
To other souls; but had ourselves been struck   5
With the like planet, had our loves, like his,
Been ravished from us by injurious [[Death|death]],

The heartiest strife of manhood is scarce proof.
We may read constancy and fortitude
To other souls — but had ourselves been struck
Even in the height and heat of our keen wishing,
It might have made our heart-strings jar, like his.

No. III.  
[For [[Elegy]] Imitated from one of Akenside’s ‘[[Blank Verse]] Inscriptions’,

Whoe’er thou art whose path in [[Summer]] lies  
Through yonder village, turn thee where the Grove  
Of branching oaks a rural palace old  
Embosoms — there dwells Albert, generous lord  
Of all the harvest round. And onward thence   5  
A low plain chapel fronts the morning light  
Fast by a silent rivulet. Humbly walk,  
O stranger, o’er the consecrated ground;  
And on that verdant Hillock, which thou seest  
Beset with osiers, let thy pious hand   10  
Sprinkle fresh [[Water|water]] from the brook, and strew  
Sweet-smelling [[Flowers|flowers]] — for there doth [[Edmund]] rest,  
The learned shepherd; for each rural art  
Famed, and for [[songs]] harmonious, and the woes  
Of ill-requited [[Love|love]]. The faithless [[Pride|pride]]   15  
Of fair Matilda sank him to the [[Grave|grave]]  
In manhood’s prime. But soon did righteous Heaven  
With [[Tears|tears]], with sharp remorse, and pining care  
Avenge her falsehood. Nor could all the gold  
And nuptial pomp, which lured her plighted [[Faith|faith]]   20  
From [[Edmund]] to a loftier husband’s [[Home|home]],  
Relieve her breaking heart, or turn aside  
The strokes of [[Death|death]]. Go, traveller, relate  
The mournful story. Haply some fair maid  
May hold it in remembrance, and be taught   25  
That riches cannot pay for [[Truth|truth]] or [[Love|love]].
