VERSE 1ST  
Hail! festal Easter that dost bring  
Approach of sweetly-smiling [[motifs/spring|spring]],  
When [[concepts/nature|Nature]]’s clad in green:  
When feather’d songsters through the grove  
With beasts confess the power of [[motifs/love|love]]   5  
And brighten all the scene.  
VERSE 2ND  
Now youths the breaking stages load  
That swiftly rattling o’er the [[motifs/road|road]]  
To Greenwich haste away:  
While some with sounding oars divide   10  
Of smoothly-flowing Thames the tide  
All sing the festive lay.  
VERSE 3RD  
With mirthful [[motifs/dance|dance]] they beat the ground,  
Their shouts of joy the hills resound  
And catch the jocund noise:   15  
Without a tear, without a sigh  
Their moments all in transports fly  
Till evening ends their joys.  
VERSE 4TH  
But little think their joyous hearts  
Of dire Misfortune’s varied smarts   20  
Which youthful years conceal:  
Thoughtless of bitter-smiling Woe  
Which all mankind are born to know  
And they themselves must feel.  
VERSE 5TH  
Yet he who Wisdom’s paths shall keep   25  
And Virtue firm that scorns to weep  
At ills in Fortune’s power,  
Through this life’s variegated scene  
In raging storms or calm serene  
Shall cheerful spend the hour.   30  
VERSE 6TH  
While steady Virtue guides his mind  
Heav’n-born Content he still shall find  
That never sheds a tear:  
Without respect to any tide  
His hours away in bliss shall glide    35  
Like Easter all the year.  
1787.

STROFA 1ª  
Salve! festosa Pasqua che rechi  
l’avvicinarsi della [[motifs/spring|primavera]] dolcemente ridente,  
quando la [[concepts/nature|Natura]] si veste di verde:  
quando i cantori pennuti per il boschetto  
con le bestie confessano il potere d’[[motifs/love|amore]]   5  
e rallegrano tutta la scena.  
STROFA 2ª  
Ora i giovani caricano le diligenze che si sfasciano  
e, sferragliando veloci per la [[motifs/road|strada]],  
s’affrettano verso Greenwich:  
mentre altri con remi sonanti fendono   10  
la corrente del Tamigi che scorre placido,  
tutti cantano il canto festivo.  
STROFA 3ª  
Con [[motifs/dance|danza]] gioiosa battono il suolo,  
i loro gridi di gioia i colli rimandano  
e colgono il festoso frastuono:   15  
senza una lacrima, senza un sospiro  
i loro istanti volano tutti in trasporti  
finché la sera pone fine alle loro gioie.  
STROFA 4ª  
Ma poco pensano i loro cuori gioiosi  
alle varie fitte della dura Sventura   20  
che gli anni giovanili nascondono:  
incuranti dell’Affanno dal sorriso amaro  
che tutti gli uomini nascono per conoscere  
e che essi stessi dovranno provare.  
STROFA 5ª  
Ma chi terrà i sentieri della Saggezza   25  
e la Virtù salda che sdegna di piangere  
sui mali in potere della Fortuna,  
per la scena screziata di questa vita,  
in tempeste furiose o in calma serena,  
passerà lieto le sue ore.   30  
STROFA 6ª  
Finché salda Virtù gli guida la mente  
troverà sempre la Contentezza nata dal cielo  
che mai versa una lacrima:  
senza riguardo ad alcuna marea  
le sue ore scivoleranno via in beatitudine    35  
come Pasqua tutto l’anno.  
1787.



<span class="atom-split" data-atom="032_happiness" data-title="HAPPINESS" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


On wide or narrow scale shall Man  
Most happily describe Life’s plan?  
Say shall he bloom and wither there,  
Where first his infant buds appear;  
Or upwards dart with soaring force,   5  
And tempt some more ambitious course?  
Obedient now to [[concepts/hope|Hope]]’s command,  
I bid each humble wish expand,  
And fair and bright Life’s prospects seem.  
While [[concepts/hope|Hope]] displays her cheering beam,   10  
And Fancy’s vivid colourings stream,  
While Emulation stands me nigh  
The Goddess of the eager eye.  
With foot advanc’d and anxious heart  
Now for the fancied goal I start: —  15  
Ah! why will Reason intervene  
Me and my promis’d joys between!  
She stops my course, she [[motifs/chains|chains]] my [[characters/speed|speed]],  
While thus her forceful words proceed: —  
Ah! listen, Youth, ere yet too late,   20  
What evils on thy course may wait!  
To bow the head, to bend the knee,  
A minion of Servility,  
At low [[concepts/pride|Pride]]’s frequent frowns to sigh,  
And watch the glance in Folly’s eye;   25  
To toil intense, yet toil in vain,  
And feel with what a hollow [[motifs/pain|pain]]  
Pale Disappointment hangs her head  
O’er darling Expectation dead!  
‘The scene is changed and Fortune’s gale   30  
Shall belly out each prosperous sail.  
Yet sudden wealth full [[motifs/well|well]] I know  
Did never happiness bestow.  
That wealth to which we were not born  
Dooms us to sorrow or to scorn.   35  
Behold yon flock which long had trod  
O’er the short grass of Devon’s sod,  
To [[characters/lincoln|Lincoln]]’s rank rich meads transferr’d,  
And in their fate thy own be [[concepts/fear|fear]]’d;  
Through every limb contagions fly,   40  
Deform’d and choked they burst and die.  
‘When Luxury opens wide her arms,  
And smiling wooes thee to those charms,  
Whose fascination thousands own,  
Shall thy brows wear the stoic frown?   45  
And when her goblet she extends  
Which maddening myriads press around,  
What power divine thy [[motifs/soul|soul]] befriends  
That thou should’st dash it to the ground? —  
No, thou shalt drink, and thou shalt know   50  
Her transient bliss, her lasting woe,  
Her maniac joys, that know no measure,  
And Riot rude and painted Pleasure; —  
Till (sad reverse!) the Enchantress vile  
To frowns converts her magic smile;    55  
Her train impatient to destroy,  
Observe her frown with gloomy joy;  
On thee with harpy fangs they seize  
The hideous offspring of Disease,  
Swoln Dropsy ignorant of Rest,   60  
And Fever garb’d in scarlet vest,  
Consumption driving the quick hearse,  
And Gout that howls the frequent curse,  
With Apoplex of heavy head  
That surely aims his dart of lead.   65  
‘But say Life’s joys unmix’d were given  
To thee some favourite of Heaven:  
Within, without, tho’ all were health —  
Yet what e’en thus are Fame, Power, Wealth,  
But sounds that variously express,   70  
What’s thine already — Happiness!  
‘Tis thine the converse deep to hold  
With all the famous sons of old;  
And thine the happy waking dream  
While [[concepts/hope|Hope]] pursues some favourite theme,   75  
As oft when [[motifs/night|Night]] o’er Heaven is spread,  
Round this maternal seat you tread,  
Where far from splendour, far from riot,  
In [[motifs/silence|silence]] wrapt sleeps careless Quiet.  
‘Tis thine with Fancy oft to talk,   80  
And thine the peaceful evening walk;  
And what to thee the sweetest are —  
The setting sun, the [[works/evening-star-(poe)|Evening Star]] —  
The tints, which live along the sky,  
And [[motifs/moon|Moon]] that meets thy raptur’d eye,   85  
Where oft the tear shall grateful start,  
Dear silent pleasures of the Heart!  
Ah! Being blest, for Heaven shall lend  
To share thy simple joys a friend!  
Ah! doubly blest, if [[motifs/love|Love]] supply   90  
His influence to complete thy joy,  
If chance some lovely maid thou find  
To read thy visage in thy mind.  
‘One blessing more demands thy care: —  
Once more to Heaven address the [[motifs/prayer|prayer]]:   95  
For humble independence pray  
The guardian genius of thy way;  
Whom (sages say) in days of yore  
Meek Competence to Wisdom bore,  
So shall thy little vessel glide   100  
With a fair breeze adown the tide,  
And [[concepts/hope|Hope]], if e’er thou ‘ginst to sorrow,  
Remind thee of some fair to-morrow,  
Till [[motifs/death|Death]] shall close thy tranquil eye  
While [[concepts/faith|Faith]] proclaims “Thou shalt not die!”’   105  
1791.

Su scala ampia o ristretta l’Uomo  
traccerà più felicemente il disegno della Vita?  
Dimmi, fiorirà e appassirà là  
dove prime spuntarono le sue gemme infantili;  
o schizzerà in alto con forza librante,   5  
tentando una via più ambiziosa?  
Obbediente ora al comando della [[concepts/hope|Speranza]],  
ordino a ogni umile desiderio di espandersi,  
e belle e lucenti appaiono le prospettive della Vita.  
Mentre la [[concepts/hope|Speranza]] dispiega il suo raggio confortante,   10  
e i vividi colori della Fantasia fluiscono,  
mentre l’Emulazione mi sta accanto,  
Dea dall’occhio avido.  
Col piede avanzato e il cuore ansioso  
ora muovo verso la meta immaginata: —  15  
Ah! perché la Ragione s’interpone  
fra me e le mie gioie promesse!  
Arresta il mio corso, [[motifs/chains|incatena]] la mia [[characters/speed|corsa]],  
mentre così procedono le sue parole possenti: —  
Ah! ascolta, Giovane, prima che sia troppo tardi,   20  
quali mali possono attendere il tuo cammino!  
Chinare il capo, piegare il ginocchio,  
favorito della Servilità,  
sospirare ai frequenti cipigli della bassa [[concepts/pride|Superbia]],  
e spiare lo sguardo nell’occhio della Follia;   25  
faticare intensamente, e faticare invano,  
e sentire con quale vuota [[motifs/pain|pena]]  
la pallida Delusione china il capo  
sulla cara Attesa morta!  
‘La scena è mutata e il vento della Fortuna   30  
gonfierà ogni vela propizia.  
Pure so [[motifs/well|bene]] che la ricchezza improvvisa  
non diede mai felicità.  
Quella ricchezza per cui non siamo nati  
ci condanna al dolore o allo scherno.   35  
Guarda quel gregge che a lungo aveva calcato  
l’erba corta della zolla del Devon,  
trasferito ai prati folti e grassi di [[characters/lincoln|Lincoln]],  
e nella loro sorte sia [[concepts/fear|temuta]] la tua;  
per ogni membro volano i contagi,   40  
deformati e soffocati scoppiano e muoiono.  
‘Quando il Lusso apre largo le braccia,  
e sorridendo ti alletta a quelle lusinghe  
il cui fascino mille confessano,  
porterà la tua fronte il cipiglio stoico?   45  
E quando ella porge la sua coppa  
attorno a cui premono miriadi impazzite,  
quale potere divino soccorre la tua [[motifs/soul|anima]]  
perché tu la scagli a terra? —  
No, tu berrai, e conoscerai   50  
la sua beatitudine fugace, il suo affanno duraturo,  
le sue gioie forsennate, che non conoscono misura,  
e la rozza Gozzoviglia e il Piacere dipinto; —  
finché (triste rovescio!) la vile Incantatrice  
converte in cipigli il suo sorriso magico;    55  
il suo corteo impaziente di distruggere  
osserva il suo cipiglio con cupa gioia;  
su di te con zanne d’arpia s’avventano  
l’orrenda progenie della Malattia,  
la gonfia Idropisia che ignora il Riposo,   60  
e la Febbre vestita di scarlatto,  
la Tisi che guida il carro funebre veloce,  
e la Gotta che ulula la bestemmia frequente,  
con l’Apoplessia dal capo pesante  
che sicura mira il suo dardo di piombo.   65  
‘Ma poniamo che le gioie della Vita ti fossero date pure,  
a te, prediletto del Cielo:  
dentro, fuori, fosse tutto salute —  
pure che sono, anche così, Fama, Potere, Ricchezza,  
se non suoni che variamente esprimono   70  
ciò che è già tuo — la Felicità!  
È tuo tenere il profondo colloquio  
con tutti i famosi figli antichi;  
e tuo il felice sogno da desto  
mentre la [[concepts/hope|Speranza]] insegue un tema prediletto,   75  
come spesso quando la [[motifs/night|Notte]] è distesa sul Cielo  
tu passeggi intorno a questa sede materna,  
dove lungi dallo splendore, lungi dal tumulto,  
avvolta nel [[motifs/silence|silenzio]] dorme la Quiete spensierata.  
È tuo parlare spesso con la Fantasia,   80  
e tua la pacifica passeggiata serale;  
e ciò che per te è più dolce —  
il sole che tramonta, la Stella della Sera —  
le tinte che vivono lungo il cielo,  
e la [[motifs/moon|Luna]] che incontra il tuo occhio rapito,   85  
dove spesso la lacrima grata sgorgherà,  
cari silenziosi piaceri del Cuore!  
Ah! Essere beato, ché il Cielo concederà  
un amico con cui dividere le tue gioie semplici!  
Ah! due volte beato, se l’[[motifs/love|Amore]] presterà   90  
il suo influsso a compiere la tua gioia,  
se per caso trovi una fanciulla leggiadra  
che sappia leggere il tuo volto nella tua mente.  
‘Un’altra benedizione richiede la tua cura: —  
ancora una volta rivolgi al Cielo la [[motifs/prayer|preghiera]]:   95  
per un’umile indipendenza prega  
il genio custode del tuo cammino;  
che (dicono i savi) nei giorni antichi  
la mite Sufficienza generò alla Saggezza,  
così scivolerà il tuo piccolo vascello   100  
con brezza propizia giù per la corrente,  
e la [[concepts/hope|Speranza]], se mai cominci a dolerti,  
ti rammenterà qualche bel domani,  
finché la [[motifs/death|Morte]] chiuderà il tuo occhio tranquillo  
mentre la [[concepts/faith|Fede]] proclama “Tu non morrai!”’   105  
1791.

WRITTEN IN JESUS WOOD, FEB. 10, 1792  
Lo! through the dusky [[motifs/silence|silence]] of the groves,  
Thro’ vales irriguous, and thro’ green retreats,  
With languid murmur creeps the placid stream  
And works its secret way.  
Awhile meand’ring round its native fields   5  
It rolls the playful wave and winds its flight:  
Then downward flowing with awaken’d [[characters/speed|speed]]  
Embosoms in the Deep!  
Thus thro’ its silent tenor may my Life  
Smooth its meek stream by sordid wealth unclogg’d,   10  
Alike unconscious of forensic storms,  
And Glory’s [[motifs/blood|blood]]-stain’d palm!  
And when dark Age shall close Life’s little day,  
Satiate of sport, and weary of its toils,  
E’en thus may slumbrous [[motifs/death|Death]] my decent limbs   15  
Compose with icy hand!  
1792.

SCRITTO NEL BOSCO DEL JESUS COLLEGE, 10 FEBB. 1792  
Ecco! per il [[motifs/silence|silenzio]] fosco dei boschetti,  
per valli irrigue e per verdi ritiri,  
con languido mormorio striscia il placido ruscello  
e si apre la sua via segreta.  
Per un poco serpeggiando intorno ai campi natii   5  
volge l’onda giocosa e piega il suo volo:  
poi scorrendo giù con [[characters/speed|corsa]] ridestata  
s’insena nel Profondo!  
Così per il suo silenzioso tenore possa la mia Vita  
spianare il suo mite corso, non ingombro di sordida ricchezza,   10  
del pari ignaro di tempeste forensi  
e della palma macchiata di [[motifs/blood|sangue]] della Gloria!  
E quando la cupa Vecchiaia chiuderà il breve giorno della Vita,  
sazio di svago e stanco delle sue fatiche,  
possa così la [[motifs/death|Morte]] sonnolenta comporre le mie decorose membra   15  
con gelida mano!  
1792.



<span class="atom-split" data-atom="035_to_disappointment" data-title="TO DISAPPOINTMENT" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


Hence! thou fiend of gloomy sway,  
That lov’st on withering blast to ride  
O’er fond Illusion’s air-built [[concepts/pride|pride]].  
Sullen Spirit! Hence! Away!  
Where Avarice lurks in sordid cell,   5  
Or mad [[concepts/ambition|Ambition]] builds the dream,  
Or Pleasure plots th’ unholy scheme  
There with [[concepts/guilt|Guilt]] and Folly dwell!  
But oh! when [[concepts/hope|Hope]] on Wisdom’s wing  
Prophetic whispers pure delight,   10  
Be distant far thy cank’rous blight,  
Demon of envenom’d sting.  
Then haste thee, Nymph of balmy gales!  
Thy poet’s [[motifs/prayer|prayer]], sweet May! attend!  
Oh! place my parent and my friend   15  
‘Mid her lovely native vales.  
Peace, that lists the woodlark’s strains,  
Health, that breathes divinest treasures,  
Laughing Hours, and Social Pleasures  
Wait my friend in Cambria’s plains.   20  
Affection there with mingled ray  
Shall pour at once the raptures high  
Of filial and maternal Joy;  
Haste thee then, delightful May!  
And oh! may [[motifs/spring|Spring]]’s fair flowerets fade,   25  
May [[motifs/summer|Summer]] cease her limbs to lave  
In cooling stream, may [[motifs/autumn|Autumn]] [[motifs/grave|grave]]  
Yellow o’er the corn-cloath’d glade;  
Ere, from sweet retirement torn,  
She seek again the crowded mart:   30  
Nor thou, my selfish, selfish heart  
Dare her slow return to mourn!  
1792.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="042_songs_of_the_pixies" data-title="SONGS OF THE PIXIES" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


The Pixies, in the superstition of Devonshire, are a race of beings  
invisibly small, and harmless or friendly to man. At a small distance  
from a village in that county, half-way up a wood-covered [[motifs/hill|hill]], is an  
excavation called the Pixies’ Parlour. The roots of old trees form its  
ceiling; and on its sides are innumerable cyphers, among which the  
author discovered his own cypher and those of his brothers, cut by the  
hand of their [[motifs/childhood|childhood]]. At the foot of the [[motifs/hill|hill]] flows the [[motifs/river|river]] Otter.  
To this place the Author, during the [[motifs/summer|summer]] months of the year 1793,  
conducted a party of young ladies; one of whom, of stature elegantly  
small, and of complexion colourless yet clear, was proclaimed the Faery  
[[characters/queen|Queen]]. On which occasion the following Irregular Ode was written.  
I  
Whom the untaught Shepherds call  
Pixies in their madrigal,  
Fancy’s children, here we dwell:  
Welcome, Ladies! to our cell.  
Here the wren of softest note   5  
Builds its nest and warbles [[motifs/well|well]];  
Here the blackbird strains his throat;  
Welcome, Ladies! to our cell.  
II  
When fades the [[motifs/moon|moon]] to shadowy-pale,  
And scuds the cloud before the gale,   10  
Ere the Morn all gem-bedight  
Hath streak’d the East with rosy light,  
We sip the furze-flower’s fragrant dews  
Clad in robes of rainbow hues;  
Or sport amid the shooting gleams   15  
To the tune of distant-tinkling teams,  
While lusty Labour scouting sorrow  
Bids the Dame a glad good-morrow,  
Who jogs the accustom’d [[motifs/road|road]] along,  
And paces cheery to her cheering song.   20  
III  
But not our filmy pinion  
We scorch amid the blaze of day,  
When Noontide’s fiery-tresséd minion  
Flashes the fervid ray.  
Aye from the sultry heat   25  
We to the [[motifs/cave|cave]] retreat  
O’ercanopied by huge roots intertwin’d  
With wildest texture, blacken’d o’er with age:  
Round them their mantle green the ivies bind,  
Beneath whose foliage pale   30  
Fann’d by the unfrequent gale  
We shield us from the [[archetypes/tyrant|Tyrant]]’s mid-day rage.  
IV  
Thither, while the murmuring throng  
Of wild-[[motifs/bees|bees]] hum their drowsy song,  
By [[concepts/indolence|Indolence]] and Fancy brought,    35  
A youthful Bard, ‘unknown to Fame,’  
Wooes the [[characters/queen|Queen]] of Solemn Thought,  
And heaves the gentle misery of a sigh  
Gazing with tearful eye,  
As round our sandy grot appear   40  
Many a rudely-sculptur’d name  
To pensive [[motifs/memory|Memory]] dear!  
Weaving gay [[motifs/dreams|dreams]] of sunny-tinctur’d hue,  
We glance before his view:  
O’er his hush’d [[motifs/soul|soul]] our soothing witcheries shed   45  
And twine the future garland round his head.  
V  
When Evening’s dusky car  
[[motifs/crown|Crown]]’d with her dewy star  
Steals o’er the fading sky in shadowy flight;  
On [[motifs/leaves|leaves]] of aspen trees   50  
We tremble to the breeze

Sermoni propriora. — HOR.  
Low was our pretty Cot: our tallest [[motifs/rose|Rose]]  
Peep’d at the chamber-[[motifs/window|window]]. We could hear  
At silent [[motifs/noon|noon]], and eve, and early morn,  
The Sea’s faint murmur. In the open air  
Our Myrtles blossom’d; and across the porch   5  
Thick Jasmins twined: the little landscape round  
Was green and woody, and refresh’d the eye.  
It was a spot which you might aptly call  
The Valley of Seclusion! Once I saw  
(Hallowing his Sabbath-day by quietness)   10  
A wealthy son of Commerce saunter by,  
Bristowa’s citizen: methought, it calm’d  
His thirst of idle gold, and made him muse  
With wiser feelings: for he paus’d, and look’d  
With a pleas’d sadness, and gaz’d all around,   15  
Then eyed our Cottage, and gaz’d round again,  
And sigh’d, and said, it was a Blesséd Place.  
And we were bless’d. Oft with patient ear  
Long-listening to the viewless sky-lark’s note  
(Viewless, or haply for a moment seen   20  
Gleaming on sunny wings) in whisper’d tones  
I’ve said to my Belovéd, ‘Such, sweet Girl!  
The inobtrusive song of Happiness,  
Unearthly minstrelsy! then only heard  
When the [[motifs/soul|Soul]] seeks to hear; when all is hush’d,   25  
And the Heart listens!’  
But the [[motifs/time|time]], when first  
From that low Dell, steep up the stony Mount  
I climb’d with perilous toil and reach’d the top,  
Oh! what a goodly scene! Here the bleak mount,  
The bare bleak mountain speckled thin with sheep;   30  
Grey clouds, that shadowing spot the sunny fields;  
And [[motifs/river|river]], now with bushy rocks o’er-brow’d,  
Now winding bright and full, with naked banks;  
And seats, and lawns, the Abbey and the wood,  
And cots, and hamlets, and faint [[motifs/city|city]]-spire;   35  
The Channel there, the Islands and white sails,  
Dim coasts, and cloud-like hills, and shoreless Ocean —  
It seem’d like Omnipresence! God, methought,  
Had built him there a Temple: the whole World  
Seem’d imag’d in its vast circumference:   40  
No wish profan’d my overwhelméd heart.  
Blest hour! It was a luxury, — to be!  
Ah! quiet Dell! dear Cot, and Mount [[concepts/sublime|sublime]]!  
I was constrain’d to quit you. Was it right,  
While my unnumber’d brethren toil’d and bled,   45  
That I should dream away the entrusted hours  
On [[motifs/rose|rose]]-leaf beds, pampering the coward heart  
With feelings all too delicate for use?  
Sweet is the tear that from some Howard’s eye  
Drops on the cheek of one he lifts from earth:   50  
And he that works me good with unmov’d face,  
Does it but half: he chills me while he aids,  
My benefactor, not my brother man!  
Yet even this, this cold beneficence  
Praise, praise it, O my [[motifs/soul|Soul]]! oft as thou scann’st   55  
The sluggard Pity’s vision-weaving tribe!  
Who sigh for Wretchedness, yet shun the Wretched,  
Nursing in some delicious [[concepts/solitude|solitude]]  
Their slothful loves and dainty sympathies!  
I therefore go, and join head, heart, and hand,   60  
Active and firm, to fight the bloodless fight  
Of Science, [[concepts/freedom|Freedom]], and the [[concepts/truth|Truth]] in [[characters/christ|Christ]].  
Yet oft when after honourable toil  
Bests the tir’d mind, and waking loves to dream,  
My spirit shall revisit thee, dear Cot!   65  
Thy Jasmin and thy [[motifs/window|window]]-peeping [[motifs/rose|Rose]],  
And Myrtles fearless of the mild sea-air.  
And I shall sigh fond wishes — sweet Abode!  
Ah! — had none greater! And that all had such!  
It might be so — but the [[motifs/time|time]] is not yet.   70  
[[characters/speed|Speed]] it, O Father! Let thy Kingdom come!  
1795.

I  
Ye Clouds! that far above me float and pause,  
Whose pathless march no mortal may controul!  
Ye Ocean-Waves! that, wheresoe’er ye roll,  
Yield homage only to eternal laws!  
Ye Woods! that listen to the [[motifs/night|night]]-[[motifs/birds|birds]] singing,   5  
Midway the smooth and perilous slope reclined,  
Save when your own imperious branches swinging,  
Have made a solemn music of the [[motifs/wind|wind]]!  
Where, like a man beloved of God,  
Through glooms, which never woodman trod,   10  
How oft, pursuing fancies holy,  
My moonlight way o’er flowering weeds I wound,  
Inspired, beyond the guess of folly,  
By each rude shape and wild unconquerable sound!  
O ye loud Waves! and O ye Forests high!   15  
And O ye Clouds that far above me soared!  
Thou rising Sun! thou blue rejoicing Sky!  
Yea, every thing that is and will be free!  
Bear witness for me, wheresoe’er ye be,  
With what deep worship I have still adored   20  
The spirit of divinest Liberty.  
II  
When France in wrath her giant-limbs upreared,  
And with that oath, which smote air, earth, and sea,  
Stamped her strong foot and said she would be free,  
Bear witness for me, how I hoped and feared!   25  
With what a joy my lofty gratulation  
Unawed I sang, amid a slavish band:  
And when to whelm the disenchanted nation,  
Like fiends embattled by a wizard’s wand,  
The Monarchs marched in evil day,   30  
And Britain joined the dire array;  
Though dear her shores and circling ocean,  
Though many friendships, many youthful loves  
Had swoln the patriot emotion  
And flung a magic light o’er all her hills and groves;   35  
Yet still my voice, unaltered, sang defeat  
To all that braved the [[archetypes/tyrant|tyrant]]-quelling lance,  
And shame too long delayed and vain retreat!  
For ne’er, O Liberty! with partial aim  
I dimmed thy light or damped thy holy flame;   40  
But blessed the paeans of delivered France,  
And hung my head and wept at Britain’s name.  
III  
‘And what,’ I said, ‘though Blasphemy’s loud scream  
With that sweet music of deliverance strove!  
Though all the fierce and drunken passions wove   45  
A [[motifs/dance|dance]] more wild than e’er was maniac’s dream!  
Ye storms, that round the dawning East assembled,  
The Sun was rising, though ye hid his light!’  
And when, to soothe my [[motifs/soul|soul]], that hoped and trembled,  
The dissonance ceased, and all seemed calm and bright;   50  
When France her front deep-scarr’d and gory  
Concealed with clustering wreaths of glory;  
When, insupportably advancing,  
Her arm made mockery of the warrior’s ramp;  
While timid looks of fury glancing,   55  
Domestic treason, crushed beneath her fatal stamp,  
Writhed like a wounded dragon in his gore;  
Then I reproached my fears that would not flee;  
‘And soon,’ I said, ‘shall Wisdom teach her lore  
In the low huts of them that toil and groan!   60  
And, conquering by her happiness alone,  
Shall France compel the nations to be free,  
Till [[motifs/love|Love]] and Joy look round, and call the Earth their own.’  
IV  
Forgive me, [[concepts/freedom|Freedom]]! O forgive those [[motifs/dreams|dreams]]!  
I hear thy voice, I hear thy loud lament,   65  
From bleak Helvetia’s icy caverns sent —  
I hear thy groans upon her [[motifs/blood|blood]]-stained streams!  
Heroes, that for your peaceful country perished,  
And ye that, fleeing, spot your mountain-snows  
With bleeding wounds; forgive me, that I cherished   70  
One thought that ever blessed your cruel foes!  
To scatter rage, and traitorous [[concepts/guilt|guilt]],  
Where Peace her jealous [[motifs/home|home]] had built;  
A patriot-race to disinherit  
Of all that made their stormy wilds so dear;   75  
And with inexpiable spirit  
To taint the bloodless [[concepts/freedom|freedom]] of the mountaineer —  
O France, that mockest Heaven, adulterous, blind,  
And patriot only in pernicious toils!  
Are these thy boasts, Champion of human kind?   80  
To mix with Kings in the low lust of sway,  
Yell in the hunt, and share the murderous prey;  
To insult the shrine of Liberty with spoils  
From freemen torn; to tempt and to betray?  
V  
The Sensual and the Dark [[archetypes/rebel|rebel]] in vain,   85  
Slaves by their own compulsion! In mad game  
They burst their manacles and wear the name  
Of [[concepts/freedom|Freedom]], graven on a heavier chain!  
O Liberty! with profitless endeavour  
Have I pursued thee, many a weary hour;   90  
But thou nor swell’st the victor’s strain, nor ever  
Didst breathe thy [[motifs/soul|soul]] in forms of human power.  
Alike from all, howe’er they praise thee,  
(Nor [[motifs/prayer|prayer]], nor boastful name delays thee)  
Alike from Priestcraft’s harpy minions,   95  
And factious Blasphemy’s obscener slaves,  
Thou speedest on thy subtle pinions,  
The guide of homeless winds, and playmate of the waves!  
And there I felt thee! — on that sea-cliff’s verge,  
Whose pines, scarce travelled by the breeze above,   100  
Had made one murmur with the distant surge!  
Yes, while I stood and gazed, my temples bare,  
And shot my being through earth, sea, and air,  
Possessing all things with intensest [[motifs/love|love]],  
O Liberty! my spirit felt thee there.   105  
February, 1798.

APRIL 20, 1798. THE [[motifs/nightingale|NIGHTINGALE]]: A [[forms/conversation-poem|CONVERSATION POEM]], APRIL, 1798  
No cloud, no relique of the sunken day  
Distinguishes the West, no long thin slip  
Of sullen light, no obscure trembling hues.  
Come, we will rest on this old mossy bridge!  
You see the glimmer of the stream beneath,   5  
But hear no murmuring: it flows silently,  
O’er its soft bed of verdure. All is still,  
A balmy [[motifs/night|night]]! and though the [[motifs/stars|stars]] be dim,  
Yet let us think upon the vernal showers  
That gladden the green earth, and we shall find   10  
A pleasure in the dimness of the [[motifs/stars|stars]].  
And hark! the [[motifs/nightingale|Nightingale]] begins its song,  
‘Most musical, most [[concepts/melancholy|melancholy]]’ bird!  
A [[concepts/melancholy|melancholy]] bird? Oh! idle thought!  
In [[concepts/nature|Nature]] there is nothing [[concepts/melancholy|melancholy]].   15  
But some [[motifs/night|night]]-wandering man whose heart was pierced  
With the remembrance of a grievous wrong,  
Or slow distemper, or neglected [[motifs/love|love]],  
(And so, poor wretch! filled all things with himself,  
And made all gentle sounds tell back the tale   20  
Of his own sorrow) he, and such as he,  
First named these notes a [[concepts/melancholy|melancholy]] strain.  
And many a poet echoes the conceit;  
Poet who hath been building up the rhyme  
When he had better far have stretched his limbs   25  
Beside a brook in mossy forest-dell,  
By sun or [[motifs/moon|moon]]-light, to the influxes  
Of shapes and sounds and shifting elements  
Surrendering his whole spirit, of his song  
And of his fame forgetful! so his fame   30  
Should share in [[concepts/nature|Nature]]’s [[concepts/immortality|immortality]],  
A venerable thing! and so his song  
Should make all [[concepts/nature|Nature]] lovelier, and itself  
Be loved like [[concepts/nature|Nature]]! But ‘twill not be so;  
And youths and maidens most poetical,   35  
Who lose the deepening twilights of the [[motifs/spring|spring]]  
In ball-rooms and hot theatres, they still  
Full of meek sympathy must heave their sighs  
O’er Philomela’s pity-pleading strains.  
My Friend, and thou, our Sister! we have learnt   40  
A different lore: we may not thus profane  
[[concepts/nature|Nature]]’s sweet voices, always full of [[motifs/love|love]]  
And joyance! ‘Tis the merry [[motifs/nightingale|Nightingale]]  
That crowds, and hurries, and precipitates  
With fast thick warble his delicious notes,   45  
As he were fearful that an April [[motifs/night|night]]  
Would be too short for him to utter forth  
His [[motifs/love|love]]-chant, and disburthen his full [[motifs/soul|soul]]  
Of all its music!  
And I know a grove  
Of large extent, hard by a [[settings/castle|castle]] huge,   50  
Which the great lord inhabits not; and so  
This grove is wild with tangling underwood,  
And the trim walks are broken up, and grass,  
Thin grass and [[archetypes/king|king]]-cups grow within the paths.  
But never elsewhere in one place I knew   55  
So many nightingales; and far and near,  
In wood and thicket, over the wide grove,  
They answer and provoke each other’s song,  
With skirmish and capricious passagings,  
And murmurs musical and swift jug jug,   60  
And one low piping sound more sweet than all —  
Stirring the air with such a harmony,  
That should you close your eyes, you might almost  
Forget it was not day! On moonlight bushes,  
Whose dewy leaflets are but half-disclosed.   65  
You may perchance behold them on the twigs,  
Their bright, bright eyes, their eyes both bright and full,  
Glistening, while many a glow-worm in the shade  
Lights up her [[motifs/love|love]]-torch.  
A most gentle Maid,  
Who dwelleth in her hospitable [[motifs/home|home]]    70  
Hard by the [[settings/castle|castle]], and at latest eve  
(Even like a Lady vowed and dedicate  
To something more than [[concepts/nature|Nature]] in the grove)  
Glides through the pathways; she knows all their notes,  
That gentle Maid! and oft, a moment’s space,   75  
What [[motifs/time|time]] the [[motifs/moon|moon]] was lost behind a cloud,  
Hath heard a pause of [[motifs/silence|silence]]; till the [[motifs/moon|moon]]  
Emerging, hath awakened earth and sky  
With one sensation, and those wakeful [[motifs/birds|birds]]  
Have all burst forth in choral minstrelsy,   80  
As if some sudden gale had swept at once  
A hundred airy harps! And she hath watched  
Many a [[motifs/nightingale|nightingale]] perch giddily  
On blossomy twig still swinging from the breeze,  
And to that motion tune his wanton song   85  
Like tipsy Joy that reels with tossing head.  
Farewell, O Warbler! till to-morrow eve,  
And you, my friends! farewell, a short farewell!  
We have been loitering long and pleasantly,  
And now for our dear homes. — That strain again!   90  
Full fain it would delay me! My dear babe,  
Who, capable of no articulate sound,  
Mars all things with his imitative lisp,  
How he would place his hand beside his ear,  
His little hand, the small forefinger up,   95  
And bid us listen! And I deem it wise  
To make him [[concepts/nature|Nature]]’s play-mate. He knows [[motifs/well|well]]  
The evening-star; and once, when he awoke  
In most distressful mood (some inward [[motifs/pain|pain]]  
Had made up that strange thing, an infant’s dream — )   100  
I hurried with him to our orchard-plot,  
And he beheld the [[motifs/moon|moon]], and, hushed at once,  
Suspends his sobs, and laughs most silently,  
While his fair eyes, that swam with undropped [[motifs/tears|tears]],  
Did glitter in the yellow [[motifs/moon|moon]]-beam! [[motifs/well|Well]]! — 105  
It is a father’s tale: But if that Heaven  
Should give me life, his [[motifs/childhood|childhood]] shall grow up  
Familiar with these songs, that with the [[motifs/night|night]]  
He may associate joy. — Once more, farewell,  
Sweet [[motifs/nightingale|Nightingale]]! once more, my friends! farewell.   110  
1798.

20 APRILE 1798. L’[[motifs/nightingale|USIGNOLO]]: [[forms/conversation-poem|POEMETTO DI CONVERSAZIONE]], APRILE 1798  
Nessuna nube, nessuna reliquia del giorno tramontato  
distingue l’Occidente, nessuna lunga sottile striscia  
di luce imbronciata, nessuna oscura tinta tremula.

ma non odi mormorio: scorre silenzioso

una [[motifs/night|notte]] balsamica! e benché le [[motifs/stars|stelle]] siano fioche,  
pensiamo pure agli acquazzoni primaverili  
che rallegrano la verde terra, e troveremo   10  
un piacere nel fioco lume delle [[motifs/stars|stelle]].  
E ascolta! l’[[motifs/nightingale|Usignolo]] comincia il suo canto,  
‘Musicalissimo, [[concepts/melancholy|malinconico]]’ uccello!  
Un uccello [[concepts/melancholy|malinconico]]? Oh! vano pensiero!  
Nella [[concepts/nature|Natura]] non v’è nulla di [[concepts/melancholy|malinconico]].   15  
Ma qualche uomo errante di [[motifs/night|notte]], il cui cuore fu trafitto  
dal ricordo d’un grave torto,  
o da lento malore, o da [[motifs/love|amore]] trascurato,  
(e così, povero infelice, riempì ogni cosa di sé,

del proprio dolore) egli, e quelli come lui,  
chiamarono per primi queste note un canto [[concepts/melancholy|malinconico]].  
E più d’un poeta fa eco al concetto;  
poeta che è andato costruendo la rima

presso un ruscello in muscosa conca boschiva,  
al sole o al lume di [[motifs/moon|luna]], agli influssi  
di forme e suoni ed elementi mutevoli  
arrendendo tutto il suo spirito, del suo canto  
e della sua fama immemore! così la sua fama   30  
dividerebbe l’[[concepts/immortality|immortalità]] della [[concepts/nature|Natura]],  
cosa veneranda! e così il suo canto  
renderebbe più bella tutta la [[concepts/nature|Natura]], e sarebbe  
esso stesso amato come la [[concepts/nature|Natura]]! Ma non sarà così;  
e giovani e fanciulle poeticissimi,   35

e a lenire col [[motifs/silence|silenzio]] ciò che le parole non guariscono,  
vidi solo a metà quella tua mano quieta  
posare sul mio scrittoio questo disegno squisito.  
Il [[motifs/garden|Giardino]] del Boccaccio e la sua magia,   15  
l’[[motifs/love|amore]], la letizia e la galanteria!  
Un Idillio, caldo dello spirito del Boccaccio,  
incorniciato nella silenziosa poesia della forma.  
Come greggi giù per un’erta appena bagnata  
che emergono da una nebbia: o come un fiume   20  
di musica molle che non dissipa il [[motifs/sleep|sonno]],  
ma getta in stampi più felici il sogno di chi dorme,  
sfiorato da un occhio ozioso, con silenziosa potenza  
il quadro s’insinuò nella mia vista interiore.  
Un tepore tremulo strisciò a poco a poco sul mio petto,   25  
come se il dito d’un bimbo mi toccasse il seno.  
E a uno a uno (non so donde) furono recati  
tutti gli spiriti di potenza che più avevano mosso il mio pensiero  
nella fanciullezza senza io, sbalzata in un mondo nuovo  
di [[concepts/wonder|meraviglia]], e persa nelle proprie fantasie;   30  
o che incantarono la mia giovinezza, che, accesa dall’alto,  
amò prima d’amare, e cercò una forma per l’[[motifs/love|amore]];  
o prestarono lustro allo scrutare serio  
della virilità, che medita che cosa e donde sia l’uomo!  
Canto selvaggio degli Scaldi, che nelle caverne rose dal mare   35  
ripetevano il loro incanto di guerra ai venti e alle onde;  
o [[forms/hymn|inno]] fatale di quelle vergini profetiche,  
che invocavano Hertha nelle radure profonde della foresta;  
o lai di menestrello, che rallegrava il banchetto del barone;  
o rima di pompa di [[motifs/city|città]], di monaco e prete,   40  
giudice, sindaco, e molte corporazioni in lunga schiera,  
che sfilano alla cattedrale nel giorno del gran santo:  
e più d’un verso che cantavo a me stesso,  
che destava la lacrima, eppure rubava via la fitta  
di speranze, che lamentandole rinnovavo:   45  
E infine, ora matrona, di contegno sobrio,  
pure ancora raggiante e di splendore non terreno,  
che come [[archetypes/child|fanciulla]] fatata la mia [[motifs/childhood|infanzia]] corteggiò  
fin dall’[[motifs/dawn|alba]] del mio pensiero — la Filosofia;  
benché allora inconsapevole di sé, perdio,   50  
non portava altro nome che Poesia;  
e, come dono dal cielo, in gioia piena di vita,  
appena lasciato il ginocchio d’una madre,  
ciarlava e giocava con uccello e fiore e pietra,  
come con compagni elfici [[motifs/well|ben]] noti,   55  
e la vita rivelata all’innocenza soltanto.  
Grazie, gentile [[archetypes/artist|artista]]! ora posso discernere  
la tua bella creazione con occhio padrone,  
e tutto desto! E ora sto in sguardo fisso,  
ora vago per l’Eden della tua mano;   60  
lodo le verdi arcate, sulla fontana chiara  
vedo ombre a [[forms/fragment|frammenti]] dei cervi che passano;  
e con quella ninfa servizievole mi chino  
ad attingere il cristallo dalla sua pozza inquieta.  
Non vedo più! io stesso sono là,    65  
seggo sull’erba del suolo, e divido il banchetto.  
Sono io che sfioro le corde di quel liuto che echeggia d’[[motifs/love|amore]],  
e contemplo la fanciulla che, contemplando, canta:  
o mi fermo ad ascoltare le [[motifs/bells|campane]] tintinnanti  
dall’alta torre, e penso che là ella dimora.  
Dell’[[motifs/soul|anima]] del vecchio Boccaccio mi sento posseduto,   71  
e respiro un’aria simile a vita, che mi gonfia il petto.  
Fulgore del mondo, o tu un tempo libera,  
e sempre bella, rara terra di cortesia!  
O Firenze! coi campi e i colli toscani   75  
e il famoso Arno, nutrito di tutti i loro rivi;  
tu stella più lucente della lucente d’astri [[settings/italy|Italia]]!  
Ricca, ornata, popolosa, — tuoi tutti i tesori,  
il grano d’oro, l’olivo e la vite.  
Belle città, ville gagliarde, castelli antichi,   80  
e foreste, dove accanto al suo covo frondoso  
il cinghiale arcigno ha udito il corno lontano,  
e affila le zanne contro il pruno nodoso;  
palazzo palladiano con le sue sale istoriate;  
fontane, dove l’[[motifs/love|Amore]] giace ascoltandone le cadute;   85  
giardini, dove il ponte lancia la sua arcata aerea,  
e la [[concepts/nature|Natura]] fa la sua felice [[motifs/home|casa]] con l’uomo;  
dove più d’un fiore splendido è nutrito a dovere  
dal suo proprio rivo, sulla sua propria aiuola trapunta,  
e inghirlanda l’urna di marmo, o china il capo,   90  
piagnona per finta, che a velo scostato  
piange gemme liquide, i doni dell’[[motifs/dawn|alba]]; —  
tuoi tutti i diletti, e ogni musa è tua;  
e più di tutto, l’abbraccio e l’intreccio  
di tutti con tutti in gaia e scintillante [[motifs/dance|danza]]!   95  
Fra dèi di Grecia e guerrieri di romanzo,  
ecco! siede il Boccaccio, dispiegando sulle ginocchia  
il rotolo ritrovato del vecchio Meonide;  
ma dalla piega del mantello, e presso il cuore,  
occhieggia il Sacro Libro d’Ovidio della dolce fitta d’[[motifs/love|Amore]]!   100  
O saggio che tutto godi e tutto fondi,  
a lungo sia mio meditare la tua pagina labirintica,  
dove, mezzo celati, l’occhio della fantasia vede  
fauni, ninfe e santi alati, tutti benigni alla tua musa!  
Ancora nel tuo [[motifs/garden|giardino]] lasciami spiare i loro scherzi,   105  
e vedere, in veste di Diana, fra i filari  
delle viti ordinate, qualche fanciulla che mezzo crede  
ai fuochi vestali, di cui il suo [[archetypes/lover|amante]] si duole,  
con quel satiro furbo che sbircia fra le [[motifs/leaves|foglie]]!  
1828.

For ever in the world of Fame  
We live and yet abide the same:  
Clouds may intercept our rays,  
Or [[motifs/desert|desert]] Lands reflect our blaze.  
The beauteous Month of May began,  
And all was Mirth and Sport,  
When Baron Guelph of Adelstan  
Took leave and left the [[settings/court|Court]].  
From Fête and Rout and Opera far  
The full town he forsook,  
And changed his wand and golden star  
For Shepherd’s [[motifs/crown|Crown]] and Crook.  
The knotted net of light and shade  
Beneath the budding tree,  
A sweeter day-bed for him made  
Than Couch and Canopy.  
In copse or lane, as Choice or Chance  
Might lead him was he seen;  
And join’d at eve the village [[motifs/dance|dance]]  
Upon the village green.  
Nor endless —  
Undated. Now first published from an MS.  


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

Per sempre nel mondo della Fama  
viviamo e pure restiamo gli stessi:  
le nubi possono intercettare i nostri raggi,  
o Terre [[motifs/desert|deserte]] riflettere il nostro fulgore.  
Cominciò il bel Mese di Maggio,  
e tutto era Allegria e Svago,  
quando il Barone Guelph di Adelstan  
prese congedo e lasciò la [[settings/court|Corte]].  
Lontano da Feste e Ricevimenti e Opera  
abbandonò la città affollata,  
e mutò la bacchetta e la stella d’oro  
nella [[motifs/crown|Corona]] e nel Vincastro del Pastore.  
La rete annodata di luce e ombra  
sotto l’albero in gemma  
gli fece giaciglio diurno più dolce  
che Divano e Baldacchino.  
In boschetto o viottolo, secondo che Scelta o Caso  
lo guidasse, era veduto;  
e a sera s’univa alla [[motifs/dance|danza]] del villaggio  
sul prato del villaggio.  
Né senza fine —  
Senza data. Ora pubblicato per la prima volta da un MS.

Erde, du Mutter zahlloser Kinder, Mutter und Amme!  
Sei mir gegrüsst! Sei mir gesegnet im Feiergesange!  
Sieh, O Mutter, hier lieg’ ich an deinen schwellenden Brüsten!  
Lieg’, O Grüngelockte, von deinem wallenden Haupthaar  
Sanft umsäuselt und sanft geküsst von thauenden Lüften!   5  
Ach, du säuselst Wonne mir zu, und thauest mir Wehmuth  
In das Herz, dass Wehmuth und Wonn’ aus schmelzender Seele  
Sich in Thränen und Dank und heiligen Liedern ergiessen!  
Erde, du Mutter zahlloser Kinder, Mutter und Amme!  
Schwester der allesfreuenden Sonne, des freundlichen Mondes   10  
Und der strahlenden Stern’, und flammenbeschweiften Kometen,  
Eine der jüngsten Töchter der allgebärenden Schöpfung,  
Immer blühendes Weib des segenträufelnden Himmels!  
Sprich, O Erde, wie war dir als du am ersten der Tage  
Deinen heiligen Schooss dem buhlenden Himmel enthülltest?   15  
Dein Erröthen war die erste der Morgenröthen,  
Als er im blendenden Bette von weichen schwellenden Wolken  
Deine gürtende Binde mit siegender Stärke dir löste!  
Schauer durchbebten die stille Natur und tausend und tausend  
Leben keimten empor aus der mächtigen Liebesumarmung.   20  
Freudig begrüssten die Fluthen des Meeres neuer Bewohner  
Mannigfaltige Schaaren; es staunte der werdende Wallfisch  
Ueber die steigenden Ströme die seiner Nasen entbrausten;  
Junges Leben durchbrüllte die Auen, die Wälder, die Berge,  
Irrte blökend im Thal, und sang in blühenden Stauden.   25
