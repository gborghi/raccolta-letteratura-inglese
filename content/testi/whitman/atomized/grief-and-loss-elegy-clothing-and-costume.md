_Cluster: CHILDREN OF ADAM_  


Once I pass’d through a populous [[motifs/city|city]] imprinting my brain for future  
use with its shows, architecture, customs, traditions,  
Yet now of all that [[motifs/city|city]] I remember only a woman I casually met  
there who detain’d me for [[motifs/love|love]] of me,  
Day by day and [[motifs/night|night]] by [[motifs/night|night]] we were together--all else has long  
been forgotten by me,  
I remember I say only that woman who passionately clung to me,  
Again we wander, we [[motifs/love|love]], we separate again,  
Again she holds me by the hand, I must not go,  
I see her close beside me with silent lips sad and tremulous.

_Cluster: Figli di Adamo_  


Una volta attraversai una popolosa [[motifs/city|città]], imprimendomi nella mente i suoi spettacoli, la sua architettura, le sue usanze, le sue tradizioni, per poterli utilizzare in futuro.
Ora, di tutta quella [[motifs/city|città]], ricordo solo una donna che incontrai casualmente lì; lei mi tenne con sé per amore.
Giorno dopo giorno, notte dopo notte, eravamo insieme. Tutto il resto è da tempo stato dimenticato.
Ricordo soltanto quella donna che si aggrappò appassionatamente a me.
Di nuovo vaghiamo insieme, ci amiamo. Poi ci separiamo di nuovo.
Di nuovo lei mi prende per mano e dice: “Non devi andare, ”  
La vedo accanto a me, con labbra silenziose, tristi e tremolanti.

_Cluster: CALAMUS_  


Scented herbage of my breast,  
[[motifs/leaves|Leaves]] from you I glean, I write, to be perused best afterwards,  
Tomb-[[motifs/leaves|leaves]], [[concepts/body|body]]-[[motifs/leaves|leaves]] growing up above me above [[motifs/death|death]],  
Perennial roots, tall [[motifs/leaves|leaves]], O the [[motifs/winter|winter]] shall not freeze you  
delicate [[motifs/leaves|leaves]],  
Every year shall you bloom again, out from where you retired you  
shall emerge again;  
O I do not know whether many passing by will discover you or inhale  
your faint odor, but I believe a few will;  
O slender [[motifs/leaves|leaves]]! O blossoms of my [[motifs/blood|blood]]! I permit you to tell in  
your own way of the heart that is under you,  
O I do not know what you mean there underneath yourselves, you are  
not happiness,  
You are often more bitter than I can bear, you burn and sting me,  
Yet you are beautiful to me you faint tinged roots, you make me  
think of [[motifs/death|death]],  
[[motifs/death|Death]] is beautiful from you, (what indeed is finally beautiful  
except [[concepts/death-and-love|death and love]]?)  
O I think it is not for life I am chanting here my chant of lovers,  
I think it must be for [[motifs/death|death]],  
For how calm, how solemn it grows to ascend to the atmosphere of lovers,  
[[motifs/death|Death]] or life I am then indifferent, my [[motifs/soul|soul]] declines to prefer,  
(I am not sure but the high [[motifs/soul|soul]] of lovers welcomes [[motifs/death|death]] most,)  
Indeed O [[motifs/death|death]], I think now these [[motifs/leaves|leaves]] mean precisely the same as  
you mean,  
Grow up taller sweet [[motifs/leaves|leaves]] that I may see! grow up out of my breast!  
[[motifs/spring|Spring]] away from the conceal’d heart there!  
Do not fold yourself so in your pink-tinged roots timid [[motifs/leaves|leaves]]!  
Do not remain down there so ashamed, herbage of my breast!  
Come I am determin’d to unbare this broad breast of mine, I have  
long enough stifled and choked;  
Emblematic and capricious blades I leave you, now you serve me not,  
I will say what I have to say by itself,  
I will sound myself and comrades only, I will never again utter a  
call only their call,  
I will raise with it immortal reverberations through the States,  
I will give an example to lovers to take permanent shape and will  
through the States,  
Through me shall the words be said to make [[motifs/death|death]] exhilarating,  
Give me your tone therefore O [[motifs/death|death]], that I may accord with it,  
Give me yourself, for I see that you belong to me now above all, and  
are folded inseparably together, you [[motifs/love|love]] and [[motifs/death|death]] are,  
Nor will I allow you to balk me any more with what I was calling life,  
For now it is convey’d to me that you are the purports essential,  
That you hide in these shifting forms of life, for reasons, and that  
they are mainly for you,  
That you beyond them come forth to remain, the real reality,  
That behind the mask of materials you patiently wait, no matter how long,  
That you will one day perhaps take control of all,  
That you will perhaps dissipate this entire show of appearance,  
That may-be you are what it is all for, but it does not last so very long,  
But you will last very long.

_Cluster: CALAMUS_  


Erba profumata del mio petto,  
Foglie tue raccoglio e scrivo, affinché possano essere lette più tardi con attenzione,  
Foglie di tomba, foglie di corpo che crescono sopra di me, al di là della morte,  
Radici perenni, alte foglie. Oh, l’inverno non vi congelerà mai,  
Delicate foglie. Ogni anno fiorirete di nuovo, emergendo da dove vi siete ritirate;  
Oh, non so se molti che passano vi scopriranno o respireranno il vostro debole profumo, ma credo che alcuni lo faranno;  
Oh, sottili foglie. Oh fiori del mio sangue. Vi permetto di raccontare a modo vostro ciò che si trova sotto di voi.
Oh, non so cosa significhi ciò che nascondete lì sotto. Non siete felicità. Spesso siete più amarevoli di quanto io possa sopportare; mi bruciate e mi pungono. Eppure per me siete belli. Vostre radici tenue mi fanno pensare alla morte.
La morte è bella attraverso di voi, (Cosa, in fondo, può essere veramente bello se non la morte e l’amore?)  
Oh, credo che il mio canto d’amore non sia destinato alla vita. Ma alla morte.
Perché diventa così calmo, così solenne quando sale nell’atmosfera dell’amore.
Che sia morte o vita, ormai non mi importa più. La mia anima non preferisce nulla, (Non ne sono sicuro. Ma l’anima nobile degli amanti accoglie con gioia la morte, )  
Davvero. Oh morte. Penso che queste foglie significhino esattamente ciò che tu significhi.
Crescite più alte, dolci foglie. Affinché possa vedervi! Crescete dal mio petto. Lasciate che la primavera si allontani da quel cuore nascosto.
Non vi arrotolate timidamente nelle vostre radici rosa. Non rimanete lì, vergognose. Erba del mio petto.
Vienite. Sono deciso a scoprire completamente questo mio petto. Ne ho soffocato e stretto i segreti per troppo tempo.
Lame simboliche e capricciose. Ora non mi servite più. Dirò ciò che devo dire da solo. Farò risuonare le mie parole, soltanto per me stesso e i miei compagni. Non pronuncerò mai più un richiamo destinato soltanto a loro.
Farò risuonare queste parole attraverso tutti gli Stati. Darò un esempio agli amanti affinché prendano forma permanente. Attraverso di me, queste parole renderanno la morte emozionante.
Dammi quindi il tuo tono, oh morte. Affinché possa accordarmici. Dammi te stessa. Perché ora vedo che appartieni a me più di ogni altra cosa. E che sei inseparabilmente legata a me. Tu, amore. E tu, morte.
Non permetterò più che mi distraggiate con ciò che chiamavo vita. Perché ora so che voi siete lo scopo essenziale. Che vi nascondete in queste forme mutevoli della vita. Per motivi specifici. E che queste forme esistono principalmente per voi.
Che, al di là di esse, voi emergete per rimanere. La vera realtà.
Che dietro la maschera dei materiali tu aspetti pazientemente, per quanto tempo ci voglia,  
Che un giorno forse prenderai il controllo di tutto,  
Che forse dissiperai tutta questa messinscena di apparenze,  
Che forse sei proprio tu lo scopo di tutto questo, ma non dura molto a lungo,  
Tuttavia, tu durerai molto a lungo.



<span class="atom-split" data-atom="084_crossing_brooklyn_ferry" data-title="Crossing Brooklyn Ferry" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="topos/immortality_through_verse,motif/the_city,motif/river,concept/memory_and_time,concept/comradeship,concept/self_and_soul,form/free_verse,setting/the_modern_metropolis,cluster/grief_and_loss,cluster/elegy,cluster/clothing_and_costume"></span>



1  
Flood-tide below me! I see you face to face!  
Clouds of the west--sun there half an hour high--I see you also face  
to face.  

Crowds of men and women attired in the usual costumes, how curious  
you are to me!  
On the ferry-boats the hundreds and hundreds that [[motifs/cross|cross]], returning  
[[motifs/home|home]], are more curious to me than you suppose,  
And you that shall [[motifs/cross|cross]] from shore to shore years hence are more  
to me, and more in my meditations, than you might suppose.  

2  
The impalpable sustenance of me from all things at all hours of the day,  
The simple, compact, [[motifs/well|well]]-join’d scheme, myself disintegrated, every  
one disintegrated yet part of the scheme,  
The similitudes of the past and those of the future,  
The glories strung like beads on my smallest sights and hearings, on  
the walk in the street and the passage over the [[motifs/river|river]],  
The current rushing so swiftly and swimming with me far away,  
The others that are to follow me, the ties between me and them,  
The certainty of others, the life, [[motifs/love|love]], sight, hearing of others.  

Others will enter the gates of the ferry and [[motifs/cross|cross]] from shore to shore,  
Others will watch the run of the flood-tide,  
Others will see the shipping of [[settings/manhattan|Manhattan]] north and west, and the  
heights of Brooklyn to the south and east,  
Others will see the islands large and small;  
Fifty years hence, others will see them as they [[motifs/cross|cross]], the sun half  
an hour high,  
A hundred years hence, or ever so many hundred years hence, others  
will see them,  
Will enjoy the sunset, the pouring-in of the flood-tide, the  
falling-back to the sea of the ebb-tide.  

3  
It avails not, [[motifs/time|time]] nor place--distance avails not,  
I am with you, you men and women of a generation, or ever so many  
generations hence,  
Just as you feel when you look on the [[motifs/river|river]] and sky, so I felt,  
Just as any of you is one of a living crowd, I was one of a crowd,  
Just as you are refresh’d by the gladness of the [[motifs/river|river]] and the  
bright flow, I was refresh’d,  
Just as you stand and lean on the rail, yet hurry with the swift  
current, I stood yet was hurried,  
Just as you look on the numberless masts of ships and the  
thick-stemm’d pipes of steamboats, I look’d.  

I too many and many a [[motifs/time|time]] [[motifs/cross|cross]]’d the [[motifs/river|river]] of old,  
Watched the Twelfth-month sea-gulls, saw them high in the air  
floating with motionless wings, oscillating their bodies,  
Saw how the glistening yellow lit up parts of their bodies and left  
the rest in strong [[archetypes/shadow|shadow]],  
Saw the slow-wheeling circles and the gradual edging toward the south,  
Saw the reflection of the [[motifs/summer|summer]] sky in the [[motifs/water|water]],  
Had my eyes dazzled by the shimmering track of beams,  
Look’d at the fine centrifugal spokes of light round the shape of my  
head in the sunlit [[motifs/water|water]],  
Look’d on the haze on the hills southward and south-westward,  
Look’d on the vapor as it flew in fleeces tinged with violet,  
Look’d toward the lower bay to notice the vessels arriving,  
Saw their approach, saw aboard those that were near me,  
Saw the white sails of schooners and sloops, saw the ships at anchor,  
The sailors at work in the rigging or out astride the spars,  
The round masts, the swinging motion of the hulls, the slender  
serpentine pennants,  
The large and small steamers in motion, the pilots in their pilothouses,  
The white wake left by the passage, the quick tremulous whirl of the wheels,  
The flags of all nations, the falling of them at sunset,  
The scallop-edged waves in the twilight, the ladled cups, the  
frolic-some crests and glistening,  
The stretch afar growing dimmer and dimmer, the gray walls of the  
granite storehouses by the docks,  
On the [[motifs/river|river]] the shadowy group, the big steam-tug closely flank’d on  
each side by the barges, the hay-boat, the belated lighter,  
On the neighboring shore the fires from the foundry chimneys burning  
high and glaringly into the [[motifs/night|night]],  
Casting their flicker of black contrasted with wild red and yellow  
light over the tops of houses, and down into the clefts of streets.  

4  
These and all else were to me the same as they are to you,  
I loved [[motifs/well|well]] those cities, loved [[motifs/well|well]] the stately and rapid [[motifs/river|river]],  
The men and women I saw were all near to me,  
Others the same--others who look back on me because I look’d forward  
to them,  
(The [[motifs/time|time]] will come, though I stop here to-day and to-[[motifs/night|night]].)  

5  
What is it then between us?  
What is the count of the scores or hundreds of years between us?  

Whatever it is, it avails not--distance avails not, and place avails not,  
I too lived, Brooklyn of ample hills was mine,  
I too walk’d the streets of [[settings/manhattan|Manhattan]] island, and bathed in the  
waters around it,  
I too felt the curious abrupt questionings stir within me,  
In the day among crowds of people sometimes they came upon me,  
In my walks [[motifs/home|home]] late at [[motifs/night|night]] or as I lay in my bed they came upon me,  
I too had been struck from the float forever held in solution,  
I too had receiv’d [[concepts/identity|identity]] by my [[concepts/body|body]],  
That I was I knew was of my [[concepts/body|body]], and what I should be I knew I  
should be of my [[concepts/body|body]].  

6  
It is not upon you alone the dark patches fall,  
The dark threw its patches down upon me also,  
The best I had done seem’d to me blank and suspicious,  
My great thoughts as I supposed them, were they not in reality meagre?  
Nor is it you alone who know what it is to be evil,  
I am he who knew what it was to be evil,  
I too knitted the old knot of contrariety,  
Blabb’d, blush’d, resented, lied, stole, grudg’d,  
Had guile, anger, lust, hot wishes I dared not speak,  
Was wayward, vain, greedy, shallow, sly, cowardly, malignant,  
The wolf, the snake, the hog, not wanting in me.  
The cheating look, the frivolous word, the adulterous wish, not wanting,  

Refusals, hates, postponements, meanness, laziness, none of these wanting,  
Was one with the rest, the days and haps of the rest,  
Was call’d by my nighest name by clear loud voices of young men as  
they saw me approaching or passing,  
Felt their arms on my neck as I stood, or the negligent leaning of  
their flesh against me as I sat,  
Saw many I loved in the street or ferry-boat or public assembly, yet  
never told them a word,  
Lived the same life with the rest, the same old laughing, gnawing, sleeping,  
Play’d the part that still looks back on the actor or actress,  
The same old role, the role that is what we make it, as great as we like,  
Or as small as we like, or both great and small.  

7  
Closer yet I approach you,  
What thought you have of me now, I had as much of you--I laid in my  
stores in advance,  
I consider’d long and seriously of you before you were born.  

Who was to know what should come [[motifs/home|home]] to me?  
Who knows but I am enjoying this?  
Who knows, for all the distance, but I am as good as looking at you  
now, for all you cannot see me?  

8  
Ah, what can ever be more stately and admirable to me than  
mast-hemm’d [[settings/manhattan|Manhattan]]?  
[[motifs/river|River]] and sunset and scallop-edg’d waves of flood-tide?  
The sea-gulls oscillating their bodies, the hay-boat in the  
twilight, and the belated lighter?  
What gods can exceed these that clasp me by the hand, and with voices I  
[[motifs/love|love]] call me promptly and loudly by my nighest name as approach?  
What is more subtle than this which ties me to the woman or man that  
looks in my face?  
Which fuses me into you now, and pours my meaning into you?  

We understand then do we not?  
What I promis’d without mentioning it, have you not accepted?  
What the study could not teach--what the preaching could not  
accomplish is accomplish’d, is it not?  

9  
Flow on, [[motifs/river|river]]! flow with the flood-tide, and ebb with the ebb-tide!  
Frolic on, crested and scallop-edg’d waves!  
Gorgeous clouds of the sunset! drench with your splendor me, or the  
men and women generations after me!  
[[motifs/cross|Cross]] from shore to shore, countless crowds of passengers!  
Stand up, tall masts of Mannahatta! stand up, beautiful hills of Brooklyn!  
Throb, baffled and curious brain! throw out questions and answers!  
Suspend here and everywhere, eternal float of solution!  
Gaze, loving and thirsting eyes, in the house or street or public assembly!  
Sound out, voices of young men! loudly and musically call me by my  
nighest name!  
Live, old life! play the part that looks back on the actor or actress!  
Play the old role, the role that is great or small according as one  
makes it!  
Consider, you who peruse me, whether I may not in unknown ways be  
looking upon you;  
Be firm, rail over the [[motifs/river|river]], to support those who lean idly, yet  
haste with the hasting current;  
Fly on, sea-[[motifs/birds|birds]]! fly sideways, or [[motifs/wheel|wheel]] in large circles high in the air;  
Receive the [[motifs/summer|summer]] sky, you [[motifs/water|water]], and faithfully hold it till all  
downcast eyes have [[motifs/time|time]] to take it from you!  
Diverge, fine spokes of light, from the shape of my head, or any  
one’s head, in the sunlit [[motifs/water|water]]!  
Come on, ships from the lower bay! pass up or down, white-sail’d  
schooners, sloops, lighters!  
Flaunt away, flags of all nations! be duly lower’d at sunset!  
Burn high your fires, foundry chimneys! cast black shadows at  
nightfall! cast red and yellow light over the tops of the houses!  
Appearances, now or henceforth, indicate what you are,  
You necessary film, continue to envelop the [[motifs/soul|soul]],  
About my [[concepts/body|body]] for me, and your [[concepts/body|body]] for you, be hung our divinest aromas,  
Thrive, cities--bring your freight, bring your shows, ample and  
sufficient rivers,  
Expand, being than which none else is perhaps more spiritual,  
Keep your places, objects than which none else is more lasting.  

You have waited, you always wait, you dumb, beautiful ministers,  
We receive you with free sense at last, and are insatiate henceforward,  
Not you any more shall be able to foil us, or withhold yourselves from us,  
We use you, and do not cast you aside--we plant you permanently within us,  
We fathom you not--we [[motifs/love|love]] you--there is perfection in you also,  
You furnish your parts toward [[concepts/eternity|eternity]],  
Great or small, you furnish your parts toward the [[motifs/soul|soul]].

1  
La marea in piena sotto di me. Ti vedo davanti a me, faccia a faccia!  
Le nuvole dell’ovest. Il sole è già alto, a metà cielo. Anche tu sei lì, faccia a faccia con me.  

Crowds di uomini e donne vestiti con i soliti costumi, quanto siete curiosi per me!  
Sui traghetti, quelle centinaia di persone che [[motifs/cross|attraversano]] il fiume, tornando [[motifs/home|a casa]], sono ancora più curiose per me di quanto possiate immaginare.
E voi, che tra anni attraverserete il fiume da una riva all’altra, siete ancora più importanti per me, e occupate maggiormente i miei pensieri, di quanto possiate credere.  

2  
La sostanza intangibile che mi deriva da tutte le cose, in ogni momento della giornata;  
Il disegno semplice, compatto e ben congegnato: io stesso mi dissolvo, ma ognuno di questi elementi rimane comunque parte di quel disegno;  
Le somiglianze tra il passato e il futuro;  
Le meraviglie che si susseguono, come perle disposte lungo le mie più piccole esperienze visive e uditive – durante una passeggiata in strada o mentre attraverso un [[motifs/river|fiume]];  
Il flusso che scorre velocemente, portandomi lontano con sé;  
Gli altri che seguiranno me, i legami che mi uniscono a loro;  
La certezza dell’esistenza degli altri, la loro vita, il loro [[motifs/love|amore]], le loro esperienze visive e uditive.  

Alcuni entreranno attraverso i porti del traghetto e [[motifs/cross|attraverseranno]] da una riva all'altra;  
altri osserveranno il flusso della marea alta;  
altri vedranno le navi che navigano verso nord e ovest, lungo la costa di [[settings/manhattan|Manhattan]], e le altezze di Brooklyn a sud e est;  
altri ancora ammireranno le isole, grandi e piccole.  
Cinquanta anni da ora, altri le vedranno mentre attraversano il fiume, con il sole già alto circa mezz’ora nel cielo;  
cento anni da ora, o anche molti altri cento anni dopo, altri ancora le vedranno.
E godranno del tramonto, del flusso della marea alta, e del ritiro della marea bassa verso il mare.  

3  
Non conta il [[motifs/time|tempo]] né il luogo; non conta nemmeno la distanza.
Sono con voi, voi uomini e donne di questa generazione, o anche di molte generazioni a venire.  
Esattamente come voi provate quando guardate il [[motifs/river|fiume]] e il cielo, così ho provato io;  
esattamente come uno qualsiasi di voi fa parte di una folla viva, anch’io facevo parte di una folla;  
esattamente come voi vi rinfrescate alla gioia del [[motifs/river|fiume]] e al suo corso luminoso, anch’io mi rinfrescavo.
Esattamente come voi state in piedi appoggiati al parapetto, ma siete spinti dalla corrente veloce, anch’io stavo lì, ma ero spinto dalla corrente.  
Esattamente come voi guardate i numerosi alberi maestri delle navi e le ciminiere fumanti dei piroscafi, anch’io guardo.  

Molte e troppe volte ho attraversato quel vecchio [[motifs/river|fiume]],  
ho osservato le gabbiani del dodicesimo mese, li ho visti volare in alto,  
con ali immobili e corpi che oscillavano;  
ho visto il loro corpo illuminarsi di un giallo scintillante, mentre il resto rimaneva avvolto nell’ombra;  
ho visto i loro cerchi lenti e il movimento graduale verso sud;  
ho visto il riflesso del cielo estivo sull’acqua;  
i raggi di luce che si diffondevano intorno alla mia testa, immersa nell’acqua solare;  
la nebbia sulle colline a sud e a sud-ovest;  
il vapore che si disperdeva in filamenti color viola;  
i velivoli che arrivavano nella baia; le vele bianche delle imbarcazioni, i bastimenti all’ancora;  
i marinai al lavoro sul sartiame o in piedi sui pennoni;  
gli alberi rotondi, il movimento ondulato delle imbarcazioni, le bandiere svolazzanti;  
i grandi e piccoli vapori in movimento, i piloti nelle loro cabine;  
la scia bianca lasciata dalle imbarcazioni, il vortice rapido delle eliche;  
le bandiere di tutte le nazioni, che si abbassavano al tramonto;  
le onde increspate nel crepuscolo, le creste schiumose e i riflessi scintillanti sull’acqua.
Il paesaggio in lontananza diventava sempre più sfocato; i muri grigi dei magazzini di granito lungo il porto;  
sul fiume, il gruppo di imbarcazioni avvolte nell’ombra, il grande rimorchiatore a vapore fiancheggiato da chiatte, barche del carico e altri velivoli in ritardo;  
sulla riva vicina, i fuochi delle ciminiere della fonderia che bruciavano alta, illuminando la notte con luci rosse e gialle;

Dall’immensità dell’Ovest, dalla Virginia, dalle Caroline, dal Texas.
Anche qui, nella penombra della mia stanza, al suono delle fiamme tremolanti,  
rivedo quelle file di soldati che avanzavano, sento il ritmo cadenzato dei loro passi.

Una poesia dedicata a voi, un riconoscimento per un dovere troppo a lungo trascurato.
