IV. [[Death]] by [[Water]]  
Phlebas the Phoenician, a fortnight dead,  
Forgot the cry of gulls, and the deep sea swell  
And the profit and loss.  
                                               A current under sea  
Picked his bones in whispers. As he [[Rose|rose]] and fell  
He passed the stages of his age and youth  
Entering the whirlpool.  
                                               Gentile or Jew  
320 O you who turn the [[Wheel|wheel]] and look to windward,  


Consider Phlebas, who was once handsome and tall as you.  

V. What the Thunder said  
    After the torchlight red on sweaty faces  
After the frosty [[Silence|silence]] in the gardens  
After the agony in stony places  
The shouting and the crying  
[[Prison]] and palace and reverberation  
Of thunder of [[Spring|spring]] over distant [[Mountains|mountains]]  
He who was living is now dead  
We who were living are now dying  
330 With a little patience  


    Here is no [[Water|water]] but only rock  
Rock and no [[Water|water]] and the sandy [[Road|road]]  
The [[Road|road]] winding above among the [[Mountains|mountains]]  
Which are [[Mountains|mountains]] of rock without [[Water|water]]  
If there were [[Water|water]] we should stop and drink  
Amongst the rock one cannot stop or think  
Sweat is dry and feet are in the sand  
If there were only [[Water|water]] amongst the rock  
Dead mountain mouth of carious teeth that cannot spit  
340 Here one can neither stand nor lie nor sit  


There is not even [[Silence|silence]] in the [[Mountains|mountains]]  
But dry sterile thunder without rain  
There is not even [[Solitude|solitude]] in the [[Mountains|mountains]]  
But red sullen faces sneer and snarl  
From doors of mudcracked houses  
                                                 If there were [[Water|water]]  
    And no rock  
    If there were rock  
    And also [[Water|water]]  
350    And [[Water|water]]  


    A [[Spring|spring]]  
    A pool among the rock  
    If there were the sound of [[Water|water]] only  
    Not the cicada  
    And dry grass singing  
    But sound of [[Water|water]] over a rock  
    Where the hermit-thrush sings in the pine trees  
    Drip drop drip drop drop drop drop  
    But there is no [[Water|water]]  
360    Who is the third who walks always beside you?  


When I count, there are only you and I together  
But when I look ahead up the white [[Road|road]]  
There is always another one walking beside you  
Gliding wrapt in a brown mantle, hooded  
I do not know whether a man or a woman  
— But who is that on the other side of you?  
    What is that sound high in the air  
Murmur of maternal lamentation  
Who are those hooded hordes swarming  
370 Over endless plains, stumbling in cracked earth  


Ringed by the flat horizon only  
What is the [[City|city]] over the [[Mountains|mountains]]  
Cracks and reforms and bursts in the violet air  
Falling towers  
Jerusalem Athens Alexandria  
Vienna [[London]]  
Unreal  
    A woman drew her long black hair out tight  
And fiddled whisper music on those strings  
380 And bats with baby faces in the violet light  


Whistled, and beat their wings  
And crawled head downward down a blackened wall  
And upside down in air were towers  
Tolling reminiscent [[Bells|bells]], that kept the hours  
And voices singing out of empty cisterns and exhausted wells.  
    In this decayed hole among the [[Mountains|mountains]]  
In the faint moonlight, the grass is singing  
Over the tumbled graves, about the chapel  
There is the empty chapel, only the [[Wind|wind]]’s [[Home|home]].  
390 It has no windows, and the door swings,  


Dry bones can harm no one.  
Only a cock stood on the rooftree  
Co co rico co co rico  
In a flash of [[Lightning|lightning]]. Then a damp gust  
Bringing rain  
    Ganga was sunken, and the limp [[Leaves|leaves]]  
Waited for rain, while the black clouds  
Gathered far distant, over Himavant.  
The jungle crouched, humped in [[Silence|silence]].  
400 Then spoke the thunder  


DA  
Datta: what have we given?  


My friend, [[Blood|blood]] shaking my heart  
The awful daring of a moment’s surrender  
Which an age of prudence can never retract  
By this, and this only, we have existed  
Which is not to be found in our obituaries  
Or in memories draped by the beneficent spider  
Or under seals broken by the lean solicitor  
410 In our empty rooms  


DA  
Dayadhvam: I have heard the key  


Turn in the door once and turn once only  
We think of the key, each in his [[Prison|prison]]  
Thinking of the key, each confirms a [[Prison|prison]]  
Only at nightfall, aethereal rumours  
Revive for a moment a broken [[Coriolanus]]
