I. The Burial of the Dead  
    April is the cruellest month, breeding  
Lilacs out of the dead land, mixing  
[[Memory]] and [[Desire|desire]], stirring  
Dull roots with [[Spring|spring]] rain.  
[[Winter]] kept us warm, covering  
Earth in forgetful [[Snow|snow]], feeding  
A little life with dried tubers.  
[[Summer]] surprised us, coming over the Starnbergersee  
With a shower of rain; we stopped in the colonnade,  
10 And went on in sunlight, into the Hofgarten,  


And drank coffee, and talked for an hour.  
Bin gar keine Russin, stamm’ aus Litauen, echt deutsch.  
And when we were children, staying at the archduke’s,  
My cousin’s, he took me out on a sled.  
And I was frightened. He said, Marie,  
Marie, hold on tight. And down we went.  
In the [[Mountains|mountains]], there you feel free.  
I read, much of the [[Night|night]], and go south in the [[Winter|winter]].  
    What are the roots that clutch, what branches grow  
20 Out of this stony rubbish? Son of man,  


You cannot say, or guess, for you know only  
A heap of broken images, where the sun beats,  
And the dead tree gives no shelter, the cricket no relief,  
And the dry stone no sound of [[Water|water]]. Only  
There is [[Shadow|shadow]] under this red rock,  
(Come in under the [[Shadow|shadow]] of this red rock),  
And I will show you something different from either  
Your [[Shadow|shadow]] at morning striding behind you  
Or your [[Shadow|shadow]] at evening rising to meet you;  
30 I will show you [[Fear|fear]] in a handful of [[Dust|dust]].

    [[Madame Sosostris]], famous clairvoyante,  
Had a bad cold, nevertheless  
Is known to be the wisest woman in Europe,  
With a wicked pack of cards. Here, said she,  
Is your card, the drowned Phoenician Sailor,  
(Those are pearls that were his eyes. Look!)

Which is blank, is something he carries on his back,  
Which I am forbidden to see. I do not find  
The Hanged Man. [[Fear]] [[Death|death]] by [[Water|water]].  
I see crowds of people, walking round in a ring.  
Thank you. If you see dear Mrs. Equitone,  
Tell her I bring the horoscope myself:  
One must be so careful these days.  
60    Unreal [[City]],  


Under the brown fog of a [[Winter|winter]] [[Dawn|dawn]],  
A crowd flowed over [[London]] Bridge, so many,  
I had not thought [[Death|death]] had undone so many.  
Sighs, short and infrequent, were exhaled,  
And each man fixed his eyes before his feet.  
Flowed up the [[Hill|hill]] and down [[King]] William Street,  
To where Saint Mary Woolnoth kept the hours  
With a dead sound on the final stroke of nine.  
There I saw one I knew, and stopped him, crying: ‘Stetson!  
70 ‘You who were with me in the ships at Mylae!  
‘That corpse you planted last year in your [[Garden|garden]],  
‘Has it begun to sprout? Will it bloom this year?  
‘Or has the sudden [[Frost|frost]] disturbed its bed?  
‘O keep the Dog far hence, that’s friend to men,  
‘Or with his nails he’ll dig it up again!  
‘You! hypocrite lecteur! — mon semblable, — mon frère!’  

II. A Game of Chess  
    The Chair she sat in, like a burnished throne,  
Glowed on the marble, where the glass  
Held up by standards wrought with fruited vines  
80 From which a golden Cupidon peeped out  


(Another hid his eyes behind his wing)  
Doubled the flames of sevenbranched candelabra  
Reflecting light upon the table as  
The glitter of her jewels [[Rose|rose]] to meet it,  
From satin cases poured in rich profusion.  
In vials of ivory and coloured glass  
Unstoppered, lurked her strange synthetic perfumes,  
Unguent, powdered, or liquid — troubled, confused  
And drowned the sense in odours; stirred by the air  
90 That freshened from the [[Window|window]], these ascended  


In fattening the prolonged candle-flames,  
Flung their smoke into the laquearia,  
Stirring the pattern on the coffered ceiling.  
Huge sea-wood fed with copper  
Burned green and orange, framed by the coloured stone,  
In which sad light a carvèd dolphin swam.  
Above the antique mantel was displayed  
As though a [[Window|window]] gave upon the sylvan scene  
The change of Philomel, by the barbarous [[King|king]]  
100 So rudely forced; yet there the [[Nightingale|nightingale]]  


Filled all the [[Desert|desert]] with inviolable voice  
And still she cried, and still the world pursues,  
‘Jug Jug’ to dirty ears.  
And other withered stumps of [[Time|time]]  
Were told upon the walls; staring forms  
Leaned out, leaning, hushing the room enclosed.  
Footsteps shuffled on the stair.  
Under the firelight, under the brush, her hair  
Spread out in fiery points  
110 Glowed into words, then would be savagely still.
