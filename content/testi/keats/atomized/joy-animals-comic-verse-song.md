OR,  

THE POT OF BASIL.  

A STORY FROM BOCCACCIO.  

Fair Isabel, poor simple Isabel!  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], a young palmer in [[motifs/love|Love]]’s eye!  
They could not in the [[concepts/self|self]]-same mansion dwell  
Without some stir of heart, some malady;  
They could not sit at meals but feel how [[motifs/well|well]]  
It soothed each to be the other by;  
They could not, sure, beneath the same roof [[motifs/sleep|sleep]]  
But to each other dream, and nightly weep.  

With every morn their [[motifs/love|love]] grew tenderer,  
With every eve deeper and tenderer still;  10  
He might not in house, field, or [[motifs/garden|garden]] stir,  
But her full shape would all his seeing fill;  
And his continual voice was pleasanter  
To her, than noise of trees or hidden rill;  
Her lute-string gave an echo of his name,  
She spoilt her half-done broidery with the same.  

He knew whose gentle hand was at the latch,  
Before the door had given her to his eyes;  
And from her chamber-[[motifs/window|window]] he would catch  
Her [[concepts/beauty|beauty]] farther than the falcon spies;  20  
And constant as her vespers would he watch,  
Because her face was turn’d to the same skies;  
And with sick [[concepts/longing|longing]] all the [[motifs/night|night]] outwear,  
To hear her morning-step upon the stair.  

A whole long month of May in this sad plight  
Made their cheeks paler by the break of June:  
“To-morrow will I bow to my delight,  
To-morrow will I ask my lady’s boon.”--  
“O may I never see another [[motifs/night|night]],  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], if thy lips breathe not [[motifs/love|love]]’s tune.”--  30  
So spake they to their pillows; but, alas,  
Honeyless days and days did he let pass;  

Until sweet [[characters/isabella|Isabella]]’s untouch’d cheek  
Fell sick within the [[motifs/rose|rose]]’s just domain,  
Fell thin as a young mother’s, who doth seek  
By every lull to cool her infant’s [[motifs/pain|pain]]:  
“How ill she is,” said he, “I may not speak,  
And yet I will, and tell my [[motifs/love|love]] all plain:  
If looks speak [[motifs/love|love]]-laws, I will drink her [[motifs/tears|tears]],  
And at the least ‘twill startle off her cares.” 40  

So said he one fair morning, and all day  
His heart beat awfully against his side;  
And to his heart he inwardly did pray  
For power to speak; but still the ruddy tide  
Stifled his voice, and puls’d resolve away--  
Fever’d his high conceit of such a bride,  
Yet brought him to the meekness of a [[archetypes/child|child]]:  
Alas! when passion is both meek and wild!  

So once more he had wak’d and anguished  
A dreary [[motifs/night|night]] of [[motifs/love|love]] and misery,  50  
If Isabel’s quick eye had not been wed  
To every symbol on his forehead high;  
She saw it waxing very pale and dead,  
And straight all flush’d; so, lisped tenderly,  
“[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]]!”--here she ceas’d her timid [[topoi/quest|quest]],  
But in her tone and look he read the rest.  

“O [[characters/isabella|Isabella]], I can half perceive  
That I may speak my grief into thine ear;  
If thou didst ever any thing believe,  
Believe how I [[motifs/love|love]] thee, believe how near  60  
My [[motifs/soul|soul]] is to its doom: I would not grieve  
Thy hand by unwelcome pressing, would not [[concepts/fear|fear]]  
Thine eyes by gazing; but I cannot live  
Another [[motifs/night|night]], and not my passion shrive.  

“[[motifs/love|Love]]! thou art leading me from wintry cold,  
Lady! thou leadest me to [[motifs/summer|summer]] clime,  
And I must taste the blossoms that unfold  
In its ripe warmth this gracious morning [[motifs/time|time]].”  
So said, his erewhile timid lips grew bold,  
And poesied with hers in dewy rhyme:  70  
Great bliss was with them, and great happiness  
Grew, like a lusty flower in June’s caress.  

Parting they seem’d to tread upon the air,  
Twin roses by the zephyr blown apart  
Only to meet again more close, and share  
The inward fragrance of each other’s heart.  
She, to her chamber gone, a ditty fair  
Sang, of delicious [[motifs/love|love]] and honey’d dart;  
He with light steps went up a western [[motifs/hill|hill]],  
And bade the sun farewell, and joy’d his fill.  80  

All close they met again, before the dusk  
Had taken from the [[motifs/stars|stars]] its pleasant veil,  
All close they met, all eyes, before the dusk  
Had taken from the [[motifs/stars|stars]] its pleasant veil,  
Close in a bower of hyacinth and musk,  
Unknown of any, free from whispering tale.  
Ah! better had it been for ever so,  
Than idle ears should pleasure in their woe.  

Were they unhappy then?--It cannot be--  
Too many [[motifs/tears|tears]] for lovers have been shed,  90  
Too many sighs give we to them in fee,  
Too much of pity after they are dead,  
Too many doleful stories do we see,  
Whose matter in bright gold were best be read;  
Except in such a page where [[characters/theseus|Theseus]]’ spouse  
Over the pathless waves towards him bows.  

But, for the general award of [[motifs/love|love]],  
The little sweet doth kill much bitterness;  
Though Dido silent is in under-grove,  
And [[characters/isabella|Isabella]]’s was a great distress,  100  
Though young [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] in warm Indian clove  
Was not embalm’d, this [[concepts/truth|truth]] is not the less--  
Even [[motifs/bees|bees]], the little almsmen of [[motifs/spring|spring]]-bowers,  
Know there is richest juice in [[motifs/poison|poison]]-[[motifs/flowers|flowers]].  

With her two brothers this fair lady dwelt,  
Enriched from ancestral merchandize,  
And for them many a weary hand did swelt  
In torched mines and noisy factories,  
And many once proud-quiver’d loins did melt  
In [[motifs/blood|blood]] from stinging whip;--with hollow eyes  110  
Many all day in dazzling [[motifs/river|river]] stood,  
To take the rich-ored driftings of the flood.  

For them the Ceylon diver held his breath,  
And went all naked to the hungry shark;  
For them his ears gush’d [[motifs/blood|blood]]; for them in [[motifs/death|death]]  
The seal on the cold ice with piteous bark  
Lay full of darts; for them alone did seethe  
A thousand men in troubles wide and dark:  
Half-ignorant, they turn’d an easy [[motifs/wheel|wheel]],  
That set sharp racks at work, to pinch and peel.  120  

Why were they proud? Because their marble founts  
Gush’d with more [[concepts/pride|pride]] than do a wretch’s [[motifs/tears|tears]]?--  
Why were they proud? Because fair orange-mounts  
Were of more soft ascent than lazar stairs?--  
Why were they proud? Because red-lin’d accounts  
Were richer than the songs of Grecian years?--  
Why were they proud? again we ask aloud,  
Why in the name of Glory were they proud?  

Yet were these Florentines as [[concepts/self|self]]-retired  
In hungry [[concepts/pride|pride]] and gainful cowardice,  130  
As two close Hebrews in that land inspired,  
Paled in and vineyarded from beggar-spies;  
The hawks of [[motifs/ship|ship]]-mast forests--the untired  
And pannier’d mules for ducats and old lies--  
Quick cat’s-paws on the generous stray-away,--  
Great wits in Spanish, Tuscan, and Malay.  

How was it these same ledger-men could spy  
Fair [[characters/isabella|Isabella]] in her downy nest?  
How could they find out in [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]]’s eye  
A straying from his toil? Hot [[settings/egypt|Egypt]]’s pest  140  
Into their vision covetous and sly!  
How could these money-bags see east and west?--  
Yet so they did--and every dealer fair  
Must see behind, as doth the hunted hare.  

O eloquent and famed Boccaccio!  
Of thee we now should ask forgiving boon;  
And of thy spicy myrtles as they blow,  
And of thy roses amorous of the [[motifs/moon|moon]],  
And of thy lilies, that do paler grow  
Now they can no more hear thy ghittern’s tune,  150  
For venturing syllables that ill beseem  
The quiet glooms of such a piteous theme.  

Grant thou a pardon here, and then the tale  
Shall move on soberly, as it is meet;  
There is no other crime, no mad assail  
To make old prose in modern rhyme more sweet:  
But it is done--succeed the verse or fail--  
To [[concepts/honour|honour]] thee, and thy gone spirit greet;  
To stead thee as a verse in English tongue,  
An echo of thee in the north-[[motifs/wind|wind]] sung. 160  

These brethren having found by many signs  
What [[motifs/love|love]] [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] for their sister had,  
And how she lov’d him too, each unconfines  
His bitter thoughts to other, [[motifs/well|well]] nigh mad  
That he, the servant of their trade designs,  
Should in their sister’s [[motifs/love|love]] be blithe and glad,  
When ’twas their plan to coax her by degrees  
To some high noble and his olive-trees.  

And many a jealous conference had they,  
And many times they bit their lips alone,  170  
Before they fix’d upon a surest way  
To make the youngster for his crime atone;  
And at the last, these men of cruel clay  
Cut [[concepts/mercy|Mercy]] with a sharp knife to the bone;  
For they resolved in some forest dim  
To kill [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], and there bury him.  

So on a pleasant morning, as he leant  
Into the sun-rise, o’er the balustrade  
Of the [[motifs/garden|garden]]-terrace, towards him they bent  
Their footing through the dews; and to him said,  180  
“You seem there in the quiet of content,  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], and we are most loth to invade  
Calm speculation; but if you are wise,  
Bestride your steed while cold is in the skies.  

“To-day we purpose, ay, this hour we mount  
To spur three leagues towards the Apennine;  
Come down, we pray thee, ere the hot sun count  
His dewy rosary on the eglantine.”  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], courteously as he was wont,  
Bow’d a fair greeting to these serpents’ whine;  190  
And went in haste, to get in readiness,  
With belt, and spur, and bracing huntsman’s dress.  

And as he to the [[settings/court|court]]-yard pass’d along,  
Each third step did he pause, and listen’d oft  
If he could hear his lady’s matin-song,  
Or the light whisper of her footstep soft;  
And as he thus over his passion hung,  
He heard a laugh full musical aloft;  
When, looking up, he saw her features bright  
Smile through an in-door lattice, all delight. 200  

“[[motifs/love|Love]], Isabel!” said he, “I was in [[motifs/pain|pain]]  
Lest I should miss to bid thee a good morrow  
Ah! what if I should lose thee, when so fain  
I am to stifle all the heavy sorrow  
Of a poor three hours’ absence? but we’ll gain  
Out of the amorous dark what day doth borrow.  
Goodbye! I’ll soon be back.”--”Goodbye!” said she:--  
And as he went she chanted merrily.  

So the two brothers and their murder’d man  
Rode past fair Florence, to where Arno’s stream  210  
Gurgles through straiten’d banks, and still doth fan  
Itself with dancing bulrush, and the bream  
Keeps head against the freshets. Sick and wan  
The brothers’ faces in the ford did seem,  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]]’s flush with [[motifs/love|love]].--They pass’d the [[motifs/water|water]]  
Into a forest quiet for the slaughter.  

There was [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] slain and buried in,  
There in that forest did his great [[motifs/love|love]] cease;  
Ah! when a [[motifs/soul|soul]] doth thus its [[concepts/freedom|freedom]] win,  
It aches in [[concepts/loneliness|loneliness]]--is ill at peace  220  
As the break-covert [[motifs/blood|blood]]-hounds of such sin:  
They dipp’d their swords in the [[motifs/water|water]], and did tease  
Their horses homeward, with convulsed spur,  
Each richer by his being a murderer.  

They told their sister how, with sudden [[characters/speed|speed]],  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] had ta’en [[motifs/ship|ship]] for foreign lands,  
Because of some great urgency and need  
In their affairs, requiring trusty hands.  
Poor Girl! put on thy stifling widow’s weed,  
And ‘scape at once from [[concepts/hope|Hope]]’s accursed bands;  230  
To-day thou wilt not see him, nor to-morrow,  
And the next day will be a day of sorrow.  

She weeps alone for pleasures not to be;  
Sorely she wept until the [[motifs/night|night]] came on,  
And then, instead of [[motifs/love|love]], O misery!  
She brooded o’er the luxury alone:  
His image in the dusk she seem’d to see,  
And to the [[motifs/silence|silence]] made a gentle moan,  
Spreading her perfect arms upon the air,  
And on her couch low murmuring “Where? O where?”  240  

But Selfishness, [[motifs/love|Love]]’s cousin, held not long  
Its fiery vigil in her single breast;  
She fretted for the golden hour, and hung  
Upon the [[motifs/time|time]] with feverish unrest--  
Not long--for soon into her heart a throng  
Of higher occupants, a richer zest,  
Came tragic; passion not to be subdued,  
And sorrow for her [[motifs/love|love]] in travels rude.  

In the mid days of [[motifs/autumn|autumn]], on their eves  
The breath of [[motifs/winter|Winter]] comes from far away,  250  
And the sick west continually bereaves  
Of some gold tinge, and plays a roundelay  
Of [[motifs/death|death]] among the bushes and the [[motifs/leaves|leaves]],  
To make all bare before he dares to stray  
From his north cavern. So sweet Isabel  
By gradual [[motifs/decay|decay]] from [[concepts/beauty|beauty]] fell,  

Because [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] came not. Oftentimes  
She ask’d her brothers, with an eye all pale,  
Striving to be itself, what dungeon climes  
Could keep him off so long? They spake a tale  260  
[[motifs/time|Time]] after [[motifs/time|time]], to quiet her. Their crimes  
Came on them, like a smoke from Hinnom’s vale;  
And every [[motifs/night|night]] in [[motifs/dreams|dreams]] they groan’d aloud,  
To see their sister in her snowy shroud.  

And she had died in drowsy ignorance,  
But for a thing more deadly dark than all;  
It came like a fierce potion, drunk by chance,  
Which saves a sick man from the feather’d pall  
For some few gasping moments; like a lance,  
Waking an Indian from his cloudy hall 270  
With cruel pierce, and bringing him again  
Sense of the gnawing [[motifs/fire|fire]] at heart and brain.  

It was a vision.--In the drowsy gloom,  
The dull of midnight, at her couch’s foot  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] stood, and wept: the forest tomb  
Had marr’d his glossy hair which once could shoot  
Lustre into the sun, and put cold doom  
Upon his lips, and taken the soft lute  
From his lorn voice, and past his loamed ears  
Had made a miry channel for his [[motifs/tears|tears]].  280  

Strange sound it was, when the pale [[archetypes/shadow|shadow]] spake;  
For there was striving, in its piteous tongue,  
To speak as when on earth it was awake,  
And [[characters/isabella|Isabella]] on its music hung:  
Languor there was in it, and tremulous shake,  
As in a palsied Druid’s harp unstrung;  
And through it moan’d a ghostly under-song,  
Like hoarse [[motifs/night|night]]-gusts sepulchral briars among.  

Its eyes, though wild, were still all dewy bright  
With [[motifs/love|love]], and kept all [[characters/phantom|phantom]] [[concepts/fear|fear]] aloof  290  
From the poor girl by magic of their light,  
The while it did unthread the horrid woof  
Of the late darken’d [[motifs/time|time]],--the murderous spite  
Of [[concepts/pride|pride]] and avarice,--the dark pine roof  
In the forest,--and the sodden turfed dell,  
Where, without any word, from stabs he fell.  

Saying moreover, “Isabel, my sweet!  
Red whortle-berries droop above my head,  
And a large flint-stone weighs upon my feet;  
Around me beeches and high chestnuts shed  300  
Their [[motifs/leaves|leaves]] and prickly nuts; a sheep-fold bleat  
Comes from beyond the [[motifs/river|river]] to my bed:  
Go, shed one tear upon my heather-bloom,  
And it shall comfort me within the tomb.  

“I am a [[archetypes/shadow|shadow]] now, alas! alas!  
Upon the skirts of human-[[concepts/nature|nature]] dwelling  
[[works/alone-(poe)|Alone]]: I chant alone the holy mass,  
While little sounds of life are round me knelling,  
And glossy [[motifs/bees|bees]] at [[motifs/noon|noon]] do fieldward pass,  
And many a chapel bell the hour is telling,  310  
Paining me through: those sounds grow strange to me,  
And thou art distant in Humanity.  

“I know what was, I feel full [[motifs/well|well]] what is,  
And I should rage, if spirits could go mad;  
Though I forget the taste of earthly bliss,  
That paleness warms my [[motifs/grave|grave]], as though I had  
A Seraph chosen from the bright abyss  
To be my spouse: thy paleness makes me glad;  
Thy [[concepts/beauty|beauty]] grows upon me, and I feel  
A greater [[motifs/love|love]] through all my essence steal.”  320  

The Spirit mourn’d “Adieu!”--dissolv’d, and left  
The atom darkness in a slow turmoil;  
As when of healthful midnight [[motifs/sleep|sleep]] bereft,  
Thinking on rugged hours and fruitless toil,  
We put our eyes into a pillowy cleft,  
And see the spangly gloom froth up and boil:  
It made sad [[characters/isabella|Isabella]]’s eyelids ache,  
And in the [[motifs/dawn|dawn]] she started up awake;  

“Ha! ha!” said she, “I knew not this hard life,  
I thought the worst was simple misery;  330  
I thought some Fate with pleasure or with strife  
Portion’d us--happy days, or else to die;  
But there is crime--a brother’s bloody knife!  
Sweet Spirit, thou hast school’d my infancy:  
I’ll visit thee for this, and kiss thine eyes,  
And greet thee morn and even in the skies.”  

When the full morning came, she had devised  
How she might secret to the forest hie;  
How she might find the clay, so dearly prized,  
And sing to it one latest lullaby;  340  
How her short absence might be unsurmised,  
While she the inmost of the dream would try.  
Resolv’d, she took with her an aged [[characters/nurse|nurse]],  
And went into that dismal forest-hearse.  

See, as they creep along the [[motifs/river|river]] side,  
How she doth whisper to that aged Dame,  
And, after looking round the champaign wide,  
Shows her a knife.--”What feverous hectic flame  
Burns in thee, [[archetypes/child|child]]?--What good can thee betide,  
That thou should’st smile again?”--The evening came,  350  
And they had found [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]]’s earthy bed;  
The flint was there, the berries at his head.  

Who hath not loiter’d in a green church-yard,  
And let his spirit, like a demon-mole,  
Work through the clayey soil and gravel hard,  
To see scull, coffin’d bones, and funeral stole;  
Pitying each form that hungry [[motifs/death|Death]] hath marr’d,  
And filling it once more with human [[motifs/soul|soul]]?  
Ah! this is holiday to what was felt  
When [[characters/isabella|Isabella]] by [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] knelt.  360  

She gaz’d into the fresh-thrown mould, as though  
One glance did fully all its [[motifs/secrets|secrets]] tell;  
Clearly she saw, as other eyes would know  
Pale limbs at [[characters/bottom|bottom]] of a crystal [[motifs/well|well]];  
Upon the murderous spot she seem’d to grow,  
Like to a native lily of the dell:  
Then with her knife, all sudden, she began  
To dig more fervently than misers can.  

Soon she turn’d up a soiled glove, whereon  
Her silk had play’d in purple phantasies,  370  
She kiss’d it with a lip more chill than stone,  
And put it in her bosom, where it dries  
And freezes utterly unto the bone  
Those dainties made to still an infant’s cries:  
Then ‘gan she work again; nor stay’d her care,  
But to throw back at times her veiling hair.  

That old [[characters/nurse|nurse]] stood beside her wondering,  
Until her heart felt pity to the core  
At sight of such a dismal labouring,  
And so she kneeled, with her locks all hoar, 380  
And put her lean hands to the horrid thing:  
Three hours they labour’d at this travail sore;  
At last they felt the kernel of the [[motifs/grave|grave]],  
And [[characters/isabella|Isabella]] did not stamp and rave.  

Ah! wherefore all this wormy circumstance?  
Why linger at the yawning tomb so long?  
O for the gentleness of old [[works/romance-(poe)|Romance]],  
The simple plaining of a minstrel’s song!  
Fair reader, at the old tale take a glance,  
For here, in [[concepts/truth|truth]], it doth not [[motifs/well|well]] belong  390  
To speak:--O turn thee to the very tale,  
And taste the music of that vision pale.  

With duller steel than the Perséan sword  
They cut away no formless [[archetypes/monster|monster]]’s head,  
But one, whose gentleness did [[motifs/well|well]] accord  
With [[motifs/death|death]], as life. The ancient harps have said,  
[[motifs/love|Love]] never dies, but lives, immortal Lord:  
If [[motifs/love|Love]] impersonate was ever dead,  
Pale [[characters/isabella|Isabella]] kiss’d it, and low moan’d.  
’Twas [[motifs/love|love]]; cold,--dead indeed, but not dethroned.  400  

In anxious secrecy they took it [[motifs/home|home]],  
And then the prize was all for Isabel:  
She calm’d its wild hair with a golden comb,  
And all around each eye’s sepulchral cell  
Pointed each fringed lash; the smeared loam  
With [[motifs/tears|tears]], as chilly as a dripping [[motifs/well|well]],  
She drench’d away:--and still she comb’d, and kept  
Sighing all day--and still she kiss’d, and wept.  

Then in a silken scarf,--sweet with the dews  
Of precious [[motifs/flowers|flowers]] pluck’d in Araby, 410  
And divine liquids come with odorous ooze  
Through the cold serpent-pipe refreshfully,--  
She wrapp’d it up; and for its tomb did choose  
A [[motifs/garden|garden]]-pot, wherein she laid it by,  
And cover’d it with mould, and o’er it set  
Sweet Basil, which her [[motifs/tears|tears]] kept ever wet.  

And she forgot the [[motifs/stars|stars]], the [[motifs/moon|moon]], and sun,  
And she forgot the blue above the trees,  
And she forgot the dells where waters run,  
And she forgot the chilly [[motifs/autumn|autumn]] breeze;  420  
She had no knowledge when the day was done,  
And the new morn she saw not: but in peace  
Hung over her sweet Basil evermore,  
And moisten’d it with [[motifs/tears|tears]] unto the core.  

And so she ever fed it with thin [[motifs/tears|tears]],  
Whence thick, and green, and beautiful it grew,  
So that it smelt more balmy than its peers  
Of Basil-tufts in Florence; for it drew  
Nurture besides, and life, from human fears,  
From the fast mouldering head there shut from view: 430  
So that the [[motifs/jewel|jewel]], safely casketed,  
Came forth, and in perfumed leafits spread.  

O [[concepts/melancholy|Melancholy]], linger here awhile!  
O Music, Music, breathe despondingly!  
O Echo, Echo, from some sombre isle,  
Unknown, Lethean, sigh to us--O sigh!  
Spirits in grief, lift up your heads, and smile;  
Lift up your heads, sweet Spirits, heavily,  
And make a pale light in your cypress glooms,  
Tinting with silver wan your marble tombs.  440  

Moan hither, all ye syllables of woe,  
From the deep throat of sad Melpomene!  
Through bronzed lyre in tragic order go,  
And touch the strings into a mystery;  
Sound mournfully upon the winds and low;  
For simple Isabel is soon to be  
Among the dead: She withers, like a palm  
Cut by an Indian for its juicy balm.  

O leave the palm to wither by itself;  
Let not quick [[motifs/winter|Winter]] chill its dying hour!-- 450  
It may not be--those Baälites of pelf,  
Her brethren, noted the continual shower  
From her dead eyes; and many a curious elf,  
Among her kindred, [[concepts/wonder|wonder]]’d that such dower  
Of youth and [[concepts/beauty|beauty]] should be thrown aside  
By one mark’d out to be a Noble’s bride.  

And, furthermore, her brethren [[concepts/wonder|wonder]]’d much  
Why she sat drooping by the Basil green,  
And why it flourish’d, as by magic touch;  
Greatly they [[concepts/wonder|wonder]]’d what the thing might mean:  460  
They could not surely give belief, that such  
A very nothing would have power to wean  
Her from her own fair youth, and pleasures gay,  
And even remembrance of her [[motifs/love|love]]’s delay.  

Therefore they watch’d a [[motifs/time|time]] when they might sift  
This hidden whim; and long they watch’d in vain;  
For seldom did she go to chapel-shrift,  
And seldom felt she any hunger-[[motifs/pain|pain]];  
And when she left, she hurried back, as swift  
As bird on wing to breast its eggs again;  470  
And, patient, as a hen-bird, sat her there  
Beside her Basil, weeping through her hair.  

Yet they contriv’d to steal the Basil-pot,  
And to examine it in secret place:  
The thing was vile with green and livid spot,  
And yet they knew it was [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]]’s face:  
The guerdon of their murder they had got,  
And so left Florence in a moment’s space,  
Never to turn again.--Away they went,  
With [[motifs/blood|blood]] upon their heads, to banishment.  480  

O [[concepts/melancholy|Melancholy]], turn thine eyes away!  
O Music, Music, breathe despondingly!  
O Echo, Echo, on some other day,  
From isles Lethean, sigh to us--O sigh!  
Spirits of grief, sing not your “[[motifs/well|Well]]-a-way!”  
For Isabel, sweet Isabel, will die;  
Will die a [[motifs/death|death]] too lone and incomplete,  
Now they have ta’en away her Basil sweet.  

Piteous she look’d on dead and senseless things,  
Asking for her lost Basil amorously;  490  
And with melodious chuckle in the strings  
Of her lorn voice, she oftentimes would cry  
After the [[archetypes/pilgrim|Pilgrim]] in his wanderings,  
To ask him where her Basil was; and why  
’Twas hid from her: “For cruel ’tis,” said she,  
“To steal my Basil-pot away from me.”  

And so she pined, and so she died forlorn,  
Imploring for her Basil to the last.  
No heart was there in Florence but did mourn  
In pity of her [[motifs/love|love]], so overcast.  500  
And a sad ditty of this story born  
From mouth to mouth through all the country pass’d:  
Still is the burthen sung--”O cruelty,  
To steal my Basil-pot away from me!”

IL VASO DI BASILICO.  

UNA STORIA DA BOCCACCIO.  

Bella Isabel, povera semplice Isabel!  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], giovane pellegrino agli occhi d’[[motifs/love|Amore]]!  
Non potevano abitare nella [[concepts/self|medesima]] dimora  
senza qualche moto del cuore, qualche malanno;  
non potevano sedere a mensa senza sentire quanto [[motifs/well|bene]]  
consolasse ciascuno l’essere accanto all’altro;  
non potevano, certo, [[motifs/sleep|dormire]] sotto lo stesso tetto  
senza sognarsi a vicenda, e piangere ogni notte.  

A ogni mattino il loro [[motifs/love|amore]] si faceva più tenero,  
a ogni sera più profondo e più tenero ancora;  10  
egli non poteva muoversi in casa, in campo o in [[motifs/garden|giardino]],  
senza che la piena figura di lei gli riempisse tutta la vista;  
e la voce continua di lui era più dolce  
a lei che il rumore degli alberi o d’un rivo nascosto;  
la corda del suo liuto rendeva l’eco del nome di lui,  
e col medesimo guastò il ricamo lasciato a metà.  

Sapeva quale mano gentile fosse al saliscendi,  
prima che la porta la desse ai suoi occhi;  
e dalla [[motifs/window|finestra]] della camera di lei coglieva  
la sua [[concepts/beauty|bellezza]] più lontano di quanto scorga il falco;  20  
e costante come i vespri di lei vegliava,  
perché il volto di lei era volto agli stessi cieli;  
e con malato [[concepts/longing|struggimento]] consumava tutta la [[motifs/night|notte]],  
per udire il suo passo mattutino sulla scala.  

Un intero lungo mese di maggio in questo triste stato  
rese più pallide le loro guance allo spuntar di giugno:  
“Domani mi inchinerò alla mia delizia,  
domani chiederò il dono della mia signora.”--  
“Oh, ch’io non veda mai un’altra [[motifs/night|notte]],  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], se le tue labbra non spirano il canto d’[[motifs/love|amore]].”--  30  
Così parlavano ai loro guanciali; ma, ahimè,  
giorni e giorni senza miele egli lasciò passare;  

finché la guancia intatta della dolce [[characters/isabella|Isabella]]  
s’ammalò dentro il giusto dominio della [[motifs/rose|rosa]],  
si fece magra come quella d’una giovane madre, che cerca  
con ogni ninnananna di lenire il [[motifs/pain|dolore]] del suo bambino:  
“Com’è malata,” disse lui, “non posso parlare,  
eppure lo farò, e dirò tutto aperto il mio [[motifs/love|amore]]:  
se gli sguardi dicono le leggi d’[[motifs/love|amore]], berrò le sue [[motifs/tears|lacrime]],  
e almeno questo scaccerà le sue pene.” 40  

Così disse un bel mattino, e tutto il giorno  
il cuore gli batté paurosamente contro il fianco;  
e al suo cuore pregò dentro di sé  
la forza di parlare; ma sempre la marea rossa  
gli soffocava la voce, e ne pulsava via il proposito--  
gli infebbrava l’alto pensiero d’una tale sposa,  
eppure lo riduceva alla mitezza d’un [[archetypes/child|fanciullo]]:  
Ahimè! quando la passione è insieme mite e selvaggia!  

Così ancora una volta si sarebbe destato, angosciato,  
da una [[motifs/night|notte]] tetra d’[[motifs/love|amore]] e di miseria,  50  
se l’occhio pronto d’Isabel non fosse stato sposato  
a ogni segno sulla sua fronte alta;  
lo vide farsi pallidissimo e morto,  
e subito tutto acceso; così, balbettò teneramente,  
“[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]]!”--qui cessò la sua timida [[topoi/quest|richiesta]],  
ma nel tono e nello sguardo egli lesse il resto.  

“O [[characters/isabella|Isabella]], quasi m’accorgo  
che posso dire il mio dolore al tuo orecchio;  
se mai credesti a qualche cosa,  
credi quanto ti [[motifs/love|amo]], credi quanto vicina  60  
sia la mia [[motifs/soul|anima]] alla sua fine: non vorrei affliggere  
la tua mano con una stretta sgradita, non vorrei [[concepts/fear|spaventare]]  
i tuoi occhi fissandoli; ma non posso vivere  
un’altra [[motifs/night|notte]] senza confessare la mia passione.  

“[[motifs/love|Amore]]! tu mi conduci via dal freddo invernale,  
Signora! tu mi conduci a un clima d’[[motifs/summer|estate]],  
e devo gustare i fiori che s’aprono  
nel suo caldo maturo in questo grazioso [[motifs/time|tempo]] del mattino.”  
Detto questo, le labbra prima timide si fecero ardite,  
e poetarono con le sue in rima rugiadosa:  70  
grande beatitudine era con loro, e grande felicità  
crebbe, come fiore rigoglioso nella carezza di giugno.  

Separandosi parevano camminare sull’aria,  
rose gemelle divise dal soffio dello zefiro  
solo per ritrovarsi più strette, e dividere  
l’intima fragranza del cuore l’una dell’altra.  
Lei, andata in camera, cantò una bella canzone  
di [[motifs/love|amore]] delizioso e di dardo mielato;  
lui con passi leggeri salì un [[motifs/hill|colle]] a occidente,  
e diede addio al sole, e godette a sazietà.  80  

Tutti stretti s’incontrarono di nuovo, prima che il crepuscolo  
avesse tolto alle [[motifs/stars|stelle]] il suo velo gradito,  
tutti stretti s’incontrarono, tutti occhi, prima che il crepuscolo  
avesse tolto alle [[motifs/stars|stelle]] il suo velo gradito,  
stretti in un pergolato di giacinto e di muschio,  
ignoti a chiunque, liberi da ogni diceria sussurrata.  
Ah! meglio fosse stato così per sempre,  
che orecchie oziose godessero della loro sventura.  

Erano dunque infelici?--Non può essere--  
troppe [[motifs/tears|lacrime]] sono state versate per gli amanti,  90  
troppi sospiri diamo loro in tributo,  
troppa pietà dopo che sono morti,  
troppe storie dolenti vediamo,  
la cui materia meglio si leggerebbe in oro lucente;  
salvo in quella pagina dove la sposa di [[characters/theseus|Teseo]]  
sopra le onde senza sentiero si china verso di lui.  

Ma, per il verdetto generale d’[[motifs/love|amore]],  
il poco dolce uccide molta amarezza;  
benché Didone taccia nel boschetto sotterraneo,  
e quello d’[[characters/isabella|Isabella]] fosse un grande strazio,  100  
benché il giovane [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] in caldo garofano d’India  
non fosse imbalsamato, questa [[concepts/truth|verità]] non è da meno--  
perfino le [[motifs/bees|api]], piccole elemosiniere dei pergolati di [[motifs/spring|primavera]],  
sanno che il succo più ricco è nei [[motifs/flowers|fiori]] di [[motifs/poison|veleno]].  

Con i suoi due fratelli viveva questa bella dama,  
arricchiti da mercatura avita,

in miniere a torce e in fabbriche rumorose,  
e più d’un fianco un tempo fiero di faretra si struggeva  
nel [[motifs/blood|sangue]] della frusta che morde;--con occhi incavati  110  
molti tutto il giorno stavano nel [[motifs/river|fiume]] abbagliante,  
a raccogliere i detriti ricchi di minerale della piena.  

Per loro il palombaro di Ceylon tratteneva il fiato,  
e andava tutto nudo allo squalo affamato;  
per loro gli orecchi gli sprizzavano [[motifs/blood|sangue]]; per loro in [[motifs/death|morte]]  
la foca sul ghiaccio freddo, con pietoso latrato,  
giaceva piena di dardi; per loro soli ribollivano  
mille uomini in travagli vasti e oscuri:  
mezzo ignari, giravano una [[motifs/wheel|ruota]] facile,  
che metteva in opera aguzzi cavalletti, a stringere e a scorticare.  120  

Perché erano superbi? Perché le loro fonti di marmo

Perché erano superbi? di nuovo chiediamo ad alta voce,  
perché in nome della Gloria erano superbi?  

Eppure questi fiorentini erano chiusi in [[concepts/self|sé]] stessi

Eppure lo fecero--e ogni mercante onesto

O eloquente e famoso Boccaccio!

non c’è altro delitto, nessun folle assalto

ma è fatto--riesca il verso o fallisca--  
per [[concepts/honour|onorare]] te, e salutare il tuo spirito andato;  
per servirti come un verso in lingua inglese,  
un’eco di te cantata nel [[motifs/wind|vento]] del nord. 160  

Questi fratelli, avendo trovato per molti segni  
quale [[motifs/love|amore]] [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] avesse per la loro sorella,  
e come anche lei l’amasse, ciascuno svela  
all’altro i suoi pensieri amari, [[motifs/well|quasi]] pazzo  
che lui, servo dei loro disegni di commercio,  
dovesse esser lieto e gaio nell’[[motifs/love|amore]] della loro sorella,  
quando era loro piano indurla a poco a poco  
verso qualche alto nobile e i suoi uliveti.  

E più d’un geloso conciliabolo ebbero,  
e molte volte si morsero le labbra da soli,  170  
prima di fissare il modo più sicuro  
di far espiare al giovane il suo delitto;  
e alla fine, questi uomini di creta crudele  
tagliarono fino all’osso la [[concepts/mercy|Misericordia]] con un coltello aguzzo;  
ché decisero, in qualche foresta oscura,  
d’uccidere [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], e là seppellirlo.  

Così un mattino gradevole, mentre egli si sporgeva  
verso il levar del sole, sopra la balaustra  
della terrazza del [[motifs/garden|giardino]], verso di lui volsero  
il loro passo tra le rugiade; e gli dissero,  180  
“Sembri lì nella quiete della contentezza,  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], e assai ci duole invadere  
una calma meditazione; ma se sei saggio,  
inforca il tuo destriero mentre c’è freddo nei cieli.  

“Oggi ci proponiamo, sì, in quest’ora montiamo  
per spronare tre leghe verso l’Appennino;  
scendi, ti preghiamo, prima che il sole caldo conti  
il suo rosario di rugiada sulla rosa canina.”  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]], cortese com’era solito,  
inchinò un bel saluto a questo sibilo di serpi;  190  
e andò in fretta a prepararsi,  
con cintura, e sprone, e attillato abito da cacciatore.  

E mentre passava verso il [[settings/court|cortile]],  
ogni terzo passo si fermava, e spesso ascoltava  
se potesse udire il canto mattutino della sua signora,  
o il lieve sussurro del suo passo molle;  
e mentre così stava sospeso sulla sua passione,  
udì un riso pieno di musica là in alto;  
e, alzando gli occhi, vide i suoi lineamenti lucenti  
sorridere da una grata interna, tutta delizia. 200  

“[[motifs/love|Amore]], Isabel!” disse, “ero in [[motifs/pain|pena]]  
temendo di non riuscire a darti il buongiorno  
Ah! e se ti perdessi, mentre tanto bramo  
di soffocare tutto il grave dolore  
d’una povera assenza di tre ore? ma guadagneremo  
dal buio amoroso ciò che il giorno prende in prestito.  
Addio! tornerò presto.”--”Addio!” disse lei:--

cavalcarono oltre la bella Firenze, là dove la corrente d’Arno  210  
gorgoglia tra rive strette, e ancora si fa vento  
col giunco danzante, e l’abramide  
tiene testa alle piene. Malati e smorti  
parvero nel guado i volti dei fratelli,  
quello di [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] acceso d’[[motifs/love|amore]].--Passarono l’[[motifs/water|acqua]]  
dentro una foresta quieta per il massacro.  

Là fu [[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] ucciso e sepolto,  
là in quella foresta cessò il suo grande [[motifs/love|amore]];  
Ah! quando un’[[motifs/soul|anima]] conquista così la sua [[concepts/freedom|libertà]],  
duole nella [[concepts/loneliness|solitudine]]--sta male in pace  220  
quanto i segugi da [[motifs/blood|sangue]] che scovano tale peccato:  
Immersero le spade nell’[[motifs/water|acqua]], e spinsero  
i cavalli verso casa, con sprone convulso,  
ciascuno più ricco per essere un assassino.  

Dissero alla sorella come, con improvvisa [[characters/speed|fretta]],  
[[characters/lorenzo|Lorenzo]] avesse preso la [[motifs/ship|nave]] per terre straniere,  
per qualche grande urgenza e bisogno  
nei loro affari, che richiedeva mani fidate.  
Povera Fanciulla! metti la tua soffocante veste di vedova,

oggi non lo vedrai, né domani,  
e il giorno dopo sarà un giorno di dolore.

pianse amaramente finché scese la [[motifs/night|notte]],  
e poi, invece dell’[[motifs/love|amore]], o miseria!  
meditò sola su quel lusso:  
l’immagine di lui pareva vedere nel crepuscolo,  
e al [[motifs/silence|silenzio]] fece un gemito gentile,  
stendendo le braccia perfette sull’aria,  
e sul giaciglio mormorando piano “Dove? Oh dove?”  240  

Ma l’Egoismo, cugino d’[[motifs/love|Amore]], non tenne a lungo  
la sua veglia infuocata in quel petto solitario;  
si crucciava per l’ora d’oro, e stava sospesa  
sul [[motifs/time|tempo]] con febbrile inquietudine--  
non a lungo--ché presto nel suo cuore una folla  
d’ospiti più alti, un gusto più ricco,  
venne tragica; passione da non domare,  
e dolore per il suo [[motifs/love|amore]] in rudi viaggi.  

Nei giorni di mezzo [[motifs/autumn|autunno]], alle loro sere

e l’occidente malato spoglia di continuo  
di qualche tinta d’oro, e suona un girotondo  
di [[motifs/death|morte]] tra i cespugli e le [[motifs/leaves|foglie]],

dalla sua caverna del nord. Così la dolce Isabel  
per graduale [[motifs/decay|sfacelo]] decadde dalla [[concepts/beauty|bellezza]],

potessero tenerlo lontano così a lungo. Raccontarono una favola  260  
[[motifs/time|volta]] dopo [[motifs/time|volta]], per acquietarla. I loro delitti  
vennero su di loro come un fumo dalla valle di Hinnom;  
e ogni [[motifs/night|notte]] nei [[motifs/dreams|sogni]] gemevano forte,  
a veder la sorella nel suo sudario di neve.  

E sarebbe morta in sonnolenta ignoranza,  
se non fosse per una cosa più mortalmente oscura di tutte;  
venne come una pozione violenta, bevuta per caso,  
che salva un malato dal drappo di piume  
per pochi momenti d’ansimo; come una lancia  
che desta un Indiano dalla sua sala di nuvole 270  
con crudele trafittura, e gli ridà  
il senso del [[motifs/fire|fuoco]] che rode cuore e cervello.  

Era una visione.--Nella tenebra sonnolenta,

lucentezza nel sole, e aveva posto fredda condanna

Strano suono fu, quando la pallida [[archetypes/shadow|ombra]] parlò;  
ché c’era uno sforzo, nella sua lingua pietosa,  
di parlare come quando in terra era desta,  
e [[characters/isabella|Isabella]] pendeva dalla sua musica:  
v’era languore in essa, e tremulo scuotersi,  
come in arpa scordata d’un Druido paralitico;  
e attraverso essa gemeva un controcanto spettrale,  
come rauche raffiche di [[motifs/night|notte]] fra rovi sepolcrali.  

I suoi occhi, benché selvaggi, erano ancora lucidi di rugiada  
d’[[motifs/love|amore]], e tenevano lontana ogni [[concepts/fear|paura]] di [[characters/phantom|fantasma]]  290  
dalla povera fanciulla per la magia della loro luce,  
mentre disfaceva la trama orrenda  
del [[motifs/time|tempo]] da poco oscurato,--il livore assassino  
dell’[[concepts/pride|orgoglio]] e dell’avarizia,--il tetto scuro di pini  
nella foresta,--e la valletta d’erba zuppa,  
dove, senza una parola, cadde sotto le pugnalate.

Rossi mirtilli pendono sopra il mio capo,

intorno a me faggi e alti castagni lasciano cadere  300  
le loro [[motifs/leaves|foglie]] e i ricci spinosi; un belato d’ovile

che abita ai margini della [[concepts/nature|natura]] umana

mentre piccoli suoni di vita mi rintoccano intorno,  
e lucide [[motifs/bees|api]] a [[motifs/noon|mezzogiorno]] passano verso i campi,  
e più d’una campana di cappella dice l’ora,  310

“So quel che fu, sento fin troppo [[motifs/well|bene]] quel che è,

benché io scordi il sapore della beatitudine terrena,  
quel pallore scalda la mia [[motifs/grave|tomba]], come se avessi  
un Serafino scelto dal lucente abisso  
per mia sposa: il tuo pallore mi fa lieto;  
la tua [[concepts/beauty|bellezza]] cresce su di me, e sento  
un [[motifs/love|amore]] più grande insinuarsi per tutta la mia essenza.”  320
