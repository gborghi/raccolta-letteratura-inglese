| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger to More. Messenger. T. Goodal |
| MESSENGER | My honourable lord, the Mayor of [[London]]<br>Accompanied with his lady and her train<br>Are coming hither, and are hard at hand<br>To feast with you. A sergeant’s come before<br>To tell your lordship of their near approach. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Messenger |
| MORE | Why, this is cheerful news. Friends go and come.<br>Reverend Erasmus, whose delicious words<br>Express the very [[Soul|soul]] and life of wit,<br>Newly took sad leave of me, with [[Tears|tears]]<br>Troubled the silver channel of the Thames,<br>Which, glad of such a burden, proudly swelled<br>And on her bosom bore him toward the sea.<br>He’s gone to Rotterdam. Peace go with him!<br>He left me heavy when he went from hence,<br>But this recomforts me. The kind [[Lord Mayor]],<br>His brethren aldermen, with their fair wives<br>Will feast this [[Night|night]] with us. Why, so’t should be.<br>More’s merry heart lives by good company. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Master Roper and Servingmen |
| MORE | Good gentlemen, be careful; give great charge<br>Our diet be made dainty for the taste.<br>For, of all people that the earth affords,<br>The Londoners fare richest at their boards.<br><br>[Addition V (playhouse scribe)]<br>[Original Text (Munday)]<br>Come, my good fellows, stir, be diligent.<br>Sloth is an idle fellow. Leave him now.<br>The [[Time|time]] requires your expeditious service.<br>Place me here stools to set the ladies on. |
| *(didascalia)* | Servingmen set stools |
| MORE | Son Roper, you have given order for the banquet? |
| ROPER | I have, my lord, and everything is ready. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lady More |
| MORE | O welcome, wife. Give you direction<br>How women should be placed; you know it best.<br>For my [[Lord Mayor]], his brethren, and the rest,<br>Let me [[Alone|alone]]. Men best can order men. |
| LADY MORE | I warrant ye, my lord, all shall be [[Well|well]].<br>There’s one without that stays to speak with ye,<br>And bade me tell ye that he is a player. |
| MORE | A player, wife?—One of ye bid him come in. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one, [a Servingman] |
| MORE | Nay, stir there, fellows. Fie, ye are too slow!<br>See that your lights be in a readiness.<br>The banquet shall be here.—God’s me, madam,<br>Leave my Lady Mayoress? Both of us from the board?<br>And my son Roper too? What may our guests think? |
| LADY MORE | My lord, they are risen, and sitting by the [[Fire|fire]]. |
| MORE | Why, yet go you, and keep them company.<br>It is not meet we should be absent both. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Lady |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Player |
| MORE | Welcome, good friend. What is your will with me? |
| PLAYER | My lord, my fellows and myself<br>Are come to tender ye our willing service,<br>So please you to command us. |
| MORE | What, for a play, you mean?<br>Whom do ye serve? |
| PLAYER | My Lord Cardinal’s grace. |
| MORE | My Lord Cardinal’s players? Now trust me, welcome.<br>You happen hither in a lucky [[Time|time]]<br>To pleasure me and benefit yourselves.<br>The Mayor of [[London]] and some aldermen,<br>His lady, and their wives are my kind guests<br>This [[Night|night]] at supper. Now, to have a play<br>Before the banquet will be excellent.<br>How think you, son Roper? |
| ROPER | ’Twill do [[Well|well]], my lord,<br>And be right pleasing pastime to your guests. |
| MORE | I prithee tell me, what plays have ye? |
| PLAYER | Diverse, my lord: The Cradle of Security,<br><br>Hit Nail o’th’ Head, Impatient [[Poverty]],<br><br>The Play of Four Ps, Dives and Lazarus,<br><br>Lusty Juventus, and The [[Marriage]] of Wit and Wisdom. |
| MORE | The [[Marriage]] of Wit and Wisdom? That, my lads,<br>I’ll none but that. The theme is very good,<br>And may maintain a liberal argument.<br>To marry wit to wisdom asks some cunning.<br>Many have wit that may come short of wisdom.<br>We’ll see how Master Poet plays his part,<br>And whether wit or wisdom grace his art.<br>Go, make him drink, and all his fellows too.<br>How many are ye? |
| PLAYER | Four men and a boy, sir. |
| MORE | But one boy? Then I see There’s but few women in the play. |
| PLAYER | Three, my lord: Dame Science, Lady [[Vanity]],<br>And Wisdom she herself. |
| MORE | And one boy play them all? By‘r Lady, he’s loaden.<br>[[Well]], my good fellow, get ye straight together<br>And make ye ready with what haste ye may. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Servingmen Provide their supper’gainst the play be |
| MORE | done,<br>Else shall we stay our guests here overlong. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Player Make haste, I pray ye. |
| PLAYER | We will, my lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Servingmen and Player |
| MORE | Where are the waits? [To Roper] Go, bid them play,<br>To spend the [[Time|time]] a while. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lady More |
| MORE | How now, madam? |
| LADY MORE | My lord, they’re coming hither. |
| MORE | They’re welcome. Wife, I’ll tell ye one thing.<br>Our sport is somewhat mended: we shall have<br>A play tonight, The [[Marriage]] of Wit and Wisdom,<br>And acted by my good Lord Cardinal’s players.<br>How like ye that, wife? |
| LADY MORE | My Lord, I like it [[Well|well]].<br>See, they are coming.<br>Waits play hautbois<br><br>The waits plays. Enters [[Lord Mayor]], so many Aldermen<br><br>as may, the Lady Mayoress, in scarlet, with other<br><br>ladies and [[Sir Thomas More]]’s daughters, [one of them<br><br>Roper’s wife]; Servants carrying lighted torches by them |
| MORE | Once again, welcome, welcome, my good [[Lord Mayor]],<br>And brethren all—for once I was your brother,<br>And so am still in heart. It is not state<br>That can our [[Love|love]] from [[London]] separate.<br>〈〉<br>〈 〉 naught but [[Pride|pride]];<br>But they that cast an eye still whence they came<br>Know how they [[Rose|rose]], and how to use the same. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | My lord, you set a gloss on [[London]]’s fame,<br>And make it happy ever by your name.<br>Needs must we say when we remember More,<br>’Twas he that drove rebellion from our door<br>With [[Grave|grave]] discretions, mild and gentle breath,<br>Shielding a many subjects’ lives from [[Death|death]].<br>O, how our [[City|city]] is by you renowned,<br>And with your virtues our endeavours crowned. |
| MORE | No more, my good [[Lord Mayor]]; but thanks to all<br>That on so short a summons you would come<br>To visit him that holds your kindness dear.<br>[To Lady More] Madam, you are not merry with my Lady<br>Mayoress<br>And these fair ladies. Pray ye, seat them all.<br>[To [[Lord Mayor]]] And here, my lord, let me appoint your<br>place;<br>The rest to seat themselves. Nay, I’ll weary ye;<br>You will not long in haste to visit me. |
| LADY MORE | Good madam, sit. In sooth, you shall sit here. |
| LADY MAYORESS | Good madam, pardon me, it may not be. |
| LADY MORE | In troth, I’ll have it so. I’ll sit here by ye.<br>Good ladies, sit.—More stools here, ho! |
| LADY MAYORESS | It is your favour, madam, makes me thus<br>Presume above my merit. |
| LADY MORE | When we come to you,<br>Then shall you rule us as we rule you here.<br>[They sit]<br>Now must I tell ye, madam, we have a play<br>To welcome ye withal. How good soe’er<br>That know not I; my lord will have it so. |
| MORE | Wife, [[Hope|hope]] the best; I am sure they’ll do their best.<br>They that would better comes not at their feast.<br>My good Lord Cardinal’s players, I thank them for it,<br>Play us a play, to lengthen out your welcome,<br>My good [[Lord Mayor]] and all my other friends.<br><br>They say it is The [[Marriage]] of Wit and Wisdom—<br>A theme of some import, howe’er it prove.<br>But if art fail, we’ll inch it out with [[Love|love]].<br>What, are they ready? |
| SERVANT | My lord, one of the players craves to speak with you. |
| MORE | With me? Where is he? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [the Player of] Inclination the Vice, ready, [with a bridle in his hand] |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION Here, my lord. |
| MORE | How now, what’s the matter? |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION We would [[Desire|desire]] your [[Honour|honour]] but to stay a little. One of my fellows is but run to Ogle’s for a long beard for young Wit, and he’ll be here presently. |
| MORE | A long beard for young Wit? Why, man, he may be without a beard till he come to [[Marriage|marriage]], for wit goes not all by the hair. When comes Wit in? |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION In the second scene, next to the Prologue, my lord. |
| MORE | Why, play on till that scene come, and by that [[Time|time]] Wit’s beard will be grown, or else the fellow returned with it. And what part play’st thou? |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION Inclination, the Vice, my lord. |
| MORE | Gramercies, now I may take the Vice if I list. And wherefore hast thou that bridle in thy hand? |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION I must be bridled anon, my lord. |
| MORE | An thou beest not saddled too it makes no matter, for then Wit’s Inclination may gallop so fast that he will outstrip wisdom and fall to folly. |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION Indeed, so he does, to Lady [[Vanity]]; but we have no Folly in our play. |
| MORE | Then there’s no wit in’t, I’ll be sworn. Folly waits on wit as the [[Shadow|shadow]] on the [[Body|body]], and where wit is ripest, there folly still is readiest. But begin, I prithee. We’ll rather allow a beardless Wit than Wit, all beard, to have no brain. |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION Nay, he has his apparel on too, my lord, and therefore he is the readier to enter. |
| MORE | Then, good Inclination, begin at a venture. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Player of Inclination |
| MORE | My [[Lord Mayor]], Wit lacks a beard, or else they would<br>begin.<br>I’d lend him mine, but that it is too thin.<br>[[Silence]], they come.<br>The trumpet sounds. Enter the Prologue |
| PROLOGUE | Now for as much as in these latter days<br>Throughout the whole world in every land<br>Vice doth increase and virtue decays,<br>Iniquity having the upper hand,<br>We therefore intend, good gentle audience,<br>A pretty, short interlude to play at this present,<br>Desiring your leave and quiet [[Silence|silence]]<br>To show the same as is meet and expedient.<br>It is called The [[Marriage]] of Wit and Wisdom,<br>A matter right pithy and pleasing to hear,<br>Whereof in brief we will show the whole sum.<br>But I must be gone, for Wit doth appear. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Wit, ruffling, and Inclination, the Vice |
| WIT | [sings]<br>In an arbour green, asleep whereas I lay—<br>The [[Birds|birds]] sang sweetly in the midst of the day—<br>I dreamèd fast of mirth and play.<br>In youth is pleasure, in youth is pleasure.<br>Methought I walked still to and fro,<br>And from her company I could not go;<br>But when I waked it was not so. iso<br>In youth is pleasure, in youth is pleasure.<br>Therefor my heart is surely plight<br>Of her [[Alone|alone]] to have a sight<br>Which is my joy and heart’s delight.<br>In youth is pleasure, in youth is pleasure. |
| *(didascalia)* | MORE to [[Lord Mayor]] Mark ye, my lord, this is Wit without a beard. What will he be by that [[Time|time]] he comes to the commodity of a beard? |
| INCLINATION | [to Wit]<br>O sir, the ground is the better on which she doth go,<br>For she will make better cheer with a little she can get<br>Than many a one can with a great banquet of meat. |
| WIT | And is her name Wisdom? |
| INCLINATION | Ay, sir, a wife most fit<br>For you, my good master, my dainty sweet Wit. |
| WIT | To be in her company my heart it is set.<br>Therefore I prithee to let us be gone,<br>For unto Wisdom Wit hath inclination. |
| INCLINATION | O sir, she will come herself even anon,<br>For I told her before where we would stand,<br>And then she said she would beck us with her hand.<br>(Flourishing his dagger)<br>Back with those boys and saucy great knaves.<br>What, stand ye here so big in your braves?<br>My dagger about your coxcombs shall walk<br>If I may but so much as hear ye chat or talk. |
| WIT | But will she take pains to come for us hither? |
| INCLINATION | I warrant ye, therefore you must be familiar with her.<br>When she cometh in place<br>You must her embrace<br>Somewhat handsomely,<br>Lest she think it danger<br>Because you are a stranger<br>To come in your company. |
| WIT | I warrant thee, Inclination, I will be busy.<br>O, how Wit longs to be in Wisdom’s company! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lady [[Vanity]], singing and beckoning with her hand |
| [[Vanity|VANITY]] | Come hither, come hither, come hither, come.<br>Such cheer as I have, thou shalt have some. |
| MORE | This is Lady [[Vanity]], I’ll hold my life.<br>Beware, good Wit, you take not her to wife. |
| INCLINATION | [to Lady [[Vanity]]]<br>What, Unknown Honesty, a word in your ear.<br>[Lady [[Vanity]]] offers to depart<br>You shall not be gone as yet, I swear.<br>Here’s none but your friends; you need not to fray.<br>This young gentleman loves ye, therefore you must stay. |
| WIT | I trust in me she will think no danger;<br>For I [[Love|love]] [[Well|well]] the company of fair women—<br>And, though to you I am a stranger,<br>Yet Wit may pleasure you now and then. |
| [[Vanity|VANITY]] | Who, you? Nay, you are such a holy man<br>That to touch one you dare not be bold.<br>I think you would not kiss a young woman<br>If one would give ye twenty pound in gold. |
| WIT | Yes, in good sadness, lady, that I would.<br>I could find in my heart to kiss you in your smock. |
| [[Vanity|VANITY]] | My back is broad enough to bear that mock;<br>For it hath been told me many a [[Time|time]]<br>That you would be seen in no such company as mine. |
| WIT | Not Wit in the company of Lady Wisdom?<br>O Jove, for what do I hither come? |
| INCLINATION | Sir, she did this nothing else but to prove<br>Whether a little thing would you move<br>To be angry and fret.<br>What an if one said so?<br>Let such trifling matters go,<br>And with a kind kiss come out of her debt. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Player |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION Is Luggins come yet with the beard? |
| OTHER PLAYER | No, [[Faith|faith]], he is not come. Alas, what shall we do? |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION [to More] Forsooth, we can go no further till our fellow Luggins come, for he plays Good Counsel, and now he should enter to admonish Wit that this is Lady [[Vanity]] and not Lady Wisdom. |
| MORE | Nay, an it be no more but so, ye shall not tarry at a stand for that. We’ll not have our play marred for lack of a little good counsel. Till your fellow come I’ll give him the best counsel that I can. Pardon me, my [[Lord Mayor]], I [[Love|love]] to be merry.<br>[He rises and joins the players] |
| MORE | as GOOD COUNSEL<br>O good Master Wit, thou art now on the bow hand,<br>And blindly in thine own opinion dost stand.<br>I tell thee, this naughty lewd Inclination<br>Does lead thee amiss in a very strange fashion.<br>This is not Wisdom, but Lady [[Vanity]].<br>Therefore list to Good Counsel, and be ruled by me. |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION In troth, my lord, it is as right to Luggins’s part as can be. Speak, Wit. |
| MORE | Nay, we will not have our audience disappointed if I can help it. |
| WIT | Art thou Good Counsel, and wilt tell me so?<br>Wouldst thou have Wit from Lady Wisdom to go?<br>Thou art some deceiver, I tell thee verily,<br>In saying that this is Lady [[Vanity]]. |
| MORE | as GOOD COUNSEL<br>Wit, judge not things by the outward show.<br>The eye oft mistakes, right [[Well|well]] you do know.<br>Good Counsel assures thee upon his honesty<br>That this is not Wisdom, but Lady [[Vanity]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Luggins, with the beard |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION O my lord, he is come. Now we shall go forward. |
| MORE | Art thou come? [[Well]], fellow, I have holp to save<br>thine honesty a little. Now, if thou canst give Wit any<br>better counsel than I have done, spare not. There I leave<br>him to thy [[Mercy|mercy]].<br>But by this [[Time|time]] I am sure our banquet’s ready.<br>My lord and ladies, we will taste that first,<br>And then they shall begin the play again,<br>Which through the fellow’s absence, and by me,<br>Instead of helping, hath been hindered.<br>[To Servants] Prepare against we come. Lights there, I<br>say.—<br>Thus fools oft-times do help to mar the play. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt. Players remain |
| PLAYER | of WIT Fie, fellow Luggins, you serve us handsomely, do ye not think ye? |
| JUGGINS | Why, Ogle was not within, and his wife would not let me have the beard, and, by my troth, I ran so fast that I sweat again. |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION Do ye hear, fellows? Would not my lord make a rare player? O, he would uphold a company beyond all ho, better than Mason among the [[King]]’s players. Did ye mark how extemp’rically he fell to the matter, and spake Luggins’s part almost as it is in the very book set down? |
| PLAYER | of WIT Peace, do ye know what ye say? My lord a player? Let us not meddle with any such matters. Yet I may be a little proud that my lord hath answered me in my part. But come, let us go and be ready to begin the play again. |
| LUGGINS | Ay, that’s the best, for now we lack nothing.<br><br>[Original Text (Munday)]<br>[Addition VI (Heywood)] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servingman to the players, with a reward |
| SERVINGMAN | Where be these players? |
| ALL THE PLAYERS | Here, sir. |
| SERVINGMAN | My lord is sent for to the [[Court|court]],<br>And all the guests do after supper part;<br>And, for he will not trouble you again,<br>By me for your reward a sends eight angels,<br>With many thanks. But sup before you go.<br>It is his will you should be fairly entreated.<br>Follow, I pray ye. |
| PLAYER | of WIT This, Luggins, is your negligence.<br>Wanting Wit’s beard brought things into dislike;<br>For otherwise the play had been all seen,<br>Where now some curious citizen disgraced it,<br>And, discommending it, all is dismissed. |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION Fore God, a says true. But hear ye, sirs: eight angels, ha! My lord would never give’s eight angels. More or less, for twelve pence: either it should be three pounds, five pounds, or ten pounds, there twenty shillings wanting, sure. |
| PLAYER | of WIT Twenty to one, ’tis so. I have a trick. My lord comes; stand aside. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter More with attendants, with purse and mace |
| MORE | In haste, to Council? What’s the business now<br>That all so late his highness sends for me?—<br>What seek’st thou, fellow? |
| PLAYER | of WIT<br>Nay, nothing. Your lordship sent eight angels by your<br>man,<br>And I have lost two of them in the rushes. |
| MORE | Wit, look to that! Eight angels? I did send them ten.<br>Who gave it them? |
| SERVINGMAN | I, my lord. I had no more about me;<br>But by and by they shall receive the rest. |
| MORE | [[Well]], Wit, ‘twas wisely done. Thou play’st Wit [[Well|well]]<br>indeed<br>Not to be thus deceived of thy right.<br>Am I a man by office truly ordained<br>Equally to divide true right his own,<br>And shall I have deceivers in my [[house]]?<br>Then what avails my bounty, when such servants<br>Deceive the poor of what the master gives?<br>Go one and pull his coat over his ears.<br>There are too many such. Give them their right.<br>Wit, let thy fellows thank thee; ’twas [[Well|well]] done.<br>Thou now deservest to match with Lady Wisdom.<br>[Exeunt More and attendants] |
| PLAYER | of INCLINATION God-a-[[Mercy|mercy]], Wit. [To the Servingman ] Sir, you had a master, [[Sir Thomas More]]. More? But now we shall have more. |
| LUGGINS | God bless him, I would there were more of his mind! A loves our quality, and yit he’s a larned man and knows what the world is.<br>[PLAYER of INCLINATION] [[Well]], a kind man, and more loving than many other, but I think we ha’ met with the first— |
| LUGGINS | First sarved his man that had our angels; and he may chance dine with Duke Humphrey tomorrow, being turned away today. Come, let’s go. |
| *(didascalia)* | [PLAYER of INCLINATION] And many such rewards would make us all ride, and [[Horse|horse]] us with the best nags in Smithfield.Exeunt |
