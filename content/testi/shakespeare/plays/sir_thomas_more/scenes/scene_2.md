| *(didascalia)* | An arras is drawn, and behind it, as in sessions, sit the [[Lord Mayor]], Justice Suresby, and other Justices, and the Recorder, Sheriff More and the other Sheriff sitting by. Smart is the plaintiff, Lifter the prisoner at the bar |

| MORE | My lord, he stands indicted for a purse.<br>He hath been tried; the jury is together. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | Who sent him in? |
| SURESBY | That did I, my lord.<br>Had he had right, he had been hanged ere this,<br>The only captain of the cutpurse crew. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | What is his name? |
| SURESBY | As his profession is: Lifter, my lord,<br>One that can lift a purse right cunningly. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | And is that he accuses him? |
| SURESBY | The same, my lord, whom, by your [[Honour|honour]]’s leave,<br>I must say somewhat too, because I find<br>In some respects he is [[Well|well]] worthy blame. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | Good Master Justice Suresby, speak your mind.<br>We are [[Well|well]] pleased to give you audience. |

| LIFTER | There be, sir, divers very cunning fellows<br>That while you stand and look them in the face<br>Will have your purse. |
| SURESBY | Thou’rt an honest knave.<br>Tell me, what are they, where they may be caught.<br>Ay, those are they I look for. |
| LIFTER | You talk of me, sir—<br>Alas, I am a puny. There’s one, indeed,<br>Goes by my name; he puts down all for purses |

| SURESBY | In plainer English, Lifter, if my purse<br>Be stored or no? |
| LIFTER | Ye have it, sir. |
| SURESBY | Excellent, excellent! |
| LIFTER | Then, sir, you cannot but for manners’ sake<br>Walk on with him, for he will walk your way,<br>Alleging either you have much forgot him,<br>Or he mistakes you. |
