| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Sheriffs of [[London]] and their Officers at one door, the Warders with their halberds at another |
| FIRST SHERIFF | Officers, what [[Time|time]] of day is’t? |
| OFFICER | Almost eight o’[[Clock|clock]]. |
| SECOND SHERIFF | We must make haste then, lest we stay too long. |
| FIRST WARDER | Good morrow, Master Sheriffs of [[London]]. Master<br>Lieutenant<br>Wills ye repair to the limits of the Tower,<br>There to receive your prisoner. |
| FIRST SHERIFF | (to Officer)<br>Go back and tell his worship we are ready. |
| SECOND SHERIFF | Go bid the officers make clear the way,<br>There may be passage for the prisoner. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lieutenant and his guard, with More |
| MORE | Yet God be thanked, here’s a fair day toward<br>To take our [[Journey|journey]] in. Master Lieutenant,<br>It were fair walking on the Tower leads. |
| LIEUTENANT | An so it might have liked my sovereign lord,<br>I would to God you might have walked there still.<br>He weeps |
| MORE | Sir, we are walking to a better place.<br>O sir, your kind and loving [[Tears|tears]]<br>Are like sweet odours to embalm your friend.<br>Thank your good lady; since I was your guest<br>She has made me a very wanton, in good sooth. |
| LIEUTENANT | O, I had hoped we should not yet have parted! |
| MORE | But I must leave ye for a little while. |
| *(didascalia)* | Within an hour or two you may look for me. |
| MORE | But there will be so many come to see me<br>That I shall be so proud I will not speak;<br>And sure my [[Memory|memory]] is grown so ill<br>I [[Fear|fear]] I shall forget my head behind me. |
| LIEUTENANT | God and his blessed angels be about ye!—<br>Here, Master Sheriffs, receive your prisoner. |
| MORE | Good morrow, Master Sheriffs of [[London]], to ye both.<br>I thank ye that ye will vouchsafe to meet me.<br>I see by this you have not quite forgot<br>That I was in times past as you are now,<br>A sheriff of [[London]]. |
| FIRST SHERIFF | Sir, then you know our duty doth require it. |
| MORE | I know it [[Well|well]], sir, else I would have been glad<br>You might have saved a labour at this [[Time|time]].<br>[To Second Sheriff] Ah, Master Sheriff,<br>You and I have been of old acquaintance.<br>You were a patient auditor of mine<br>When I read the divinity lecture<br>At St Laurence’s. |
| SECOND SHERIFF | [[Sir Thomas More]],<br>I have heard you oft, as many other did,<br>To our great comfort. |
| MORE | Pray God you may so now, with all my heart.<br>And, as I call to mind,<br>When I studied the law in [[Lincoln]]’s Inn<br>I was of counsel with ye in a cause. |
| SECOND SHERIFF | I was about to say so, good Sir Thomas.<br>〈 〉<br>[They pass over the stage. A ladder to the scaffold is<br><br>revealed, with the Hangman attending it] |
| MORE | O, is this the place?<br>I promise ye, it is a goodly scaffold.<br>In sooth, I am come about a headless errand,<br>For I have not much to say, now I am here.<br>[[Well]], let’s ascend, i’ God’s name.<br>In troth, methinks your stair is somewhat weak.<br>[To the Hangman] I prithee, honest friend, lend me thy<br>hand<br>To help me up. As for my coming down,<br>Let me [[Alone|alone]], I’ll look to that myself.<br>As he is going up the stairs, enters the Earls of Surrey<br>and Shrewsbury<br>My lords of Surrey and of Shrewsbury, give me your<br>hands yet before we part. Ye see, though it pleaseth the<br>[[King]] to raise me thus high, yet I am not proud; for the<br>higher I mount the better I can see my friends about me.<br>I am now on a far voyage, and this strange wooden<br>[[Horse|horse]] must bear me thither. Yet I perceive by your looks<br>you like my bargain so ill that there’s not one of ye all<br>dare venture with me. (Walking) Truly, here’s a most<br>sweet gallery. I like the air of it better than my [[Garden|garden]] at<br>Chelsea. By your patience, good people that have<br>pressed thus into my bedchamber, if you’ll not trouble<br>me I’ll take a sound [[Sleep|sleep]] here. |
| SHREWSBURY | My lord, ’twere good you’d publish to the world<br>Your great offence unto his majesty. |
| MORE | My lord, I’ll bequeath this legacy to the hangman, and do it instantly.<br>Gives him his gown<br>I confess his majesty hath been ever good to me, and my<br>offence to his highness makes me of a state pleader, a<br>stage player—though I am old and have a bad voice—to<br>act this last scene of my [[Tragedy|tragedy]]. I’ll send him, for my<br>trespass, a reverend head: somewhat bald, for it is not<br>requisite any head should stand covered to so high<br>majesty. If that content him not, because I think my<br>[[Body|body]] will then do me small pleasure, let him but bury it<br>and take it. |
| SURREY | My lord, my lord, hold conference with your [[Soul|soul]].<br>You see, my lord, the [[Time|time]] of life is short. |
| MORE | I see it, my good lord. I dispatched that business the last [[Night|night]]. I come hither only to be let [[Blood|blood]] by the hangman. My doctor here tells me it is good for the headache. |
| HANGMAN | I beseech ye, my lord, forgive me. |
| MORE | Forgive thee, honest fellow? Why? |
| HANGMAN | For your [[Death|death]], my lord. |
| MORE | O, my [[Death|death]]! I had rather it were in thy power to forgive me, for thou hast the sharpest action against me. The law, my honest friend, lies in thy hands now. Here’s thy fee.<br>[He gives him] his purse<br>And, my good fellow, let my suit be dispatched presently;<br>for ‘tis all one [[Pain|pain]] to die a lingering [[Death|death]] and to live<br>in the continual mill of a lawsuit. But I can tell thee,<br>my neck is so short that if thou shouldst behead an<br>hundred noblemen like myself, thou wouldst ne’er get<br>credit by it. Therefore—look ye, sir—do it handsomely,<br>or, of my word, thou shalt never deal with me<br>hereafter. |
| HANGMAN | I’ll take an order for that, my lord. |
| MORE | One thing more: take heed thou cutt’st not off my beard. O, I forgot, execution passed upon that last [[Night|night]], and the [[Body|body]] of it lies buried in the Tower. Stay, is’t not possible to make a scape from all this strong guard? It is.<br>There is a thing within me that will raise<br>And elevate my better part ’bove sight<br>Of these same weaker eyes. And Master Sheriffs,<br>For all this troop of steel that tends my [[Death|death]],<br>I shall break from you and fly up to heaven.<br>Let’s seek the means for this. |
| HANGMAN | My lord, I pray ye put off your doublet. 115 |
| MORE | Speak not so coldly to me; I am hoarse already.<br>I would be loath, good fellow, to take more.<br>Point me the block; I ne’er was here before. |
| HANGMAN | To the east side, my lord. |
| MORE | Then to the east.<br>We go to sigh; that o’er, to [[Sleep|sleep]] in rest.<br>Here More forsakes all mirth; good reason why:<br>The [[Fool|fool]] of flesh must with her frail life die.<br>No eye salute my trunk with a sad tear.<br>Our birth to heaven should be thus: void of [[Fear|fear]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| SURREY | A very learnèd worthy gentleman<br>Seals error with his [[Blood|blood]]. Come, we’ll to [[Court|court]].<br>Let’s sadly hence to perfect unknown fates,<br>Whilst he tends progress to the state of states. [Exeunt]<br><br>[Original Text (Munday)] |
