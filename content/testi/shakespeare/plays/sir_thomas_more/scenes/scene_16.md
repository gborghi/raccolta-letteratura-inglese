| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Sir Thomas More]], the Lieutenant, and a Servant attending, as in his chamber in the Tower |
| MORE | Master Lieutenant, is the warrant come?<br>If it be so, i’ God’s name let us know it. |
| LIEUTENANT | My lord, it is. |
| MORE | ’Tis welcome, sir, to me with all my heart.<br>His blessèd will be done. |
| LIEUTENANT | Your wisdom, sir, hath been so [[Well|well]] approved,<br>And your fair patience in [[Imprisonment|imprisonment]]<br>Hath ever shown such constancy of mind<br>And Christian resolution in all troubles,<br>As warrants us you are not unprepared. |
| MORE | No, Master Lieutenant. I thank my God<br>I have peace of [[Conscience|conscience]], though the world and I<br>Are at a little odds. But we’ll be even now, I [[Hope|hope]],<br>Ere long. When is the execution of your warrant? |
| LIEUTENANT | Tomorrow morning. |
| MORE | So, sir, I thank ye.<br>I have not lived so ill I [[Fear|fear]] to die.<br>Master Lieutenant,<br>I have had a sore fit of the stone tonight;<br>But the [[King]] hath sent me such a rare receipt,<br>I thank him, as I shall not need to [[Fear|fear]] it much. |
| LIEUTENANT | In life and [[Death|death]], still merry [[Sir Thomas More]].<br>[To Servant] Sirrah fellow, reach me the urinal.<br>He gives it him<br>Ha, let me see. There’s gravel in the [[Water|water]].<br>And yet, in very sober [[Truth|truth]] I swear,<br>The man were likely to live long enough,<br>So pleased the [[King]]. Here, fellow, take it. |
| SERVANT | Shall I go with it to the doctor, sir? |
| MORE | No, save thy labour. We’ll cozen him of a fee.<br>Thou shalt see me take a dram tomorrow morning<br>Shall cure the stone, I warrant, [[Doubt|doubt]] it not.—<br>Master Lieutenant, what news of my lord of Rochester? |
| LIEUTENANT | Yesterday morning was he put to [[Death|death]]. |
| MORE | The peace of [[Soul|soul]] [[Sleep|sleep]] with him!<br>He was a learned and a reverend prelate,<br>And a rich man, believe me. |
| LIEUTENANT | If he were rich, what is [[Sir Thomas More]],<br>That all this while hath been Lord Chancellor? |
| MORE | Say ye so, Master Lieutenant? What do you think<br>A man that with my [[Time|time]] had held my place<br>Might purchase? |
| LIEUTENANT | Perhaps, my lord, two thousand pound a year. |
| MORE | Master Lieutenant, I protest to you,<br>I never had the means in all my life<br>To purchase one poor hundred pound a year.<br>I think I am the poorest chancellor<br>That ever was in England, though I could wish,<br>For credit of the place, that my estate were better. |
| LIEUTENANT | It’s very strange. |
| MORE | It will be found as true.<br>I think, sir, that with most part of my coin<br>I have purchased as strange commodities<br>As ever you heard tell of in your life. |
| LIEUTENANT | Commodities, my lord?<br>Might I without offence enquire of them? |
| MORE | Crutches, Master Lieutenant, and bare cloaks,<br>For halting soldiers and poor needy scholars,<br>Have had my gettings in the Chancery.<br>To think but what acheat the [[Crown|crown]] shall have<br>By my attainder! I prithee, if thou beest a gentleman,<br>Get but a copy of my inventory.<br>That part of poet that was given me<br>Made me a very unthrift;<br>For this is the disease attends us all:<br>Poets were never thrifty, never shall. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lady More, mourning, Daughters, [one of them Roper’s Wife,] Master Roper |
| LIEUTENANT | O noble More!<br>My lord, your wife, your son-in-law and daughters. |
| MORE | Son Roper, welcome. Welcome, wife and girls.<br>Why do you weep? Because I live at ease?<br>Did you not see, when I was Chancellor<br>I was so cloyed with suitors every hour<br>I could not [[Sleep|sleep]] nor dine nor sup in quiet.<br>Here’s none of this. Here I can sit and talk<br>With my honest keeper half a day together,<br>Laugh and be merry. Why then should you weep? |
| ROPER | These [[Tears|tears]], my lord, for this your long restraint<br>[[Hope]] had dried up, with comfort that we yet,<br>Although imprisoned, might have had your life. |
| MORE | To live in [[Prison|prison]]: what a life were that?<br>The [[King]], I thank him, loves me more than so.<br>Tomorrow I shall be at liberty<br>To go even whither I can,<br>After I have dispatched my business. |
| LADY MORE | Ah husband, husband, yet submit yourself.<br>Have care of your poor wife and children. |
| MORE | Wife, so I have, and I do leave you all<br>To His protection hath the power to keep<br>You safer than I can,<br>The father of the widow and the [[Orphan|orphan]]. |
| ROPER | The world, my lord, hath ever held you wise,<br>And ’t shall be no distaste unto your wisdom<br>To yield to the opinion of the state. |
| MORE | I have deceived myself, I must acknowledge;<br>And as you say, son Roper, to confess the same<br>It will be no disparagement at all. |
| LADY MORE | (offering to depart)<br>His highness shall be certified thereof, immediately. |
| MORE | Nay, hear me, wife. First let me tell ye how<br>I thought to have had a barber for my beard;<br>Now I remember that were labour lost:<br>The headsman now shall cut off head and all. |
| ROPER’S WIFE | Father, his majesty, upon your meek submission,<br>Will yet, they say, receive you to his grace,<br>In as great credit as you were before. |
| MORE | ( ) wench. [[Faith]], my lord the [[King]]<br>Has appointed me to do a little business.<br>If that were past, my girl, thou then shouldst see<br>What I would say to him about that matter.<br>But I shall be so busy until then<br>I shall not tend it. |
| BOTH DAUGHTERS | Ah, my dear father! |
| LADY MORE | Dear lord and husband! |
| MORE | Be comforted, good wife, to live and [[Love|love]] my children,<br>For with thee leave I all my care of them.<br>Son Roper, for my sake that have loved thee [[Well|well]],<br>And for her virtue’s sake, cherish my [[Child|child]].—<br>Girl, be not proud, but of thy husband’s [[Love|love]].<br>Ever retain thy virtuous modesty.<br>That modesty is such a comely garment<br>As it is never out of fashion, sits as fair<br>Upon the meaner woman as the empress.<br>No stuff that gold can buy is half so rich,<br>Nor ornament that so becomes a woman.<br>Live all, and [[Love|love]] together, and thereby<br>You give your father a rich obsequy. |
| BOTH DAUGHTERS | Your blessing, dear father. |
| MORE | I must be gone—God bless you—<br>To talk with God, who now doth call. |
| LADY MORE | Ah, my dear husband— |
| MORE | Sweet wife, goodnight, goodnight.<br>God send us all his everlasting light. |
| ROPER | I think before this hour<br>More heavy hearts ne’er parted in the Tower. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [Lady More, Daughters, and Roper one way; More, |
| ROPER | Lieutenant, and Servant as into the Tower] |
