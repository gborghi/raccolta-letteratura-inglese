| *(didascalia)* | Enter at one door [[Sir Thomas More]] and [[Lord Mayor]]; at another door Sir John Munday, hurt |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | What, Sir John Munday, are you hurt? |
| SIR JOHN | A little knock, my lord. There was even now<br><br>A sort of prentices playing at cudgels.<br><br>I did command them to their masters’ houses,<br><br>But one of them, backed by the other crew,<br><br>Wounded me in the forehead with his cudgel;<br><br>And now, I [[Fear|fear]] me, they are gone to join<br><br>With [[Lincoln]], Sherwin, and their dangerous train. |
| MORE | The captains of this insurrection<br>Have ta‘en themselves to arms, and came but now<br>To both the Counters, where they have released<br>Sundry indebted prisoners, and from thence<br>I hear that they are gone into St Martin’s,<br>Where they intend to offer violence<br>To the amazed Lombards. Therefore, my lord,<br>If we expect the safety of the [[City|city]],<br>’Tis [[Time|time]] that force or parley do encounter<br>With these displeased men. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | How now, what news? |
| MESSENGER | My lord, the rebels have broke open Newgate,<br>From whence they have delivered many prisoners, zo<br>Both felons and notorious murderers<br>That desperately cleave to their lawless train. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | Up with the drawbridge! Gather some forces<br>To Cornhill and Cheapside. And, gentlemen,<br>If diligence be used on every side,<br>A quiet ebb will follow this rough tide. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Shrewsbury, Surrey, Palmer; Cholmley |
| SHREWSBURY | [[Lord Mayor]], his majesty, receiving notice<br>Of this most dangerous insurrection,<br>Hath sent my lord of Surrey and myself,<br>Sir Thomas Palmer, and our followers<br>To add unto your forces our best means<br>For pacifying of this mutiny.<br>In God’s name, then, set on with happy [[Speed|speed]].<br>The [[King]] laments if one true subject bleed. |
| SURREY | I hear they mean to [[Fire|fire]] the Lombards’ houses.<br>O power, what art thou in a madman’s eyes!<br>Thou mak’st the plodding idiot bloody-wise. |
| MORE | My lords, I [[Doubt|doubt]] not but we shall appease<br>With a calm breath this flux of discontent. |
| PALMER | To call them to a parley questionless<br>May fall out good. ’Tis [[Well|well]] said, Master More. |
| MORE | Let’s to these simple men, for many sweat<br>Under this act that knows not the law’s debt<br>Which hangs upon their lives. For silly men<br><br>Plod on they know not how; like a [[Fool|fool]]’s pen<br><br>That, ending, shows not any sentence writ<br><br>Linked but to common reason or slightest wit.<br><br>These follow for no harm, but yet incur<br>[[Self]] penalty with those that raised this stir.<br>I’ God’s name on, to calm our private foes<br>With breath of gravity, not dangerous blows. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
