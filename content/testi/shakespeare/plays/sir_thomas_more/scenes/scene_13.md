| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Sir Thomas More]], his Lady, Daughters, one of them Roper’s Wife, Master Roper, Gentlemen and |
| *(didascalia)* | Servants amongst them Catesby and Gough as in his [[house]] |
| *(didascalia)* | at Chelsea. Low stools |
| MORE | Good morrow, good son Roper. [To Lady More] Sit, good<br>madam,<br>Upon an humble seat; the [[Time|time]] so craves.<br>Rest your good heart on earth, the roof of graves.<br>You see the floor of [[Greatness|greatness]] is uneven,<br>The cricket and high throne alike near heaven.<br>Now, daughters, you that like to branches spread<br>And give best [[Shadow|shadow]] to a private [[house]]:<br>Be comforted, my girls. Your hopes stand fair.<br>Virtue breeds gentry; she makes the best heir. |
| BOTH DAUGHTERS | Good morrow to your [[Honour|honour]]. |
| MORE | Nay, good [[Night|night]] rather.<br>Your [[Honour|honour]]’s crest-fall’n with your happy father. |
| ROPER | O, what formality, what square observance,<br>Lives in a little room! Here public care<br>Gags not the eyes of slumber. Here fierce riot<br>Ruffles not proudly in a coat of trust<br>Whilst, like a pawn at chess, he keeps in rank<br>With kings and mighty fellows. Yet indeed,<br>Those men that stand on tiptoe smile to see<br>Him pawn his fortunes. |
| MORE | True, son, here’s not so,<br>Nor does the wanton tongue here screw itself<br>Into the ear, that like a vice drinks up<br>The iron instrument. |
| LADY MORE | We are here at peace. |
| MORE | Then peace, good wife. |
| LADY MORE | For keeping still in compass—a strange point<br>In [[Time|time]]’s new navigation—we have sailed<br>Beyond our course. |
| MORE | Have done. |
| LADY MORE | We are exiled the [[Court|court]]. |
| MORE | Still thou harp’st on that.<br>‘Tis sin for to deserve that banishment;<br>But he that ne’er knew [[Court|court]] courts sweet content. |
| LADY MORE | O, but dear husband— |
| MORE | I will not hear thee, wife.<br>The winding labyrinth of thy strange discourse<br>Will ne’er have end. Sit still, and, my good wife,<br>Entreat thy tongue be stilt—or, credit me,<br>Thou shalt not understand a word we speak.<br>We’ll talk in Latin.<br>[To Roper] Humida vallis raros patitur fulminis ictus.<br>More rest enjoys the subject meanly bred<br>Than he that bears the kingdom in his head. |
| ROPER | Great men are still musicians, else the world lies:<br><br>They learn low strains after the notes that rise.<br><br>Good sir, be still yourself, and but remember<br>How in this general [[Court|court]] of short-lived pleasure<br>The world, creation is the ample food<br>That is digested in the maw of [[Time|time]].<br>If man himself be subject to such ruin,<br>How shall his garment then, or the loose points<br>That tie respect unto his awe-ful place,<br>Avoid destruction? Most honoured father-in-law,<br>The [[Blood|blood]] you have bequeathed these several hearts<br>To nourish your posterity stands firm;<br>And as with joy you led us first to rise,<br>So with like hearts we’ll lock preferment’s eyes.<br>[Original Text (Munday)]<br>[Addition I (Chettle)] |
| MORE | Now will I speak like More in [[Melancholy|melancholy]];<br>For if griefs power could with her sharpest darts<br>Pierce my firm bosom, here’s sufficient cause<br>To take my farewell of mirth’s hurtless laws.<br>Poor humbled lady, thou that wert of late<br><br>Placed with the noblest women of the land,<br><br>Invited to their angel companies,<br><br>Seeming a bright star in the courtly sphere:<br><br>Why shouldst thou like a widow sit thus low,<br><br>And all thy fair consorts move from the clouds<br><br>That overdrip thy [[Beauty|beauty]] and thy worth?<br><br>I’ll tell thee the true cause. The [[Court|court]], like heaven,<br><br>Examines not the anger of the prince,<br><br>And, being more frail-composed of gilded earth,<br><br>Shines upon them on whom the [[King|king]] doth shine,<br><br>Smiles if he smile, declines if he decline,<br><br>Yet, seeing both are mortal, [[Court|court]] and [[King|king]]<br><br>Shed not one tear for any earthly thing.<br><br>For, so God pardon me, in my saddest hour<br>Thou hast no more occasion to lament,<br>Nor these, nor those, my [[Exile|exile]] from the [[Court|court]]-<br>No, nor this [[Body|body]]’s torture, were’t imposed,<br>As commonly disgraces of great men<br>Are the forewarnings of a hasty [[Death|death]]—<br>Than to behold me after many a toil<br>Honoured with endless rest. Perchance the [[King]],<br>Seeing the [[Court|court]] is full of [[Vanity|vanity]],<br>Has pity lest our souls should be misled<br>And sends us to a life contemplative.<br>O, happy banishment from worldly [[Pride|pride]],<br>When souls by private life are sanctified! |
| WIFE | O, but I [[Fear|fear]] some plot against your life. |
| MORE | Why then, ‘tis thus: the [[King]], of his high grace,<br>Seeing my faithful service to his state,<br>Intends to send me to the [[King]] of Heaven<br>For a rich present; where my [[Soul|soul]] shall prove<br>A true rememb’rer of his majesty.<br>Come, prithee mourn not. The worst chance is [[Death|death]],<br>And that brings endless joy for fickle breath. |
| WIFE | Ah, but your children. |
| MORE | Tush, let them [[Alone|alone]].<br>Say they be stripped from this poor painted cloth,<br>This outside of the earth, left houseless, bare;<br>They have minds instructed how to gather more.<br>There’s no man that’s ingenious can be poor.<br>And therefore do not weep, my little ones,<br>Though you lose all the earth. Keep your souls even<br>And you shall find inheritance in heaven.<br>But for my servants: there’s my chiefest care.<br>[To Catesby] Come hither, faithful steward. Be not<br>grieved<br>That in thy person I discharge both thee<br>And all thy other fellow officers;<br>For my great master hath discharged me.<br>If thou by serving me hast suffered loss,<br><br>Then benefit thyself by leaving me.<br><br>I [[Hope|hope]] thou hast not; for such times as these<br><br>Bring gain to officers, whoever leese.<br><br>Great lords have only name; but in the fall<br><br>Lord Spend-All’s steward’s Master Gather-All.<br><br>But I suspect not thee. Admit thou hast.<br><br>It’s good the servants save when masters waste.<br><br>But you, poor gentlemen, that had no place<br>T’enrich yourselves but by loathed bribery,<br>Which I abhorred, and never found you loved:<br>Think, when an oak falls, underwood shrinks down,<br>And yet may live, though bruised. I pray ye strive<br>To shun my ruin; for the axe is set<br>Even at my root, to fell me to the ground.<br>The best I can do to prefer you all<br>With my mean store expect; for heaven can tell<br>That More loves all his followers more than [[Well|well]].<br>[Addition I (Chettle)]<br>[Original Text (Munday)] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servant |
| SERVANT | My lord, there are new lighted at the gate<br>The Earls of Surrey and of Shrewsbury,<br>And they expect you in the inner [[Court|court]]. |
| MORE | Entreat their lordships come into the hall. |
| LADY MORE | O God, what news with them? |
| MORE | Why, how now, wife?<br>They are but come to visit their old friend. |
| LADY MORE | O God, I [[Fear|fear]], I [[Fear|fear]]. |
| MORE | What shouldst thou [[Fear|fear]], fond woman?<br>Iustum, si fractus illabatur orbis, impavidum ferient ruinae.<br><br>Here let me live estranged from great men’s looks.<br>They are like golden flies on leaden hooks. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earls for Surrey and Shrewsbury], Downes, with his mace, and Attendants |
| SHREWSBURY | Good morrow, good Sir Thomas. |
| SURREY | [to Lady More]<br>Good day, good madam.<br>Kind salutations |
| MORE | Welcome, my good lords.<br>What ails your lordships look so [[Melancholy|melancholy]]?<br>O, I know: you live in [[Court|court]], and the [[Court|court]] diet<br>Is only friend to physic. |
| SURREY | O Sir Thomas,<br>Our words are now the [[King]]‘s, and our sad looks<br>The interest of your [[Love|love]]. We are sent to you<br>From our mild sovereign once more to demand<br>If you’ll subscribe unto those articles<br>He sent ye th’other day. Be [[Well|well]] advised,<br>For, on my [[Honour|honour]], lord, [[Grave|grave]] Doctor Fisher,<br>Bishop of Rochester, at the [[Self|self]]-same instant<br>Attached with you, is sent unto the Tower<br>For the like obstinacy. His majesty<br>Hath only sent you prisoner to your [[house]],<br>But, if you now refuse for to subscribe,<br>A stricter course will follow. |
| LADY MORE | (kneeling and weeping)<br>O dear husband— |
| BOTH DAUGHTERS | (kneeling and weeping) Dear father— |
| MORE | See, my lords,<br>This partner and these subjects to my flesh<br>Prove rebels to my [[Conscience|conscience]]. But, my good lords,<br>If I refuse, must I unto the Tower? |
| SHREWSBURY | You must, my lord. [Gesturing to Downes] Here is an officer<br>Ready for to arrest you of high treason. |
| LADY MORE | and DAUGHTERS<br>O God, O God! |
| ROPER | Be patient, good madam. |
| MORE | Ay, Downes, is’t thou? I once did save thy life,<br>When else by cruel riotous assault<br>Thou hadst been torn in pieces. Thou art reserved<br>To be my summ‘ner to yon spiritual [[Court|court]].<br>Give me thy hand, good fellow. Smooth thy face.<br>The diet that thou drink’st is spiced with mace,<br>And I could ne‘er abide it. ’Twill not digest,<br>’Twill lie too heavy, man, on my weak breast. |
| SHREWSBURY | Be brief, my lord, for we are limited<br>Unto an hour. |
| MORE | Unto an hour? ’Tis [[Well|well]].<br>The bell, earth’s thunder, soon shall toll my knell. |
| LADY MORE | (kneeling)<br>Dear loving husband, if you respect not me,<br>Yet think upon your daughters. |
| MORE | (pondering to himself) Wife, stand up.<br>I have bethought me;<br>And I’ll now satisfy the [[King]]’s good pleasure. |
| BOTH DAUGHTERS | O happy alteration! |
| SHREWSBURY | Come then, subscribe, my lord. |
| SURREY | I am right glad of this your fair [[Conversion|conversion]]. |
| MORE | O pardon me,<br>I will subscribe to go unto the Tower<br>With all submissive willingness, and thereto add<br>My bones to strengthen the foundation<br>Of [[Julius Caesar]]’s palace. Now, my lord,<br>I’ll satisfy the [[King]] even with my [[Blood|blood]].<br>Nor will I wrong your patience. [To Downes] Friend, do<br>thine office. |
| DOWNES | [[Sir Thomas More]], Lord Chancellor of England, I arrest you in the [[King]]’s name of high treason. |
| MORE | Gramercies, friend.<br>To a great [[Prison|prison]], to discharge the strife<br>Commenced ‘twixt [[Conscience|conscience]] and my frailer life, 185<br>More now must march. Chelsea, adieu, adieu.<br>Strange farewell: thou shalt ne’er more see More true,<br>For I shall ne‘er see thee more.—Servants, farewell.—<br>Wife, mar not thine indifferent face. Be wise.<br>More’s widow’s husband, he must make thee rise.—<br>Daughters, ( ) what’s here, what’s here?<br>Mine eye had almost parted with a tear.—<br>Dear son, possess my virtue; that I ne’er gave.<br>[[Grave]] More thus lightly walks to a quick [[Grave|grave]]. |
| ROPER | Curae leves loquuntur, ingentes stupent. |
| MORE | You that way in. Mind you my course in [[Prayer|prayer]].<br>By [[Water|water]] I to [[Prison|prison]], to heaven through air. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [More, Downes, and Attendants at one door, the rest at another] |
