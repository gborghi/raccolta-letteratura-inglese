| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Rosalind]] as Ganymede, [[Celia]] as Aliena, and [[Jaques]] |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | I prithee, pretty youth, let me be better acquainted with thee. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | They say you are a [[Melancholy|melancholy]] fellow. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | I am so. I do [[Love|love]] it better than laughing. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Those that are in extremity of either are abominable fellows, and betray themselves to every modern censure worse than drunkards. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | Why, ’tis good to be sad and say nothing. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Why then, ’tis good to be a post. 9 |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | I have neither the scholar’s [[Melancholy|melancholy]], which is emulation, nor the musician‘s, which is fantastical, nor the courtier’s, which is proud, nor the soldier‘s, which is ambitious, nor the lawyer’s, which is politic, nor the lady‘s, which is nice, nor the [[Lover|lover]]’s, which is all these; but it is a [[Melancholy|melancholy]] of mine own, compounded of many simples, extracted from many objects, and indeed the sundry contemplation of my travels, in which my often rumination wraps me in a most humorous sadness. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | A traveller! By my [[Faith|faith]], you have great reason to be sad. I [[Fear|fear]] you have sold your own lands to see other men’s. Then to have seen much and to have nothing is to have rich eyes and poor hands. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | Yes, I have gained my experience. Enter [[Orlando]] |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | And your experience makes you sad. I had rather have a [[Fool|fool]] to make me merry than experience to make me sad—and to travel for it too! |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | Good day and happiness, dear [[Rosalind]]. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | Nay then, God b’wi’you an you talk in [[Blank Verse|blank verse]]. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Farewell, Monsieur Traveller. Look you lisp, and wear strange suits; disable all the benefits of your own country; be out of [[Love|love]] with your nativity, and almost chide God for making you that countenance you are, or I will scarce think you have swam in a gondola. Exit [[Jaques]] Why, how now, [[Orlando]]? Where have you been all this while? You a [[Lover|lover]]? An you serve me such another trick, never come in my sight more. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | My fair [[Rosalind]], I come within an hour of my promise. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]]. | Break an hour’s promise in [[Love|love]]! He that will divide a minute into a thousand parts and break but a part of the thousand part of a minute in the affairs of [[Love|love]], it may be said of him that Cupid hath clapped him o’th’ shoulder, but I’ll warrant him heartwhole. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | Pardon me, dear [[Rosalind]]. 47 |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Nay, an you be so tardy, come no more in my sight. I had as lief be wooed of a snail. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | Of a snail? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Ay, of a snail; for though he comes slowly, he carries his [[house]] on his head—a better jointure, I think, than you make a woman. Besides, he brings his destiny with him. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | What’s that? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Why, horns, which such as you are fain to be beholden to your wives for. But he comes armed in his fortune, and prevents the slander of his wife. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | Virtue is no hornmaker, and my [[Rosalind]] is virtuous. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | And I am your [[Rosalind]]. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | It pleases him to call you so; but he hath a [[Rosalind]] of a better leer than you. |
