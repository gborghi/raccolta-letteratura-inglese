| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Rosalind]] and [[Celia]] |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | I pray thee [[Rosalind]], sweet my coz, be merry. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Dear [[Celia]], I show more mirth than I am mistress of; and would you yet I were merrier? Unless you could teach me to forget a banished father you must not learn me how to remember any extraordinary pleasure. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Herein I see thou lovest me not with the full weight that I [[Love|love]] thee. If my uncle, thy banished father, had banished thy uncle, the Duke my father, so thou hadst been still with me I could have taught my [[Love|love]] to take thy father for mine. So wouldst thou, if the [[Truth|truth]] of thy [[Love|love]] to me were so righteously tempered as mine is to thee. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | [[Well]], I will forget the condition of my estate to rejoice in yours. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | You know my father hath no [[Child|child]] but I, nor none is like to have. And truly, when he dies thou shalt be his heir; for what he hath taken away from thy father perforce, I will render thee again in affection. By mine [[Honour|honour]] I will, and when I break that oath, let me turn [[Monster|monster]]. Therefore, my sweet [[Rose]], my dear [[Rose]], be merry. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | From henceforth I will, coz, and devise sports. Let me see, what think you of falling in [[Love|love]]? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Marry, I prithee do, to make sport withal; but [[Love|love]] no man in good earnest, nor no further in sport neither than with safety of a pure blush thou mayst in [[Honour|honour]] come off again. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | What shall be our sport; then? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Let us sit and mock the good housewife Fortune from her [[Wheel|wheel]], that her gifts may henceforth be bestowed equally. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I would we could do so, for her benefits are mightily misplaced; and the bountiful blind woman doth most mistake in her gifts to women. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | ’Tis true; for those that she makes fair she scarce makes honest, and those that she makes honest she makes very ill-favouredly. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Nay, now thou goest from Fortune’s office to [[Nature]]’s. Fortune reigns in gifts of the world, not in the lineaments of [[Nature|nature]]. Enter [[Touchstone]] the clown |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | No. When [[Nature]] hath made a fair creature, may she not by Fortune fall into the [[Fire|fire]]? Though [[Nature]] hath given us wit to flout at Fortune, hath not Fortune sent in this [[Fool|fool]] to cut off the argument? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Indeed, there is Fortune too hard for [[Nature]], when Fortune makes [[Nature]]’s natural the cutter-off of [[Nature]]’s wit. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Peradventure this is not Fortune’s work, neither, but [[Nature]]’s, who perceiveth our natural wits too dull to reason of such goddesses, and hath sent this natural for our whetstone; for always the dullness of the [[Fool|fool]] is the whetstone of the wits. How now, wit: whither wander you? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Mistress, you must come away to your father. [[Celia|CELIA]] Were you made the messenger? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | No, by mine [[Honour|honour]], but I was bid to come for you. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Where learned you that oath, [[Fool|fool]]? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Of a certain knight that swore ‘by his [[Honour|honour]]’ they were good pancakes, and swore ’by his [[Honour|honour]]’ the mustard was naught. Now I’ll stand to it the pancakes were naught and the mustard was good, and yet was not the knight forsworn. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | How prove you that in the great heap of your knowledge? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Ay, marry, now unmuzzle your wisdom. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Stand you both forth now. Stroke your chins, and swear by your beards that I am a knave. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | By our beards—if we had them—thou art. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | By my knavery—if I had it—then I were; but if you swear by that that is not, you are not forsworn. No more was this knight, swearing by his [[Honour|honour]], for he never had any; or if he had, he had sworn it away before ever he saw those pancakes or that mustard. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Prithee, who is’t that thou meanest? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | One that old Frederick, your father, loves.<br>[[[Celia|CELIA]]] My father’s [[Love|love]] is enough to [[Honour|honour]] him. Enough, speak no more of him; you’ll be whipped for taxation one of these days. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | The more pity that fools may not speak wisely what wise men do foolishly. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | By my troth, thou sayst true; for since the little wit that fools have was silenced, the little foolery that wise men have makes a great show. Here comes Monsieur Le Beau. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Le Beau |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | With his mouth full of news. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Which he will put on us as pigeons feed their young. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Then shall we be news-crammed. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | All the better: we shall be the more marketable. Bonjour, Monsieur Le Beau, what’s the news? |
| LE BEAU | Fair princess, you have lost much good sport. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Sport? Of what colour? |
| LE BEAU | What colour, madam? How shall I answer you? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | As wit and fortune will. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Or as the destinies decrees. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | [[Well]] said. That was laid on with a trowel. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Nay, if I keep not my rank— |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Thou losest thy old smell. |
| LE BEAU | You amaze me, ladies. I would have told you of good wrestling, which you have lost the sight of. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Yet tell us the manner of the wrestling. |
| LE BEAU | I will tell you the beginning, and if it please your ladyships you may see the end, for the best is yet to do, and here, where you are, they are coming to perform it. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | [[Well]], the beginning that is dead and buried. |
| LE BEAU | There comes an old man and his three sons— |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | I could match this beginning with an old tale. |
| LE BEAU | Three proper young men, of excellent growth and presence. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | With bills on their necks: ‘Be it known unto all men by these presents’— |
| LE BEAU | The eldest of the three wrestled with Charles, the Duke’s wrestler, which Charles in a moment threw him, and broke three of his ribs, that there is little [[Hope|hope]] of life in him. So he served the second, and so the third. Yonder they lie, the poor old man their father making such pitiful dole over them that all the beholders take his part with weeping. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Alas! |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | But what is the sport, monsieur, that the ladies have lost? |
| LE BEAU | Why, this that I speak of. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Thus men may grow wiser every day. It is the first [[Time|time]] that ever I heard breaking of ribs was sport for ladies. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Or I, I promise thee. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | But is there any else longs to see this broken music in his sides? Is there yet another dotes upon rib-breaking? Shall we see this wrestling, cousin? |
| LE BEAU | You must if you stay here, for here is the place appointed for the wrestling, and they are ready to perform it. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Yonder sure they are coming. Let us now stay and see it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter [[Duke Frederick]], Lords, [[Orlando]], Charles, and attendants |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | Come on. Since the youth will not be entreated, his own peril on his forwardness. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Is yonder the man? |
| LE BEAU | Even he, madam. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Alas, he is too young. Yet he looks successfully. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | How now, daughter and cousin; are you crept hither to see the wrestling? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Ay, my liege, so please you give us leave. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | You will take little delight in it, I can tell you, there is such odds in the man. In pity of the challenger’s youth I would fain dissuade him, but he will not be entreated. Speak to him, ladies; see if you can move him. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Call him hither, good Monsieur Le Beau. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | Do SO. I’ll not be by.<br>He stands aside |
| LE BEAU | (to [[Orlando]]) Monsieur the challenger, the Princess calls for you. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | I attend them with all respect and duty. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Young man, have you challenged Charles the wrestler? |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | No, fair Princess. He is the general challenger; I come but in as others do, to try with him the strength of my youth. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Young gentleman, your spirits are too bold for your years. You have seen cruel proof of this man’s strength. If you saw yourself with your eyes, or knew yourself with your judgement, the [[Fear|fear]] of your adventure would counsel you to a more equal enterprise. We pray you for your own sake to embrace your own safety and give over this attempt. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Do, young sir. Your reputation shall not therefore be misprized. We will make it our suit to the Duke that the wrestling might not go forward. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | I beseech you, punish me not with your hard thoughts, wherein I confess me much guilty to deny so fair and excellent ladies anything. But let your fair eyes and gentle wishes go with me to my trial, wherein if I be foiled, there is but one shamed that was never gracious, if killed, but one dead that is willing to be so. I shall do my friends no wrong, for I have none to lament me; the world no injury, for in it I have nothing. Only in the world I fill up a place which may be better supplied when I have made it empty. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | The little strength that I have, I would it were with you. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | And mine, to eke out hers. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Fare you [[Well|well]]. Pray heaven I be deceived in you. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Your heart’s desires be with you. |
| CHARLES | Come, where is this young gallant that is so desirous to lie with his mother earth? |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | Ready, sir; but his will hath in it a more modest working. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | You shall try but one fall. |
| CHARLES | No, I warrant your grace you shall not entreat him to a second that have so mightily persuaded him from a first. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | You mean to mock me after; you should not have mocked me before. But come your ways. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | (to [[Orlando]]) Now Hercules be thy [[Speed|speed]], young man! |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | I would I were invisible, to catch the strong fellow by the leg.<br>Charles and [[Orlando]] wrestle |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | O excellent young man! |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | If I had a thunderbolt in mine eye, I can tell who should down.<br>[[Orlando]] throws Charles. Shout |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | No more, no more. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | Yes, I beseech your grace.<br>I am not yet [[Well|well]] breathed. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | How dost thou, Charles? |
| LE BEAU | He cannot speak, my lord. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | Bear him away.<br>Attendants carry Charles off<br>What is thy name, young man? |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | [[Orlando]], my liege, the youngest son of Sir Rowland de Bois. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | I would thou hadst been son to some man else.<br>The world esteemed thy father honourable,<br>But I did find him still mine enemy.<br>Thou shouldst have better pleased me with this deed<br>Hadst thou descended from another [[house]].<br>But fare thee [[Well|well]], thou art a gallant youth.<br>I would thou hadst told me of another father. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Duke Frederick]], Le Beau, [[[Touchstone]],] Lords, and attendants |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | (to [[Rosalind]])<br>Were I my father, coz, would I do this? |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | I am more proud to be Sir Rowland’s son,<br>His youngest son, and would not change that calling<br>To be adopted heir to Frederick. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | My father loved Sir Rowland as his [[Soul|soul]],<br>And all the world was of my father’s mind.<br>Had I before known this young man his son<br>I should have given him [[Tears|tears]] unto entreaties<br>Ere he should thus have ventured. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Gentle cousin,<br>Let us go thank him, and encourage him.<br>My father’s rough and envious disposition<br>Sticks me at heart.—Sir, you have [[Well|well]] deserved.<br>If you do keep your promises in [[Love|love]]<br>But justly, as you have exceeded all promise,<br>Your mistress shall be happy. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | (giving him a chain from her neck) Gentleman, Wear this for me—one out of suits with fortune, That could give more but that her hand lacks means. Shall we go, coz? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Ay. Fare you [[Well|well]], fair gentleman.<br>[[Rosalind]] and [[Celia]] turn to go |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | (aside)<br>Can I not say ‘I thank you’? My better parts<br>Are all thrown down, and that which here stands up<br>Is but a quintain, a mere lifeless block. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | (to [[Celia]])<br>He calls us back. My [[Pride|pride]] fell with my fortunes,<br>I’ll ask him what he would.—Did you call, sir?<br>Sir, you have wrestled [[Well|well]], and overthrown<br>More than your enemies. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Will you go, coz? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Have with you. (To [[Orlando]]) Fare you [[Well|well]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Rosalind]] and [[Celia]] |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | What passion hangs these weights upon my tongue?<br>I cannot speak to her, yet she urged conference. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Le Beau |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | O poor [[Orlando]]! Thou art overthrown.<br>Or Charles or something weaker masters thee. |
| LE BEAU | Good sir, I do in [[Friendship|friendship]] counsel you<br>To leave this place. Albeit you have deserved<br>High commendation, true applause, and [[Love|love]],<br>Yet such is now the Duke’s condition<br>That he misconsters all that you have done.<br>The Duke is humorous. What he is indeed<br>More suits you to conceive than I to speak of. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | I thank you, sir. And pray you tell me this,<br>Which of the two was daughter of the Duke<br>That here was at the wrestling? |
| LE BEAU | Neither his daughter, if we judge by manners—<br>But yet indeed the shorter is his daughter.<br>The other is daughter to the banished Duke,<br>And here detained by her usurping uncle<br>To keep his daughter company, whose loves<br>Are dearer than the natural bond of sisters.<br>But I can tell you that of late this Duke<br>Hath ta‘en displeasure ’gainst his gentle niece,<br>Grounded upon no other argument<br>But that the people praise her for her virtues<br>And pity her for her good father’s sake.<br>And, on my life, his malice ’gainst the lady<br>Will suddenly break forth. Sir, fare you [[Well|well]].<br>Hereafter, in a better world than this,<br>I shall [[Desire|desire]] more [[Love|love]] and knowledge of you. |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | I rest much bounden to you. Fare you [[Well|well]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Le Beau |
| [[Orlando|ORLANDO]] | Thus must I from the smoke into the smother,<br>From [[Tyrant|tyrant]] Duke unto a [[Tyrant|tyrant]] brother.—<br>But heavenly [[Rosalind]]! Exit<br><br>13. Enter [[Celia]] and [[Rosalind]] |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Why cousin, why [[Rosalind]]—Cupid have [[Mercy|mercy]], not a word? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Not one to throw at a dog. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | No, thy words are too precious to be cast away upon curs. Throw some of them at me. Come, lame me with reasons. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Then there were two cousins laid up, when the one should be lamed with reasons and the other mad without any. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | But is all this for your father? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | No, some of it is for my [[Child|child]]’s father. O how full of briers is this working-day world! |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | They are but burs, cousin, thrown upon thee in holiday foolery. If we walk not in the trodden paths our very petticoats will catch them. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I could shake them off my coat. These burs are in my heart. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Hem them away. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I would try, if I could cry ‘hem’ and have him. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Come, come, wrestle with thy affections. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | O, they take the part of a better wrestler than myself. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | O, a good wish upon you! You will try in [[Time|time]], in despite of a fall. But turning these jests out of service, let us talk in good earnest. Is it possible on such a sudden you should fall into so strong a liking with old Sir Rowland’s youngest son? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | The Duke my father loved his father dearly. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Doth it therefore ensue that you should [[Love|love]] his son dearly? By this kind of chase I should hate him, for my father hated his father dearly; yet I hate not [[Orlando]]. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | No, [[Faith|faith]], hate him not, for my sake. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Why should I not? Doth he not deserve [[Well|well]]? Enter [[Duke Frederick]], with Lords |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Let me [[Love|love]] him for that, and do you [[Love|love]] him because I do. Look, here comes the Duke. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | With his eyes full of anger. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | (to [[Rosalind]]) Mistress, dispatch you with your safest haste, And get you from our [[Court|court]]. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Me, uncle? |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | You, cousin. Within these ten days if that thou beest found So near our public [[Court|court]] as twenty miles, Thou diest for it. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I do beseech your grace<br>Let me the knowledge of my fault bear with me.<br>If with myself I hold intelligence,<br>Or have acquaintance with mine own desires,<br>If that I do not dream, or be not frantic—<br>As I do trust I am not—then, dear uncle,<br>Never so much as in a thought [[unborn]]<br>Did I offend your highness. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | Thus do all traitors.<br>If their purgation did consist in words<br>They are as [[Innocent|innocent]] as grace itself.<br>Let it suffice thee that I trust thee not. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Yet your mistrust cannot make me a traitor.<br>Tell me whereon the likelihood depends? |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | Thou art thy father’s daughter—there’s enough. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | So was I when your highness took his dukedom;<br>So was I when your highness banished him.<br>Treason is not inherited, my lord,<br>Or if we did derive it from our friends,<br>What’s that to me? My father was no traitor.<br>Then, good my liege, mistake me not so much<br>To think my [[Poverty|poverty]] is treacherous. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Dear sovereign, hear me speak. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | Ay, [[Celia]], we stayed her for your sake,<br>Else had she with her father ranged along. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | I did not then entreat to have her stay.<br>It was your pleasure, and your own remorse.<br>I was too young that [[Time|time]] to value her,<br>But now I know her. If she be a traitor,<br>Why, so am I. We still have slept together,<br>[[Rose]] at an instant, learned, played, eat together,<br>And wheresoe’er we went, like Juno’s swans<br>Still we went coupled and inseparable. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | She is too subtle for thee, and her smoothness,<br>Her very [[Silence|silence]], and her patience<br>Speak to the people, and they pity her.<br>Thou art a [[Fool|fool]]. She robs thee of thy name,<br>And thou wilt show more bright and seem more<br>virtuous<br>When she is gone. Then open not thy lips.<br>Firm and irrevocable is my doom<br>Which I have passed upon her. She is banished. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Pronounce that sentence then on me, my liege.<br>I cannot live out of her company. |
| [[Duke Frederick|DUKE FREDERICK]] | You are a foot.—You, niece, provide yourself.<br>If you outstay the [[Time|time]], upon mine [[Honour|honour]]<br>And in the [[Greatness|greatness]] of my word, you die. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Duke Frederick]], with Lords |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | O my poor [[Rosalind]], whither wilt thou go?<br>Wilt thou change fathers? I will give thee mine.<br>I charge thee, be not thou more grieved than I am. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I have more cause. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Thou hast not, cousin.<br>Prithee, be cheerful. Know’st thou not the Duke<br>Hath banished me, his daughter? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | That he hath not. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | No, hath not? [[Rosalind]], lack’st thou then the [[Love|love]]<br>Which teacheth thee that thou and I am one?<br>Shall we be sundered? Shall we part, sweet girl?<br>No. Let my father seek another heir.<br>Therefore devise with me how we may fly,<br>Whither to go, and what to bear with us,<br>And do not seek to take your change upon you,<br>To bear your griefs yourself, and leave me out.<br>For by this heaven, now at our sorrows pale,<br>Say what thou canst, I’ll go along with thee. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Why, whither shall we go? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | To seek my uncle in the forest of Ardenne. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Alas, what danger will it be to us,<br>Maids as we are, to travel forth so far!<br>[[Beauty]] provoketh thieves sooner than gold. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | I’ll put myself in poor and mean attire,<br>And with a kind of umber smirch my face.<br>The like do you, so shall we pass along<br>And never stir assailants. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Were it not better,<br>Because that I am more than common tall,<br>That I did suit me all points like a man,<br>A gallant curtal-axe upon my thigh,<br>A boar-spear in my hand, and in my heart,<br>Lie there what hidden woman’s [[Fear|fear]] there will.<br>We’ll have a swashing and a martial outside,<br>As many other mannish cowards have,<br>That do outface it with their semblances. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | What shall I call thee when thou art a man? |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I’ll have no worse a name than Jove’s own page,<br>And therefore look you call me Ganymede.<br>But what will you be called? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Something that hath a reference to my state.<br>No longer [[Celia]], but Aliena. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | But cousin, what if we essayed to steal<br>The clownish [[Fool|fool]] out of your father’s [[Court|court]].<br>Would he not be a comfort to our travel? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | He’ll go along o’er the wide world with me.<br>Leave me [[Alone|alone]] to woo him. Let’s away,<br>And get our jewels and our wealth together,<br>Devise the fittest [[Time|time]] and safest way<br>To hide us from pursuit that will be made<br>After my flight. Now go we in content,<br>To liberty, and not to banishment. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
