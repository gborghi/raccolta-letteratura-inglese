| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Rosalind]] as Ganymede and [[Celia]] as Aliena |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Never talk to me. I will weep. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Do, I prithee, but yet have the grace to consider that [[Tears|tears]] do not become a man. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | But have I not cause to weep? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | As good cause as one would [[Desire|desire]]; therefore weep. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | His very hair is of the dissembling colour. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Something browner than Judas’s. Marry, his kisses are Judas’s own children. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I’[[Faith|faith]], his hair is of a good colour. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | An excellent colour. Your chestnut was ever the only colour. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | And his kissing is as full of sanctity as the touch of holy bread. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | He hath bought a pair of cast lips of [[Diana]]. A nun of [[Winter|winter]]’s sisterhood kisses not more religiously. The very ice of chastity is in them. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | But why did he swear he would come this morning, and comes not? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Nay, certainly, there is no [[Truth|truth]] in him. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Do you think so? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Yes. I think he is not a pick-purse, nor a [[Horse|horse]]-stealer; but for his verity in [[Love|love]], I do think him as concave as a covered goblet, or a worm-eaten nut. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Not true in [[Love|love]]? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | Yes, when he is in. But I think he is not in. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | You have heard him swear downright he was. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | ‘Was’ is not ‘is’. Besides, the oath of a [[Lover|lover]] is no stronger than the word of a tapster. They are both the confirmer of false reckonings. He attends here in the forest on the Duke your father. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I met the Duke yesterday, and had much question with him. He asked me of what parentage I was. I told him, of as good as he, so he laughed and let me go. But what talk we of fathers when there is such a man as [[Orlando]]? |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | O that’s a brave man. He writes brave verses, speaks brave words, swears brave oaths, and breaks them bravely, quite traverse, athwart the heart of his [[Lover|lover]], as a puny tilter that spurs his [[Horse|horse]] but on one side breaks his staff, like a noble goose. But all’s brave that youth mounts, and folly guides. Who comes here? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Corin |
| CORIN | Mistress and master, you have oft enquired<br>After the shepherd that complained of [[Love|love]]<br>Who you saw sitting by me on the turf,<br>Praising the proud disdainful shepherdess<br>That was his mistress. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | [[Well]], and what of him? |
| CORIN | If you will see a pageant truly played<br>Between the pale complexion of true [[Love|love]]<br>And the red glow of scorn and proud disdain,<br>Go hence a little, and I shall conduct you,<br>If you will mark it. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | (to [[Celia]]) O come, let us remove.<br>The sight of [[lovers]] feedeth those in [[Love|love]].<br>(To Corin) Bring us to this sight, and you shall say<br>I’ll prove a busy actor in their play. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
