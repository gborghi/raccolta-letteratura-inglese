| PRINCE HARRY | No, I will sit and watch here by the [[King]]. Exeunt all but the [[King]] and Prince Harry Why doth the [[Crown|crown]] lie there upon his pillow, Being so troublesome a bedfellow? O polished perturbation, golden care, That keep‘st the ports of slumber open wide To many a watchful [[Night|night]]!—[[Sleep]] with it now; Yet not so sound, and half so deeply sweet, As he whose brow with homely biggen bound Snores out the watch of [[Night|night]]. O majesty, When thou dost pinch thy bearer, thou dost sit Like a rich armour worn in heat of day, That scald’st with safety.—By his gates of breath There lies a downy feather which stirs not. Did he suspire, that light and weightless down Perforce must move.—My gracious lord, my father!— This [[Sleep|sleep]] is sound indeed. This is a [[Sleep|sleep]] That from this golden rigol hath divorced So many English kings.—Thy due from me Is [[Tears|tears]] and heavy sorrows of the [[Blood|blood]], Which [[Nature|nature]], [[Love|love]], and filial tenderness Shall, O dear father, pay thee plenteously. My due from thee is this imperial [[Crown|crown]], Which, as immediate from thy place and [[Blood|blood]], Derives itself to me. He puts the [[Crown|crown]] on his head Lo where it sits, Which God shall guard; and put the world’s whole strength Into one giant arm, it shall not force This lineal [[Honour|honour]] from me. This from thee Will I to mine leave, as ’tis left to me. Exit Music ceases. The [[King]] awakes |
