| *(didascalia)* | Enter in arms the Archbishop of York, Thomas [[Mowbray]], Lord [[Hastings]], and Coleville, within the Forest of Gaultres |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | What is this forest called? |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | ’Tis Gaultres Forest, an’t shall please your grace. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Here stand, my lords, and send discoverers forth<br>To know the numbers of our enemies. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | We have sent forth already. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | ’Tis [[Well|well]] done.<br>My friends and brethren in these great affairs,<br>I must acquaint you that I have received<br>New-dated letters from Northumberland,<br>Their cold intent, tenor, and substance, thus:<br>Here doth he wish his person, with such powers<br>As might hold sortance with his quality,<br>The which he could not levy; whereupon<br>He is retired to ripe his growing fortunes<br>To Scotland, and concludes in hearty prayers<br>That your attempts may overlive the hazard 15<br>And fearful meeting of their opposite. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Thus do the hopes we have in him touch ground<br>And dash themselves to pieces. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | Now, what news? |
| MESSENGER | West of this forest, scarcely off a mile,<br>In goodly form comes on the enemy;<br>And, by the ground they hide, I judge their number<br>Upon or near the rate of thirty thousand. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | The just proportion that we gave them out.<br>Let us sway on, and face them in the field. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Westmorland |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | What [[Well|well]]-appointed leader fronts us here? |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | I think it is my lord of Westmorland. |
| WESTMORLAND | Health and fair greeting from our general,<br>The Prince, Lord John and Duke of Lancaster. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Say on, my lord of Westmorland, in peace,<br>What doth concern your coming. |
| WESTMORLAND | Then, my lord,<br>Unto your grace do I in chief address<br>The substance of my speech. If that rebellion<br>Came like itself, in base and abject routs,<br>Led on by bloody youth, guarded with rags,<br>And countenanced by boys and beggary;<br>I say, if damned commotion so appeared<br>In his true native and most proper shape,<br>You, reverend father, and these noble lords<br>Had not been here to dress the ugly form<br>Of base and bloody insurrection<br>With your fair honours. You, Lord Archbishop,<br>Whose see is by a civil peace maintained,<br>Whose beard the silver hand of peace hath touched,<br>Whose learning and good letters peace hath tutored,<br>Whose white investments figure [[innocence]],<br>The dove and very blessed spirit of peace,<br>Wherefore do you so ill translate yourself<br>Out of the speech of peace that bears such grace<br>Into the harsh and boist’rous tongue of war,<br>Turning your books to graves, your ink to [[Blood|blood]],<br>Your pens to lances, and your tongue divine<br>To a loud trumpet and a point of war? |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Wherefore do I this? So the question stands.<br>Briefly, to this end: we are all diseased,<br>And with our surfeiting and wanton hours<br>Have brought ourselves into a burning fever,<br>And we must bleed for it—of which disease<br>Our late [[King]] [[Richard]], being infected, died.<br>But, my most noble lord of Westmorland,<br>I take not on me here as a physician,<br>Nor do I as an enemy to peace<br>Troop in the throngs of military men;<br>But rather show a while like fearful war<br>To diet rank minds, sick of happiness,<br>And purge th’obstructions which begin to stop<br>Our very veins of life. Hear me more plainly.<br>I have in equal balance justly weighed<br>What wrongs our arms may do, what wrongs we suffer,<br>And find our griefs heavier than our offences.<br>We see which way the stream of [[Time|time]] doth run,<br>And are enforced from our most quiet shore<br>By the rough torrent of occasion;<br>And have the summary of all our griefs,<br>When [[Time|time]] shall serve, to show in articles,<br>Which long ere this we offered to the [[King]],<br>And might by no suit gain our audience.<br>When we are wronged, and would unfold our griefs,<br>We are denied access unto his person<br>Even by those men that most have done us wrong.<br>The dangers of the days but newly gone, 80<br>Whose [[Memory|memory]] is written on the earth<br>With yet appearing [[Blood|blood]], and the examples<br>Of every minute’s instance, present now,<br>Hath put us in these ill-beseeming arms,<br>Not to break peace, or any branch of it,<br>But to establish here a peace indeed,<br>Concurring both in name and quality. |
| WESTMORLAND | Whenever yet was your appeal denied?<br>Wherein have you been gallèd by the [[King]]?<br>What peer hath been suborned to grate on you,<br>That you should seal this lawless bloody book<br>Of forged rebellion with a seal divine? |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | My brother general, the commonwealth<br>I make my quarrel in particular. |
| WESTMORLAND | There is no need of any such redress;<br>Or if there were, it not belongs to you. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Why not to him in part, and to us all<br>That feel the bruises of the days before,<br>And suffer the condition of these times<br>To lay a heavy and unequal hand<br>Upon our honours? |
| WESTMORLAND | O my good Lord [[Mowbray]],<br>Construe the times to their necessities,<br>And you shall say indeed it is the [[Time|time]],<br>And not the [[King]], that doth you injuries.<br>Yet for your part, it not appears to me, 105<br>Either from the [[King]] or in the present [[Time|time]],<br>That you should have an inch of any ground<br>To build a grief on. Were you not restored<br>To all the Duke of Norfolk’s signories,<br>Your noble and right [[Well|well]]-remembered father’s? 110 |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | What thing in [[Honour|honour]] had my father lost<br>That need to be revived and breathed in me?<br>The [[King]] that loved him, as the state stood then,<br>Was force perforce compelled to banish him;<br>And then that [[Henry Bolingbroke]] and he, 115<br>Being mounted and both roused in their seats,<br>Their neighing coursers daring of the spur,<br>Their armed staves in charge, their beavers down,<br>Their eyes of [[Fire|fire]] sparkling through sights of steel,<br>And the loud trumpet blowing them together,<br>Then, then, when there was nothing could have stayed<br>My father from the breast of Bolingbroke—<br>O, when the [[King]] did throw his warder down,<br>His own life hung upon the staff he threw;<br>Then threw he down himself and all their lives 125<br>That by indictment and by dint of [[Sword|sword]]<br>Have since miscarried under Bolingbroke. |
| WESTMORLAND | You speak, Lord [[Mowbray]], now you know not what.<br>The Earl of Hereford was reputed then<br>In England the most valiant gentleman.<br>Who knows on whom fortune would then have<br>smiled?<br>But if your father had been victor there,<br>He ne’er had borne it out of Coventry;<br>For all the country in a general voice<br>Cried hate upon him, and all their prayers and [[Love|love]]<br>Were set on Hereford, whom they doted on<br>And blessed and graced, indeed, more than the [[King]].<br>But this is mere digression from my purpose.<br>Here come I from our princely general<br>To know your griefs, to tell you from his grace<br>That he will give you audience; and wherein<br>It shall appear that your demands are just,<br>You shall enjoy them, everything set off<br>That might so much as think you enemies. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | But he hath forced us to compel this offer,<br>And it proceeds from policy, not [[Love|love]]. |
| WESTMORLAND | [[Mowbray]], you overween to take it so.<br>This offer comes from [[Mercy|mercy]], not from [[Fear|fear]];<br>For lo, within a ken our army lies,<br>Upon mine [[Honour|honour]], all too confident<br>To give admittance to a thought of [[Fear|fear]].<br>Our battle is more full of names than yours,<br>Our men more perfect in the use of arms,<br>Our armour all as strong, our cause the best.<br>Then reason will our hearts should be as good.<br>Say you not then our offer is compelled. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | [[Well]], by my will we shall admit no parley. |
| WESTMORLAND | That argues but the shame of your offence.<br>A rotten case abides no handling. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | Hath the [[Prince John]] a full commission,<br>In very ample virtue of his father,<br>To hear and absolutely to determine<br>Of what conditions we shall stand upon? |
| WESTMORLAND | That is intended in the general’s name.<br>I muse you make so slight a question. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Then take, my lord of Westmorland, this schedule;<br>For this contains our general grievances.<br>Each several article herein redressed,<br>All members of our cause, both here and hence,<br>That are ensinewed to this action<br>Acquitted by a true substantial form,<br>And present execution of our wills<br>To us and to our purposes consigned,<br>We come within our awe-full banks again,<br>And knit our powers to the arm of peace. |
| WESTMORLAND | (taking the schedule)<br>This will I show the general. Please you, lords,<br>In sight of both our battles we may meet,<br>And either end in peace—which God so frame—<br>Or to the place of diff’rence call the swords<br>Which must decide it. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | My lord, we will do so. 180 |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Westmorland |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | There is a thing within my bosom tells me<br>That no conditions of our peace can stand. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | [[Fear]] you not that. If we can make our peace<br>Upon such large terms and so absolute<br>As our conditions shall consist upon,<br>Our peace shall stand as firm as rocky [[Mountains|mountains]]. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Yea, but our valuation shall be such<br>That every slight and false-derivèd cause,<br>Yea, every idle, nice, and wanton reason,<br>Shall to the [[King]] taste of this action,<br>That, were our royal faiths martyrs in [[Love|love]],<br>We shall be winnowed with so rough a [[Wind|wind]]<br>That even our corn shall seem as light as chaff,<br>And good from bad find no partition. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | No, no, my lord; note this. The [[King]] is weary<br>Of dainty and such picking grievances,<br>For he hath found to end one [[Doubt|doubt]] by [[Death|death]]<br>Revives two greater in the heirs of life;<br>And therefore will he wipe his tables clean,<br>And keep no tell-tale to his [[Memory|memory]]<br>That may repeat and [[history]] his loss<br>To new remembrance; for full [[Well|well]] he knows<br>He cannot so precisely weed this land<br>As his misdoubts present occasion.<br>His foes are so enrooted with his friends<br>That, plucking to unfix an enemy,<br>He doth unfasten so and shake a friend;<br>So that this land, like an offensive wife<br>That hath enraged him on to offer strokes,<br>As he is striking, holds his infant up, 210<br>And hangs resolved correction in the arm<br>That was upreared to execution. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | Besides, the [[King]] hath wasted all his rods<br>On late offenders, that he now doth lack<br>The very instruments of chastisement;<br>So that his power, like to a fangless [[Lion|lion]],<br>May offer, but not hold. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | ’Tis very true.<br>And therefore be assured, my good Lord Marshal,<br>If we do now make our atonement [[Well|well]],<br>Our peace will, like a broken limb united,<br>Grow stronger for the breaking. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Be it so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Westmorland |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Here is returned my lord of Westmorland. |
| WESTMORLAND | The Prince is here at hand. Pleaseth your lordship<br>To meet his grace just distance ’tween our armies? |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Your grace of York, in God’s name then set forward. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Before, and greet his grace!—My lord, we come. |
| *(didascalia)* | They march over the stage. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Prince John]] with one or more soldiers |
| *(didascalia)* | carrying [[Wind|wind]] |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | You are [[Well|well]] encountered here, my cousin [[Mowbray]].<br>Good day to you, gentle lord Archbishop;<br>And so to you, Lord [[Hastings]], and to all.<br>My lord of York, it better showed with you<br>When that your flock, assembled by the bell,<br>Encircled you to hear with reverence<br>Your exposition on the holy text,<br>Than now to see you here an iron man,<br>Cheering a rout of rebels with your drum,<br>Turning the word to [[Sword|sword]], and life to [[Death|death]].<br>That man that sits within a monarch’s heart<br>And ripens in the sunshine of his favour,<br>Would he abuse the countenance of the [[King]],<br>Alack, what mischiefs might he set abroach<br>In [[Shadow|shadow]] of such [[Greatness|greatness]]! With you, Lord Bishop,<br>It is even so. Who hath not heard it spoken<br>How deep you were within the books of God—<br>To us, the speaker in his parliament,<br>To us, th‘imagined voice of God himself,é<br>The very opener and intelligencer<br>Between the grace, the sanctities of heaven<br>And our dull workings? O, who shall believe<br>But you misuse the reverence of your place,<br>Employ the countenance and grace of heav’n<br>As a false favourite doth his prince’s name<br>In deeds dishonourable? You have ta’en up,<br>Under the counterfeited zeal of God,<br>The subjects of his substitute, my father;<br>And, both against the peace of heaven and him,<br>Have here upswarmèd them. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Good my lord of Lancaster,<br>I am not here against your father’s peace;<br>But, as I told my lord of Westmorland,<br>The [[Time|time]] misordered doth, in common sense,<br>Crowd us and crush us to this monstrous form, 260<br>To hold our safety up. I sent your grace<br>The parcels and particulars of our grief,<br>The which hath been with scorn shoved from the<br>[[Court|court]],<br>Whereon this Hydra son of war is born;<br>Whose dangerous eyes may [[Well|well]] be charmed asleep<br>With grant of our most just and right desires,<br>And true obedience, of this [[Madness|madness]] cured,<br>Stoop tamely to the foot of majesty. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | If not, we ready are to try our fortunes<br>To the last man. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | And though we here fall down,<br>We have supplies to second our attempt.<br>If they miscarry, theirs shall second them;<br>And so [[success]] of mischief shall be born,<br>And heir from heir shall hold this quarrel up,<br>Whiles England shall have generation. |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | You are too shallow, [[Hastings]], much too shallow,<br>To sound the [[Bottom|bottom]] of the after-times. |
| WESTMORLAND | Pleaseth your grace to answer them directly<br>How far forth you do like their articles? |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | I like them all, and do allow them [[Well|well]], 280<br>And swear here, by the [[Honour|honour]] of my [[Blood|blood]],<br>My father’s purposes have been mistook,<br>And some about him have too lavishly<br>Wrested his meaning and authority.<br>(To the Archbishop)<br>My lord, these griefs shall be with [[Speed|speed]] redressed;<br>Upon my [[Soul|soul]] they shall. If this may please you,<br>Discharge your powers unto their several counties,<br>As we will ours; and here between the armies<br>Let’s drink together friendly and embrace,<br>That all their eyes may bear those tokens [[Home|home]]<br>Of our restored [[Love|love]] and amity. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | I take your princely word for these redresses. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | I give it you, and will maintain my word;<br>And thereupon I drink unto your grace.<br>He drinks |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] to Coleville |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Go, captain, and deliver to the army<br>This news of peace. Let them have pay, and part.<br>I know it will [[Well|well]] please them. Hie thee, captain. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Coleville |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | To you, my noble lord of Westmorland!<br>He drinks |
| WESTMORLAND | (drinking)<br>I pledge your grace. An if you knew what pains<br>I have bestowed to breed this present peace,<br>You would drink freely; but my [[Love|love]] to ye<br>Shall show itself more openly hereafter. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | I do not [[Doubt|doubt]] you. |
| WESTMORLAND | I am glad of it.<br>(Drinking) Health to my lord and gentle cousin [[Mowbray]]! |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | You wish me health in very happy season,<br>For I am on the sudden something ill. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Against ill chances men are ever merry;<br>But heaviness foreruns the good event. |
| WESTMORLAND | Therefore be merry, coz, since sudden sorrow<br>Serves to say thus: some good thing comes tomorrow. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Believe me, I am passing light in spirit. 311 |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | So much the worse, if your own rule be true. |
| *(didascalia)* | Shout within |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | The word of peace is rendered. Hark how they shout. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | This had been cheerful after victory. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | A peace is of the [[Nature|nature]] of a conquest,<br>For then both parties nobly are subdued,<br>And neither party loser. |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | (to Westmorland) Go, my lord,<br>And let our army be discharged too. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Westmorland |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | (To the Archbishop) And, good my lord, so please you,<br>let our trains<br>March by us, that we may peruse the men<br>We should have coped withal. |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Go, good Lord [[Hastings]],<br>And ere they be dismissed, let them march by. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Hastings]] |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | I trust, lords, we shall lie tonight together. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Westmorland, with captains |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | Now, cousin, wherefore stands our army still? |
| WESTMORLAND | The leaders, having charge from you to stand,<br>Will not go off until they hear you speak. |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | They know their duties. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord [[Hastings]] |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] to the Archbishop Our army is dispersed. |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | Like youthful steers unyoked, they take their courses,<br>East, west, north, south; or, like a school broke up,<br>Each hurries toward his [[Home|home]] and sporting place. |
| WESTMORLAND | Good tidings, my lord [[Hastings]], for the which<br>I do arrest thee, traitor, of high treason;<br>And you, Lord Archbishop, and you, Lord [[Mowbray]],<br>Of capital treason I attach you both. |
| *(didascalia)* | The captains guard [[Hastings]], the Archbishop, and [[Mowbray]] |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Is this proceeding just and honourable? |
| WESTMORLAND | Is your assembly so? |
| ARCHBISHOP OF YORK | Will you thus break your [[Faith|faith]]? |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | I pawned thee none.<br>I promised you redress of these same grievances<br>Whereof you did complain; which, by mine [[Honour|honour]],<br>I will perform with a most Christian care.<br>But for you rebels, look to taste the due<br>Meet for rebellion and such acts as yours.<br>Most shallowly did you these arms commence,<br>Fondly brought here, and foolishly sent hence.—345<br>Strike up our drums, pursue the scattered stray.<br>God, and not we, hath safely fought today.<br>Some guard these traitors to the block of [[Death|death]],<br>Treason’s true bed and yielder up of breath. Exeunt |
