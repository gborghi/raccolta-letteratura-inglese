| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Mistress Quickly]] (the hostess of a tavern), and an officer, Fang followed at a distance by another officer, Snare |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Master Fang, have you entered the action ? |
| FANG | It is entered. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Where’s your yeoman? Is’t a lusty yeoman? Will a stand to’t? |
| FANG | Sirrah!—Where’s Snare? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | O Lord, ay, good Master Snare. |
| *(didascalia)* | SNARE coming forward Here, here. |
| FANG | Snare, we must arrest Sir John [[Falstaff]]. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Yea, good Master Snare, I have entered him and all. |
| SNARE | It may chance cost some of us our lives, for he will stab. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Alas the day, take heed of him; he stabbed me in mine own [[house]], most beastly, in good [[Faith|faith]]. A cares not what mischief he does; if his weapon be out, he will foin like any devil, he will spare neither man, woman, nor [[Child|child]]. |
| FANG | If I can close with him, I care not for his thrust. [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] No, nor I neither. I’ll be at your elbow. FANG An I but fist him once, an a come but within my vice— |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | I am undone by his going, I warrant you; he’s an infinitive thing upon my score. Good Master Fang, hold him sure. Good Master Snare, let him not scape. A comes continuantly to Pie Corner—saving your manhoods—to buy a saddle, and he is indited to dinner to the Lubber’s Head in Lombard Street, to Master Smooth’s the silkman. I pray you, since my exion is entered, and my case so openly known to the world, let him be brought in to his answer. A hundred mark is a long one for a poor lone woman to bear; and I have borne, and borne, and borne, and have been fobbed off, and fobbed off, and fobbed off, from this day to that day, that it is a shame to be thought on. There is no honesty in such dealing, unless a woman should be made an ass and a beast, to bear every knave’s wrong. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir John [[Falstaff]], Bardolph, and the Page |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Yonder he comes, and that arrant malmsey-nose knave Bardolph with him. Do your offices, do your offices, Master Fang and Master Snare; do me, do me, do me your offices. |
| SIR JOHN | How now, whose mare’s dead? What’s the matter ? |
| FANG | Sir John, I arrest you at the suit of [[Mistress Quickly]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | SIR JOHN drawing Away, varlets! Draw, Bardolph! Cut me off the [[Villain|villain]]’s head! Throw the quean in the channel! |
| *(didascalia)* | Bardolph draws |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Throw me in the channel? I’ll throw thee in the channel!<br>A brawl<br>Wilt thou, wilt thou, thou bastardly rogue? Murder, murder! Ah, thou honeysuckle [[Villain|villain]], wilt thou kill God’s officers, and the [[King]]’s? Ah, thou honeyseed rogue! Thou art a honeyseed, a man-queller, and a woman-queller. |
| SIR JOHN | Keep them off, Bardolph ! |
| FANG | A rescue, a rescue! |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Good people, bring a rescue or two. Thou wot, wot thou? Thou wot, wot’a? Do, do, thou rogue, do, thou hempseed! |
| PAGE | Away, you scullion, you rampallian, you fustilarian! I’ll tickle your catastrophe! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Lord Chief Justice]] and his men |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | What is the matter? Keep the peace here, ho! |
| *(didascalia)* | Brawl ends. Fang seizes Sir John |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Good my lord, be good to me; I beseech you, stand to me. |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | How now, Sir John? What, are you brawling here?<br>Doth this become your place, your [[Time|time]] and business?<br>You should have been [[Well|well]] on your way to York. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Fang Stand from him, fellow. Wherefore hang’st |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | thou upon him? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | O my most worshipful lord, an’t please your grace, I am a poor widow of Eastcheap, and he is arrested at my suit. |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | For what sum? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | It is more than for some, my lord, it is for all, all I have. He hath eaten me out of [[house]] and [[Home|home]]. He hath put all my substance into that fat belly of his; (to Sir John) but I will have some of it out again, or I will ride thee a-nights like the mare. |
| SIR JOHN | I think I am as like to ride the mare, if I have any vantage of ground to get up. |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | How comes this, Sir John ? Fie, what man of good temper would endure this tempest of exclamation? Are you not ashamed, to enforce a poor widow to so rough a course to come by her own? |
| SIR JOHN | (to the Hostess) What is the gross sum that I owe thee? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Marry, if thou wert an honest man, thyself, and the money too. Thou didst swear to me upon a parcel-gilt goblet, sitting in my Dolphin chamber, at the round table, by a sea-coal [[Fire|fire]], upon Wednesday in Wheeson week, when the Prince broke thy head for liking his father to a singing-man of Windsor—thou didst swear to me then, as I was washing thy wound, to marry me, and make me my lady thy wife. Canst thou deny it? Did not goodwife Keech the butcher’s wife come in then, and call me ‘Gossip Quickly’—coming in to borrow a mess of vinegar, telling us she had a good dish of prawns, whereby thou didst [[Desire|desire]] to eat some, whereby I told thee they were ill for a green wound ? And didst thou not, when she was gone downstairs, [[Desire|desire]] me to be no more so familiarity with such poor people, saying that ere long they should call me ‛madam’? And didst thou not kiss me, and bid me fetch thee thirty shillings? I put thee now to thy book-oath; deny it if thou canst. |
| *(didascalia)* | She weeps |
| SIR JOHN | My lord, this is a poor mad [[Soul|soul]], and she says up and down the town that her eldest son is like you. She hath been in good case, and the [[Truth|truth]] is, [[Poverty|poverty]] hath distracted her. But for these foolish officers, I beseech you I may have redress against them. |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | Sir John, Sir John, I am [[Well|well]] acquainted with your manner of wrenching the true cause the false way. It is not a confident brow, nor the throng of words that come with such more than impudent sauciness from you, can thrust me from a level consideration. You have, as it appears to me, practised upon the easy-yielding spirit of this woman, and made her serve your uses both in purse and in person. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Yea, in [[Truth|truth]], my lord. |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | Pray thee, peace. (To Sir John) Pay her the debt you owe her, and unpay the villainy you have done with her. The one you may do with sterling money, and the other with current repentance. |
| SIR JOHN | My lord, I will not undergo this sneap without reply. You call honourable boldness ‘impudent sauciness’; if a man will make curtsy and say nothing, he is virtuous. No, my lord, my humble duty remembered, I will not be your suitor. I say to you I do [[Desire|desire]] deliverance from these officers, being upon hasty employment in the [[King]]’s affairs. |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | You speak as having power to do wrong; but answer in th’effect of your reputation, and satisfy the poor woman. |
| SIR JOHN | (drawing apart) Come hither, hostess.<br>She goes to him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Master [[Gower]], a messenger |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | Now, Master [[Gower]], what news? |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | The [[King]], my lord, and Harry Prince of Wales<br>Are near at hand; the rest the paper tells. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Lord Chief Justice]] reads the paper, and converses apart with [[Gower]] |
| SIR JOHN | As I am a gentleman! |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | [[Faith]], you said so before. |
| SIR JOHN | As I am a gentleman! Come, no more words of it. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | By this heavenly ground I tread on, I must be fain to pawn both my plate and the tapestry of my dining-chambers. |
| SIR JOHN | Glasses, glasses, is the only drinking; and for thy walls, a pretty slight drollery, or the story of the Prodigal, or the German hunting in waterwork, is worth a thousand of these bed-hangers and these fly-bitten tapestries. Let it be ten pound if thou canst. Come, an ’twere not for thy humours, there’s not a better wench in England. Go, wash thy face, and draw the action. Come, thou must not be in this [[Humour|humour]] with me. Dost not know me? Come, I know thou wast set on to this. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Pray thee, Sir John, let it be but twenty nobles. I’[[Faith|faith]], I am loath to pawn my plate, so God save me, la! |
| SIR JOHN | Let it [[Alone|alone]]; I’ll make other shift. You’ll be a [[Fool|fool]] still. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | [[Well]], you shall have it, though I pawn my gown. I [[Hope|hope]] you’ll come to supper. You’ll pay me altogether? |
| *(didascalia)* | SIR JOHN Will I live? To Bardolph and the Page Go with her, with her. Hook on, hook on! |
| MISTRESS | QUICKLY, Will you have [[Doll Tearsheet]] meet you at supper? |
| SIR JOHN | No more words; let’s have her. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Mistress Quickly]], Bardolph, the Page, Fang and Snare |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | (to [[Gower]]) I have heard better news. |
| SIR JOHN | What’s the news, my good lord? |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | (to [[Gower]]) Where lay the [[King]] tonight? |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | At Basingstoke, my lord. |
| SIR JOHN | (to [[Lord Chief Justice]]) I [[Hope|hope]], my lord, all’s [[Well|well]].<br>What is the news, my lord? |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | (to [[Gower]]) Come all his forces back? |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | No; fifteen hundred foot, five hundred [[Horse|horse]],<br>Are marched up to my lord of Lancaster<br>Against Northumberland and the Archbishop. |
| SIR JOHN | (to [[Lord Chief Justice]])<br>Comes the [[King]] back from Wales, my noble lord? |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | (to [[Gower]])<br>You shall have letters of me presently.<br>Come, go along with me, good Master [[Gower]].<br>They are going |
| SIR JOHN | My lord! |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | What’s the matter? |
| SIR JOHN | Master [[Gower]], shall I entreat you with me to dinner? |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | I must wait upon my good lord here, I thank you, good Sir John. |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | Sir John, you loiter here too long, being you are to take soldiers up in counties as you go. |
| SIR JOHN | Will you sup with me, Master [[Gower]]? |
| [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] | What foolish master taught you these manners, Sir John? |
| SIR JOHN | Master [[Gower]], if they become me not, he was a [[Fool|fool]] that taught them me. (To [[Lord Chief Justice]]) This is the right fencing grace, my lord—tap for tap, and so part fair. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Lord Chief Justice|LORD CHIEF JUSTICE]] Now the Lord lighten thee; thou art a great [[Fool|fool]]. Exeunt [[Lord Chief Justice]] and [[Gower]] at one door, Sir John at another |
