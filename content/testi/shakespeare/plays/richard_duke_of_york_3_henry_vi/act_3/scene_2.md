| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF GLOUCEST’ER | Ay, Edward will use women honourably.<br>Would he were wasted, marrow, bones, and all,<br>That from his loins no hopeful branch may [[Spring|spring]]<br>To [[Cross|cross]] me from the golden [[Time|time]] I look for.<br>And yet, between my [[Soul|soul]]’s [[Desire|desire]] and me—<br>The lustful Edward’s title burièd—<br>Is [[Clarence]], Henry, and his son young Edward,<br>And all the unlooked-for issue of their bodies,<br>To take their rooms ere I can place myself.<br>A cold premeditation for my purpose.<br>Why, then, I do but dream on sovereignty<br>Like one that stands upon a promontory<br>And spies a far-off shore where he would tread,<br>Wishing his foot were equal with his eye,<br>And chides the sea that sunders him from thence,<br>Saying he’ll lade it dry to have his way—<br>So do I wish the [[Crown|crown]] being so far off,<br>And so I chide the means that keeps me from it,<br>And so I say I’ll cut the causes off,<br>Flattering me with impossibilities.<br>My eye’s too quick, my heart o‘erweens too much,<br>Unless my hand and strength could equal them.<br>[[Well]], say there is no kingdom then for [[Richard]]—<br>What other pleasure can the world afford?<br>I’ll make my heaven in a lady’s lap,<br>And deck my [[Body|body]] in gay ornaments,<br>And ’[[Witch|witch]] sweet ladies with my words and looks.<br>O, miserable thought! And more unlikely<br>Than to accomplish twenty golden crowns.<br>Why, [[Love|love]] forswore me in my mother’s womb,<br>And, for I should not deal in her soft laws,<br>She did corrupt frail [[Nature|nature]] with some bribe<br>To shrink mine arm up like a withered shrub,<br>To make an envious mountain on my back—<br>Where sits deformity to mock my [[Body|body]]—<br>To shape my legs of an unequal size,<br>To disproportion me in every part,<br>Like to a chaos, or an unlicked bear whelp<br>That carries no impression like the dam.<br>And am I then a man to be beloved?<br>O, monstrous fault, to harbour such a thought!<br>Then, since this earth affords no joy to me<br>But to command, to check, to o‘erbear such<br>As are of better person than myself,<br>I’ll make my heaven to dream upon the [[Crown|crown]],<br>And whiles I live, t’account this world but hell,<br>Until my misshaped trunk that bears this head<br>Be round impaled with a glorious [[Crown|crown]].<br>And yet I know not how to get the [[Crown|crown]],<br>For many lives stand between me and [[Home|home]].<br>And I—like one lost in a thorny wood,<br>That rends the thorns and is rent with the thorns,<br>Seeking a way and straying from the way,<br>Not knowing how to find the open air,<br>But toiling desperately to find it out—<br>Torment myself to catch the English [[Crown|crown]].<br>And from that torment I will free myself,<br>Or hew my way out with a bloody axe.<br>Why, I can smile, and murder whiles I smile,<br>And cry ‘Content!’ to that which grieves my heart,<br>And wet my cheeks with artificial [[Tears|tears]],<br>And frame my face to all occasions.<br>I’ll drown more sailors than the [[Mermaid|mermaid]] shall;<br>I’ll slay more gazers than the basilisk;<br>I’ll play the orator as [[Well|well]] as Nestor,<br>Deceive more slyly than [[Ulysses]] could,<br>And, like a Sinon, take another Troy.<br>I can add colours to the chameleon,<br>Change shapes with [[Proteus]] for advantages,<br>And set the murderous Machiavel to school.<br>Can I do this, and cannot get a [[Crown|crown]]?<br>Tut, were it farther off, I’ll pluck it down. Exit |

| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | I told your majesty as much before—<br>This proveth Edward’s [[Love|love]] and Warwick’s honesty. |
| WARWICK | [[King]] Louis, I here protest in sight of heaven<br>And by the [[Hope|hope]] I have of heavenly bliss,<br>That I am clear from this misdeed of Edward‘s,<br>No more my [[King|king]], for he dishonours me,<br>But most himself, if he could see his shame.<br>Did I forget that by the [[house]] of York<br>My father came untimely to his [[Death|death]]?<br>Did I let pass th’abuse done to my niece?<br>Did I impale him with the regal [[Crown|crown]]?<br>Did I put Henry from his native right?<br>And am I guerdoned at the last with shame?<br>Shame on himself, for my [[Desert|desert]] is [[Honour|honour]].<br>And to repair my [[Honour|honour]], lost for him,<br>I here renounce him and return to Henry.<br>(To [[Queen Margaret]]) My noble [[Queen]], let former<br>grudges pass,<br>And henceforth I am thy true servitor.<br>I will [[Revenge|revenge]] his wrong to Lady Bona<br>And replant Henry in his former state. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Warwick, these words have turned my hate to [[Love|love]],<br>And I forgive and quite forget old faults,<br>And joy that thou becom’st [[King]] Henry’s friend. |
| WARWICK | So much his friend, ay, his unfeigned friend,<br>That if [[King]] Louis vouchsafe to furnish us<br>With some few bands of chosen soldiers,<br>I’ll undertake to land them on our coast<br>And force the [[Tyrant|tyrant]] from his seat by war.<br>’Tis not his new-made bride shall succour him.<br>And as for [[Clarence]], as my letters tell me,<br>He’s very likely now to fall from him<br>For matching more for wanton lust than [[Honour|honour]],<br>Or than for strength and safety of our country. |
| LADY BONA | (to [[King]] Louis)<br>Dear brother, how shall Bona be revenged,<br>But by thy help to this distressed [[Queen]]? |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (to [[King]] Louis)<br>Renowned Prince, how shall poor Henry live<br>Unless thou rescue him from foul [[Despair|despair]]? |
| LADY BONA | (to [[King]] Louis)<br>My quarrel and this English [[Queen]]’s are one. |
| WARWICK | And mine, fair Lady Bona, joins with yours. |
| [[King|KING]] LOUIS | And mine with hers, and thine, and Margaret’s.<br>Therefore at last I firmly am resolved:<br>You shall have aid. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Let me give humble thanks for all at once. |
| [[King|KING]] LOUIS | (to the Post)<br>Then, England’s messenger, return in post<br>And tell false Edward, thy supposed [[King|king]],<br>That Louis of France is sending over masquers<br>To revel it with him and his new bride.<br>Thou seest what’s passed, go [[Fear|fear]] thy [[King|king]] withal. |
| LADY BONA | (to the Post)<br>Tell him, in [[Hope|hope]] he’ll prove a widower shortly,<br>I’ll wear the willow garland for his sake. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (to the Post)<br>Tell him my mourning weeds are laid aside,<br>And I am ready to put armour on. |
| WARWICK | (to the Post)<br>Tell him from me that he hath done me wrong,<br>And therefore I’ll uncrown him ere’t be long.<br>(Giving money) There’s thy reward—be gone. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Post |
| [[King|KING]] LOUIS | But, Warwick, thou and [[Oxford]], with five thousand<br>men,<br>Shall [[Cross|cross]] the seas and bid false Edward battle;<br>And, as occasion serves, this noble [[Queen]]<br>And Prince shall follow with a fresh supply.<br>Yet, ere thou go, but answer me one [[Doubt|doubt]]:<br>What pledge have we of thy firm loyalty? |
| WARWICK | This shall assure my constant loyalty :<br>That if our [[Queen]] and this young Prince agree,<br>I’ll join mine eldest daughter and my joy<br>To him forthwith in holy wedlock bands. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Yes, I agree, and thank you for your motion.<br>(To Prince Edward) Son Edward, she is fair and virtuous,<br>Therefore delay not. Give thy hand to Warwick,<br>And with thy hand thy [[Faith|faith]] irrevocable<br>That only Warwick’s daughter shall be thine. |
| PRINCE EDWARD | Yes, I accept her, for she [[Well|well]] deserves it,<br>And here to pledge my vow I give my hand.<br>He and Warwick clasp hands |
| [[King|KING]] LOUIS | Why stay we now? These soldiers shall be levied,<br>And thou, Lord Bourbon, our high admiral,<br>Shall waft them over with our royal fleet.<br>I long till Edward fall by war’s mischance<br>For mocking [[Marriage|marriage]] with a dame of France. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Warwick |
| WARWICK | I came from Edward as ambassador,<br>But I return his sworn and mortal foe.<br>Matter of [[Marriage|marriage]] was the charge he gave me,<br>But dreadful war shall answer his demand.<br>Had he none else to make a stale but me?<br>Then none but I shall turn his jest to sorrow.<br>I was the chief that raised him to the [[Crown|crown]],<br>And I’ll be chief to bring him down again.<br>Not that I pity Henry’s misery,<br>But seek [[Revenge|revenge]] on Edward’s mockery. Exit |
