| *(didascalia)* | A chair of state. Alarum. Enter [[Richard]] Plantagenet, [[Duke of York]], his two sons Edward, Earl of March, and Crookback [[Richard]], the Duke of Norfolk, the Marquis of Montague, and the [[Earl of Warwick]], with drummers and soldiers. They all wear white roses in their hats |
| WARWICK | I [[Wonder|wonder]] how the [[King]] escaped our hands? |
| YORK | While we pursued the horsemen of the north,<br>He slyly stole away and left his men;<br>Whereat the great lord of Northumberland,<br>Whose warlike ears could never brook retreat,<br>Cheered up the drooping army; and himself,<br>Lord [[Clifford]], and Lord Stafford, all abreast,<br>Charged our main battle’s front, and, breaking in,<br>Were by the swords of common soldiers slain. |
| EDWARD | Lord Stafford’s father, [[Duke of Buckingham]],<br>Is either slain or wounded dangerous.<br>I cleft his beaver with a downright blow.<br>That this is true, father, behold his [[Blood|blood]].<br>He shows a bloody [[Sword|sword]] |
| *(didascalia)* | MONTAGUE to York |
| EDWARD | And, brother, here’s the Earl of Wiltshire’s [[Blood|blood]],<br>He shows a bloody [[Sword|sword]]<br>Whom I encountered as the battles joined. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (to Somerset’s head, which he shows) Speak thou for me, and tell them what I did. |
| YORK | [[Richard]] hath best deserved of all my sons.<br>(To the head) But is your grace dead, my lord of<br>Somerset? |
| NORFOLK | Such hap have all the line of [[John of Gaunt]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Thus do I [[Hope|hope]] to shake [[King]] Henry’s head. |
| *(didascalia)* | He holds aloft the head, then throws it down |
| WARWICK | And so do I, victorious prince of York.<br>Before I see thee seated in that throne<br>Which now the [[house]] of Lancaster usurps,<br>I vow by heaven these eyes shall never close.<br>This is the palace of the fearful [[King]],<br>And this (pointing to the chair of state), the regal<br>seat—possess it, York,<br>For this is thine, and not [[King]] Henry’s heirs’. |
| YORK | Assist me then, sweet Warwick, and I will,<br>For hither we have broken in by force. |
| NORFOLK | We’ll all assist you—he that flies shall die. |
| YORK | Thanks, gentle Norfolk. Stay by me, my lords<br>And soidiers—stay, and lodge by me this [[Night|night]].<br>They go up upon the state |
| WARWICK | And when the [[King]] comes, offer him no violence<br>Unless he seek to thrust you out perforce. |
| *(didascalia)* | The soldiers withdraw |
| YORK | The [[Queen]] this day here holds her Parliament,<br>But little thinks we shall be of her council;<br>By words or blows here let us win our right. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Armed as we are, let’s stay within this [[house]]. |
| WARWICK | ‘The Bloody Parliament’ shall this be called,<br>Unless Plantagenet, [[Duke of York]], be [[King|king]],<br>And bashful Henry deposed, whose cowardice<br>Hath made us bywords to our enemies. |
| YORK | Then leave me not, my lords. Be resolute—<br>I mean to take possession of my right. |
| WARWICK | Neither the [[King]] nor he that loves him best—<br>The proudest he that holds up Lancaster—<br>Dares stir a wing if Warwick shake his [[Bells|bells]].<br>I’ll plant Plantagenet, root him up who dares.<br>Resolve thee, [[Richard]]—claim the English [[Crown|crown]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | York sits in the chair. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter [[King]] Henry, Lord [[Clifford]], the Earls |
| WARWICK | of Northumberland and Westmorland, the Duke of |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeter, and the rest. They all wear red roses in |
| *(didascalia)* | their hats |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | My lords, look where the sturdy [[Rebel|rebel]] sits—<br>Even in the chair of state! Belike he means,<br>Backed by the power of Warwick, that false peer,<br>To aspire unto the [[Crown|crown]] and reign as [[King|king]].<br>[[Earl of Northumberland]], he slew thy father—<br>And thine, Lord [[Clifford]]—and you both have vowed<br>[[Revenge|revenge]]<br>On him, his sons, his favourites, and his friends. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | If I be not, heavens be revenged on me. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | The [[Hope|hope]] thereof makes [[Clifford]] mourn in steel. |
| WESTMORLAND | What, shall we suffer this? Let’s pluck him down.<br>My heart for anger burns—I cannot brook it. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Be patient, gentle Earl of Westmorland. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | Patience is for poltroons, such as he (indicating York).<br>He durst not sit there had your father lived.<br>My gracious lord, here in the Parliament<br>Let us assail the family of York. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | [[Well]] hast thou spoken, cousin, be it so. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Ah, know you not the [[City|city]] favours them,<br>And they have troops of soldiers at their beck? |
| EXETER | But when the Duke is slain, they’ll quickly fly. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Far be the thought of this from Henry’s heart,<br>To make a shambles of the Parliament [[house|House]].<br>Cousin of Exeter, frowns, words, and threats<br>Shall be the war that Henry means to use.<br>(To York) Thou factious [[Duke of York]], descend my<br>throne<br>And kneel for grace and [[Mercy|mercy]] at my feet.<br>I am thy sovereign. |
| YORK | I am thine. |
| EXETER | For shame, come down—he made thee [[Duke of York]]. |
| YORK | It was mine inheritance, as the earldom was. |
| EXETER | Thy father was a traitor to the [[Crown|crown]]. |
| WARWICK | Exeter, thou art a traitor to the [[Crown|crown]]<br>In following this usurping Henry. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | Whom should he follow but his natural [[King|king]]? |
| WARWICK | True, [[Clifford]], and that’s [[Richard]] [[Duke of York]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to York)<br>And shall I stand and thou sit in my throne? |
| YORK | It must and shall be so—content thyself. |
| WARWICK | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>Be Duke of Lancaster, let him be [[King|king]]. |
| WESTMORLAND | He is both [[King|king]] and Duke of Lancaster—<br>And that, the Lord of Westmorland shall maintain. |
| WARWICK | And Warwick shall disprove it. You forget<br>That we are those which chased you from the field,<br>And slew your fathers, and, with colours spread,<br>Marched through the [[City|city]] to the palace gates. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Yes, Warwick, I remember it to my grief,<br>And, by his [[Soul|soul]], thou and thy [[house]] shall rue it. |
| WESTMORLAND | (to York)<br>Plantagenet, of thee, and these thy sons,<br>Thy kinsmen, and thy friends, I’ll have more lives<br>Than drops of [[Blood|blood]] were in my father’s veins. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | (to Warwick)<br>Urge it no more, lest that, instead of words,<br>I send thee, Warwick, such a messenger<br>As shall [[Revenge|revenge]] his [[Death|death]] before I stir. |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK to York |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | Poor [[Clifford]], how I scorn his worthless threats. |
| *(didascalia)* | YORK to [[King]] Henry |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | Will you we show our title to the [[Crown|crown]]?<br>If not, our swords shall plead it in the field. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | What title hast thou, traitor, to the [[Crown|crown]]?<br>Thy father was, as thou art, [[Duke of York]];<br>Thy grandfather, Roger Mortimer, Earl of March.<br>I am the son of Henry the Fifth,<br>Who made the [[Dauphin]] and the French to stoop<br>And seized upon their towns and provinces. |
| WARWICK | Talk not of France, sith thou hast lost it all. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | The Lord Protector lost it, and not I.<br>When I was crowned, I was but nine months old. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | You are old enough now, and yet, methinks, you lose.<br>(To York) Father, tear the [[Crown|crown]] from the [[Usurper|usurper]]’s<br>head. |
| EDWARD | (to York)<br>Sweet father, do so—set it on your head. |
| MONTAGUE | (to York)<br>Good brother, as thou lov‘st and [[Honour|honour]]’st arms,<br>Let’s fight it out and not stand cavilling thus. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound drums and trumpets, and the [[King]] will fly. |
| YORK | Sons, peace! |
| *(didascalia)* | NORTHUMBERLAND |
| YORK | Peace, thou—and give [[King]] Henry leave to speak. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Ah, York, why seekest thou to depose me?<br>Are we not both Plantagenets by birth,<br>And from two brothers lineally descent?<br>Suppose by right and equity thou be [[King|king]]—<br>Think’st thou that I will leave my kingly throne,<br>Wherein my grandsire and my father sat?<br>No—first shall war unpeople this my realm;<br>Ay, and their colours, often borne in France,<br>And now in England to our heart’s great sorrow,<br>Shall be my winding-sheet. Why faint you, lords?<br>My title’s good, and better far than his. |
| WARWICK | Prove it, Henry, and thou shalt be [[King|king]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Henry the Fourth by conquest got the [[Crown|crown]]. |
| YORK | ’Twas by rebellion against his [[King|king]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King|KING]] HENRY aside |
| YORK | I know not what to say—my title’s weak.<br>(To York) Tell me, may not a [[King|king]] adopt an heir? |
| YORK | What then? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | An if he may, then am I lawful [[King|king]]—<br>For [[Richard]], in the view of many lords,<br>Resigned the [[Crown|crown]] to Henry the Fourth,<br>Whose heir my father was, and I am his. |
| YORK | He [[Rose|rose]] against him, being his sovereign,<br>And made him to resign his [[Crown|crown]] perforce. |
| WARWICK | Suppose, my lords, he did it unconstrained—<br>Think you ’twere prejudicial to his [[Crown|crown]]? |
| EXETER | No, for he could not so resign his [[Crown|crown]]<br>But that the next heir should succeed and reign. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Art thou against us, [[Duke of Exeter]]? |
| EXETER | His is the right, and therefore pardon me. |
| YORK | Why whisper you, my lords, and answer not? |
| *(didascalia)* | EXETER to [[King]] Henry |
| YORK | My [[Conscience|conscience]] tells me he is lawful [[King|king]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King|KING]] HENRY aside |
| YORK | All will revolt from me and turn to him. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | (to York)<br>Plantagenet, for all the claim thou lay’st,<br>Think not that Henry shall be so deposed. |
| WARWICK | Deposed he shall be, in despite of all. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Thou art deceived—’tis not thy southern power<br>Of Essex, Norfolk, Suffolk, nor of [[Kent]],<br>Which makes thee thus presumptuous and proud,<br>Can set the Duke up in despite of me. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | [[King]] Henry, be thy title right or wrong,<br>Lord [[Clifford]] vows to fight in thy defence.<br>May that ground gape and [[Swallow|swallow]] me alive<br>Where I shall kneel to him that slew my father. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O, [[Clifford]], how thy words revive my heart! |
| YORK | Henry of Lancaster, resign thy [[Crown|crown]].<br>What mutter you, or what conspire you, lords? |
| WARWICK | Do right unto this princely [[Duke of York]],<br>Or I will fill the [[house]] with armed men<br>And over the chair of state, where now he sits,<br>Write up his title with usurping [[Blood|blood]].<br>He stamps with his foot and the soldiers show<br><br>themselves |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | My lord of Warwick, hear me but one word—<br>Let me for this my lifetime reign as [[King|king]]. |
| YORK | Confirm the [[Crown|crown]] to me and to mine heirs,<br>And thou shalt reign in quiet while thou liv’st. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | I am content. [[Richard]] Plantagenet,<br>Enjoy the kingdom after my decease. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | What wrong is this unto the prince your son? |
| WARWICK | What good is this to England and himself? |
| WESTMORLAND | Base, fearful, and despairing Henry. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | How hast thou injured both thyself and us? |
| WESTMORLAND | I cannot stay to hear these articles. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Nor I. |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | Come, cousin, let us tell the [[Queen]] these news. |
| WESTMORLAND | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>Farewell, faint-hearted and degenerate [[King|king]],<br>In whose cold [[Blood|blood]] no spark of [[Honour|honour]] bides. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with his soldiers |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>Be thou a prey unto the [[house]] of York,<br>And die in bands for this unmanly deed. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with his soldiers |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>In dreadful war mayst thou be overcome,<br>Or live in peace, abandoned and despised. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with his soldiers |
| WARWICK | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>Turn this way, Henry, and regard them not. |
| EXETER | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>They seek [[Revenge|revenge]] and therefore will not yield. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Ah, Exeter. |
| WARWICK | Why should you sigh, my lord? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Not for myself, Lord Warwick, but my son,<br>Whom I unnaturally shall disinherit.<br>But be it as it may. (To York) I here entail<br>The [[Crown|crown]] to thee and to thine heirs for ever,<br>Conditionally, that here thou take thine oath<br>To cease this civil war, and whilst I live<br>To [[Honour|honour]] me as thy [[King|king]] and sovereign,<br>And nor by treason nor hostility<br>To seek to put me down and reign thyself. |
| YORK | This oath I willingly take and will perform. |
| WARWICK | Long live [[King]] Henry. (To York) Plantagenet, embrace him. |
| *(didascalia)* | York descends. Henry and York embrace |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to York)<br>And long live thou, and these thy forward sons. |
| YORK | Now York and Lancaster are reconciled. |
| EXETER | Accursed be he that seeks to make them foes. Sennet. Here York’s train comes down from the state |
| YORK | (to [[King]] Henry) |
| *(didascalia)* | Farewell, my gracious lord, I’ll to my [[Castle|castle]]. Exeunt York, Edward, and [[Richard]], with soldiers |
| WARWICK | And I’ll keep [[London]] with my soldiers. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit With soldiers |
| NORFOLK | And I to Norfolk with my followers. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit With soldiers |
| MONTAGUE | And I unto the sea from whence I came. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with soldiers |
| *(didascalia)* | And I with grief and sorrow to the [[Court|court]]. [[King]] Henry and Exeter turn to leave. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Queen Margaret]] and Prince Edward |
| EXETER | Here comes the [[Queen]], whose looks bewray her anger. I’ll steal away. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Exeter, so will I. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Nay, go not from me—I will follow thee. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Be patient, gentle [[Queen]], and I will stay. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Who can be patient in such extremes?<br>Ah, wretched man, would I had died a maid<br>And never seen thee, never borne thee son,<br>Seeing thou hast proved so unnatural a father.<br>Hath he deserved to lose his birthright thus?<br>Hadst thou but loved him half so [[Well|well]] as I,<br>Or felt that [[Pain|pain]] which I did for him once,<br>Or nourished him as I did with my [[Blood|blood]],<br>Thou wouldst have left thy dearest heart-[[Blood|blood]] there<br>Rather than have made that savage Duke thine heir<br>And disinherited thine only son. |
| PRINCE EDWARD | Father, you cannot disinherit me.<br>If you be [[King|king]], why should not I succeed? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Pardon me, Margaret; pardon me, sweet son—<br>The [[Earl of Warwick]] and the Duke enforced me. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Enforced thee? Art thou [[King|king]], and wilt be forced?<br>I shame to hear thee speak! Ah, timorous wretch,<br>Thou hast undone thyself, thy son, and me,<br>And giv’n unto the [[house]] of York such head<br>As thou shalt reign but by their sufferance.<br>To entail him and his heirs unto the [[Crown|crown]]—<br>What is it, but to make thy sepulchre<br>And creep into it far before thy [[Time|time]]?<br>Warwick is Chancellor and the Lord of Calais;<br>Stern Falconbridge commands the narrow seas;<br>The Duke is made Protector of the Realm;<br>And yet shalt thou be safe? Such safety finds<br>The trembling [[Lamb|lamb]] environèd with wolves.<br>Had I been there, which am a seely woman,<br>The soldiers should have tossed me on their pikes<br>Before I would have granted to that act.<br>But thou preferr’st thy life before thine [[Honour|honour]].<br>And seeing thou dost, I here [[divorce]] myself<br>Both from thy table, Henry, and thy bed,<br>Until that act of Parliament be repealed<br>Whereby my son is disinherited.<br>The northern lords that have forsworn thy colours<br>Will follow mine, if once they see them spread—<br>And spread they shall be, to thy foul disgrace<br>And the utter ruin of the [[house]] of York.<br>Thus do I leave thee. (To Prince Edward) Come, son,<br>let’s away.<br>Our army is ready—come, we’ll after them. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Stay, gentle Margaret, and hear me speak. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Thou hast spoke too much already. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Prince Edward Get thee gone. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Gentle son Edward, thou wilt stay with me? |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Ay, to be murdered by his enemies. |
| PRINCE EDWARD | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>When I return with victory from the field,<br>I’ll see your grace. Till then, I’ll follow her. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Come, son, away—we may not linger thus. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with Prince Edward |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Poor [[Queen]], how [[Love|love]] to me and to her son<br>Hath made her break out into terms of rage.<br>Revenged may she be on that hateful Duke,<br>Whose haughty spirit, winged with [[Desire|desire]],<br>Will coast my [[Crown|crown]], and, like an empty eagle,<br>Tire on the flesh of me and of my son.<br>The loss of those three lords torments my heart.<br>I’ll write unto them and entreat them fair.<br>Come, cousin, you shall be the messenger. |
| EXETER | And I, I [[Hope|hope]], shall reconcile them all. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exeunt |
