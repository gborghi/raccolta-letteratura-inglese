| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Earl of Warwick]], the Mayar of Coventry, two Messengers, and others upon the walls |
| WARWICK | Where is the post that came from valiant [[Oxford]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | The First Messenger steps forward |
| WARWICK | How far hence is thy lord, mine honest fellow? |
| FIRST MESSENGER | By this at Dunsmore, marching hitherward. |
| WARWICK | How far off is our brother Montague?<br>Where is the post that came from Montague? |
| *(didascalia)* | the Second Messenger steps forward |
| SECOND MESSENGER | By this at Da’ntry, with a puissant troop. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Somerville to them, abovel |
| WARWICK | Say, Somerville-what says my loving son?<br>And, by thy guess, how nigh is [[Clarence]] now? |
| SOMERVILLE | At Southam I did leave him with his forces,<br>And do expect him here some two hours hence.<br>A march afar off |
| WARWICK | Then [[Clarence]] is at hand-I hear his drum. |
| SOMERVILLE | It is not his, my lord. Here Southam lies.<br>The drum your [[Honour|honour]] hears marcheth from Warwick. |
| WARWICK | Who should that be? Belike, unlooked-for friends. |
| SOMERVILLE | They are at hand, and you shall quickly know. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter below [[King]] Edward and [[Richard]] [[Duke of Gloucester]], with soldiers |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Go, trumpet, to the walls, and sound a parley. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound a parley |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | See how the surly Warwick mans the wall. |
| WARWICK | O, unbid spite—is sportful Edward come?<br>Where slept our scouts, or how are they seduced,<br>That we could hear no news of his repair? |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Now, Warwick, wilt thou ope the [[City|city]] gates,<br>Speak gentle words, and humbly bend thy knee,<br>Call Edward [[King|king]], and at his hands beg [[Mercy|mercy]]?<br>And he shall pardon thee these outrages. |
| WARWICK | Nay, rather, wilt thou draw thy forces hence,<br>Confess who set thee up and plucked thee down,<br>Call Warwick patron, and be penitent?<br>And thou shalt still remain the [[Duke of York]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I thought at least he would have said ‘the [[King]]’.<br>Or did he make the jest against his will? |
| WARWICK | Is not a dukedom, sir, a goodly gift? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Ay, by my [[Faith|faith]], for a poor earl to give.<br>I’ll do thee service for so good a gift. |
| WARWICK | ’Twas I that gave the kingdom to thy brother. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Why then, ’tis mine, if but by Warwick’s gift. |
| WARWICK | Thou art no Atlas for so great a weight;<br>And, weakling, Warwick takes his gift again;<br>And Henry is my [[King|king]], Warwick his subject. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | But Warwick’s [[King|king]] is Edward’s prisoner,<br>And, gallant Warwick, do but answer this:<br>What is the [[Body|body]] when the head is off? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Alas, that Warwick had no more forecast,<br>But whiles he thought to steal the single ten,<br>The [[King|king]] was slyly fingered from the deck. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Warwick You left poor Henry at the Bishop’s palace, |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | And ten to one you’ll meet him in the Tower. |
| *(didascalia)* | ’Tis even so—to Warwick yet you are Warwick still. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Come, Warwick, take the [[Time|time]]—kneel down, kneel<br>down.<br>Nay, when? Strike now, or else the iron cools. |
| WARWICK | I had rather chop this hand off at a blow,<br>And with the other fling it at thy face,<br>Than bear so low a sail to strike to thee. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Sail how thou canst, have [[Wind|wind]] and tide thy friend,<br>This hand, fast wound about thy coal-black hair,<br>Shall, whiles thy head is warm and new cut off,<br>Write in the [[Dust|dust]] this sentence with thy [[Blood|blood]]:<br>‘[[Wind]]-changing Warwick now can change no more’. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of [[Oxford]], with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| WARWICK | O cheerful colours! See where [[Oxford]] comes. |
| [[Oxford|OXFORD]] | [[Oxford]], [[Oxford]], for Lancaster! |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Oxford]] and his men pass over the stage and exeunt into the [[City|city]] |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to [[King]] Edward)<br>The gates are open—let us enter too. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | So other foes may set upon our backs?<br>Stand we in good array, for they no [[Doubt|doubt]]<br>Will issue out again and bid us battle.<br>If not, the [[City|city]] being but of small defence,<br>We’ll quickly rouse the traitors in the same. |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK to [[Oxford]], within |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | O, welcome, [[Oxford]]—for we want thy help. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Marquis of Montague with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| MONTAGUE | Montague, Montague, for Lancaster! |
| *(didascalia)* | Montague and his men pass over the stage and exeunt into the [[City|city]] |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Thou and thy brother both shall bye this treason<br>Even with the dearest [[Blood|blood]] your bodies bear. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | The harder matched, the greater victory.<br>My mind presageth happy gain and conquest. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Duke of Somerset with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| SOMERSET | Somerset, Somerset, for Lancaster! |
| *(didascalia)* | Somerset and his men pass over the stage and exeunt into the [[City|city]] |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Two of thy name, both dukes of Somerset,<br>Have sold their lives unto the [[house]] of York—<br>And thou shalt be the third, an this [[Sword|sword]] hold. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter George Duke of [[Clarence]] with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| WARWICK | And lo, where George of [[Clarence]] sweeps along,<br>Of force enough to bid his brother battle;<br>With whom an upright zeal to right prevails<br>More than the [[Nature|nature]] of a brother’s [[Love|love]]. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | [[Clarence]], [[Clarence]], for Lancaster! |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Et tu, Brute—wilt thou stab Caesar too?<br>(To a trumpeter) A parley, sirra, to George of [[Clarence]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound a parley. [[Richard]] of [[Gloucester]] and George of |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | [[Clarence]] whisper together |
| WARWICK | Come, [[Clarence]], come—thou wilt if Warwick call. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | Father of Warwick, know you what this means? |
| *(didascalia)* | He takes his red [[Rose|rose]] out of his hat and throws it at Warwick |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | Look—here I throw my infamy at thee!<br>I will not ruinate my father’s [[house]],<br>Who gave his [[Blood|blood]] to lime the stones together,<br>And set up Lancaster. Why, trowest thou, Warwick,<br>That [[Clarence]] is so harsh, so blunt, unnatural,<br>To bend the fatal instruments of war<br>Against his brother and his lawful [[King|king]]?<br>Perhaps thou wilt object my holy oath.<br>To keep that oath were more impiety<br>Than Jephthah, when he sacrificed his daughter.<br>I am so sorry for my trespass made<br>That, to deserve [[Well|well]] at my brothers’ hands,<br>I here proclaim myself thy mortal foe,<br>With resolution, wheresoe’er I meet thee—<br>As I will meet thee, if thou stir abroad—<br>To plague thee for thy foul misleading me.<br>And so, proud-hearted Warwick, I defy thee,<br>And to my brothers turn my blushing cheeks.<br>(To [[King]] Edward)<br>Pardon me, Edward—I will make amends.<br>(To [[Richard]])<br>And, [[Richard]], do not frown upon my faults,<br>For I will henceforth be no more unconstant. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Now welcome more, and ten times more beloved,<br>Than if thou never hadst deserved our hate. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to George)<br>Welcome, good [[Clarence]]—this is brother-like. |
| WARWICK | (to George)<br>O, passing traitor—perjured and unjust! |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | What, Warwick, wilt thou leave the town and fight?<br>Or shall we beat the stones about thine ears? |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK aside |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Alas, I am not cooped here for defence.<br><br>(To [[King]] Edward)<br>I will away towards Barnet presently,<br>And bid thee battle, Edward, if thou dar’st. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Yes, Warwick—Edward dares, and leads the way.<br>Lords, to the Held—Saint George and victory! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt below [[King]] Edward and his company. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | March. The [[Earl of Warwick]] and his company<br>descend and follow |
