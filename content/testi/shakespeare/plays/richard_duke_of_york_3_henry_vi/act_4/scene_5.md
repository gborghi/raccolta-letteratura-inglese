| *(didascalia)* | Enter Earl Rivers and his sister, Lady Gray, Edward’s [[Queen|queen]] |
| RIVERS | Madam, what makes you in this sudden change? |
| LADY GRAY | Why, brother Rivers, are you yet to learn<br>What late misfortune is befall’n [[King]] Edward? |
| RIVERS | What? Loss of some pitched battle against Warwick? |
| LADY GRAY | No, but the loss of his own royal person. |
| RIVERS | Then is my sovereign slain? |
| LADY GRAY | Ay, almost stain—for he is taken prisoner,<br>Either betrayed by falsehood of his guard<br>Or by his foe surprised at unawares,<br>And, as I further have to understand,<br>Is new committed to the Bishop of York,<br>Fell Warwick’s brother, and by that our foe. |
| RIVERS | These news, I must confess, are full of grief.<br>Yet, gracious madam, bear it as you may.<br>Warwick may lose, that now hath won the day. |
| LADY GRAY | Till then fair [[Hope|hope]] must hinder life’s [[Decay|decay]],<br>And I the rather wean me from [[Despair|despair]]<br>For [[Love|love]] of Edward’s offspring in my womb.<br>This is it that makes me bridle passion<br>And bear with mildness my misfortune’s [[Cross|cross]].<br>Ay, ay, for this I draw in many a tear<br>And stop the rising of [[Blood|blood]]-sucking sighs,<br>Lest with my sighs or [[Tears|tears]] I blast or drown<br>[[King]] Edward’s fruit, true heir to th’English [[Crown|crown]]. |
| RIVERS | But, madam, where is Warwick then become? |
| LADY GRAY | I am informed that he comes towards [[London]]<br>To set the [[Crown|crown]] once more on Henry’s head.<br>Guess thou the rest—[[King]] Edward’s friends must down.<br>But to prevent the [[Tyrant|tyrant]]’s violence—<br>For trust not him that hath once broken [[Faith|faith]]—<br>I’ll hence forthwith unto the sanctuary,<br>To save at least the heir of Edward’s right.<br>There shall I rest secure from force and fraud.<br>Come, therefore, let us fly while we may fly.<br>If Warwick take us, we are sure to die. Exeunt |
