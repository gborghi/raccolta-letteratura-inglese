| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter [[King]] Edward, [[Richard]] [[Duke of Gloucester]], and Lord [[Hastings]], With a troop of Hollanders |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Now, brother [[Richard]], Lord [[Hastings]], and the rest,<br>Yet thus far fortune maketh us amends,<br>And says that once more I shall interchange<br>My waned state for Henry’s regal [[Crown|crown]].<br>[[Well]] have we passed and now repassed the seas<br>And brought desired help from Burgundy.<br>What then remains, we being thus arrived<br>From Ravenspurgh haven before the gates of York,<br>But that we enter, as into our dukedom ? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Hastings]] knocks at the gates of York |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | The gates made fast? Brother, I like not this.<br>For many men that stumble at the threshold<br>Are [[Well|well]] foretold that danger lurks within. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Tush, man, abodements must not now affright us.<br>By fair or foul means we must enter in,<br>For hither will our friends repair to us. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | My liege, I’ll knock once more to summon them.<br>He knocks. Enter, on the walls, the Mayor and aldermen of York |
| MAYOR | My lords, we were forewarned of your coming,<br>And shut the gates for safety of oursetves—<br>For now we owe allegiance unto Henry. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | But, Master Mayor, if Henry be your [[King|king]],<br>Yet Edward at the least is [[Duke of York]]. |
| MAYOR | True, my good lord, I know you for no less. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Why, and I challenge nothing but my dukedom,<br>As being [[Well|well]] content with that [[Alone|alone]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (aside)<br>But when the fox hath once got in his nose,<br>He’ll soon find means to make the [[Body|body]] follow. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | Why, Master Mayor, why stand you in a [[Doubt|doubt]]?<br>Open the gates—we are [[King]] Henry’s friends. |
| MAYOR | Ay, say you so? The gates shall then be opened.<br>They descend |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | A wise stout captain, and soon persuaded. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | The good old man would fain that all were [[Well|well]],<br>So ’twere not long of him; but being entered,<br>I [[Doubt|doubt]] not, I, but we shall soon persuade<br>Both him and all his brothers unto reason. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter below the Mayor and two aldermen |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | So, Master Mayor, these gates must not be shut<br>But in the [[Night|night]] or in the [[Time|time]] of war.<br>What—[[Fear|fear]] not, man, but yield me up the keys,<br>[[King]] Edward takes some keys from the Mayor<br>For Edward will defend the town and thee,<br>And all those friends that deign to follow me.<br>March. Enter Sir John Montgomery with a drummer and soldiers |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Brother, this is Sir John Montgomery,<br>Our trusty friend, unless I be deceived. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Welcome, Sir John—but why come you in arms? |
| MONTGOMERY | To help [[King]] Edward in his [[Time|time]] of [[Storm|storm]],<br>As every loyal subject ought to do. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Thanks, good Montgomery, but we now forget<br>Our title to the [[Crown|crown]], and only claim<br>Our dukedom till God please to send the rest. |
| MONTGOMERY | Then fare you [[Well|well]], for I will hence again.<br>I came to serve a [[King|king]] and not a duke.<br>Drummer, strike up, and let us march away. 50<br>The drummer begins to sound a march |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Nay, stay, Sir John, a while, and we’ll [[debate]]<br>By what safe means the [[Crown|crown]] may be recovered. |
| MONTGOMERY | What talk you of debating? In few words,<br>If you’ll not here proclaim yourself our [[King|king]]<br>I’ll leave you to your fortune and be gone<br>To keep them back that come to succour you.<br>Why shall we fight, if you pretend no title? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to [[King]] Edward)<br>Why, brother, wherefore stand you on nice points? |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | When we grow stronger, then we’ll make our claim.<br>Till then ’tis wisdom to conceal our meaning. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | Away with scrupulous wit! Now arms must rule. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | And fearless minds climb soonest unto crowns.<br>Brother, we will proclaim you out of hand,<br>The bruit thereof will bring you many friends. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Then be it as you will, for ’tis my right,<br>And Henry but usurps the diadem. |
| MONTGOMERY | Ay, now my sovereign speaketh like himself,<br>And now will I be Edward’s champion. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound trumpet, Edward shall be here proclaimed. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Montgomery |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | Come, fellow soldier, make thou proclamation. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish |
| *(didascalia)* | MONTGOMERY Edward the Fourth, by the grace of God |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | [[King]] of England and France, and Lord of Ireland—<br>And whosoe’er gainsays [[King]] Edward’s right,<br>By this I challenge him to single fight.<br>He throws down his gauntlet |
| ALL | Long live Edward the Fourth! |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Thanks, brave Montgomery, and thanks unto you all.<br>If fortune serve me I’ll requite this kindness.<br>Now, for this [[Night|night]], let’s harbour here in York;<br>And when the morning sun shall raise his car<br>Above the border of this horizon,<br>We’ll forward towards Warwick and his mates.<br>For [[Well|well]] I wot that Henry is no soldier.<br>Ah, froward [[Clarence]], how evil it beseems thee<br>To flatter Henry and forsake thy brother!<br>Yet, as we may, we’ll meet both thee and Warwick.<br>Come on, brave soldiers—[[Doubt|doubt]] not of the day<br>And, that once gotten, [[Doubt|doubt]] not of large pay. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
