| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Richard]] [[Duke of Gloucester]], George Duke of [[Clarence]], the Duke of Somerset, and the Marquis of Montague |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Now tell me, brother [[Clarence]], what think you<br>Of this new [[Marriage|marriage]] with the Lady Gray?<br>Hath not our brother made a worthy choice ? |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | Alas, you know ’tis far from hence to France;<br>How could he stay till Warwick made return? |
| SOMERSET | My lords, forbear this talk—here comes the [[King]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter [[King]] Edward, the Lady Gray his |
| SOMERSET | [[Queen]], the Earl of Pembroke, and the Lords |
| *(didascalia)* | Stafford and [[Hastings]]. Four stand on one side of |
| *(didascalia)* | the [[King]], and four on the other |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | And his [[Well|well]]-chosen bride. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | I mind to tell him plainly what I think. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Now, brother of [[Clarence]], how like you our choice,<br>That you stand pensive, as half-malcontent? |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | As [[Well|well]] as Louis of France, or the [[Earl of Warwick]],<br>Which are so weak of [[Courage|courage]] and in judgement<br>That they’ll take no offence at our abuse. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Suppose they take offence without a cause—<br>They are but Louis and Warwick; I am Edward,<br>Your [[King|king]] and Warwick’s, and must have my will. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | And you shall have your will, because our [[King|king]].<br>Yet hasty [[Marriage|marriage]] seldom proveth [[Well|well]]. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Yea, brother [[Richard]], are you offended too ? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Not I, no—God forbid that I should wish them severed<br>Whom God hath joined together. Ay, and ’twere pity<br>To sunder them that yoke so [[Well|well]] together. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Setting your scorns and your mislike aside,<br>Tell me some reason why the Lady Gray<br>Should not become my wife and England’s [[Queen|queen]].<br>And you too, Somerset and Montague,<br>Speak freely what you think. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | Then this is my opinion: that [[King]] Louis<br>Becomes your enemy for mocking him<br>About the [[Marriage|marriage]] of the Lady Bona. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | And Warwick, doing what you gave in charge,<br>Is now dishonoured by this new [[Marriage|marriage]]. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | What if both Louis and Warwick be appeased<br>By such invention as I can devise? |
| MONTAGUE | Yet, to have joined with France in such alliance<br>Would more have strengthened this our<br>commonwealth<br>’Gainst foreign storms than any [[Home|home]]-bred [[Marriage|marriage]]. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | Why, knows not Montague that of itself<br>England is safe, if true within itself? |
| MONTAGUE | But the safer when ’tis backed with France. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | ’Tis better using France than trusting France.<br>Let us be backed with God and with the seas<br>Which he hath giv’n for fence impregnable,<br>And with their helps only defend ourselves.<br>In them and in ourselves our safety lies. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | For this one speech Lord [[Hastings]] [[Well|well]] deserves<br>To have the heir of the Lord Hungerford. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Ay, what of that? It was my will and grant—<br>And for this once my will shall stand for law. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | And yet, methinks, your grace hath not done [[Well|well]]<br>To give the heir and daughter of Lord Scales<br>Unto the brother of your loving bride.<br>She better would have fitted me or [[Clarence]],<br>But in your bride you bury brotherhood. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | Or else you would not have bestowed the heir<br>Of the Lord Bonville on your new wife’s son,<br>And leave your brothers to go [[Speed|speed]] elsewhere. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Alas, poor [[Clarence]], is it for a wife<br>That thou art malcontent? I will provide thee. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | In choosing for yourself you showed your judgement,<br>Which being shallow, you shall give me leave<br>To play the broker in mine own behalf,<br>And to that end I shortly mind to leave you. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Leave me, or tarry. Edward will be [[King|king]],<br>And not be tied unto his brother’s will. |
| LADY GRAY | My lords, before it pleased his majesty<br>To raise my state to title of a [[Queen|queen]],<br>Do me but right, and you must all confess<br>That I was not ignoble of descent—<br>And meaner than myself have had like fortune.<br>But as this title honours me and mine,<br>So your dislikes, to whom I would be pleasing,<br>Doth cloud my joys with danger and with sorrow. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | My [[Love|love]], forbear to fawn upon their frowns.<br>What danger or what sorrow can befall thee<br>So long as Edward is thy constant friend,<br>And their true sovereign, whom they must obey?<br>Nay, whom they shall obey, and [[Love|love]] thee too—<br>Unless they seek for hatred at my hands,<br>Which if they do, yet will I keep thee safe,<br>And they shall feel the vengeance of my wrath. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (aside)<br>I hear, yet say not much, but think the more. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Post from France |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Now, messenger, what letters or what news from France ? |
| POST | My sovereign liege, no letters and few words,<br>But such as I, without your special pardon,<br>Dare not relate. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Go to, we pardon thee. Therefore, in brief,<br>Tell me their words as near as thou canst guess them.<br>What answer makes [[King]] Louis unto our letters? |
| POST | At my depart these were his very words:<br>‘Go tell false Edward, thy supposed [[King|king]],<br>That Louis of France is sending over masquers<br>To revel it with him and his new bride.’ |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Is Louis so brave ? Belike he thinks me Henry.<br>But what said Lady Bona to my [[Marriage|marriage]]? |
| POST | These were her words, uttered with mild disdain:<br>‘Tell him in [[Hope|hope]] he’ll prove a widower shortly,<br>I’ll wear the willow garland for his sake.’ |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | ame not her, she could say little less;<br>She had the wrong. But what said Henry’s [[Queen|queen]]?<br>For I have heard that she was there in place. |
| POST | ‘Tell him‘, quoth she, ’my mourning weeds are done,<br>And I am ready to put armour on.’ |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Belike she minds to play the Amazon.<br>But what said Warwick to these injuries? |
| POST | He, more incensed against your majesty<br>Than all the rest, discharged me with these words:<br>‘Tell him from me that he hath done me wrong,<br>And therefore I’ll uncrown him ere’t be long.’ |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Ha! Durst the traitor breathe out so proud words?<br>[[Well]], I will arm me, being thus forewarned.<br>They shall have wars and pay for their presumption.<br>But say, is Warwick friends with Margaret? |
| POST | Ay, gracious sovereign, they are so linked in [[Friendship|friendship]]<br>That young Prince Edward marries Warwick’s daughter. |
| GEORGE OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | Belike the elder; [[Clarence]] will have the younger.<br>Now, brother [[King]], farewell, and sit you fast,<br>For I will hence to Warwick’s other daughter,<br>That, though I want a kingdom, yet in [[Marriage|marriage]]<br>I may not prove inferior to yourself.<br>You that [[Love|love]] me and Warwick, follow me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Clarence]], and Somerset follows |
| *(didascalia)* | Not I—aside my thoughts aim at a further matter. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I stay not for the [[Love|love]] of Edward, but the [[Crown|crown]]. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | [[Clarence]] and Somerset both gone to Warwick?<br>Yet am I armed against the worst can happen,<br>And haste is needful in this desp’rate case.<br>Pembroke and Stafford, you in our behalf<br>Go levy men and make prepare for war.<br>They are already, or quickly will be, landed.<br>Myself in person will straight follow you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Pembroke and Stafford |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | But ere I go, [[Hastings]] and Montague,<br>Resolve my [[Doubt|doubt]]. You twain, of all the rest,<br>Are near’st to Warwick by [[Blood|blood]] and by alliance.<br>Tell me if you [[Love|love]] Warwick more than me.<br>If it be so, then both depart to him—<br>I rather wish you foes than hollow friends.<br>But if you mind to hold your true obedience,<br>Give me assurance with some friendly vow<br>That I may never have you in suspect. |
| MONTAGUE | So God help Montague as he proves true. |
| [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | And [[Hastings]] as he favours Edward’s cause. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Now, brother [[Richard]], will you stand by us? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] OF [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Ay, in despite of all that shall withstand you. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Why, so. Then am I sure of victory.<br>Now, therefore, let us hence and lose no hour<br>Till we meet Warwick with his foreign power. Exeunt |
