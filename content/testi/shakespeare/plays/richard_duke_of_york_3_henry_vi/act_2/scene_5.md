| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter [[King]] Henry |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | This battle fares like to the morning’s war,<br>When dying clouds contend with growing light,<br>What [[Time|time]] the shepherd, blowing of his nails,<br>Can neither call it perfect day nor [[Night|night]].<br>Now sways it this way like a mighty sea<br>Forced by the tide to combat with the [[Wind|wind]],<br>Now sways it that way like the selfsame sea<br>Forced to retire by fury of the [[Wind|wind]].<br>Sometime the flood prevails, and then the [[Wind|wind]];<br>Now one the better, then another best—<br>Both tugging to be victors, breast to breast,<br>Yet neither conqueror nor conquered.<br>So is the equal poise of this fell war.<br>Here on this molehill will I sit me down.<br>To whom God will, there be the victory.<br>For Margaret my [[Queen|queen]], and [[Clifford]], too,<br>Have chid me from the battle, swearing both<br>They prosper best of all when I am thence.<br>Would I were dead, if God’s good will were so—<br>For what is in this world but grief and woe?<br>O God! Methinks it were a happy life<br>To be no better than a homely swain.<br>To sit upon a [[Hill|hill]], as I do now;<br>To carve out dials quaintly, point by point,<br>Thereby to see the minutes how they run:<br>How many makes the hour full complete,<br>How many hours brings about the day,<br>How many days will finish up the year,<br>How many years a mortal man may live.<br>When this is known, then to divide the times:<br>So many hours must I tend my flock,<br>So many hours must I take my rest,<br>So many hours must I contemplate,<br>So many hours must I sport myself,<br>So many days my ewes have been with young,<br>So many weeks ere the poor fools will ean,<br>So many years ere I shall shear the fleece.<br>So minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, and years,<br>Passed over to the end they were created,<br>Would bring white hairs unto a quiet [[Grave|grave]].<br>Ah, what a life were this! How sweet! How lovely!<br>Gives not the hawthorn bush a sweeter shade<br>To shepherds looking on their seely sheep<br>Than doth a rich embroidered canopy<br>To kings that [[Fear|fear]] their subjects’ treachery?<br>O yes, it doth—a thousandfold it doth.<br>And to conclude, the shepherd’s homely curds,<br>His cold thin drink out of his leather bottle,<br>His wonted [[Sleep|sleep]] under a fresh tree’s shade,<br>All which secure and sweetly he enjoys,<br>Is far beyond a prince’s delicates,<br>His viands sparkling in a golden cup,<br>His [[Body|body]] couched in a curious bed,<br>When care, mistrust, and treason waits on him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter at one door a Soldier with a dead man in his arms. [[King]] Henry stands apart |
| SOLDIER | Ill blows the [[Wind|wind]] that profits nobody.<br>This man, whom hand to hand I slew in fight,<br>May be possessed with some store of crowns;<br>And I, that haply take them from him now,<br>May yet ere [[Night|night]] yield both my life and them<br>To some man else, as this dead man doth me. |
| *(didascalia)* | He removes the dead man’s helmet |
| SOLDIER | Who’s this? O God! It is my father’s face<br>Whom in this conflict I, unwares, have killed.<br>O, heavy times, begetting such events!<br>From [[London]] by the [[King]] was I pressed forth;<br>My father, being the [[Earl of Warwick]]’s man,<br>Came on the part of York, pressed by his master;<br>And I, who at his hands received my life,<br>Have by my hands of life bereaved him.<br>Pardon me, God, I knew not what I did;<br>And pardon, father, for I knew not thee.<br>My [[Tears|tears]] shall wipe away these bloody marks,<br>And no more words till they have flowed their fill.<br>He weeps |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O piteous spectacle! O bloody times!<br>Whiles lions war and battle for their dens,<br>Poor harmless lambs abide their enmity.<br>Weep, wretched man, I’ll aid thee tear for tear;<br>And let our hearts and eyes, like civil war,<br>Be blind with [[Tears|tears]], and break, o’ercharged with grief. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter at another door another Soldier with a dead man in his arms |
| SECOND SOLDIER | Thou that so stoutly hath resisted me,<br>Give me thy gold, if thou hast any gold—<br>For I have bought it with an hundred blows. |
| *(didascalia)* | He removes the dead man’s helmet |
| SECOND SOLDIER | But let me see: is this our foeman’s face?<br>Ah, no, no, no—it is mine only son!<br>Ah, boy, if any life be left in thee,<br>Throw up thine eye! (Weeping) See, see, what showers<br>arise,<br>Blown with the windy tempest of my heart,<br>Upon thy wounds, that kills mine eye and heart!<br>O, pity, God, this miserable age!<br>What stratagems, how fell, how butcherly,<br>Erroneous, mutinous, and unnatural,<br>This deadly quarrel daily doth beget!<br>O boy, thy father gave thee life too soon,<br>And hath bereft thee of thy life too late! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Woe above woe! Grief more than common grief!<br>O that my [[Death|death]] would stay these ruthful deeds!<br>O, pity, pity, gentle heaven, pity!<br>The red [[Rose|rose]] and the white are on his face,<br>The fatal colours of our striving houses;<br>The one his purple [[Blood|blood]] right [[Well|well]] resembles,<br>The other his pale cheeks, methinks, presenteth.<br>Wither one [[Rose|rose]], and let the other flourish—<br>If you contend, a thousand lives must wither. |
| FIRST SOLDIER | How will my mother for a father’s [[Death|death]]<br>Take on with me, and ne’er be satisfied! |
| SECOND SOLDIER | How will my wife for slaughter of my son<br>Shed seas of [[Tears|tears]], and ne’er be satisfied! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How will the country for these woeful chances<br>Misthink the [[King]], and not be satisfied! |
| FIRST SOLDIER | Was ever son so rued a father’s [[Death|death]]? |
| SECOND SOLDIER | Was ever father so bemoaned his son? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Was ever [[King|king]] so grieved for subjects’ woe?<br>Much is your sorrow, mine ten times so much. |
| FIRST SOLDIER | (to his father’s [[Body|body]])<br>I’ll bear thee hence where I may weep my fill. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit at one door with the [[Body|body]] of his father |
| SECOND SOLDIER | (to his son’s [[Body|body]])<br>These arms of mine shall be thy winding sheet;<br>My heart, sweet boy, shall be thy sepulchre,<br>For from my heart thine image ne‘er shall go.<br>My sighing breast shall be thy funeral bell,<br>And so obsequious will thy father be,<br>E’en for the loss of thee, having no more,<br>As Priam was for all his valiant sons.<br>I’ll bear thee hence, and let them fight that will—<br>For I have murdered where I should not kill. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit at another door with the [[Body|body]] of his son |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Sad-hearted men, much overgone with care,<br>Here sits a [[King|king]] more woeful than you are. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarums. Excursions. Enter Prince Edward |
| PRINCE EDWARD | Fly, father, fly—for all your friends are fled,<br>And Warwick rages like a chafed bull!<br>Away—for [[Death|death]] doth hold us in pursuit! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Queen Margaret]] |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Mount you, my lord—towards Berwick post amain.<br>Edward and [[Richard]], like a brace of greyhounds<br>Having the fearful flying hare in sight,<br>With fiery eyes sparkling for very wrath,<br>And bloody steel grasped in their ireful hands,<br>Are at our backs—and therefore hence amain. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Exeter |
| EXETER | Away—for vengeance comes along with them!<br>Nay—stay not to expostulate—make [[Speed|speed]]—<br>Or else come after. I’ll away before. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Nay, take me with thee, good sweet Exeter.<br>Not that I [[Fear|fear]] to stay, but [[Love|love]] to go<br>Whither the [[Queen]] intends. Forward, away. Exeunt |
