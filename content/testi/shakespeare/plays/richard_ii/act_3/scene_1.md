| *(didascalia)* | Enter Bolingbroke Duke of Lancaster and Hereford, the [[Duke of York]], the [[Earl of Northumberland]], Lord Ross, Harry Percy, and Lord Willoughby |
| BOLINGBROKE | Bring forth these men. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Bushy and Green, guarded as prisoners |
| BOLINGBROKE | Bushy and Green, I will not vex your souls,<br>Since presently your souls must part your bodies,<br>With too much urging your pernicious lives,<br>For ’twere no [[Charity|charity]]. Yet to wash your [[Blood|blood]]<br>From off my hands, here in the view of men<br>I will unfold some causes of your deaths.<br>You have misled a prince, a royal [[King|king]],<br>A happy gentleman in [[Blood|blood]] and lineaments,<br>By you unhappied and disfigured clean.<br>You have, in manner, with your sinful hours<br>Made a [[divorce]] betwixt his [[Queen|queen]] and him,<br>Broke the possession of a royal bed,<br>And stained the [[Beauty|beauty]] of a fair [[Queen|queen]]’s cheeks<br>With [[Tears|tears]] drawn from her eyes by your foul wrongs.<br>Myself—a prince by fortune of my birth,<br>Near to the [[King]] in [[Blood|blood]], and near in [[Love|love]]<br>Till you did make him misinterpret me—<br>Have stooped my neck under your injuries,<br>And sighed my English breath in foreign clouds,<br>Eating the bitter bread of banishment,<br>Whilst you have fed upon my signories,<br>Disparked my parks and felled my forest woods,<br>From my own windows torn my household coat,<br>Razed out my imprese, leaving me no sign,<br>Save men’s opinions and my living [[Blood|blood]],<br>To show the world I am a gentleman.<br>This and much more, much more than twice all this,<br>Condemns you to the [[Death|death]].—See them delivered over<br>To execution and the hand of [[Death|death]]. |
| BUSHY | More welcome is the stroke of [[Death|death]] to me<br>Than Bolingbroke to England. |
| GREEN | My comfort is that heaven will take our souls,<br>And plague injustice with the pains of hell. |
| BOLINGBROKE | My lord Northumberland, see them dispatched. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Northumberland, with Bushy and Green, guarded |
| BOLINGBROKE | Uncle, you say the [[Queen]] is at your [[house]].<br>For God’s sake, fairly let her be intreated.<br>Tell her I send to her my kind commends.<br>Take special care my greetings be delivered. |
| YORK | A gentleman of mine I have dispatched<br>With letters of your [[Love|love]] to her at large. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Thanks, gentle uncle.—Come, lords, away,<br>To fight with Glyndwr and his complices.<br>A while to work, and after, holiday. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
| *(didascalia)* | 3.2 Flourish. Enter [[King]] [[Richard]], the [[Duke of Aumerle]], the Bishop of Carlisle, and soldiers, with drum and colours |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Harlechly [[Castle]] call they this at hand? |
| AUMERLE | Yea, my lord. How brooks your grace the air<br>After your late tossing on the breaking seas? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Needs must I like it [[Well|well]]. I weep for joy<br>To stand upon my kingdom once again.<br>He touches the ground<br>Dear earth, I do salute thee with my hand,<br>Though rebels wound thee with their horses’ hoofs.<br>As a long-parted mother with her [[Child|child]]<br>Plays fondly with her [[Tears|tears]], and smiles in meeting,<br>So, weeping, smiling, greet I thee my earth,<br>And do thee favours with my royal hands.<br>Feed not thy sovereign’s foe, my gentle earth,<br>Nor with thy sweets comfort his ravenous sense;<br>But let thy spiders that suck up thy venom<br>And heavy-gaited toads lie in their way,<br>Doing annoyance to the treacherous feet<br>Which with usurping steps do trample thee.<br>Yield stinging nettles to mine enemies,<br>And when they from thy bosom pluck a flower<br>Guard it, I pray thee, with a lurking adder,<br>Whose [[Double|double]] tongue may with a mortal touch<br>Throw [[Death|death]] upon thy sovereign’s enemies.—<br>Mock not my senseless conjuration, lords.<br>This earth shall have a feeling, and these stones<br>Prove armed soldiers, ere her native [[King|king]]<br>Shall falter under foul rebellion’s arms. |
| BISHOP OF CARLISLE | [[Fear]] not, my lord. That power that made you [[King|king]]<br>Hath power to keep you [[King|king]] in spite of all. |
| AUMERLE | He means, my lord, that we are too remiss,<br>Whilst Bolingbroke, through our security,<br>Grows strong and great in substance and in friends. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Discomfortable cousin, know‘st thou not<br>That when the searching eye of heaven is hid<br>Behind the globe, that lights the lower world,<br>Then thieves and robbers range abroad unseen<br>In murders and in outrage bloody here;<br>But when from under this terrestrial ball<br>He fires the proud tops of the eastern pines,<br>And darts his light through every guilty hole,<br>Then murders, treasons, and detested sins,<br>The cloak of [[Night|night]] being plucked from off their backs,<br>Stand bare and naked, trembling at themselves?<br>So when this thief, this traitor, Bolingbroke,<br>Who all this while hath revelled in the [[Night|night]]<br>Whilst we were wand’ring with the Antipodes,<br>Shall see us rising in our throne, the east,<br>His treasons will sit blushing in his face,<br>Not able to endure the sight of day,<br>But, [[Self|self]]-affrighted, tremble at his sin.<br>Not all the [[Water|water]] in the rough rude sea<br>Can wash the balm from an anointed [[King|king]].<br>The breath of worldly men cannot depose<br>The deputy elected by the Lord.<br>For every man that Bolingbroke hath pressed<br>To lift shrewd steel against our golden [[Crown|crown]],<br>God for his [[Richard]] hath in heavenly pay<br>A glorious angel. Then if angels fight,<br>Weak men must fall; for heaven still guards the right. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Earl of Salisbury]] |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Welcome, my lord. How far off lies your power? |
| SALISBURY | Nor nea’er nor farther off, my gracious lord,<br>Than this weak arm. Discomfort guides my tongue,<br>And bids me speak of nothing but [[Despair|despair]].<br>One day too late, I [[Fear|fear]] me, noble lord,<br>Hath clouded all thy happy days on earth.<br>O, call back yesterday, bid [[Time|time]] return,<br>And thou shalt have twelve thousand fighting men.<br>Today, today, unhappy day too late,<br>Overthrows thy joys, friends, fortune, and thy state;<br>For all the Welshmen, hearing thou wert dead,<br>Are gone to Bolingbroke, dispersed, and fled. |
| AUMERLE | Comfort, my liege. Why looks your grace so pale? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | But now the [[Blood|blood]] of twenty thousand men<br>Did triumph in my face, and they are fled;<br>And till so much [[Blood|blood]] thither come again<br>Have I not reason to look pale and dead?<br>All souls that will be safe fly from my side,<br>For [[Time|time]] hath set a blot upon my [[Pride|pride]]. |
| AUMERLE | Comfort, my liege. Remember who you are. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | I had forgot myself. Am I not [[King]]?<br>Awake, thou sluggard majesty, thou [[Sleep|sleep]]’st!<br>Is not the [[King]]’s name forty thousand names?<br>Arm, arm, my name! A puny subject strikes<br>At thy great glory. Look not to the ground,<br>Ye favourites of a [[King|king]]: are we not high?<br>High be our thoughts. I know my uncle York<br>Hath power enough to serve our turn. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Scrope |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | But who comes here? |
| SCROPE | More health and happiness betide my liege<br>Than can my care-tuned tongue deliver him. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Mine ear is open and my heart prepared.<br>The worst is worldly loss thou canst unfold.<br>Say, is my kingdom lost? Why ’twas my care,<br>And what loss is it to be rid of care?<br>Strives Bolingbroke to be as great as we?<br>Greater he shall not be. If he serve God<br>We’ll serve Him too, and be his fellow so.<br>Revolt our subjects? That we cannot mend.<br>They break their [[Faith|faith]] to God as [[Well|well]] as us.<br>Cry woe, destruction, ruin, loss, [[Decay|decay]]:<br>The worst is [[Death|death]], and [[Death|death]] will have his day. |
| SCROPE | Glad am I that your highness is so armed<br>To bear the tidings of calamity.<br>Like an unseasonable stormy day,<br>Which makes the silver rivers drown their shores<br>As if the world were all dissolved to [[Tears|tears]],<br>So high above his limits swells the rage<br>Of Bolingbroke, covering your fearful land<br>With hard bright steel, and hearts harder than steel.<br>Whitebeards have armed their thin and hairless scalps<br>Against thy majesty. Boys with women’s voices<br>Strive to speak big, and clap their female joints no<br>In stiff unwieldy arms against thy [[Crown|crown]].<br>Thy very beadsmen learn to bend their bows<br>Of [[Double|double]]-fatal yew against thy state.<br>Yea, distaff-women manage rusty bills<br>Against thy seat. Both young and old [[Rebel|rebel]],<br>And all goes worse than I have power to tell. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Too [[Well|well]], too [[Well|well]] thou tell’st a tale so ill.<br>Where is the Earl of Wiltshire? Where is Bagot?<br>What is become of Bushy, where is Green,<br>That they have let the dangerous enemy<br>Measure our confines with such peaceful steps?<br>If we prevail, their heads shall pay for it.<br>I warrant they have made peace with Bolingbroke. |
| SCROPE | Peace have they made with him indeed, my lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | O villains, vipers damned without redemption!<br>[[dogs|Dogs]] easily won to fawn on any man I<br>Snakes in my heart-[[Blood|blood]] warmed, that sting my heart!<br>Three Judases, each one thrice-worse than Judas<br>Would they make peace? Terrible hell make war<br>Upon their spotted souls for this offence! |
| SCROPE | Sweet [[Love|love]], I see, changing his property,<br>Turns to the sourest and most deadly hate.<br>Again uncurse their souls. Their peace is made<br>With heads, and not with hands. Those whom you<br>curse<br>Have felt the worst of [[Death|death]]’s destroying wound,<br>And lie full low, graved in the hollow ground. |
| AUMERLE | Is Bushy, Green, and the Earl of Wiltshire dead? |
| SCROPE | Ay, all of them at Bristol lost their heads. |
| AUMERLE | Where is the Duke my father, with his power? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | No matter where. Of comfort no man speak.<br>Let’s talk of graves, of worms and epitaphs,<br>Make [[Dust|dust]] our paper, and with rainy eyes<br>Write sorrow on the bosom of the earth.<br>Let’s choose executors and talk of wills—<br>And yet not so, for what can we bequeath<br>Save our deposed bodies to the ground?<br>Our lands, our lives, and all are Bolingbroke’s;<br>And nothing can we call our own but [[Death|death]],<br>And that small model of the barren earth<br>Which serves as paste and cover to our bones. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sitting For God’s sake, let us sit upon the ground, |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | And tell sad stories of the [[Death|death]] of kings—<br>How some have been deposed, some slain in war,<br>Some haunted by the [[Ghosts|ghosts]] they have deposed,<br>Some poisoned by their wives, some sleeping killed,<br>All murdered. For within the hollow [[Crown|crown]]<br>That rounds the mortal temples of a [[King|king]]<br>Keeps [[Death]] his [[Court|court]]; and there the antic sits,<br>Scoffing his state and grinning at his pomp,<br>Allowing him a breath, a little scene,<br>To monarchize, be feared, and kill with looks,<br>Infusing him with [[Self|self]] and vain conceit,<br>As if this flesh which walls about our life<br>Were brass impregnable; and humoured thus,<br>Comes at the last, and with a little pin<br>Bores through his [[Castle|castle]] wall; and farewell, [[King|king]].<br>Cover your heads, and mock not flesh and [[Blood|blood]]<br>With solemn reverence. Throw away respect,<br>Tradition, form, and ceremonious duty,<br>For you have but mistook me all this while.<br>I live with bread, like you; feel want,<br>Taste grief, need friends. Subjected thus,<br>How can you say to me I am a [[King|king]]? |
| BISHOP OF CARLISLE | My lord, wise men ne’er wail their present woes,<br>But presently prevent the ways to wail.<br>To [[Fear|fear]] the foe, since [[Fear|fear]] oppresseth strength,<br>Gives in your weakness strength unto your foe;<br>And so your follies fight against yourself.<br>[[Fear]], and be slain. No worse can come to fight;<br>And fight and die is [[Death|death]] destroying [[Death|death]],<br>Where fearing dying pays [[Death|death]] servile breath. |
| AUMERLE | My father hath a power. Enquire of him,<br>And learn to make a [[Body|body]] of a limb. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] standing |
| AUMERLE | Thou chid’st me [[Well|well]]. Proud Bolingbroke, I come<br>To change blows with thee for our day of doom.<br>This ague-fit of [[Fear|fear]] is overblown.<br>An easy task it is to win our own.<br>Say, Scrope, where lies our uncle with his power?<br>Speak sweetly, man, although thy looks be sour. |
| SCROPE | Men judge by the complexion of the sky<br>The state and inclination of the day.<br>So may you by my dull and heavy eye<br>My tongue hath but a heavier tale to say.<br>I play the torturer by small and small<br>To lengthen out the worst that must be spoken.<br>Your uncle York is joined with Bolingbroke,<br>And all your northern castles yielded up,<br>And all your southern gentlemen in arms<br>Upon his faction. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Thou hast said enough.<br>(To Aumerle) Beshrew thee, cousin, which didst lead me forth<br>Of that sweet way I was in to [[Despair|despair]].<br>What say you now? What comfort have we now?<br>By heaven, I’ll hate him everlastingly<br>That bids me be of comfort any more.<br>Go to Flint [[Castle]]; there I’ll pine away.<br>A [[King|king]], woe’s slave, shall kingly woe obey.<br>That power I have, discharge, and let them go<br>To ear the land that hath some [[Hope|hope]] to grow;<br>For I have none. Let no man speak again<br>To alter this, for counsel is but vain. |
| AUMERLE | My liege, one word. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | He does me [[Double|double]] wrong<br>That wounds me with the flatteries of his tongue.<br>Discharge my followers. Let them hence away<br>From [[Richard]]’s [[Night|night]] to Bolingbroke’s fair day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
