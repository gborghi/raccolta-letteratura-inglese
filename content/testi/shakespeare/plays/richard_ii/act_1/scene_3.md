| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord Marshal [with officers setting out chairs], and the [[Duke of Aumerle]] |
| LORD MARSHAL | My lord Aumerle, is Harry Hereford armed? |
| AUMERLE | Yea, at all points, and longs to enter in. |
| LORD MARSHAL | The Duke of Norfolk, sprightfully and bold,<br>Stays but the summons of the appellant’s trumpet. |
| AUMERLE | Why then, the champions are prepared, and stay<br>For nothing but his majesty’s approach. |
| *(didascalia)* | The trumpets sound, and [[King]] [[Richard]] enters, with [[John of Gaunt]], Duke of Lancaster, Bushy, Bagot, Green,] and other nobles. When they are set, enter [[Mowbray]] Duke of Norfolk, defendant, in arms, Fand a Herald] |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Marshal, demand of yonder champion<br>The cause of his arrival here in arms.<br>Ask him his name, and orderly proceed<br>To swear him in the justice of his cause. |
| LORD MARSHAL | (to [[Mowbray]])<br>In God’s name and the [[King]]‘s, say who thou art,<br>And why thou com’st thus knightly clad in arms,<br>Against what man thou com’st, and what thy<br>quarrel.<br>Speak truly on thy knighthood and thy oath,<br>As so defend thee heaven and thy valour! |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | My name is Thomas [[Mowbray]], Duke of Norfolk,<br>Who hither come engaged by my oath—<br>Which God defend a knight should violate—<br>Both to defend my loyalty and [[Truth|truth]]<br>To God, my [[King|king]], and my succeeding issue,<br>Against the Duke of Hereford that appeals me;<br>And by the grace of God and this mine arm<br>To prove him, in defending of myself,<br>A traitor to my God, my [[King|king]], and me.<br>And as I truly fight, defend me heaven! |
| *(didascalia)* | He sits. |
| *(didascalia)* | The trumpets sound. Enter Bolingbroke Duke of Hereford, appellant, in armour, and a Herald |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Marshal, ask yonder knight in arms<br>Both who he is and why he cometh hither<br>Thus plated in habiliments of war;<br>And formally, according to our law,<br>Depose him in the justice of his cause. |
| LORD MARSHAL | (to Bolingbroke)<br>What is thy name? And wherefore com’st thou hither<br>Before [[King]] [[Richard]] in his royal lists?<br>Against whom comest thou? And what’s thy quarrel?<br>Speak like a true knight, so defend thee heaven! |
| BOLINGBROKE | Harry of Hereford, Lancaster, and Derby<br>Am I, who ready here do stand in arms<br>To prove by God’s grace and my [[Body|body]]’s valour<br>In lists on Thomas [[Mowbray]], Duke of Norfolk,<br>That he is a traitor foul and dangerous<br>To God of heaven, [[King]] [[Richard]], and to me.<br>And as I truly fight, defend me heaven! |
| *(didascalia)* | He sits |
| LORD MARSHAL | On [[Pain|pain]] of [[Death|death]], no person be so bold<br>Or daring-hardy as to touch the lists<br>Except the Marshal and such officers<br>Appointed to direct these fair designs. |
| *(didascalia)* | BOLINGBROKE standing |
| LORD MARSHAL | Lord Marshal, let me kiss my sovereign’s hand<br>And bow my knee before his majesty,<br>For [[Mowbray]] and myself are like two men<br>That vow a long and weary pilgrimage;<br>Then let us take a ceremonious leave<br>And loving farewell of our several friends. |
| LORD MARSHAL | (to [[King]] [[Richard]])<br>The appellant in all duty greets your highness,<br>And craves to kiss your hand and take his leave. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | We will descend and fold him in our arms.<br>He descends from his seat and embraces Bolingbroke<br>Cousin of Hereford, as thy cause is just,<br>So be thy fortune in this royal fight.<br>Farewell, my [[Blood|blood]], which if today thou shed,<br>Lament we may, but not [[Revenge|revenge]] thee dead. |
| BOLINGBROKE | O, let no noble eye profane a tear<br>For me if I be gored with [[Mowbray]]’s spear.<br>As confident as is the falcon’s flight<br>Against a bird do I with [[Mowbray]] fight.<br>(To the Lord Marshal) My loving lord, I take my leave of you;<br>(To Aumerle) Of you, my noble cousin, Lord Aumerle;<br>Not sick, although I have to do with [[Death|death]],<br>But lusty, young, and cheerly drawing breath.<br>Lo, as at English feasts, so I regreet<br>The daintiest last, to make the end most sweet. |
| *(didascalia)* | (To Gaunt, kneeling O thou, the earthly author of my [[Blood|blood]], |
| BOLINGBROKE | Whose youthful spirit in me regenerate<br>Doth with a two-fold vigour lift me up<br>To reach at victory above my head,<br>Add proof unto mine armour with thy prayers,<br>And with thy blessings steel my lance’s point,<br>That it may enter [[Mowbray]]’s waxen coat<br>And furbish new the name of John a Gaunt<br>Even in the lusty haviour of his son. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | God in thy good cause make thee prosperous!<br>Be swift like [[Lightning|lightning]] in the execution,<br>And let thy blows, doubly redoubled,<br>Fall like amazing thunder on the casque<br>Of thy adverse pernicious enemy.<br>Rouse up thy youthful [[Blood|blood]], be valiant, and live. |
| *(didascalia)* | BOLINGBROKE standing |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Mine [[innocence]] and Saint George to thrive! |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] standing |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | However God or fortune cast my lot,<br>There lives or dies, true to [[King]] [[Richard]]’s throne,<br>A loyal, just, and upright gentleman.<br>Never did captive with a freer heart<br>Cast off his [[Chains|chains]] of bondage and embrace<br>His golden uncontrolled enfranchisement<br>More than my dancing [[Soul|soul]] doth celebrate<br>This feast of battle with mine adversary.<br>Most mighty liege, and my companion peers,<br>Take from my mouth the wish of happy years.<br>As gentle and as jocund as to jest<br>Go I to fight. [[Truth]] hath a quiet breast. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Farewell, my lord. Securely I espy<br>Virtue with valour couched in thine eye.—<br>Order the trial, Marshal, and begin. |
| LORD MARSHAL | Harry of Hereford, Lancaster, and Derby,<br>Receive thy lance; and God defend the right! |
| *(didascalia)* | An officer bears a lance to Bolingbroke |
| BOLINGBROKE | Strong as a tower in [[Hope|hope]], I cry ‘Amen!’ |
| LORD MARSHAL | (to an officer)<br>Go bear this lance to Thomas, Duke of Norfolk. |
| *(didascalia)* | An officer bears a lance to [[Mowbray]] |
| FIRST HERALD | Harry of Hereford, Lancaster, and Derby<br>Stands here for God, his sovereign, and himself,<br>On [[Pain|pain]] to be found false and recreant,<br>To prove the Duke of Norfolk, Thomas [[Mowbray]],<br>A traitor to his God, his [[King|king]], and him,<br>And dares him to set forward to the fight. |
| SECOND HERALD | Here standeth Thomas [[Mowbray]], Duke of Norfolk,<br>On [[Pain|pain]] to be found false and recreant,<br>Both to defend himself and to approve<br>Henry of Hereford, Lancaster, and Derby<br>To God his sovereign and to him disloyal,<br>Courageously and with a free [[Desire|desire]]<br>Attending but the signal to begin. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound trumpets, and set forward combatants! |
| *(didascalia)* | A charge is sounded. |
| LORD MARSHAL | [[King]] [[Richard]] throws down his warder<br>Stay, the [[King]] hath thrown his warder down. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Let them lay by their helmets and their spears,<br>And both return back to their chairs again. |
| *(didascalia)* | Bolingbroke and [[Mowbray]] disarm and sit |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (To the nobles) Withdraw with us, and let the trumpets sound<br>While we return these dukes what we decree. |
| *(didascalia)* | A long flourish, during which [[King]] [[Richard]] and his nobles withdraw and hold council, then come forward]. [[King]] [[Richard]] addresses Bolingbroke and [[Mowbray]] |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Draw near, and list what with our council we have<br>done.<br>For that our kingdom’s earth should not be soiled<br>With that dear [[Blood|blood]] which it hath fostered,<br>And for our eyes do hate the dire aspect<br>Of civil wounds ploughed up with neighbours’ swords,<br>Which, so roused up with boist’rous untuned drums,<br>With harsh-resounding trumpets’ dreadful bray,<br>And grating shock of wrathful iron arms,<br>Might from our quiet confines fright fair peace<br>And make us wade even in our kindred’s [[Blood|blood]],<br>Therefore we banish you our territories.<br>You, cousin Hereford, upon [[Pain|pain]] of life,<br>Till twice five summers have enriched our fields<br>Shall not regreet our fair dominions,<br>But tread the stranger paths of banishment. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Your will be done. This must my comfort be:<br>That sun that warms you here shall shine on me,<br>And those his golden beams to you here lent<br>Shall point on me and gild my banishment. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Norfolk, for thee remains a heavier doom,<br>Which I with some unwillingness pronounce.<br>The sly slow hours shall not determinate<br>The dateless limit of thy dear [[Exile|exile]].<br>The hopeless word of ‘never to return’<br>Breathe I against thee, upon [[Pain|pain]] of life. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | A heavy sentence, my most sovereign liege,<br>And all unlooked-for from your highness’ mouth.<br>A dearer merit, not so deep a maim<br>As to be cast forth in the common air,<br>Have I deserved at your highness’ hands.<br>The language I have learnt these forty years,<br>My native English, now I must forgo,<br>And now my tongue’s use is to me no more<br>Than an unstringèd viol or a harp,<br>Or like a cunning instrument cased up,<br>Or, being open, put into his hands<br>That knows no touch to tune the harmony. |
| *(didascalia)* | Within my mouth you have enjailed my tongue, |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Doubly portcullised with my teeth and lips,<br>And dull unfeeling barren ignorance<br>Is made my jailer to attend on me.<br>I am too old to fawn upon a [[Nurse|nurse]],<br>Too far in years to be a pupil now.<br>What is thy sentence then but speechless [[Death|death]],<br>Which robs my tongue from breathing native breath? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | It boots thee not to be compassionate.<br>After our sentence, plaining comes too late. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | Then thus I turn me from my country’s light,<br>To dwell in solemn shades of endless [[Night|night]]. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Return again, and take an oath with thee.<br>(To both) Lay on our royal [[Sword|sword]] your banished hands.<br>Swear by the duty that you owe to God—<br>Our part therein we banish with yourselves—<br>To keep the oath that we administer.<br>You never shall, so help you [[Truth|truth]] and God,<br>Embrace each other’s [[Love|love]] in banishment,<br>Nor never look upon each other’s face,<br>Nor never write, regreet, nor reconcile<br>This low‘ring tempest of your [[Home|home]]-bred hate,<br>Nor never by advised purpose meet<br>To plot, contrive, or complot any ill<br>’Gainst us, our state, our subjects, or our land. |
| BOLINGBROKE | I swear. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | And I, to keep all this. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Norfolk, so far as to mine enemy:<br>By this [[Time|time]], had the [[King]] permitted us,<br>One of our souls had wandered in the air,<br>Banished this frail sepulchre of our flesh,<br>As now our flesh is banished from this land.<br>Confess thy treasons ere thou fly the realm.<br>Since thou hast far to go, bear not along<br>The clogging burden of a guilty [[Soul|soul]]. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | No, Bolingbroke, if ever I were traitor,<br>My name be blotted from the book of life,<br>And I from heaven banished as from hence.<br>But what thou art, God, thou, and I do know,<br>And all too soon I [[Fear|fear]] the [[King]] shall rue.<br>Farewell, my liege. Now no way can I stray:<br>Save back to England, all the world’s my way. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Uncle, even in the glasses of thine eyes<br>I see thy grieved heart. Thy sad aspect<br>Hath from the number of his banished years<br>Plucked four away. (To Bolingbroke) Six frozen winters<br>spent,<br>Return with welcome [[Home|home]] from banishment. |
| BOLINGBROKE | How long a [[Time|time]] lies in one little word!<br>Four lagging winters and four wanton springs<br>End in a word: such is the breath of kings. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | I thank my liege that in regard of me<br>He shortens four years of my son’s [[Exile|exile]].<br>But little vantage shall I reap thereby,<br>For ere the six years that he hath to spend<br>Can change their moons and bring their times about,<br>My oil-dried lamp and [[Time|time]]-bewasted light<br>Shall be extinct with age and endless [[Night|night]].<br>My inch of taper will be burnt and done,<br>And blindfold [[Death|death]] not let me see my son. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Why, uncle, thou hast many years to live. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | But not a minute, [[King]], that thou canst give.<br>Shorten my days thou canst with sudden sorrow,<br>And pluck nights from me, but not lend a morrow.<br>Thou canst help [[Time|time]] to furrow me with age,<br>But stop no wrinkle in his pilgrimage.<br>Thy word is current with him for my [[Death|death]],<br>But dead, thy kingdom cannot buy my breath. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Thy son is banished upon good advice,<br>Whereto thy tongue a party verdict gave.<br>Why at our justice seem’st thou then to lour? |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Things sweet to taste prove in digestion sour.<br>You urged me as a judge, but I had rather<br>You would have bid me argue like a father.<br>Alas, I looked when some of you should say<br>I was too strict to make mine own away,<br>But you gave leave to my unwilling tongue<br>Against my will to do myself this wrong. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Cousin, farewell; and uncle, bid him so.<br>Six years we banish him, and he shall go. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exeunt all but Aumerle, the Lord Marshal, [[John of Gaunt]], and Bolingbroke |
| AUMERLE | (to Bolingbroke)<br>Cousin, farewell. What presence must not know,<br>From where you do remain let paper show.<br>[Exit] |
| LORD MARSHAL | (to Bolingbroke)<br>My lord, no leave take I, for I will ride<br>As far as land will let me by your side. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | (to Bolingbroke)<br>O, to what purpose dost thou hoard thy words,<br>That thou return’st no greeting to thy friends? |
| BOLINGBROKE | I have too few to take my leave of you,<br>When the tongue’s office should be prodigal<br>To breathe the abundant dolour of the heart. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Thy grief is but thy absence for a [[Time|time]]. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Joy absent, grief is present for that [[Time|time]]. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | What is six winters? They are quickly gone. |
| BOLINGBROKE | To men in joy, but grief makes one hour ten. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Call it a travel that thou tak’st for pleasure. |
| BOLINGBROKE | My heart will sigh when I miscall it so,<br>Which finds it an enforced pilgrimage. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | The sullen passage of thy weary steps<br>Esteem as foil wherein thou art to set<br>The precious [[Jewel|jewel]] of thy [[Home|home]] return. |
| BOLINGBROKE | O, who can hold a [[Fire|fire]] in his hand<br>By thinking on the frosty Caucasus,<br>Or cloy the hungry edge of appetite<br>By bare [[Imagination|imagination]] of a feast,<br>Or wallow naked in December [[Snow|snow]]<br>By thinking on fantastic [[Summer|summer]]’s heat?<br>O no, the apprehension of the good<br>Gives but the greater feeling to the worse.<br>Fell sorrow’s tooth doth never rankle more<br>Than when he bites, but lanceth not the sore. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Come, come, my son, I’ll bring thee on thy way.<br>Had I thy youth and cause, I would not stay. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Then England’s ground, farewell. Sweet soil, adieu,<br>My mother and my [[Nurse|nurse]] that bears me yet!<br>Where’er I wander, boast of this I can:<br>Though banished, yet a trueborn Englishman. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
