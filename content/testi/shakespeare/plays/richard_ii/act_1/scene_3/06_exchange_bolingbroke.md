|---|---|
| BOLINGBROKE | How long a [[Time|time]] lies in one little word! Four lagging winters and four wanton springs End in a word: such is the breath of kings. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | I thank my liege that in regard of me He shortens four years of my son’s [[Exile|exile]]. But little vantage shall I reap thereby, For ere the six years that he hath to spend Can change their moons and bring their times about, My oil-dried lamp and [[Time|time]]-bewasted light Shall be extinct with age and endless [[Night|night]]. My inch of taper will be burnt and done, And blindfold [[Death|death]] not let me see my son. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Why, uncle, thou hast many years to live. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | But not a minute, [[King]], that thou canst give. Shorten my days thou canst with sudden sorrow, And pluck nights from me, but not lend a morrow. Thou canst help [[Time|time]] to furrow me with age, But stop no wrinkle in his pilgrimage. Thy word is current with him for my [[Death|death]], But dead, thy kingdom cannot buy my breath. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Thy son is banished upon good advice, Whereto thy tongue a party verdict gave. Why at our justice seem’st thou then to lour? |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Things sweet to taste prove in digestion sour. You urged me as a judge, but I had rather You would have bid me argue like a father. Alas, I looked when some of you should say I was too strict to make mine own away, But you gave leave to my unwilling tongue Against my will to do myself this wrong. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Cousin, farewell; and uncle, bid him so. Six years we banish him, and he shall go. Flourish. Exeunt all but Aumerle, the Lord Marshal, [[John of Gaunt]], and Bolingbroke |
| AUMERLE | (to Bolingbroke) Cousin, farewell. What presence must not know, From where you do remain let paper show. [Exit] |
| LORD MARSHAL | (to Bolingbroke) My lord, no leave take I, for I will ride As far as land will let me by your side. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | (to Bolingbroke) O, to what purpose dost thou hoard thy words, That thou return’st no greeting to thy friends? |
| BOLINGBROKE | I have too few to take my leave of you, When the tongue’s office should be prodigal To breathe the abundant dolour of the heart. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Thy grief is but thy absence for a [[Time|time]]. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Joy absent, grief is present for that [[Time|time]]. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | What is six winters? They are quickly gone. |
| BOLINGBROKE | To men in joy, but grief makes one hour ten. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | Call it a travel that thou tak’st for pleasure. |
| BOLINGBROKE | My heart will sigh when I miscall it so, Which finds it an enforced pilgrimage. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | The sullen passage of thy weary steps Esteem as foil wherein thou art to set The precious [[Jewel|jewel]] of thy [[Home|home]] return. |
| BOLINGBROKE | O, who can hold a [[Fire|fire]] in his hand By thinking on the frosty Caucasus, Or cloy the hungry edge of appetite By bare [[Imagination|imagination]] of a feast, Or wallow naked in December [[Snow|snow]] By thinking on fantastic [[Summer|summer]]’s heat? O no, the apprehension of the good Gives but the greater feeling to the worse. Fell sorrow’s tooth doth never rankle more Than when he bites, but lanceth not the sore. |
