| BOLINGBROKE | (throwing down his gage) Pale trembling coward, there I throw my gage, Disclaiming here the kindred of the [[King]], And lay aside my high [[Blood|blood]]’s royalty, Which [[Fear|fear]], not reverence, makes thee to except. If guilty dread have left thee so much strength As to take up mine [[Honour|honour]]’s pawn, then stoop. By that, and all the rites of knighthood else, Will I make good against thee, arm to arm, What I have spoke or thou canst worse devise. |
| [[Mowbray|MOWBRAY]] | (taking up the gage) I take it up, and by that [[Sword|sword]] I swear Which gently laid my knighthood on my shoulder, I’ll answer thee in any fair degree Or chivalrous design of knightly trial; And when I mount, alive may I not light If I be traitor or unjustly fight! |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (to Bolingbroke) What doth our cousin lay to [[Mowbray]]’s charge? It must be great that can inherit us So much as of a thought of ill in him. |
