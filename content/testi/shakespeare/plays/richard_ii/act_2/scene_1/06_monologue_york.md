| YORK | How long shall I be patient? Ah, how long Shall tender duty make me suffer wrong? Not [[Gloucester]]’s [[Death|death]], nor Hereford’s banishment, Nor Gaunt’s rebukes, nor England’s private wrongs, Nor the prevention of poor Bolingbroke About his [[Marriage|marriage]], nor my own disgrace, Have ever made me sour my patient cheek, Or bend one wrinkle on my sovereign’s face. I am the last of noble Edward’s sons, Of whom thy father, Prince of Wales, was first. In war was never [[Lion|lion]] raged more fierce, In peace was never gentle [[Lamb|lamb]] more mild, Than was that young and princely gentleman. His face thou hast, for even so looked he, Accomplished with the number of thy hours. But when he frowned it was against the French, And not against his friends. His noble hand Did win what he did spend, and spent not that Which his triumphant father’s hand had won. His hands were guilty of no kindred [[Blood|blood]], But bloody with the enemies of his kin. O, [[Richard]], York is too far gone with grief, Or else he never would compare between. |
