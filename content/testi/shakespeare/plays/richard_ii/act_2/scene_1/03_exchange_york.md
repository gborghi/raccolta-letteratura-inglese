| YORK | The [[King]] is come. Deal mildly with his youth, For young hot colts, being reined, do rage the more. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | How fares our noble uncle Lancaster? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | What comfort, man? How is’t with aged Gaunt? |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | O, how that name befits my composition I Old Gaunt indeed, and gaunt in being old. Within me grief hath kept a tedious fast, And who abstains from meat that is not gaunt? For sleeping England long [[Time|time]] have I watched. Watching breeds leanness, leanness is all gaunt. The pleasure that some fathers feed upon Is my strict fast: I mean my children’s looks. And therein fasting, hast thou made me gaunt. Gaunt am I for the [[Grave|grave]], gaunt as a [[Grave|grave]], Whose hollow womb inherits naught but bones. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Can sick men play so nicely with their names? |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | No, misery makes sport to mock itself. Since thou dost seek to kill my name in me, I mock my name, great [[King]], to flatter thee. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Should dying men flatter with those that live? |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | No, no, men living flatter those that die. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Thou now a-dying sayst thou flatt’rest me. |
| [[John of Gaunt|JOHN OF GAUNT]] | O no: thou diest, though I the sicker be. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | I am in health; I breathe, and see thee ill. |
