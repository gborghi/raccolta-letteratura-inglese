| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Queen]], Bushy, and Bagot |
| BUSHY | Madam, your majesty is too much sad.<br>You promised when you parted with the [[King]]<br>To lay aside life-harming heaviness<br>And entertain a cheerful disposition. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | To please the [[King]] I did; to please myself<br>I cannot do it. Yet I know no cause<br>Why I should welcome such a guest as grief,<br>Save bidding farewell to so sweet a guest<br>As my sweet [[Richard]]. Yet again, methinks<br>Some [[unborn]] sorrow, ripe in fortune’s womb,<br>Is coming towards me; and my inward [[Soul|soul]]<br>At nothing trembles. With something it grieves<br>More than with parting from my lord the [[King]]. |
| BUSHY | Each substance of a grief hath twenty shadows<br>Which shows like grief itself but is not so.<br>For sorrow’s eye, glazed with blinding [[Tears|tears]],<br>Divides one thing entire to many objects—<br>Like perspectives, which, rightly gazed upon,<br>Show nothing but confusion; eyed awry,<br>Distinguish form. So your sweet majesty,<br>Looking awry upon your lord’s departure,<br>Find shapes of grief more than himself to wail,<br>Which, looked on as it is, is naught but shadows<br>Of what it is not. Then, thrice-gracious [[Queen]],<br>More than your lord’s departure weep not: more is not seen,<br>Or if it be, ’tis with false sorrow’s eye,<br>Which for things true weeps things imaginary. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | It may be so, but yet my inward [[Soul|soul]]<br>Persuades me it is otherwise. Howe’er it be,<br>I cannot but be sad: so heavy-sad<br>As thought—on thinking on no thought I think—<br>Makes me with heavy nothing faint and shrink. |
| BUSHY | ’Tis nothing but conceit, my gracious lady. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | ‘Tis nothing less: conceit is still derived<br>From some forefather grief; mine is not so;<br>For nothing hath begot my something grief—<br>Or something hath the nothing that I grieve—<br>’Tis in reversion that I do possess—<br>But what it is that is not yet known what,<br>I cannot name; ’tis nameless woe, I wot. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Green |
| GREEN | God save your majesty, and [[Well|well]] met, gentlemen.<br>I [[Hope|hope]] the [[King]] is not yet shipped for Ireland. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Why hop‘st thou so? ’Tis better [[Hope|hope]] he is,<br>For his designs crave haste, his haste good [[Hope|hope]].<br>Then wherefore dost thou [[Hope|hope]] he is not shipped? |
| GREEN | That he, our [[Hope|hope]], might have retired his power,<br>And driven into [[Despair|despair]] an enemy’s [[Hope|hope]],<br>Who strongly hath set footing in this land.<br>The banished Bolingbroke repeals himself,<br>And with uplifted arms is safe arrived<br>At Ravenspurgh. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Now God in heaven forbid! |
| GREEN | Ah madam, ’tis too true! And, that is worse,<br>The Lord Northumberland, his son young Harry Percy,<br>The Lords of Ross, Beaumont, and Willoughby,<br>With all their powerful friends, are fled to him. |
| BUSHY | Why have you not proclaimed Northumberland,<br>And all the rest, revolted faction-traitors? |
| GREEN | We have; whereupon the [[Earl of Worcester]]<br>Hath broke his staff, resigned his stewardship,<br>And all the household servants fled with him<br>To Bolingbroke. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | So, Green, thou art the midwife to my woe,<br>And Bolingbroke my sorrow’s dismal heir.<br>Now hath my [[Soul|soul]] brought forth her prodigy,<br>And I, a gasping new-delivered mother,<br>Have woe to woe, sorrow to sorrow joined. |
| BUSHY | [[Despair]] not, madam. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Who shall hinder me?<br>I will [[Despair|despair]], and be at enmity<br>With cozening [[Hope|hope]]. He is a flatterer,<br>A parasite, a keeper-back of [[Death|death]],<br>Who gently would dissolve the bonds of life,<br>Which false [[Hope|hope]] lingers in extremity. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of York]], wearing a gorget |
| GREEN | Here comes the [[Duke of York]]. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | With signs of war about his aged neck.<br>O, full of careful business are his looks!<br>Uncle, for God’s sake speak comfortable words. |
| YORK | Should I do so, I should belie my thoughts.<br>Comfort’s in heaven, and we are on the earth,<br>Where nothing lives but crosses, cares, and grief.<br>Your husband, he is gone to save far off,<br>Whilst others come to make him lose at [[Home|home]].<br>Here am I, left to underprop his land,<br>Who, weak with age, cannot support myself.<br>Now comes the sick hour that his surfeit made.<br>Now shall he try his friends that flattered him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servingman |
| SERVINGMAN | My lord, your son was gone before I came. |
| YORK | He was? Why so, go all which way it will.<br>The nobles they are fled. The commons they are cold,<br>And will, I [[Fear|fear]], revolt on Hereford’s side.<br>Sirrah, get thee to Pleshey, to my sister [[Gloucester]].<br>Bid her send me presently a thousand pound—<br>Hold; take my ring. |
| SERVINGMAN | My lord, I had forgot to tell your lordship,<br>Today as I came by I called there—<br>But I shall grieve you to report the rest. |
| YORK | What is’t, knave? |
| SERVINGMAN | An hour before I came, the Duchess died. |
| YORK | God for his [[Mercy|mercy]], what a tide of woes<br>Comes rushing on this woeful land at once!<br>I know not what to do. I would to God,<br>So my untruth had not provoked him to it,<br>The [[King]] had cut off my head with my brother’s.<br>What, are there no posts dispatched for Ireland?<br>How shall we do for money for these wars?<br>(To the [[Queen]]) Come, sister—cousin, I would say; pray<br>pardon me.<br>(To the Servingman) Go, fellow, get thee [[Home|home]]. Provide<br>some carts,<br>And bring away the armour that is there. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Servingman |
| YORK | Gentlemen, will you go muster men?<br>If I know how or which way to order these affairs<br>Thus disorderly thrust into my hands,<br>Never believe me. Both are my kinsmen.<br>T‘one is my sovereign, whom both my oath<br>And duty bids defend; t’other again<br>Is my kinsman, whom the [[King]] hath wronged,<br>Whom [[Conscience|conscience]] and my kindred bids to right.<br>[[Well]], somewhat we must do. (To the [[Queen]]) Come,<br>cousin,<br>I’ll dispose of you.—<br>Gentlemen, go muster up your men,<br>And meet me presently at Berkeley [[Castle]].<br>I should to Pleshey too, but [[Time|time]] will not permit.<br>All is uneven,<br>And everything is left at six and seven. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt the [[Duke of York]] and the [[Queen]]. Bushy, Bagot, and Green remain |
| BUSHY | The [[Wind|wind]] sits fair for news to go for Ireland,<br>But none returns. For us to levy power<br>Proportionable to the enemy<br>Is all unpossible. |
| GREEN | Besides, our nearness to the [[King]] in [[Love|love]]<br>Is near the hate of those [[Love|love]] not the [[King]]. |
| BAGOT | And that is the wavering commons; for their [[Love|love]]<br>Lies in their purses, and whoso empties them<br>By so much fills their hearts with deadly hate. |
| BUSHY | Wherein the [[King]] stands generally condemned. |
| BAGOT | If judgement lie in them, then so do we,<br>Because we ever have been near the [[King]]. |
| GREEN | [[Well]], I will for refuge straight to Bristol [[Castle]].<br>The Earl of Wiltshire is already there. |
| BUSHY | Thither will I with you; for little office<br>Will the hateful commoners perform for us,<br>Except like curs to tear us all to pieces.<br>(To Bagot) Will you go along with us? |
| BAGOT | No, I will to Ireland, to his majesty.<br>Farewell: if heart’s presages be not vain<br>We three here part that ne’er shall meet again. |
| BUSHY | That’s as York thrives to beat back Bolingbroke. |
| GREEN | Alas, poor Duke, the task he undertakes<br>Is numb’ring sands and drinking oceans dry.<br>Where one on his side fights, thousands will fly. |
| *(didascalia)* | BAGOT |
| GREEN | Farewell at once, for once, for all and ever. |
| BUSHY | [[Well]], we may meet again. |
| BAGOT | I [[Fear|fear]] me never. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Bushy and Green at one door, and Bagot at another door |
