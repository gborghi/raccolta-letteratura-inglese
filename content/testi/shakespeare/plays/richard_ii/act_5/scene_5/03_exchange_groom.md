| GROOM | Hail, royal Prince! |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Thanks, noble peer. The cheapest of us is ten groats too dear. What art thou, and how com’st thou hither, Where no man never comes but that sad dog That brings me food to make misfortune live? |
| GROOM | I was a poor groom of thy stable, [[King]], When thou wert [[King|king]]; who, travelling towards York, With much ado at length have gotten leave To look upon my sometimes royal master’s face. O, how it erned my heart when I beheld In [[London]] streets, that coronation day, When Bolingbroke rode on roan Barbary, That [[Horse|horse]] that thou so often hast bestrid, That [[Horse|horse]] that I so carefully have dressed! |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Rode he on Barbary? Tell me, gentle friend, How went he under him? |
| GROOM | So proudly as if he disdained the ground. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | So proud that Bolingbroke was on his back. That jade hath eat bread from my royal hand; This hand hath made him proud with clapping him. Would he not stumble, would he not fall down- Since [[Pride|pride]] must have a fall—and break the neck Of that proud man that did usurp his back? [[Forgiveness]], [[Horse|horse]]! Why do I rail on thee, Since thou, created to be awed by man, Wast born to bear? I was not made a [[Horse|horse]], And yet I bear a burden like an ass, Spur-galled and tired by jauncing Bolingbroke. Enter Keeper to [[Richard]], with meat |
| KEEPER | (to Groom) Fellow, give place. Here is no longer stay. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (to Groom) If thou [[Love|love]] me, ’tis [[Time|time]] thou wert away. |
| GROOM | What my tongue dares not, that my heart shall say. Exit |
| KEEPER | My lord, will’t please you to fall to? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Taste of it first, as thou art wont to do. |
| KEEPER | My lord, I dare not. Sir Piers of Exton, Who lately came from the [[King]], commands the contrary. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (striking the Keeper) The devil take Henry of Lancaster and thee I Patience is stale, and I am weary of it. |
| KEEPER | Help, help, help! Exton and his men rush in |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | How now! What means [[Death|death]] in this rude assault? He seizes a weapon from a man, and kills him [[Villain]], thy own hand yields thy [[Death|death]]’s instrument. He kills another Go thou, and fill another room in hell. Here Exton strikes him down |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | That hand shall burn in never-quenching [[Fire|fire]] That staggers thus my person. Exton, thy fierce hand Hath with the [[King]]’s [[Blood|blood]] stained the [[King]]’s own land. Mount, mount, my [[Soul|soul]]; thy seat is up on high, Whilst my gross flesh sinks downward, here to die. He dies |
| EXTON | As full of valour as of royal [[Blood|blood]]. Both have I spilt. O, would the deed were good I For now the devil that told me I did [[Well|well]] Says that this deed is chronicled in hell. This dead [[King]] to the living [[King]] I’ll bear. Take hence the rest, and give them burial here. Exeunt Exton with [[Richard]]’s [[Body|body]] at one door, and his men with the other bodies at another door |
