| NORTHUMBERLAND | First, to thy sacred state wish I all happiness. The next news is, I have to [[London]] sent The heads of Salisbury, Spencer, Blunt, and [[Kent]]. The manner of their taking may appear At large discoursed in this paper here. He gives the paper to [[King]] Henry |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | We thank thee, gentle Percy, for thy pains, And to thy worth will add right worthy gains. Enter Lord Fitzwalter |
| FITZWALTER | My lord, I have from [[Oxford]] sent to [[London]] The heads of Brocas and Sir Bennet Seely, Two of the dangerous consorted traitors That sought at [[Oxford]] thy dire overthrow. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Thy pains, Fitzwalter, shall not be forgot. Right noble is thy merit, [[Well|well]] I wot. Enter Harry Percy, with the Bishop of Carlisle, guarded |
| HARRY PERCY | The grand conspirator Abbot of Westminster, With clog of [[Conscience|conscience]] and sour [[Melancholy|melancholy]], Hath yielded up his [[Body|body]] to the [[Grave|grave]]. But here is Carlisle living, to abide Thy kingly doom and sentence of his [[Pride|pride]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Carlisle, this is your doom. Choose out some secret place, some reverent room More than thou hast, and with it joy thy life. So as thou liv’st in peace, die free from strife. For though mine enemy thou hast ever been, High sparks of [[Honour|honour]] in thee have I seen. Enter Exton with his men bearing the coffin |
| EXTON | Great [[King]], within this coffin I present Thy buried [[Fear|fear]]. Herein all breathless lies The mightiest of thy greatest enemies, [[Richard]] of Bordeaux, by me hither brought. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Exton, I thank thee not, for thou hast wrought A deed of slander with thy fatal hand Upon my head and all this famous land. |
| EXTON | From your own mouth, my lord, did I this deed. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | They [[Love|love]] not [[Poison|poison]] that do [[Poison|poison]] need; Nor do I thee. Though I did wish him dead, I hate the murderer, [[Love|love]] him murdered. The [[Guilt|guilt]] of [[Conscience|conscience]] take thou for thy labour, But neither my good word nor princely favour. With [[Cain]] go wander through the shades of [[Night|night]], And never show thy head by day nor light. Exeunt Exton and his men |
| *(didascalia)* | Lords, I protest my [[Soul|soul]] is full of woe That [[Blood|blood]] should sprinkle me to make me grow. Come mourn with me for what I do lament, And put on sullen black incontinent. I’ll make a voyage to the Holy Land To wash this [[Blood|blood]] off from my guilty hand. March sadly after. Grace my mournings here In weeping after this untimely bier. Exeunt With the coffin |
