| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Philip, Louis the [[Dauphin]], Cardinal Pandolf, and attendants |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | So, by a roaring tempest on the flood,<br>A whole armada of convicted sail<br>Is scattered and disjoined from fellowship. |
| PANDOLF | [[Courage]] and comfort; all shall yet go [[Well|well]]. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | What can go [[Well|well]] when we have run so ill?<br>Are we not beaten? Is not Angers lost,<br>[[Arthur]] ta‘en prisoner, divers dear friends slain,<br>And bloody England into England gone,<br>O’erbearing interruption, spite of France? |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | What he hath won, that hath he fortified. 10<br>So hot a [[Speed|speed]], with such advice disposed,<br>Such temperate order in so fierce a cause,<br>Doth want example. Who hath read or heard<br>Of any kindred action like to this? |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | [[Well]] could I bear that England had this praise, 15<br>So we could find some pattern of our shame. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Constance]], distracted, with her hair about her ears |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Look who comes here! A [[Grave|grave]] unto a [[Soul|soul]],<br>Holding th’eternal spirit against her will<br>In the vile [[Prison|prison]] of afflicted breath.—<br>I prithee, lady, go away with me. 20 |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | Lo, now, now see the issue of your peace! |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Patience, good lady; comfort, gentle [[Constance]]. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | No, I defy all counsel, all redress,<br>But that which ends all counsel, true redress:<br>[[Death]], [[Death]], O amiable, lovely [[Death]]! 25<br>Thou odoriferous stench, sound rottenness!<br>Arise forth from the couch of lasting [[Night|night]],<br>Thou hate and terror to prosperity,<br>And I will kiss thy detestable bones,<br>And put my eyeballs in thy vaulty brows, 30<br>And ring these fingers with thy household worms,<br>And stop this gap of breath with fulsome [[Dust|dust]],<br>And be a carrion [[Monster|monster]] like thyself.<br>Come grin on me, and I will think thou smil’st,<br>And buss thee as thy wife. Misery’s [[Love|love]], 35<br>O, come to me! |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | O fair affliction, peace I |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | No, no, I will not, having breath to cry.<br>O, that my tongue were in the thunder’s mouth!<br>Then with a passion would I shake the world,<br>And rouse from [[Sleep|sleep]] that fell anatomy,<br>Which cannot hear a lady’s feeble voice,<br>Which scorns a modern invocation. |
| PANDOLF | Lady, you utter [[Madness|madness]], and not sorrow. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | Thou art not holy to belie me so.<br>I am not mad: this hair I tear is mine;<br>My name is [[Constance]]; I was Geoffrey’s wife;<br>Young [[Arthur]] is my son; and he is lost.<br>I am not mad; I would to God I were,<br>For then ’tis like I should forget myself.<br>O,if I could, what grief should I forget ! 50<br>Preach some [[philosophy]] to make me mad,<br>And thou shalt be canonized, Cardinal.<br>For, being not mad, but sensible of grief,<br>My reasonable part produces reason<br>How I may be delivered of these woes, 55<br>And teaches me to kill or hang myself.<br>If I were mad I should forget my son,<br>Or madly think a babe of clouts were he.<br>I am not mad; too [[Well|well]], too [[Well|well]] I feel<br>The different plague of each calamity. 60 |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Bind up those tresses. O,what [[Love|love]] I note<br>In the fair multitude of those her hairs!<br>Where but by chance a silver drop hath fallen,<br>Even to that drop ten thousand wiry friends<br>Do glue themselves in sociable grief, 65<br>Like true, inseparable, faithful loves,<br>Sticking together in calamity. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | To England, if you will. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Bind up your hairs. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | Yes, that I will. And wherefore will I do it?<br>I tore them from their bonds, and cried aloud,<br>‘O that these hands could so redeem my son,<br>As they have given these hairs their liberty!’<br>But now I envy at their liberty,<br>And will again commit them to their bonds,<br>Because my poor [[Child|child]] is a prisoner. 75<br>She binds up her hair<br>And Father Cardinal, I have heard you say<br>That we shall see and know our friends in heaven.<br>If that be true, I shall see my boy again;<br>For since the birth of [[Cain]], the first male [[Child|child]],<br>To him that did but yesterday suspire, 80<br>There was not such a gracious creature born.<br>But now will canker-sorrow eat my bud,<br>And chase the native [[Beauty|beauty]] from his cheek;<br>And he will look as hollow as a [[Ghost|ghost]],<br>As dim and meagre as an ague’s fit,<br>And so he’ll die; and rising so again,<br>When I shall meet him in the [[Court|court]] of heaven,<br>I shall not know him; therefore never, never<br>Must I behold my pretty [[Arthur]] more. |
| PANDOLF | You hold too heinous a respect of grief. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | He talks to me that never had a son. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | You are as fond of grief as of your [[Child|child]]. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | Grief fills the room up of my absent [[Child|child]],<br>Lies in his bed, walks up and down with me,<br>Puts on his pretty looks, repeats his words,<br>Remembers me of all his gracious parts,<br>Stuffs out his vacant garments with his form;<br>Then have I reason to be fond of grief.<br>Fare you [[Well|well]]. Had you such a loss as I,<br>I could give better comfort than you do. 100 |
| *(didascalia)* | She unbinds her hair |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | I will not keep this form upon my head<br>When there is such disorder in my wit.<br>O Lord, my boy, my [[Arthur]], my fair son,<br>My life, my joy, my food, my all the world, 104<br>My widow-comfort, and my sorrow’s cure! Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | I [[Fear|fear]] some outrage, and I’ll follow her. Exit attended |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | There’s nothing in this world can make me joy.<br>Life is as tedious as a twice-told tale,<br>Vexing the dull ear of a drowsy man;<br>And bitter shame hath spoiled the sweet world’s taste,<br>That it yields naught but shame and bitterness. 111 |
| PANDOLF | Before the curing of a strong disease,<br>Even in the instant of repair and health,<br>The fit is strongest. Evils that take leave,<br>On their departure most of all show evil. 115<br>What have you lost by losing of this day? |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | All days of glory, joy, and happiness. |
| PANDOLF | If you had won it, certainly you had.<br>No, no; when Fortune means to men most good,<br>She looks upon them with a threat‘ning eye.<br>’Tis strange to think how much [[King John]] hath lost<br>In this which he accounts so clearly won.<br>Are not you grieved that [[Arthur]] is his prisoner? |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | As heartily as he is glad he hath him. |
| PANDOLF | Your mind is all as youthful as your [[Blood|blood]]. 125<br>Now hear me speak with a prophetic spirit,<br>For even the breath of what I mean to speak<br>Shall blow each [[Dust|dust]], each straw, each little rub,<br>Out of the path which shall directly lead<br>Thy foot to England’s throne. And therefore mark.<br>John hath seized [[Arthur]], and it cannot be<br>That whiles warm life plays in that infant’s veins<br>The misplaced John should entertain an hour,<br>One minute, nay, one quiet breath of rest.<br>A sceptre snatched with an unruly hand<br>Must be as boisterously maintained as gained;<br>And he that stands upon a slipp’ry place<br>Makes nice of no vile hold to stay him up.<br>That John may stand, then [[Arthur]] needs must fall;<br>So be it, for it cannot be but so. |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | But what shall I gain by young [[Arthur]]’s fall? |
| PANDOLF | You, in the right of Lady Blanche your wife,<br>May then make all the claim that [[Arthur]] did. |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | And lose it, life and all, as [[Arthur]] did. |
| PANDOLF | How green you are, and fresh in this old world 145<br>John lays you plots; the times conspire with you;<br>For he that steeps his safety in true [[Blood|blood]]<br>Shall find but bloody safety and untrue.<br>This act, so vilely born, shall cool the hearts<br>Of all his people, and freeze up their zeal,<br>That none so small advantage shall step forth<br>To check his reign but they will cherish it;<br>No natural exhalation in the sky,<br>No scope of [[Nature|nature]], no distempered day,<br>No common [[Wind|wind]], no customèd event, 155<br>But they will pluck away his natural cause,<br>And call them meteors, prodigies, and signs,<br>Abortives, presages, and tongues of heaven<br>Plainly denouncing vengeance upon John. |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Maybe he will not touch young [[Arthur]]’s life,<br>But hold himself safe in his prisonment. |
| PANDOLF | O sir, when he shall hear of your approach,<br>If that young [[Arthur]] be not gone already,<br>Even at that news he dies; and then the hearts<br>Of all his people shall revolt from him,<br>And kiss the lips of unacquainted change,<br>And pick strong matter of revolt and wrath<br>Out of the bloody fingers’ ends of John.<br>Methinks I see this hurly all on foot,<br>And O, what better matter breeds for you 170<br>Than I have named! The Bastard Falconbridge<br>Is now in England, ransacking the Church,<br>Offending [[Charity|charity]]. If but a dozen French<br>Were there in arms, they would be as a call<br>To train ten thousand English to their side, 175<br>Or as a little [[Snow|snow]] tumbled about<br>Anon becomes a mountain. O noble [[Dauphin]],<br>Go with me to the [[King]]. ’Tis wonderful<br>What may be wrought out of their discontent<br>Now that their souls are top-full of offence. 180<br>For England, go! I will whet on the [[King]]. |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Strong reasons make strange actions. Let us go.<br>If you say ay, the [[King]] will not say no. Exeunt |
