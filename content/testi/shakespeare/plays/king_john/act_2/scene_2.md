| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lady [[Constance]], [[Arthur]] Duke of Brittaine, and the [[Earl of Salisbury]] |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | (to Salisbury)<br>Gone to be married? Gone to swear a peace?<br>False [[Blood|blood]] to false [[Blood|blood]] joined! Gone to be friends?<br>Shall Louis have Blanche, and Blanche those<br>provinces?<br>It is not so, thou hast misspoke, misheard.<br>Be [[Well|well]] advised, tell o‘er thy tale again.<br>It cannot be, thou dost but say ’tis so.<br>I trust I may not trust thee, for thy word<br>Is but the vain breath of a [[Common Man|common man]].<br>Believe me, I do not believe thee, man;<br>I have a [[King|king]]’s oath to the contrary. 10<br>Thou shalt be punished for thus frighting me;<br>For I am sick and capable of fears;<br>Oppressed with wrongs, and therefore full of fears;<br>A widow husbandless, subject to fears;<br>A woman naturally born to fears;<br>And though thou now confess thou didst but jest,<br>With my vexed spirits I cannot take a truce,<br>But they will quake and tremble all this day.<br>What dost thou mean by shaking of thy head?<br>Why dost thou look so sadly on my son? 20<br>What means that hand upon that breast of thine?<br>Why holds thine eye that lamentable rheum,<br>Like a proud [[River|river]] peering o’er his bounds?<br>Be these sad signs confirmers of thy words?<br>Then speak again—not all thy former tale,<br>But this one word: whether thy tale be true. |
| SALISBURY | As true as I believe you think them false<br>That give you cause to prove my saying true. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | O, if thou teach me to believe this sorrow,<br>Teach thou this sorrow how to make me die;<br>And let belief and life encounter so<br>As doth the fury of two desperate men<br>Which in the very meeting fall and die.<br>Louis marry Blanche! (To [[Arthur]]) O boy, then where<br>art thou?<br>France friend with England!—What becomes of me?<br>(To Salisbury) Fellow, be gone, I cannot brook thy<br>sight; 36<br>This news hath made thee a most ugly man. |
| SALISBURY | What other harm have I, good lady, done,<br>But spoke the harm that is by others done? |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | Which harm within itself so heinous is 40<br>As it makes harmful all that speak of it. |
| [[Arthur|ARTHUR]] | I do beseech you, madam, be content. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | If thou that bidd‘st me be content wert grim,<br>Ugly and sland’rous to thy mother’s womb,<br>Full of unpleasing blots and sightless stains,<br>Lame, foolish, crooked, swart, prodigious,<br>Patched with foul moles and eye-offending marks,<br>I would not care, I then would be content,<br>For then I should not [[Love|love]] thee, no, nor thou<br>Become thy great birth, nor deserve a [[Crown|crown]].<br>But thou art fair, and at thy birth, dear boy,<br>[[Nature]] and Fortune joined to make thee great.<br>Of [[Nature]]’s gifts thou mayst with lilies boast,<br>And with the half-blown [[Rose|rose]]. But Fortune, O,<br>She is corrupted, changed, and won from thee;<br>Sh’adulterates hourly with thine uncle John,<br>And with her golden hand hath plucked on France<br>To tread down fair respect of sovereignty,<br>And made his majesty the bawd to theirs.<br>France is a bawd to Fortune and [[King John]],<br>That strumpet Fortune, that usurping John.<br>(To Salisbury) Tell me, thou fellow, is not France<br>forsworn ?<br>Envenom him with words, or get thee gone<br>And leave those woes [[Alone|alone]], which I [[Alone|alone]]<br>Am bound to underbear. |
| SALISBURY | Pardon me, madam,<br>I may not go without you to the Kings. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | Thou mayst, thou shalt; I will not go with thee.<br>I will instruct my sorrows to be proud,<br>For grief is proud and makes his owner stoop. |
| *(didascalia)* | She sits upon the ground |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | To me and to the state of my great grief 70<br>Let kings assemble, for my grief’s so great<br>That no supporter but the huge firm earth<br>Can hold it up. Here I and sorrows sit;<br>Here is my throne; bid kings come bow to it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Salisbury and [[Arthur]] |
| *(didascalia)* | 3.1 Flourish. Enter [[King John]] and [[King]] Philip hand in hand; Louis the [[Dauphin]] and Lady Blanche, married; [[Queen]] [[Eleanor]], the Bastard, and the Duke of Austria |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | (to Blanche)<br>’Tis true, fair daughter, and this blessèd day<br>Ever in France shall be kept festival.<br>To solemnize this day, the glorious sun<br>Stays in his course and plays the alchemist,<br>Turning with splendour of his precious eye 5<br>The meagre cloddy earth to glittering gold.<br>The yearly course that brings this day about<br>Shall never see it but a holy day. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | (rising)<br>A wicked day, and not a holy day!<br>What hath this day deserved? What hath it done,<br>That it in golden letters should be set<br>Among the high tides in the calendar?<br>Nay, rather turn this day out of the week,<br>This day of shame, oppression, perjury.<br>Or if it must stand still, let wives with [[Child|child]] 15<br>Pray that their burdens may not fall this day,<br>Lest that their hopes prodigiously be crossed;<br>But on this day let seamen [[Fear|fear]] no wreck;<br>No bargains break that are not this day made;<br>This day all things begun come to ill end, 20<br>Yea, [[Faith|faith]] itself to hollow falsehood change. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | By heaven, lady, you shall have no cause<br>To curse the fair proceedings of this day.<br>Have I not pawned to you my majesty? |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | You have beguiled me with a counterfeit 25<br>Resembling majesty, which being touched and tried<br>Proves valueless. You are forsworn, forsworn.<br>You came in arms to spill mine enemies’ [[Blood|blood]],<br>But now in arms you strengthen it with yours.<br>The grappling vigour and rough frown of war 30<br>Is cold in amity and painted peace,<br>And our oppression hath made up this league.<br>Arm, arm, you heavens, against these perjured Kings!<br>A widow cries, be husband to me, God!<br>Let not the hours of this ungodly day 35<br>Wear out the day in peace, but ere sun set<br>Set armed discord ’twixt these perjured Kings.<br>Hear me, O hear me! |
| AUSTRIA | Lady [[Constance]], peace. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | War, war, no peace! Peace is to me a war.<br>O Limoges, O Austria, thou dost shame 40<br>That bloody spoil. Thou slave, thou wretch, thou<br>coward!<br>Thou little valiant, great in villainy;<br>Thou ever strong upon the stronger side;<br>Thou Fortune’s champion, that dost never fight<br>But when her humorous ladyship is by 45<br>To teach thee safety. Thou art perjured too,<br>And sooth‘st up [[Greatness|greatness]]. What a [[Fool|fool]] art thou,<br>A ramping [[Fool|fool]], to brag and stamp, and swear<br>Upon my party! Thou cold-blooded slave,<br>Hast thou not spoke like thunder on my side, 50<br>Been sworn my soldier, bidding me depend<br>Upon thy [[Stars|stars]], thy fortune, and thy strength?<br>And dost thou now fall over to my foes?<br>Thou wear a [[Lion|lion]]’s hide! Doff it, for shame,<br>And hang a calf’s-skin on those recreant limbs. 55 |
| AUSTRIA | O, that a man should speak those words to me! |
| BASTARD | And hang a calf’s-skin on those recreant limbs. |
| AUSTRIA | Thou dar’st not say so, [[Villain|villain]], for thy life. |
| BASTARD | And hang a calf’s-skin on those recreant limbs. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | (to the Bastard)<br>We like not this. Thou dost forget thyself. 60 |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Cardinal Pandolf |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Here comes the holy legate of the Pope. |
| PANDOLF | Hail, you anointed deputies of God.—<br>To thee, [[King John]], my holy errand is.<br>I Pandolf, of fair Milan Cardinal,<br>And from Pope [[Innocent]] the legate here, 65<br>Do in his name religiously demand<br>Why thou against the Church, our Holy Mother,<br>So wilfully dost spurn, and force perforce<br>Keep Stephen Langton, chosen Archbishop<br>Of Canterbury, from that holy see. 70<br>This, in our foresaid Holy Father’s name,<br>Pope [[Innocent]], I do demand of thee. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | What earthy name to interrogatories<br>Can task the free breath of a sacred [[King|king]]?<br>Thou canst not, Cardinal, devise a name 75<br>So slight, unworthy, and ridiculous<br>To charge me to an answer, as the Pope.<br>Tell him this tale, and from the mouth of England<br>Add thus much more: that no Italian priest<br>Shall tithe or toll in our dominions; 80<br>But as we, under God, are supreme head,<br>So, under him, that great supremacy<br>Where we do reign we will [[Alone|alone]] uphold<br>Without th’assistance of a mortal hand.<br>So tell the Pope, all reverence set apart<br>To him and his usurped authority. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Brother of England, you blaspheme in this. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | Though you and all the kings of Christendom<br>Are led so grossly by this meddling priest,<br>Dreading the curse that money may buy out, 90<br>And by the merit of vile gold, dross, [[Dust|dust]],<br>Purchase corrupted pardon of a man,<br>Who in that sale sells pardon from himself;<br>Though you and all the rest so grossly led<br>This juggling witchcraft with revenue cherish; 95<br>Yet I [[Alone|alone]], [[Alone|alone]] do me oppose<br>Against the Pope, and count his friends my foes. |
| PANDOLF | Then by the lawful power that I have<br>Thou shalt stand cursed and excommunicate;<br>And blessed shall he be that doth revolt 100<br>From his allegiance to an heretic;<br>And meritorious shall that hand be called,<br>Canonized and worshipped as a saint,<br>That takes away by any secret course<br>Thy hateful life. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | O lawful let it be 105<br>That I have room with Rome to curse awhile.<br>Good Father Cardinal, cry thou ’Amen’<br>To my keen curses, for without my wrong<br>There is no tongue hath power to curse him right. |
| PANDOLF | There’s law and warrant, lady, for my curse. 110 |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | And for mine too. When law can do no right,<br>Let it be lawful that law bar no wrong.<br>Law cannot give my [[Child|child]] his kingdom here,<br>For he that holds his kingdom holds the law.<br>Therefore, since law itself is perfect wrong,<br>How can the law forbid my tongue to curse? |
| PANDOLF | Philip of France, on peril of a curse,<br>Let go the hand of that arch-heretic,<br>And raise the power of France upon his head,<br>Unless he do submit himself to Rome. 120 |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Eleanor|ELEANOR]] | Look’st thou pale, France? Do not let go thy hand. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] to [[King John]] |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Eleanor|ELEANOR]] | Look to it, devil, lest that France repent,<br>And by disjoining hands hell lose a [[Soul|soul]]. |
| AUSTRIA | [[King]] Philip, listen to the Cardinal. |
| BASTARD | And hang a calf’s-skin on his recreant limbs. 125 |
| AUSTRIA | [[Well]], ruffian, I must pocket up these wrongs,<br>Because— |
| BASTARD | Your breeches best may carry them. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | Philip, what sayst thou to the Cardinal? |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | What should he say, but as the Cardinal? |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Bethink you, Father, for the difference 130<br>Is purchase of a heavy curse from Rome,<br>Or the light loss of England for a friend.<br>Forgo the easier. |
| BLANCHE | That’s the curse of Rome. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | O Louis, stand fast; the devil tempts thee here<br>In likeness of a new untrimmèd bride. 135 |
| BLANCHE | The Lady [[Constance]] speaks not from her [[Faith|faith]],<br>But from her need. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] to [[King]] Philip O if thou grant my need, |
| BLANCHE | Which only lives but by the [[Death|death]] of [[Faith|faith]],<br>That need must needs infer this principle:<br>That [[Faith|faith]] would live again by [[Death|death]] of need. 140<br>O, then tread down my need, and [[Faith|faith]] mounts up;<br>Keep my need up, and [[Faith|faith]] is trodden down. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | The [[King]] is moved, and answers not to this. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | (to [[King]] Philip)<br>O, be removed from him, and answer [[Well|well]]. |
| AUSTRIA | Do so, [[King]] Philip, hang no more in [[Doubt|doubt]]. 145 |
| BASTARD | Hang nothing but a calf’s-skin, most sweet lout. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | I am perplexed, and know not what to say. |
| PANDOLF | What canst thou say but will perplex thee more,<br>If thou stand excommunicate and cursed? |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Good Reverend Father, make my person yours, 150<br>And tell me how you would bestow yourself.<br>This royal hand and mine are newly knit,<br>And the conjunction of our inward souls<br>Married in league, coupled and linked together<br>With all religious strength of sacred vows; 155<br>The latest breath that gave the sound of words<br>Was deep-sworn [[Faith|faith]], peace, amity, true [[Love|love]],<br>Between our kingdoms and our royal selves;<br>And even before this truce, but new before,<br>No longer than we [[Well|well]] could wash our hands 160<br>To clap this royal bargain up of peace,<br>God knows, they were besmeared and over-stained<br>With slaughter’s pencil, where [[Revenge]] did paint<br>The fearful difference of incensed kings;<br>And shall these hands, so lately purged of [[Blood|blood]], 165<br>So newly joined in [[Love|love]], so strong in both,<br>Unyoke this seizure and this kind regreet,<br>Play fast and loose with [[Faith|faith]], so jest with heaven,<br>Make such unconstant children of ourselves,<br>As now again to snatch our palm from palm, 170<br>Unswear [[Faith|faith]] sworn, and on the [[Marriage|marriage]]-bed<br>Of smiling peace to march a bloody host,<br>And make a riot on the gentle brow<br>Of true sincerity? O holy sir,<br>My Reverend Father, let it not be so. 175<br>Out of your grace, devise, ordain, impose<br>Some gentle order, and then we shall be blessed<br>To do your pleasure and continue friends. |
| PANDOLF | All form is formless, order orderless,<br>Save what is opposite to England’s [[Love|love]].<br>Therefore to arms, be champion of our Church,<br>Or let the Church, our mother, breathe her curse,<br>A mother’s curse, on her revolting son.<br>France, thou mayst hold a serpent by the tongue,<br>A crazed [[Lion|lion]] by the mortal paw, 185<br>A fasting tiger safer by the tooth,<br>Than keep in peace that hand which thou dost hold. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | I may disjoin my hand, but not my [[Faith|faith]]. |
| PANDOLF | So mak‘st thou [[Faith|faith]] an enemy to [[Faith|faith]],<br>And like a civil war, sett’st oath to oath, 190<br>Thy tongue against thy tongue. O, let thy vow,<br>First made to heaven, first be to heaven performed;<br>That is, to be the champion of our Church.<br>What since thou swor‘st is sworn against thyself,<br>And may not be performèd by thyself; 195<br>For that which thou hast sworn to do amiss<br>Is not amiss when it is truly done;<br>And being not done where doing tends to ill,<br>The [[Truth|truth]] is then most done not doing it.<br>The better act of purposes mistook 200<br>Is to mistake again; though indirect,<br>Yet indirection thereby grows direct,<br>And falsehood falsehood cures, as [[Fire|fire]] cools [[Fire|fire]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Within the scorchèd veins of one new burned. |
| PANDOLF | It is religion that doth make vows kept;<br>But thou hast sworn against religion;<br>By what thou swear’st, against the thing thou<br>swear‘st;<br>And mak’st an oath the surety for thy troth:<br>Against an oath, the [[Truth|truth]]. Thou art unsure<br>To swear: swear’st only not to be forsworn—210<br>Else what a mockery should it be to swear!—<br>But thou dost swear only to be forsworn,<br>And most forsworn to keep what thou dost swear;<br>Therefore thy later vows against thy first<br>Is in thyself rebellion to thyself, 215<br>And better conquest never canst thou make<br>Than arm thy constant and thy nobler parts<br>Against these giddy loose suggestions;<br>Upon which better part our prayers come in<br>If thou vouchsafe them. But if not, then know 220<br>The peril of our curses light on thee<br>So heavy as thou shalt not shake them off,<br>But in [[Despair|despair]] die under their black weight. |
| AUSTRIA | Rebellion, flat rebellion! |
| BASTARD | Wilt not be?<br>Will not a calf’s-skin stop that mouth of thine? 225 |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Father, to arms! |
| BLANCHE | Upon thy wedding day?<br>Against the [[Blood|blood]] that thou hast married ?<br>What, shall our feast be kept with slaughtered men?<br>Shall braying trumpets and loud churlish drums,<br>Clamours of hell, be measures to our pomp? 230<br>She kneels<br>O husband, hear me ! Ay, alack, how new<br>Is ‘husband’ in my mouth! Even for that name<br>Which till this [[Time|time]] my tongue did ne’er pronounce,<br>Upon my knee I beg, go not to arms<br>Against mine uncle. |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | (kneeling) O, upon my knee 235<br>Made hard with kneeling, I do pray to thee,<br>Thou virtuous [[Dauphin]], alter not the doom<br>Forethought by heaven. |
| BLANCHE | (to Louis the [[Dauphin]])<br>Now shall I see thy [[Love|love]]: what motive may<br>Be stronger with thee than the name of wife? 240 |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | That which upholdeth him that thee upholds:<br>His [[Honour|honour]].—O thine [[Honour|honour]], Louis, thine [[Honour|honour]]! |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | (to [[King]] Philip)<br>I muse your majesty doth seem so cold<br>When such profound respects do pull you on. |
| PANDOLF | I will denounce a curse upon his head. 245 |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Thou shalt not need.—England, I will fall from thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | He takes his hand from [[King John]]’s hand. Blanche and [[Constance]] rise |
| [[Constance|CONSTANCE]] | O, fair return of banished majesty! |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Eleanor|ELEANOR]] | O, foul revolt of French inconstancy I |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | France, thou shalt rue this hour within this hour. |
| BASTARD | Old [[Time]] the [[Clock|clock]]-setter, that bald sexton [[Time]], 250<br>Is it as he will?—[[Well]] then, France shall rue. |
| BLANCHE | The sun’s o’ercast with [[Blood|blood]]; fair day, adieu!<br>Which is the side that I must go withal?<br>I am with both, each army hath a hand,<br>And in their rage, I having hold of both, 255<br>They whirl asunder and dismember me.<br>Husband, I cannot pray that thou mayst win.—<br>Uncle, I needs must pray that thou mayst lose.—<br>Father, I may not wish the fortune thine.—<br>Grandam, I will not wish thy wishes thrive.<br>Whoever wins, on that side shall I lose,<br>Assured loss before the match be played. |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Lady, with me, with me thy fortune lies. |
| BLANCHE | There where my fortune lives, there my life dies. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | (to the Bastard)<br>Cousin, go draw our puissance together.— |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit the Bastard |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | France, I am burned up with inflaming wrath,<br>A rage whose heat hath this condition:<br>That nothing can allay, nothing but [[Blood|blood]],<br>The [[Blood|blood]], and dearest-valued [[Blood|blood]], of France. |
| [[King|KING]] PHILIP | Thy rage shall burn thee up, and thou shalt turn 270<br>To ashes ere our [[Blood|blood]] shall quench that [[Fire|fire]].<br>Look to thyself, thou art in jeopardy. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | No more than he that threats.—To arms let’s hie! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
