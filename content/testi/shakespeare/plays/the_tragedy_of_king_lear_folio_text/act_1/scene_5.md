| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King Lear]], the Earl of [[Kent]] disguised, the First Gentleman, and Lear’s [[Fool]] |
| *(didascalia)* | LEAR to |
| *(didascalia)* | the Gentleman, giving him a letter Go you before to [[Gloucester]] with these letters. Exit Gentleman To [[Kent]], giving him a letter Acquaint my daughter no further with anything you know than comes from her demand out of the letter. If your diligence be not speedy, I shall be there afore you. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I will not [[Sleep|sleep]], my lord, till I have delivered your letter. Exit |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | If a man’s brains were in’s heels, were’t not in danger of kibes? |
| LEAR | Ay, boy. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Then, I prithee, be merry: thy wit shall not go slipshod. |
| LEAR | Ha, ha, ha! |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Shalt see thy other daughter will use thee kindly, for though she’s as like this as a crab’s like an apple, yet I can tell what I can tell. |
| LEAR | What canst tell, boy? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | She will taste as like this as a crab does to a crab. Thou canst tell why one’s nose stands i’th’ middle on ’s face? |
| LEAR | No. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Why, to keep one’s eyes of either side ’s nose, that what a man cannot smell out, a may spy into. |
| LEAR | I did her wrong. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Canst tell how an oyster makes his shell? |
| LEAR | No. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Nor I neither; but I can tell why a snail has a [[house]]. |
| LEAR | Why? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Why, to put ’s head in, not to give it away to his daughters and leave his horns without a case. |
| LEAR | I will forget my [[Nature|nature]]. So kind a father!<br>Be my horses ready? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Thy asses are gone about ’em. The reason why the seven [[Stars|stars]] are no more than seven is a pretty reason. |
| LEAR | Because they are not eight. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Yes, indeed, thou wouldst make a good [[Fool|fool]]. |
| LEAR | To take’t again perforce—[[Monster|monster]] ingratitude! |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | If thou wert my [[Fool|fool]], nuncle, I’d have thee beaten for being old before thy [[Time|time]]. |
| LEAR | How’s that? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Thou shouldst not have been old till thou hadst been wise. |
| LEAR | O, let me not be mad, not mad, sweet heaven!<br>Keep me in temper. I would not be mad. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the First Gentleman |
| LEAR | How now, are the horses ready? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN Ready, my lord. |
| LEAR | (to [[Fool]]) Come, boy. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Lear and Gentleman |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | She that’s a maid now, and laughs at my departure,<br>Shall not be a maid long, unless things be cut shorter. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
