| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Valentine]] and [[Proteus]] |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Cease to persuade, my loving [[Proteus]]. [[Home]]-keeping youth have ever homely wits. Were’t not affection [[Chains|chains]] thy tender days To the sweet glances of thy honoured [[Love|love]], I rather would entreat thy company To see the wonders of the world abroad Than, living dully sluggardized at [[Home|home]], Wear out thy youth with shapeless idleness. But since thou lov’st, [[Love|love]] still, and thrive therein— Even as I would, when I to [[Love|love]] begin. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Wilt thou be gone? Sweet [[Valentine]], adieu. Think on thy [[Proteus]] when thou haply seest Some rare noteworthy object in thy travel. Wish me partaker in thy happiness When thou dost meet good hap; and in thy danger— If ever danger do environ thee— Commend thy grievance to my holy prayers; For I will be thy beadsman, [[Valentine]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | And on a [[Love|love]]-book pray for my [[success]]? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Upon some book I [[Love|love]] I’ll pray for thee. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | That’s on some shallow story of deep [[Love|love]]— How young Leander crossed the Hellespont. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | That’s a deep story of a deeper [[Love|love]], For he was more than over-shoes in [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | ‘Tis true, for you are over-boots in [[Love|love]], And yet you never swam the Hellespont. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Over the boots? Nay, give me not the boots. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No, I will not; for it boots thee not. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | What? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | To be in [[Love|love]], where scorn is bought with groans, Coy looks with heart-sore sighs, one fading moment’s mirth With twenty watchful, weary, tedious nights. If haply won, perhaps a hapless gain; If lost, why then a grievous labour won; However, but a folly bought with wit, Or else a wit by folly vanquished. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | So by your circumstance you call me [[Fool|fool]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | So by your circumstance I [[Fear|fear]] you’ll prove. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | ‘Tis [[Love|love]] you cavil at. I am not [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | [[Love]] is your master, for he masters you, And he that is so yoked by a [[Fool|fool]] Methinks should not be chronicled for wise. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Yet writers say ‘As in the sweetest bud The eating canker dwells, so doting [[Love|love]] Inhabits in the finest wits of all.’ |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | And writers say ‘As the most forward bud Is eaten by the canker ere it blow, Even so by [[Love|love]] the young and tender wit Is turned to folly, blasting in the bud, Losing his verdure even in the prime, And all the fair effects of future hopes.’ But wherefore waste I [[Time|time]] to counsel thee That art a votary to fond [[Desire|desire]]? Once more adieu. My father at the [[Road|road]] Expects my coming, there to see me shipped. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | And thither will I bring thee, [[Valentine]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Sweet [[Proteus]], no. Now let us take our leave. To Milan let me hear from thee by letters Of thy [[success]] in [[Love|love]], and what news else Betideth here in absence of thy friend; And I likewise will visit thee with mine. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | All happiness bechance to thee in Milan. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | As much to you at [[Home|home]]; and so farewell. Exit |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | He after [[Honour|honour]] hunts, I after [[Love|love]]. He [[Leaves|leaves]] his friends to dignify them more, I leave myself, my friends, and all, for [[Love|love]]. Thou, [[Julia]], thou hast metamorphosed me, Made me neglect my studies, lose my [[Time|time]], War with good counsel, set the world at naught; Made wit with musing weak, heart sick with thought.
