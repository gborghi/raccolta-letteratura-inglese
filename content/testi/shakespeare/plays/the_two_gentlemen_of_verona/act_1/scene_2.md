| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Julia]] and Lucetta |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | But say, Lucetta, now we are [[Alone|alone]]—<br>Wouldst thou then counsel me to fall in [[Love|love]]? |
| LUCETTA | Ay, madam, so you stumble not unheedfully. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Of all the fair resort of gentlemen<br>That every day with parle encounter me,<br>In thy opinion which is worthiest [[Love|love]]? |
| LUCETTA | Please you repeat their names, I’ll show my mind<br>According to my shallow simple skill. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | What think’st thou of the fair Sir Eglamour? |
| LUCETTA | As of a knight [[Well|well]] spoken, neat, and fine,<br>But were I you, he never should be mine. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | What think’st thou of the rich Mercatio? |
| LUCETTA | [[Well]] of his wealth, but of himself, so-so. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | What think’st thou of the gentle [[Proteus]]? |
| LUCETTA | Lord, lord, to see what folly reigns in us! |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | How now? What means this passion at his name? |
| LUCETTA | Pardon, dear madam, ‘tis a passing shame<br>That I, unworthy [[Body|body]] as I am,<br>Should censure thus on lovely gentlemen. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Why not on [[Proteus]], as of all the rest? |
| LUCETTA | Then thus: of many good, I think him best. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Your reason? |
| LUCETTA | I have no other but a woman’s reason:<br>I think him so because I think him so. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | And wouldst thou have me cast my [[Love|love]] on him? |
| LUCETTA | Ay, if you thought your [[Love|love]] not cast away. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Why, he of all the rest hath never moved me. |
| LUCETTA | Yet he of all the rest I think best loves ye. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | His little speaking shows his [[Love|love]] but small. |
| LUCETTA | [[Fire]] that’s closest kept burns most of all. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | They do not [[Love|love]] that do not show their [[Love|love]]. |
| LUCETTA | O, they [[Love|love]] least that let men know their [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | I would I knew his mind. |
| LUCETTA | (giving [[Proteus]]’ letter)<br>Peruse this paper, madam. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | ‘To [[Julia]]’—say, from whom? |
| LUCETTA | That the contents will show. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Say, say—who gave it thee? |
| LUCETTA | Sir [[Valentine]]’s page; and sent, I think, from [[Proteus]]. He would have given it you, but I being in the way Did in your name receive it. Pardon the fault, I pray. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Now, by my modesty, a goodly broker.<br>Dare you presume to harbour wanton lines?<br>To whisper, and conspire against my youth?<br>Now trust me, ‘tis an office of great worth,<br>And you an officer fit for the place.<br>There. Take the paper.<br>She gives Lucetta the letter<br>See it be returned,<br>Or else return no more into my sight. |
| LUCETTA | To plead for [[Love|love]] deserves more fee than hate. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Will ye be gone? |
| LUCETTA | That you may ruminate. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | And yet I would I had o‘erlooked the letter.<br>It were a shame to call her back again<br>And pray her to a fault for which I chid her.<br>What [[Fool|fool]] is she, that knows I am a maid<br>And would not force the letter to my view,<br>Since maids in modesty say ‘No’ to that<br>Which they would have the profferer construe ‘Ay’.<br>Fie, fie, how wayward is this foolish [[Love|love]]<br>That like a testy babe will scratch the [[Nurse|nurse]]<br>And presently, all humbled, kiss the rod.<br>How churlishly I chid Lucetta hence<br>When willingly I would have had her here.<br>How angerly I taught my brow to frown<br>When inward joy enforced my heart to smile.<br>My penance is to call Lucetta back<br>And ask remission for my folly past.<br>What ho! Lucetta! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lucetta |
| LUCETTA | What would your ladyship? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Is’t near dinner-[[Time|time]]? |
| LUCETTA | I would it were,<br>That you might kill your stomach on your meat<br>And not upon your maid. |
| *(didascalia)* | She drops and picks up the letter |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | What is’t that you<br>Took up so gingerly? |
| LUCETTA | Nothing. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Why didst thou stoop then? |
| LUCETTA | To take a paper up that I let fall. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | And is that paper nothing? |
| LUCETTA | Nothing concerning me. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Then let it lie for those that it concerns. |
| LUCETTA | Madam, it will not lie where it concerns,<br>Unless it have a false interpreter. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Some [[Love|love]] of yours hath writ to you in rhyme. |
| LUCETTA | That I might sing it, madam, to a tune,<br>Give me a note. Your ladyship can set. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | As little by such toys as may be possible.<br>Best sing it to the tune of ‘Light o’ [[Love|love]]’. |
| LUCETTA | It is too heavy for so light a tune. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Heavy? Belike it hath some burden, then? |
| LUCETTA | Ay, and melodious were it, would you sing it. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | And why not you? |
| LUCETTA | I cannot reach so high. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Let’s see your [[Song|song]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | She tries to take the letters |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | How now, minion! |
| LUCETTA | Keep tune there still. So you will sing it out.<br>And yet methinks I do not like this tune. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | You do not? |
| LUCETTA | No, madam, ‘tis too sharp. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | You, minion, are too saucy. |
| LUCETTA | Nay, now you are too flat,<br>And mar the concord with too harsh a descant.<br>There wanteth but a mean to fill your [[Song|song]]. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | The mean is drowned with your unruly bass. |
| LUCETTA | Indeed, I bid the base for [[Proteus]]. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | This bauble shall not henceforth trouble me.<br>Here is a coil with protestation.<br>She [[Tears|tears]] the letter and drops the pieces<br>Go, get you gone, and let the papers lie.<br>You would be fing’ring them to anger me. |
| LUCETTA | (aside)<br>She makes it strange, but she would be best pleased<br>To be so angered with another letter. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Nay, would I were so angered with the same.<br>O hateful hands, to tear such loving words;<br>Injurious wasps, to feed on such sweet honey<br>And kill the [[Bees|bees]] that yield it with your stings.<br>I’ll kiss each several paper for amends.<br>She picks up some of the pieces of paper<br>Look, here is writ ‘Kind [[Julia]]’—unkind [[Julia]],<br>As in [[Revenge|revenge]] of thy ingratitude<br>I throw thy name against the bruising stones,<br>Trampling contemptuously on thy disdain.<br>And here is writ ‘[[Love]]-wounded [[Proteus]]’.<br>Poor wounded name, my bosom as a bed<br>Shall lodge thee till thy wound be throughly healed;<br>And thus I search it with a sovereign kiss.<br>But twice or thrice was ‘[[Proteus]]’ written down.<br>Be calm, good [[Wind|wind]], blow not a word away<br>Till I have found each letter in the letter<br>Except mine own name. That, some whirlwind bear<br>Unto a ragged, fearful, hanging rock<br>And throw it thence into the raging sea.<br>Lo, here in one line is his name twice writ:<br>‘Poor forlorn [[Proteus]]’, ‘passionate [[Proteus]]’,<br>‘To the sweet [[Julia]]’—that I’ll tear away.<br>And yet I will not, sith so prettily<br>He couples it to his complaining names.<br>Thus will I fold them, one upon another.<br>Now kiss, embrace, contend, do what you will. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lucetta |
| LUCETTA | Madam, dinner is ready, and your father stays. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | [[Well]], let us go. |
| LUCETTA | What, shall these papers lie like telltales here? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | If you respect them, best to take them up. |
| LUCETTA | Nay, I was taken up for laying them down.<br>Yet here they shall not lie, for catching cold. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | I see you have a month’s mind to them. |
| LUCETTA | Ay, madam, you may say what sights you see.<br>I see things too, although you judge I wink. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Come, come, will’t please you go? |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
