| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Valentine]] and [[Speed]] |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | (offering [[Valentine]] a glove)<br>Sir, your glove. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Not mine. My gloves are on. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Why then, this may be yours, for this is but one. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Ha, let me see. Ay, give it me, it’s mine—<br>Sweet ornament, that decks a thing divine.<br>Ah, [[Silvia]], [[Silvia]]! |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Madam [[Silvia]], Madam [[Silvia]]! |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | How now, sirrah? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | She is not within hearing, sir. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Why, sir, who bade you call her? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Your worship, sir, or else I mistook. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | [[Well]], you’ll still be too forward. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | And yet I was last chidden for being too slow. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Go to, sir. Tell me, do you know Madam [[Silvia]]? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | She that your worship loves? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Why, how know you that I am in [[Love|love]]? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Marry, by these special marks: first, you have learned, like Sir [[Proteus]], to wreath your arms, like a malcontent; to relish a [[Love|love]]-[[Song|song]], like a robin redbreast; to walk [[Alone|alone]], like one that had the pestilence; to sigh, like a schoolboy that had lost his ABC; to weep, like a young wench that had buried her grandam; to fast, like one that takes diet; to watch, like one that fears robbing; to speak puling, like a beggar at Hallowmas. You were wont, when you laughed, to crow like a cock; when you walked, to walk like one of the lions. When you fasted, it was presently after dinner; when you looked sadly, it was for want of money. And now you are metamorphosed with a mistress, that when I look on you I can hardly think you my master. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Are all these things perceived in me? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | They are all perceived without ye. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Without me? They cannot. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Without you? Nay, that’s certain, for without you were so simple, none else would. But you are so without these follies that these follies are within you, and shine through you like the [[Water|water]] in an urinal, that not an eye that sees you but is a physician to comment on your malady. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | But tell me, dost thou know my lady [[Silvia]]? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | She that you gaze on so as she sits at supper? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Hast thou observed that? Even she I mean. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Why sir, I know her not. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Dost thou know her by my gazing on her, and yet know’st her not? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Is she not hard-favoured, sir? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Not so fair, boy, as [[Well|well]] favoured. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Sir, I know that [[Well|well]] enough. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | What dost thou know? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | That she is not so fair as of you [[Well|well]] favoured. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I mean that her [[Beauty|beauty]] is exquisite but her favour infinite. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | That’s because the one is painted and the other out of all count. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | How painted? And how out of count? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Marry, sir, so painted to make her fair that no man counts of her [[Beauty|beauty]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | How esteem’st thou me? I account of her [[Beauty|beauty]]. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | You never saw her since she was deformed. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | How long hath she been deformed? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Ever since you loved her. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I have loved her ever since I saw her, and still I see her beautiful. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | If you [[Love|love]] her you cannot see her. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Why? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Because [[Love|love]] is blind. O that you had mine eyes, or your own eyes had the lights they were wont to have when you chid at Sir [[Proteus]] for going ungartered. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | What should I see then? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Your own present folly and her passing deformity; for he being in [[Love|love]] could not see to garter his hose, and you being in [[Love|love]] cannot see to put on your hose. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Belike, boy, then you are in [[Love|love]], for last morning you could not see to wipe my shoes. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | True, sir. I was in [[Love|love]] with my bed. I thank you, you swinged me for my [[Love|love]], which makes me the bolder to chide you for yours. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | In conclusion, I stand affected to her. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | I would you were set. So your affection would cease. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Last [[Night|night]] she enjoined me to write some lines to one she loves. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | And have you? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I have. 85 |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Are they not lamely writ? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No, boy, but as [[Well|well]] as I can do them. Peace, here she comes. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Silvia]] |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | (aside) O excellent motion! O exceeding puppet!<br>Now will he interpret to her. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Madam and mistress, a thousand good-morrows. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | (aside)<br>O, give ye good e’en! Here’s a million of manners. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Sir [[Valentine]] and servant, to you two thousand. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | (aside) He should give her interest, and she gives it him. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | As you enjoined me, I have writ your letter<br>Unto the secret, nameless friend of yours;<br>Which I was much unwilling to proceed in<br>But for my duty to your ladyship.<br>He gives her a letter |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | I thank you, gentle servant. ‘Tis very clerkly done. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Now trust me, madam, it came hardly off;<br>For being ignorant to whom it goes<br>I writ at random, very doubtfully. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Perchance you think too much of so much pains? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No, madam. So it stead you I will write—<br>Please you command—a thousand times as much.<br>And yet . . . |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | A pretty period. [[Well]], I guess the sequel.<br>And yet I will not name it. And yet I care not.<br>And yet, take this again.<br>She offers him the letter<br>And yet I thank you,<br>Meaning henceforth to trouble you no more. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | (aside)<br>And yet you will, and yet another yet. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | What means your ladyship? Do you not like it? |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Yes, yes. The lines are very quaintly writ,<br>But since unwillingly, take them again.<br>She presses the letter upon him<br>Nay, take them. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Madam, they are for you. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Ay, ay. You writ them, sir, at my request,<br>But I will none of them. They are for you.<br>I would have had them writ more movingly. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Please you, I’ll write your ladyship another. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | And when it’s writ, for my sake read it over,<br>And if it please you, so. If not, why, so. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | If it please me, madam? What then? |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Why, if it please you, take it for your labour.<br>And so good morrow, servant. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | (aside)<br>O jest unseen, inscrutable, invisible<br>As a nose on a man’s face or a weathercock on a<br>steeple.<br>My master sues to her, and she hath taught her suitor,<br>He being her pupil, to become her tutor.<br>O excellent device! Was there ever heard a better?—<br>That my master, being scribe, to himself should write<br>the letter. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | How now, sir—what, are you reasoning with yourself? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Nay, I was rhyming. ‘Tis you that have the reason. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | To do what? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | To be a spokesman from Madam [[Silvia]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | To whom? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | To yourself. Why, she woos you by a figure. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | What figure? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | By a letter, I should say. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Why, she hath not writ to me. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | What need she, when she hath made you write to yourself? Why, do you not perceive the jest? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No, believe me. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | No believing you indeed, sir. But did you perceive her earnest? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | She gave me none, except an angry word. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Why, she hath given you a letter. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | That’s the letter I writ to her friend. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | And that letter hath she delivered, and there an end. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I would it were no worse. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | I’ll warrant you, ‘tis as [[Well|well]].<br>For often have you writ to her, and she in modesty<br>Or else for want of idle [[Time|time]] could not again reply,<br>Or fearing else some messenger that might her mind<br>discover,<br>Herself hath taught her [[Love|love]] himself to write unto her<br>[[Lover|lover]].<br>—All this I speak in print, for in print I found it. Why<br>muse you, sir? ‘Tis dinner-[[Time|time]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I have dined. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Ay, but hearken, sir. Though the chameleon [[Love|love]] can feed on the air, I am one that am nourished by my victuals, and would fain have meat. O, be not like your mistress—be moved, be moved! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
