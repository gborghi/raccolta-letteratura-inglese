| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Valentine]], [[Silvia]], [[Thurio]], and [[Speed]] |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Servant! |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Mistress? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | (to [[Valentine]]) Master, Sir [[Thurio]] frowns on you. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Ay, boy, it’s for [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Not of you. 5 |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Of my mistress, then. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Twere good you knocked him. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | (to [[Valentine]]) Servant, you are sad. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Indeed, madam, I seem so. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | Seem you that you are not? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Haply I do. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | So do counterfeits. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | So do you. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | What seem I that I am not? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Wise. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | What instance of the contrary? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Your folly. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | And how quote you my folly? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I quote it in your jerkin. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | My ‘jerkin’ is a doublet. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | [[Well]] then, I’ll [[Double|double]] your folly. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | How! |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | What, angry, Sir [[Thurio]]? Do you change colour? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Give him leave, madam, he is a kind of chameleon. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | That hath more mind to feed on your [[Blood|blood]] than live in your air. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | You have said, sir. |
| THORIO | Ay, sir, and done too, for this [[Time|time]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I know it [[Well|well]], sir, you always end ere you begin. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | A fine volley of words, gentlemen, and quickly shot off. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | ‘Tis indeed, madam, we thank the giver. [[Silvia|SILVIA]] Who is that, servant? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Yourself, sweet lady, for you gave the [[Fire|fire]]. Sir [[Thurio]] borrows his wit from your ladyship’s looks, and spends what he borrows kindly in your company. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | Sir, if you spend word for word with me, I shall make your wit bankrupt. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I know it [[Well|well]], sir. You have an exchequer of words, and, I think, no other treasure to give your followers. For it appears by their bare liveries that they live by your bare words. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | No more, gentlemen, no more. Here comes my father. Enter the Duke |
| DUKE | Now, daughter [[Silvia]], you are hard beset. Sir [[Valentine]], your father is in good health, What say you to a letter from your friends Of much good news? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | My lord, I will be thankful To any happy messenger from thence. |
| DUKE | Know ye Don [[Antonio]], your countryman? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Ay, my good lord, I know the gentleman To be of worth, and worthy estimation, And not without [[Desert|desert]] so [[Well|well]] reputed. |
| DUKE | Hath he not a son? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Ay, my good lord, a son that [[Well|well]] deserves The [[Honour|honour]] and regard of such a father. |
| DUKE | You know him [[Well|well]]? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I knew him as myself, for from our infancy We have conversed, and spent our hours together. And though myself have been an idle truant, Omitting the sweet benefit of [[Time|time]] To clothe mine age with angel-like perfection, Yet hath Sir [[Proteus]]—for that’s his name— Made use and fair advantage of his days: His years but young, but his experience old; His head unmellowed, but his judgement ripe. And in a word—for far behind his worth Comes all the praises that I now bestow— He is complete, in feature and in mind, With all good grace to grace a gentleman. |
| DUKE | Beshrew me, sir, but if he make this good He is as worthy for an empress’ [[Love|love]] As meet to be an emperor’s counsellor. [[Well]], sir, this gentleman is come to me With commendation from great potentates, And here he means to spend his [[Time|time]] awhile. I think ’tis no unwelcome news to you. |
