| *(didascalia)* | Enter Duke, [[Thurio]], and [[Proteus]] |
| DUKE | Sir [[Thurio]], give us leave, I pray, awhile.<br>We have some [[Secrets|secrets]] to confer about. Exit [[Thurio]]<br><br>Now tell me, [[Proteus]], what’s your will with me? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | My gracious lord, that which I would discover<br>The law of [[Friendship|friendship]] bids me to conceal.<br>But when I call to mind your gracious favours<br>Done to me, undeserving as I am,<br>My duty pricks me on to utter that<br>Which else no worldly good should draw from me.<br>Know, worthy prince, Sir [[Valentine]] my friend<br>This [[Night|night]] intends to steal away your daughter.<br>Myself am one made privy to the plot.<br>I know you have determined to bestow her<br>On [[Thurio]], whom your gentle daughter hates,<br>And should she thus be stol’n away from you<br>It would be much vexation to your age.<br>Thus, for my duty’s sake, I rather chose<br>To [[Cross|cross]] my friend in his intended drift<br>Than by concealing it heap on your head<br>A pack of sorrows which would press you down,<br>Being unprevented, to your timeless [[Grave|grave]]. |
| DUKE | [[Proteus]], I thank thee for thine honest care,<br>Which to requite command me while I live.<br>This [[Love|love]] of theirs myself have often seen,<br>Haply, when they have judged me fast asleep,<br>And oftentimes have purposed to forbid<br>Sir [[Valentine]] her company and my [[Court|court]].<br>But fearing lest my jealous aim might err,<br>And so unworthily disgrace the man—<br>A rashness that I ever yet have shunned—<br>I gave him gentle looks, thereby to find<br>That which thyself hast now disclosed to me.<br>And that thou mayst perceive my [[Fear|fear]] of this,<br>Knowing that tender youth is soon suggested,<br>I nightly lodge her in an upper tower,<br>The key whereof myself have ever kept;<br>And thence she cannot be conveyed away. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Know, noble lord, they have devised a mean<br>How he her chamber-[[Window|window]] will ascend,<br>And with a corded ladder fetch her down,<br>For which the youthful [[Lover|lover]] now is gone,<br>And this way comes he with it presently,<br>Where, if it please you, you may intercept him.<br>But, good my lord, do it so cunningly<br>That my discovery be not aimed at;<br>For [[Love|love]] of you, not hate unto my friend,<br>Hath made me publisher of this pretence. |
| DUKE | Upon mine [[Honour|honour]], he shall never know<br>That I had any light from thee of this. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Adieu, my lord. Sir [[Valentine]] is coming. Exit Enter [[Valentine]] |
| DUKE | Sir [[Valentine]], whither away so fast? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Please it your grace, there is a messenger<br>That stays to bear my letters to my friends,<br>And I am going to deliver them. |
| DUKE | Be they of much import? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | The tenor of them doth but signify<br>My health and happy being at your [[Court|court]]. |
| DUKE | Nay then, no matter. Stay with me awhile.<br>I am to break with thee of some affairs<br>That touch me near, wherein thou must be secret.<br>‘Tis not unknown to thee that I have sought<br>To match my friend Sir [[Thurio]] to my daughter. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I know it [[Well|well]], my lord; and sure the match<br>Were rich and honourable. Besides, the gentleman<br>Is full of virtue, bounty, worth, and qualities<br>Beseeming such a wife as your fair daughter.<br>Cannot your grace win her to fancy him? |
| DUKE | No, trust me. She is peevish, sullen, froward,<br>Proud, disobedient, stubborn, lacking duty,<br>Neither regarding that she is my [[Child|child]]<br>Nor fearing me as if I were her father.<br>And may I say to thee, this [[Pride|pride]] of hers<br>Upon advice hath drawn my [[Love|love]] from her,<br>And where I thought the remnant of mine age<br>Should have been cherished by her [[Child|child]]-like duty,<br>I now am full resolved to take a wife,<br>And turn her out to who will take her in.<br>Then let her [[Beauty|beauty]] be her wedding dower,<br>For me and my possessions she esteems not. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | What would your grace have me to do in this? |
| DUKE | There is a lady of [[Verona]] here<br>Whom I affect, but she is nice, and coy,<br>And naught esteems my aged eloquence.<br>Now therefore would I have thee to my tutor—<br>For long agone I have forgot to [[Court|court]],<br>Besides, the fashion of the [[Time|time]] is changed—<br>How and which way I may bestow myself<br>To be regarded in her sun-bright eye. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Win her with gifts if she respect not words.<br>Dumb jewels often in their silent kind<br>More than quick words do move a woman’s mind. |
| DUKE | But she did scorn a present that I sent her. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | A woman sometime scorns what best contents her.<br>Send her another. Never give her o’er,<br>For scorn at first makes after-[[Love|love]] the more.<br>If she do frown, ‘tis not in hate of you,<br>But rather to beget more [[Love|love]] in you.<br>If she do chide, ‘tis not to have you gone,<br>Forwhy the fools are mad if left [[Alone|alone]].<br>Take no repulse, whatever she doth say:<br>For ‘Get you gone’ she doth not mean ‘Away’.<br>Flatter and praise, commend, extol their graces;<br>Though ne’er so black, say they have angels’ faces.<br>That man that hath a tongue I say is no man<br>If with his tongue he cannot win a woman. |
| DUKE | But she I mean is promised by her friends<br>Unto a youthful gentleman of worth,<br>And kept severely from resort of men,<br>That no man hath access by day to her. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Why then I would resort to her by [[Night|night]]. |
| DUKE | Ay, but the doors be locked and keys kept safe,<br>That no man hath recourse to her by [[Night|night]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | What lets but one may enter at her [[Window|window]]? |
| DUKE | Her chamber is aloft, far from the ground,<br>And built so shelving that one cannot climb it<br>Without apparent hazard of his life. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Why then, a ladder quaintly made of cords<br>To cast up, with a pair of anchoring hooks,<br>Would serve to scale another [[Hero]]’s tower,<br>So bold Leander would adventure it. |
| DUKE | Now as thou art a gentleman of [[Blood|blood]],<br>Advise me where I may have such a ladder. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | When would you use it? Pray sir, tell me that. |
| DUKE | This very [[Night|night]]; for [[Love|love]] is like a [[Child|child]]<br>That longs for everything that he can come by. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | By seven o’[[Clock|clock]] I’ll get you such a ladder. |
| DUKE | But hark thee: I will go to her [[Alone|alone]].<br>How shall I best convey the ladder thither? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | It will be light, my lord, that you may bear it<br>Under a cloak that is of any length. |
| DUKE | A cloak as long as thine will serve the turn? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Ay, my good lord. |
| DUKE | Then let me see thy cloak,<br>I’ll get me one of such another length. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Why, any cloak will serve the turn, my lord. |
| DUKE | How shall I fashion me to wear a cloak?<br>I pray thee let me feel thy cloak upon me.<br>He lifts [[Valentine]]’s cloak and finds a letter and a rope-ladder<br>What letter is this same? What’s here? ‘To [[Silvia]]’?<br>And here an engine fit for my proceeding.<br>I’ll be so bold to break the seal for once.<br>(Reads)<br>‘My thoughts do harbour with my [[Silvia]] nightly,<br>And slaves they are to me, that send them flying.<br>O, could their master come and go as lightly,<br>Himself would lodge where, senseless, they are lying.<br>My herald thoughts in thy pure bosom rest them,<br>While I, their [[King|king]], that thither them importune,<br>Do curse the grace that with such grace hath blessed<br>them,<br>Because myself do want my servants’ fortune.<br>I curse myself for they are sent by me,<br>That they should harbour where their lord should be.’<br>What’s here?<br>‘[[Silvia]], this [[Night|night]] I will enfranchise thee’?<br>‘Tis so, and here’s the ladder for the purpose.<br>Why, Phaeton, for thou art Merops’ son<br>Wilt thou aspire to guide the heavenly car,<br>And with thy daring folly burn the world?<br>Wilt thou reach [[Stars|stars]] because they shine on thee?<br>Go, base intruder, over-weening slave,<br>Bestow thy fawning smiles on equal mates,<br>And think my patience, more than thy [[Desert|desert]],<br>Is privilege for thy departure hence.<br>Thank me for this more than for all the favours<br>Which, all too much, I have bestowed on thee.<br>But if thou linger in my territories<br>Longer than swiftest expedition<br>Will give thee [[Time|time]] to leave our royal [[Court|court]],<br>By heaven, my wrath shall far exceed the [[Love|love]]<br>I ever bore my daughter or thyself.<br>Be gone. I will not hear thy vain excuse,<br>But as thou lov’st thy life, make [[Speed|speed]] from hence. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | And why not [[Death|death]], rather than living torment?<br>To die is to be banished from myself,<br>And [[Silvia]] is my [[Self|self]]. Banished from her<br>Is [[Self|self]] from [[Self|self]], a deadly banishment.<br>What light is light, if [[Silvia]] be not seen?<br>What joy is joy, if [[Silvia]] be not by—<br>Unless it be to think that she is by,<br>And feed upon the [[Shadow|shadow]] of perfection.<br>Except I be by [[Silvia]] in the [[Night|night]]<br>There is no music in the [[Nightingale|nightingale]].<br>Unless I look on [[Silvia]] in the day<br>There is no day for me to look upon.<br>She is my essence, and I leave to be<br>If I be not by her fair influence<br>Fostered, illumined, cherished, kept alive.<br>I fly not [[Death|death]] to fly his deadly doom.<br>Tarry I here I but attend on [[Death|death]],<br>But fly I hence, I fly away from life. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Proteus]] and Lance |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Run, boy, run, run, and seek him out. |
| LANCE | So-ho, so-ho! |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | What seest thou? |
| LANCE | Him we go to find. There’s not a hair on’s head but ‘tis [[A Valentine|a Valentine]]. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | [[Valentine]]? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Who then—his spirit? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Neither. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | What then? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Nothing. |
| LANCE | Can nothing speak?<br>He threatens [[Valentine]]<br>Master, shall I strike? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Who wouldst thou strike? |
| LANCE | Nothing. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | [[Villain]], forbear. |
| LANCE | Why, sir, I’ll strike nothing. I pray you— |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Sirrah, I say forbear. Friend [[Valentine]], a word. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | My ears are stopped, and cannot hear good news,<br>So much of bad already hath possessed them. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Then in dumb [[Silence|silence]] will I bury mine,<br>For they are harsh, untuneable, and bad. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | Is [[Silvia]] dead? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | No, [[Valentine]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No [[Valentine]] indeed, for sacred [[Silvia]].<br>Hath she forsworn me? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | No, [[Valentine]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No [[Valentine]], if [[Silvia]] have forsworn me.<br>What is your news? |
| LANCE | Sir, there is a proclamation that you are vanished. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | That thou art banished. O that’s the news:<br>From hence, from [[Silvia]], and from me thy friend. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | O, I have fed upon this woe already,<br>And now excess of it will make me surfeit.<br>Doth [[Silvia]] know that I am banishèd? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Ay, ay; and she hath offered to the doom,<br>Which unreversed stands in effectual force,<br>A sea of melting pearl, which some call [[Tears|tears]].<br>Those at her father’s churlish feet she tendered,<br>With them, upon her knees, her humble [[Self|self]],<br>Wringing her hands, whose whiteness so became them<br>As if but now they waxed pale, for woe.<br>But neither bended knees, pure hands held up,<br>Sad sighs, deep groans, nor silver-shedding [[Tears|tears]]<br>Could penetrate her uncompassionate sire,<br>But [[Valentine]], if he be ta’en, must die.<br>Besides, her intercession chafed him so<br>When she for thy repeal was suppliant<br>That to close [[Prison|prison]] he commanded her,<br>With many bitter threats of biding there. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No more, unless the next word that thou speak’st<br>Have some malignant power upon my life.<br>If so I pray thee breathe it in mine ear,<br>As ending anthem of my endless dolour. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Cease to lament for that thou canst not help,<br>And study help for that which thou lament‘st.<br>[[Time]] is the [[Nurse|nurse]] and breeder of all good.<br>Here if thou stay thou canst not see thy [[Love|love]].<br>Besides, thy staying will abridge thy life.<br>[[Hope]] is a [[Lover|lover]]’s staff. Walk hence with that,<br>And manage it against despairing thoughts.<br>Thy letters may be here, though thou art hence,<br>Which, being writ to me, shall be delivered<br>Even in the milk-white bosom of thy [[Love|love]].<br>The [[Time|time]] now serves not to expostulate.<br>Come, I’ll convey thee through the [[City|city]] gate,<br>And ere I part with thee confer at large<br>Of all that may concern thy [[Love|love]] affairs.<br>As thou lov’st [[Silvia]], though not for thyself,<br>Regard thy danger, and along with me. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I pray thee, Lance, an if thou seest my boy<br>Bid him make haste, and meet me at the North Gate. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Go, sirrah, find him out. Come, [[Valentine]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | O my dear [[Silvia]]! Hapless [[Valentine]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Proteus]] and [[Valentine]] |
| LANCE | I am but a [[Fool|fool]], look you, and yet I have the wit to think my master is a kind of a knave. But that’s all one, if he be but one knave. He lives not now that knows me to be in [[Love|love]], yet I am in [[Love|love]], but a team of [[Horse|horse]] shall not pluck that from me, nor who ‘tis I [[Love|love]]; and yet ’tis a woman, but what woman I will not tell myself; and yet ‘tis a milkmaid; yet ‘tis not a maid, for she hath had gossips; yet ’tis a maid, for she is her master’s maid, and serves for wages. She hath more qualities than a [[Water|water]]-spaniel, which is much in a bare Christian.<br>He takes out a paper<br>Here is the catalogue of her conditions. ‘Imprimis, she can fetch and carry’—why, a [[Horse|horse]] can do no more. Nay, a [[Horse|horse]] cannot fetch, but only carry, therefore is she better than a jade. ‘Item, she can milk.’ Look you, a sweet virtue in a maid with clean hands. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Speed]] |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | How now, Signor Lance, what news with your mastership? |
| LANCE | With my master’s [[Ship|ship]]? Why, it is at sea. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | [[Well]], your old vice still, mistake the word. What news then in your paper? |
| LANCE | The blackest news that ever thou heard’st. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Why, man, how ‘black’? |
| LANCE | Why, as black as ink. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Let me read them. |
| LANCE | Fie on thee, jolt-head, thou canst not read. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Thou liest. I can. |
| LANCE | I will try thee. Tell me this: who begot thee? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Marry, the son of my grandfather. |
| LANCE | O illiterate loiterer, it was the son of thy grand-mother. This proves that thou canst not read. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Come, [[Fool|fool]], come. Try me in thy paper. |
| LANCE | (giving [[Speed]] the paper) There: and Saint Nicholas be thy [[Speed|speed]]. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Imprimis, she can milk.’ |
| LANCE | Ay, that she can. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she brews good ale.’ |
| LANCE | And thereof comes the proverb ‘Blessing of your heart, you brew good ale’. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can sew.’ |
| LANCE | That’s as much as to say ‘Can she so?’ |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can knit.’ |
| LANCE | What need a man care for a stock with a wench when she can knit him a stock? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can wash and scour.’ |
| LANCE | A special virtue, for then she need not be washed and scoured. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can spin.’ |
| LANCE | Then may I set the world on wheels, when she can spin for her living. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she hath many nameless virtues.’ |
| LANCE | That’s as much as to say ‘bastard virtues’, that indeed know not their fathers, and therefore have no names. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Here follows her vices. |
| LANCE | Close at the heels of her virtues. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she is not to be broken with fasting, in respect of her breath.’ |
| LANCE | [[Well]], that fault may be mended with a breakfast. Read on. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she hath a sweet mouth.’ |
| LANCE | That makes amends for her sour breath. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she doth talk in her [[Sleep|sleep]].’ |
| LANCE | It’s no matter for that, so she [[Sleep|sleep]] not in her talk. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she is slow in words.’ |
| LANCE | O [[Villain|villain]], that set this down among her vices! To be slow in words is a woman’s only virtue. I pray thee out with’t, and place it for her chief virtue. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she is proud.’ |
| LANCE | Out with that, too. It was Eve’s legacy, and cannot be ta’en from her. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she hath no teeth.’ |
| LANCE | I care not for that, neither, because I [[Love|love]] crusts. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she is curst.’ |
| LANCE | [[Well]], the best is, she hath no teeth to bite. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she will often praise her liquor.’ |
| LANCE | If her liquor be good, she shall. If she will not, I will; for good things should be praised. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she is too liberal.’ |
| LANCE | Of her tongue she cannot, for that’s writ down she is slow of. Of her purse she shall not, for that I’ll keep shut. Now of another thing she may, and that cannot I help. [[Well]], proceed. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she hath more hair than wit, and more faults than hairs, and more wealth than faults.’ |
| LANCE | Stop there. I’ll have her. She was mine and not mine twice or thrice in that last article. Rehearse that once more. 347 |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she hath more hair than wit’— |
| LANCE | ‘More hair than wit.’ It may be. I’ll prove it: the cover of the salt hides the salt, and therefore it is more than the salt. The hair that covers the wit is more than the wit, for the greater hides the less. What’s next? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘And more faults than hairs’— |
| LANCE | That’s monstrous. O that that were out! |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘And more wealth than faults.’ |
| LANCE | Why, that word makes the faults gracious. [[Well]], I’ll have her, and if it be a match—as nothing is impossible— |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | What then? |
| LANCE | Why then will I tell thee that thy master stays for thee at the North Gate. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | For me? |
| LANCE | For thee? Ay, who art thou? He hath stayed for a better man than thee. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | And must I go to him? |
| LANCE | Thou must run to him, for thou hast stayed so long that going will scarce serve the turn. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Why didst not tell me sooner? Pox of your [[Love|love]] letters! Exit |
| LANCE | Now will he be swinged for reading my letter. An unmannerly slave, that will thrust himself into [[Secrets|secrets]]. I’ll after, to rejoice in the boy’s correction. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
