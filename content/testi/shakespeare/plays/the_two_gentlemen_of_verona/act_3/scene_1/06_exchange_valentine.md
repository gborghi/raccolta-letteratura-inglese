| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | I pray thee, Lance, an if thou seest my boy Bid him make haste, and meet me at the North Gate. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Go, sirrah, find him out. Come, [[Valentine]]. |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | O my dear [[Silvia]]! Hapless [[Valentine]]. Exeunt [[Proteus]] and [[Valentine]] |
| LANCE | I am but a [[Fool|fool]], look you, and yet I have the wit to think my master is a kind of a knave. But that’s all one, if he be but one knave. He lives not now that knows me to be in [[Love|love]], yet I am in [[Love|love]], but a team of [[Horse|horse]] shall not pluck that from me, nor who ‘tis I [[Love|love]]; and yet ’tis a woman, but what woman I will not tell myself; and yet ‘tis a milkmaid; yet ‘tis not a maid, for she hath had gossips; yet ’tis a maid, for she is her master’s maid, and serves for wages. She hath more qualities than a [[Water|water]]-spaniel, which is much in a bare Christian. He takes out a paper Here is the catalogue of her conditions. ‘Imprimis, she can fetch and carry’—why, a [[Horse|horse]] can do no more. Nay, a [[Horse|horse]] cannot fetch, but only carry, therefore is she better than a jade. ‘Item, she can milk.’ Look you, a sweet virtue in a maid with clean hands. Enter [[Speed]] |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | How now, Signor Lance, what news with your mastership? |
| LANCE | With my master’s [[Ship|ship]]? Why, it is at sea. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | [[Well]], your old vice still, mistake the word. What news then in your paper? |
| LANCE | The blackest news that ever thou heard’st. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Why, man, how ‘black’? |
| LANCE | Why, as black as ink. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Let me read them. |
| LANCE | Fie on thee, jolt-head, thou canst not read. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Thou liest. I can. |
| LANCE | I will try thee. Tell me this: who begot thee? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Marry, the son of my grandfather. |
| LANCE | O illiterate loiterer, it was the son of thy grand-mother. This proves that thou canst not read. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Come, [[Fool|fool]], come. Try me in thy paper. |
| LANCE | (giving [[Speed]] the paper) There: and Saint Nicholas be thy [[Speed|speed]]. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Imprimis, she can milk.’ |
| LANCE | Ay, that she can. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she brews good ale.’ |
| LANCE | And thereof comes the proverb ‘Blessing of your heart, you brew good ale’. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can sew.’ |
| LANCE | That’s as much as to say ‘Can she so?’ |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can knit.’ |
| LANCE | What need a man care for a stock with a wench when she can knit him a stock? |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can wash and scour.’ |
| LANCE | A special virtue, for then she need not be washed and scoured. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she can spin.’ |
| LANCE | Then may I set the world on wheels, when she can spin for her living. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she hath many nameless virtues.’ |
| LANCE | That’s as much as to say ‘bastard virtues’, that indeed know not their fathers, and therefore have no names. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | Here follows her vices. |
| LANCE | Close at the heels of her virtues. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she is not to be broken with fasting, in respect of her breath.’ |
| LANCE | [[Well]], that fault may be mended with a breakfast. Read on. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she hath a sweet mouth.’ |
| LANCE | That makes amends for her sour breath. |
| [[Speed|SPEED]] | ‘Item, she doth talk in her [[Sleep|sleep]].’ |
