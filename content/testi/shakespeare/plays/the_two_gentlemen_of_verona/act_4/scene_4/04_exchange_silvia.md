| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | What would you with her, if that I be she? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | If you be she, I do entreat your patience To hear me speak the message I am sent on. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | From whom? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | From my master, Sir [[Proteus]], madam. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | O, he sends you for a picture? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Ay, madam. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Ursula, bring my picture there. [An attendant brings a picture] Go, give your master this. Tell him from me One [[Julia]], that his changing thoughts forget, Would better fit his chamber than this [[Shadow|shadow]]. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Madam, please you peruse this letter. She gives [[Silvia]] a letter Pardon me, madam, I have unadvised Delivered you a paper that I should not. She takes back the letter and gives [[Silvia]] another letter This is the letter to your ladyship. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | I pray thee, let me look on that again. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | It may not be. Good madam, pardon me. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | There, hold. I will not look upon your master’s lines. I know they are stuffed with protestations, And full of new-found oaths, which he will break As easily as I do tear his paper. She [[Tears|tears]] the letter |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Madam, he sends your ladyship this ring. She offers [[Silvia]] a ring |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | The more shame for him, that he sends it me; For I have heard him say a thousand times His [[Julia]] gave it him at his departure. Though his false finger have profaned the ring, Mine shall not do his [[Julia]] so much wrong. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | She thanks you. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | What sayst thou? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | I thank you, madam, that you tender her. Poor gentlewoman, my master wrongs her much. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Dost thou know her? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Almost as [[Well|well]] as I do know myself. To think upon her woes I do protest That I have wept a hundred several times. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Belike she thinks that [[Proteus]] hath forsook her? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | I think she doth; and that’s her cause of sorrow. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | Is she not passing fair? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | She hath been fairer, madam, than she is. When she did think my master loved her [[Well|well]] She, in my judgement, was as fair as you. But since she did neglect her looking-glass, And threw her sun-expelling mask away, The air hath starved the roses in her cheeks And pinched the lily tincture of her face, That now she is become as black as I. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | How tall was she? |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | About my stature; for at Pentecost, When all our pageants of delight were played, Our youth got me to play the woman’s part, And I was trimmed in Madam [[Julia]]’s gown, Which served me as fit, by all men’s judgements, As if the garment had been made for me; Therefore I know she is about my height. And at that [[Time|time]] I made her weep agood, For I did play a lamentable part. Madam, ’twas Ariadne, passioning For [[Theseus]]’ perjury and unjust flight; Which I so lively acted with my [[Tears|tears]] That my poor mistress, moved therewithal, Wept bitterly; and would I might be dead If I in thought felt not her very sorrow. |
| [[Silvia|SILVIA]] | She is beholden to thee, gentle youth. Alas, poor lady, desolate and left. I weep myself to think upon thy words. Here, youth. There is my purse. I give thee this For thy sweet mistress’ sake, because thou lov’st her. Farewell. Exit |
