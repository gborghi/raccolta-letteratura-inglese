| [[Guiderius|GUIDERIUS]] | This [[Cloten]] was a [[Fool|fool]], an empty purse, There was no money in’t. Not Hercules Could have knocked out his brains, for he had none. Yet I not doing this, the [[Fool|fool]] had borne My head as I do his. |
| [[Belarius|BELARIUS]] | What hast thou done? |
| [[Guiderius|GUIDERIUS]] | I am perfect what: cut off one [[Cloten]]’s head, Son to the [[Queen]] after his own report, Who called me traitor, mountaineer, and swore With his own single hand he’d take us in, Displace our heads where—thanks, ye gods—they grow, And set them on Lud’s town. |
| [[Belarius|BELARIUS]] | We are all undone. |
| [[Guiderius|GUIDERIUS]] | Why, worthy father, what have we to lose But that he swore to take, our lives? The law Protects not us: then why should we be tender To let an arrogant piece of flesh threat us, Play judge and executioner all himself, For we do [[Fear|fear]] the law? What company Discover you abroad? |
| [[Belarius|BELARIUS]] | No single [[Soul|soul]] Can we set eye on, but in all safe reason He must have some attendants. Though his [[Humour|humour]] Was nothing but mutation, ay, and that From one bad thing to worse, not frenzy, Not absolute [[Madness|madness]], could so far have raved To bring him here [[Alone|alone]]. Although perhaps It may be heard at [[Court|court]] that such as we [[Cave]] here, hunt here, are outlaws, and in [[Time|time]] May make some stronger head, the which he hearing— As it is like him—might break out, and swear He’d fetch us in, yet is’t not probable To come [[Alone|alone]], either he so undertaking, Or they so [[Suffering|suffering]]. Then on good ground we [[Fear|fear]] If we do [[Fear|fear]] this [[Body|body]] hath a tail More perilous than the head. |
| [[Arviragus|ARVIRAGUS]] | Let ord’nance Come as the gods foresay it; howsoe’er, My brother hath done [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Belarius|BELARIUS]] | I had no mind To hunt this day. The boy Fidele’s sickness Did make my way long forth. GUIDERlUS With his own [[Sword|sword]], Which he did wave against my throat, I have ta’en His head from him. I’ll throw’t into the creek Behind our rock, and let it to the sea And tell the fishes he’s the [[Queen]]’s son, [[Cloten]]. That’s all I reck. Exit with [[Cloten]]’s head |
| [[Belarius|BELARIUS]] | I [[Fear|fear]] ’twill be revenged. Would, Polydore, thou hadst not done’t, though valour Becomes thee [[Well|well]] enough. |
| [[Arviragus|ARVIRAGUS]] | Would I had done’t, So the [[Revenge|revenge]] [[Alone|alone]] pursued me. Polydore, I [[Love|love]] thee brotherly, but envy much Thou hast robbed me of this deed. I would revenges That possible strength might meet would seek us through And put us to our answer. |
| [[Belarius|BELARIUS]] | [[Well]], ’tis done. We’ll hunt no more today, nor seek for danger Where there’s no profit. I prithee, to our rock. You and Fidele play the cooks. I’ll stay Till hasty Polydore return, and bring him To dinner presently. |
| [[Arviragus|ARVIRAGUS]] | Poor sick Fidele! I’ll willingly to him. To gain his colour I’d let a parish of such Clotens [[Blood|blood]], And praise myself for [[Charity|charity]]. Exit into the [[Cave|cave]] |
| [[Belarius|BELARIUS]] | O thou goddess, Thou divine [[Nature]], how thyself thou blazon‘st In these two princely boys! They are as gentle As zephyrs blowing below the violet, Not wagging his sweet head; and yet as rough, Their royal [[Blood|blood]] enchafed, as the rud’st [[Wind|wind]] That by the top doth take the mountain pine And make him stoop to th’ vale. ’Tis [[Wonder|wonder]] That an invisible instinct should frame them To royalty unlearned, [[Honour|honour]] untaught, Civility not seen from other, valour That wildly grows in them, but yields a crop As if it had been sowed. Yet still it’s strange What [[Cloten]]’s being here to us portends, Or what his [[Death|death]] will bring us. Enter [[Guiderius]] |
