| *(didascalia)* | Enter Solinus, the Duke of Ephesus, with [[Egeon]] the Merchant of Syracuse, Jailer, and other attendants |
| [[Egeon|EGEON]] | Proceed, Solinus, to procure my fall,<br>And by the doom of [[Death|death]] end woes and all. |
| DUKE | Merchant of Syracusa, plead no more.<br>I am not partial to infringe our laws.<br>The enmity and discord which of late<br>Sprung from the rancorous outrage of your Duke<br>To merchants, our [[Well|well]]-dealing countrymen,<br>Who, wanting guilders to redeem their lives,<br>Have sealed his rigorous statutes with their bloods,<br>Excludes all pity from our threat‘ning looks.<br>For since the mortal and intestine jars<br>’Twixt thy seditious countrymen and us,<br>It hath in solemn synods been decreed,<br>Both by the Syracusians and ourselves,<br>To admit no traffic to our adverse towns.<br>Nay more: if any born at Ephesus<br>Be seen at Syracusian marts and fairs;<br>Again, if any Syracusian born<br>Come to the bay of Ephesus—he dies,<br>His goods confiscate to the Duke’s dispose,<br>Unless a thousand marks be levied<br>To quit the penalty and ransom him.<br>Thy substance, valued at the highest rate,<br>Cannot amount unto a hundred marks.<br>Therefore by law thou art condemned to die. |
| [[Egeon|EGEON]] | Yet this my comfort: when your words are done,<br><br>My woes end likewise with the evening sun. |
| DUKE | [[Well]], Syracusian, say in brief the cause<br>Why thou departed‘st from thy native [[Home|home]],<br>And for what cause thou cam’st to Ephesus. |
| [[Egeon|EGEON]] | A heavier task could not have been imposed<br>Than I to speak my griefs unspeakable.<br>Yet, that the world may witness that my end<br>Was wrought by [[Nature|nature]], not by vile offence,<br>I’ll utter what my sorrow gives me leave.<br>In Syracusa was I born, and wed<br>Unto a woman happy but for me,<br>And by me happy, had not our hap been bad.<br>With her I lived in joy, our wealth increased<br>By prosperous voyages I often made<br>To Epidamnum, till my factor’s [[Death|death]],<br>And the great care of goods at random left,<br>Drew me from kind embracements of my spouse,<br>From whom my absence was not six months old<br>Before herself—almost at fainting under<br>The pleasing punishment that women bear-<br>Had made provision for her following me,<br>And soon and safe arrived where I was.<br>There had she not been long but she became<br>A joyful mother of two goodly sons;<br>And, which was strange, the one so like the other<br>As could not be distinguished but by names.<br>That very hour, and in the selfsame inn,<br>A mean-born woman was delivered<br>Of such a burden male, twins both alike.<br>Those, for their parents were exceeding poor,<br>I bought, and brought up to attend my sons.<br>My wife, not meanly proud of two such boys,<br>Made daily motions for our [[Home|home]] return.<br>Unwilling, I agreed. Alas! Too soon<br>We came aboard.<br>A league from Epidamnum had we sailed<br>Before the always-[[Wind|wind]]-obeying deep<br>Gave any tragic instance of our harm.<br>But longer did we not retain much [[Hope|hope]],<br>For what obscured light the heavens did grant<br>Did but convey unto our fearful minds<br>A doubtful warrant of immediate [[Death|death]],<br>Which though myself would gladly have embraced,<br>Yet the incessant weepings of my wife—<br>Weeping before for what she saw must come—<br>And piteous plainings of the pretty babes,<br>That mourned for fashion, ignorant what to [[Fear|fear]],<br>Forced me to seek delays for them and me.<br>And this it was—for other means was none:<br>The sailors sought for safety by our boat,<br>And left the [[Ship|ship]], then sinking-ripe, to us.<br>My wife, more careful for the latter-born,<br>Had fastened him unto a small spare mast<br>Such as seafaring men provide for storms.<br>To him one of the other twins was bound,<br>Whilst I had been like heedful of the other.<br>The children thus disposed, my wife and I,<br>Fixing our eyes on whom our care was fixed,<br>Fastened ourselves at either end the mast,<br>And floating straight, obedient to the stream,<br>Was carried towards Corinth, as we thought.<br>At length the sun, gazing upon the earth,<br>Dispersed those vapours that offended us,<br>And by the benefit of his wished light<br>The seas waxed calm, and we discovered<br>Two ships from far, making amain to us:<br>Of Corinth that, of Epidaurus this.<br>But ere they came—O let me say no more!<br>Gather the sequel by that went before. |
| DUKE | Nay, forward, old man; do not break off so,<br>For we may pity though not pardon thee. |
| [[Egeon|EGEON]] | O, had the gods done so, I had not now<br>Worthily termed them merciless to us.<br>For, ere the ships could meet by twice five leagues,<br>We were encountered by a mighty rock,<br>Which being violently borne upon,<br>Our helpful [[Ship|ship]] was splitted in the midst,<br>So that in this unjust [[divorce]] of us<br>Fortune had left to both of us alike<br>What to delight in, what to sorrow for.<br>Her part, poor [[Soul|soul]], seeming as burdened<br>With lesser weight but not with lesser woe,<br>Was carried with more [[Speed|speed]] before the [[Wind|wind]],<br>And in our sight they three were taken up<br>By fishermen of Corinth, as we thought.<br>At length another [[Ship|ship]] had seized on us,<br>And, knowing whom it was their hap to save,<br>Gave healthful welcome to their shipwrecked guests,<br>And would have reft the fishers of their prey<br>Had not their barque been very slow of sail;<br>And therefore homeward did they bend their course.<br>Thus have you heard me severed from my bliss,<br>That by misfortunes was my life prolonged<br>To tell sad stories of my own mishaps. |
| DUKE | And for the sake of them thou sorrow’st for,<br>Do me the favour to dilate at full<br>What have befall’n of them and thee till now. |
| [[Egeon|EGEON]] | My youngest boy, and yet my eldest care,<br>At eighteen years became inquisitive<br>After his brother, and importuned me<br>That his attendant—so his case was like,<br>Reft of his brother, but retained his name—<br>Might bear him company in the [[Quest|quest]] of him;<br>Whom whilst I laboured of a [[Love|love]] to see,<br>I hazarded the loss of whom I loved.<br>Five summers have I spent in farthest Greece,<br>Roaming clean through the bounds of Asia,<br>And coasting homeward came to Ephesus,<br>Hopeless to find, yet loath to leave unsought<br>Or that or any place that harbours men.<br>But here must end the story of my life,<br>And happy were I in my timely [[Death|death]]<br>Could all my travels warrant me they live. |
| DUKE | Hapless [[Egeon]], whom the fates have marked<br>To bear the extremity of dire mishap,<br>Now trust me, were it not against our laws—<br>Which princes, would they, may not disannul—<br>Against my [[Crown|crown]], my oath, my dignity,<br>My [[Soul|soul]] should sue as advocate for thee.<br>But though thou art adjudged to the [[Death|death]],<br>And passed sentence may not be recalled<br>But to our [[Honour|honour]]’s great disparagement,<br>Yet will I favour thee in what I can.<br>Therefore, merchant, I’ll limit thee this day<br>To seek thy health by beneficial help.<br>Try all the friends thou hast in Ephesus:<br>Beg thou or borrow to make up the sum,<br>And live. If no, then thou art doomed to die.<br>Jailer, take him to thy custody. |
| JAILER | I will, my lord. |
| [[Egeon|EGEON]] | Hopeless and helpless doth [[Egeon]] wend,<br>But to procrastinate his lifeless end. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
