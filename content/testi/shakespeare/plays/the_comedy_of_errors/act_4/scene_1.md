| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Second Merchant, [[Angelo]] the goldsmith, and an Officer |
| SECOND MERCHANT | (to [[Angelo]])<br>You know since Pentecost the sum is due,<br>And since I have not much importuned you;<br>Nor now I had not, but that I am bound<br>To Persia, and want guilders for my voyage.<br>Therefore make present satisfaction,<br>Or I’ll attach you by this officer. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Even just the sum that I do owe to you<br>Is growing to me by Antipholus,<br>And in the instant that I met with you<br>He had of me a chain. At five o’[[Clock|clock]]<br>I shall receive the money for the same.<br>Pleaseth you walk with me down to his [[house]],<br>I will discharge my bond, and thank you too. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Antipholus of Ephesus]] and [[Dromio of Ephesus]] from the Courtesan’s [[house]] (the Porcupine) |
| OFFICER | That labour may you save. See where he comes. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | (to Dromio)<br>While I go to the goldsmith’s [[house]], go thou<br>And buy a rope’s end. That will I bestow<br>Among my wife and her confederates<br>For locking me out of my doors by day.<br>But soft, I see the goldsmith. Get thee gone.<br>Buy thou a rope, and bring it [[Home|home]] to me. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | I buy a thousand pound a year, I buy a rope. Exit |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | (to [[Angelo]])<br>A man is [[Well|well]] holp up that trusts to you!<br>I promised your presence and the chain,<br>But neither chain nor goldsmith came to me.<br>Belike you thought our [[Love|love]] would last too long<br>If it were chained together, and therefore came not. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Saving your merry [[Humour|humour]], here’s the note<br>How much your chain weighs to the utmost carat,<br>The fineness of the gold, and chargeful fashion,<br>Which doth amount to three odd ducats more<br>Than I stand debted to this gentleman.<br>I pray you see him presently discharged,<br>For he is bound to sea, and stays but for it. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | I am not furnished with the present money.<br>Besides, I have some business in the town.<br>Good signor, take the stranger to my [[house]],<br>And with you take the chain, and bid my wife<br>Disburse the sum on the receipt thereof.<br>Perchance I will be there as soon as you. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Then you will bring the chain to her yourself? |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | No, bear it with you, lest I come not [[Time|time]] enough. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | [[Well]], sir, I will. Have you the chain about you? |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | An if I have not, sir, I [[Hope|hope]] you have;<br>Or else you may return without your money. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Nay, come, I pray you, sir, give me the chain.<br>Both [[Wind|wind]] and tide stays for this gentleman,<br>And I, to blame, have held him here too long. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Good Lord! You use this dalliance to excuse<br>Your breach of promise to the Porcupine.<br>I should have chid you for not bringing it,<br>But like a shrew you first begin to brawl. |
| SECOND MERCHANT | (to [[Angelo]])<br>The hour steals on. I pray you, sir, dispatch. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | (to Antipholus)<br>You hear how he importunes me. The chain! |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Why, give it to my wife, and fetch your money. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Come, come, you know I gave it you even now.<br>Either send the chain, or send me by some token. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Fie, now you run this [[Humour|humour]] out of breath.<br>Come, where’s the chain? I pray you let me see it. |
| SECOND MERCHANT | My business cannot brook this dalliance.<br>Good sir, say whe’er you’ll answer me or no;<br>If not, I’ll leave him to the officer. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | I answer you? What should I answer you? |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | The money that you owe me for the chain. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | I owe you none till I receive the chain. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | You know I gave it you half an hour since. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | You gave me none. You wrong me much to say so. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | You wrong me more, sir, in denying it.<br>Consider how it stands upon my credit. |
| SECOND MERCHANT | [[Well]], officer, arrest him at my suit. |
| OFFICER | (to [[Angelo]])<br>I do, and charge you in the Duke’s name to obey me. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | (to Antipholus)<br>This touches me in reputation.<br>Either consent to pay this sum for me,<br>Or I attach you by this officer. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Consent to pay thee that I never had?<br>Arrest me, foolish fellow, if thou dar’st. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Here is thy fee: arrest him, officer.<br>I would not spare my brother in this case<br>If he should scorn me so apparently. |
| OFFICER | (to Antipholus)<br>I do arrest you, sir. You hear the suit. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | I do obey thee till I give thee bail.<br>(To<br>[[Angelo]]) But, sirrah, you shall buy this sport as dear<br>As all the metal in your shop will answer. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Sir, sir, I shall have law in Ephesus,<br>To your notorious shame, I [[Doubt|doubt]] it not. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Dromio of Syracuse]], from the bay |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | Master, there’s a barque of Epidamnum<br>That stays but till her owner comes aboard,<br>And then she bears away. Our freightage, sir,<br>I have conveyed aboard, and I have bought<br>The oil, the balsamum, and aqua-vitae.<br>The [[Ship|ship]] is in her trim; the merry [[Wind|wind]]<br>Blows fair from land. They stay for naught at all<br>But for their owner, master, and yourself. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | How now? A madman? Why, thou peevish sheep,<br>What [[Ship|ship]] of Epidamnum stays for me? |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | A [[Ship|ship]] you sent me to, to hire waftage. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Thou drunken slave, I sent thee for a rope,<br>And told thee to what purpose and what end. |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | You sent me for a ropës end as soon.<br>You sent me to the bay, sir, for a barque. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | I will [[debate]] this matter at more leisure,<br>And teach your ears to list me with more heed.<br>To [[Adriana]], [[Villain|villain]], hie thee straight.<br>Give her this key, and tell her in the desk<br>That’s covered o’er with Turkish tapestry<br>There is a purse of ducats. Let her send it.<br>Tell her I am arrested in the street,<br>And that shall bail me. Hie thee, slave. Be gone !—<br>On, officer, to [[Prison|prison]], till it come. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Dromio of Syracuse]] |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | To [[Adriana]]. That is where we dined,<br>Where Dowsabel did claim me for her husband.<br>She is too big, I [[Hope|hope]], for me to compass.<br>Thither I must, although against my will;<br>For servants must their masters’ minds fulfil. Exit |
