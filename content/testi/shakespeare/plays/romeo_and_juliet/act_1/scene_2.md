| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]], [[Paris]], and Peter, a servingman |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | But Montague is bound as [[Well|well]] as I,<br>In penalty alike, and ’tis not hard, I think,<br>For men so old as we to keep the peace. |
| [[Paris|PARIS]] | Of honourable reckoning are you both,<br>And pity ’tis you lived at odds so long.<br>But now, my lord: what say you to my suit? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | But saying o’er what I have said before.<br>My [[Child|child]] is yet a stranger in the world;<br>She hath not seen the change of fourteen years.<br>Let two more summers wither in their [[Pride|pride]]<br>Ere we may think her ripe to be a bride. |
| [[Paris|PARIS]] | Younger than she are happy mothers made. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | And too soon marred are those so early made.<br>But woo her, gentle [[Paris]], get her heart;<br>My will to her consent is but a part,<br>And, she agreed, within her scope of choice<br>Lies my consent and fair-according voice.<br>This [[Night|night]] I hold an old-accustomed feast<br>Whereto I have invited many a guest<br>Such as I [[Love|love]], and you among the store,<br>One more most welcome, makes my number more.<br>At my poor [[house]] look to behold this [[Night|night]]<br>Earth-treading [[Stars|stars]] that make dark heaven light.<br>Such comfort as do lusty young men feel<br>When [[Well|well]]-apparelled April on the heel<br>Of limping [[Winter|winter]] treads—even such delight<br>Among fresh female buds shall you this [[Night|night]]<br>Inherit at my [[house]]; hear all, all see,<br>And like her most whose merit most shall be,<br>Which on more view of many, mine, being one,<br>May stand in number, though in reck’ning none. |
| *(didascalia)* | Come, go with me. (Giving Peter a paper) Go, sirrah, |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | trudge about;<br>Through fair [[Verona]] find those persons out<br>Whose names are written there, and to them say<br>My [[house]] and welcome on their pleasure stay. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Capulet]] and [[Paris]] |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Find them out whose names are written here? It |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | is written that the shoemaker should meddle with his<br>yard and the tailor with his last, the fisher with his<br>pencil and the painter with his nets; but I am sent to<br>find those persons whose names are here writ, and can<br>never find what names the writing person hath here<br>writ. I must to the learned. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Benvolio]] and [[Romeo]] |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | In good [[Time|time]]. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | (to [[Romeo]])<br>Tut, man, one [[Fire|fire]] burns out another’s burning,<br>One [[Pain|pain]] is lessened by another’s anguish.<br>Turn giddy, and be holp by backward turning.<br>One desperate grief cures with another’s languish.<br>Take thou some new infection to thy eye,<br>And the rank [[Poison|poison]] of the old will die. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Your plantain leaf is excellent for that. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | For what, I pray thee? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | For your broken shin. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | Why, [[Romeo]], art thou mad? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Not mad, but bound more than a madman is;<br>Shut up in [[Prison|prison]], kept without my food, |
| *(didascalia)* | Whipped and tormented and—(to Peter Good e’en, |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | good fellow. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | God gi‘good e’en. I pray, sir, can you read? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Ay, mine own fortune in my misery. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER erhaps you have learned it without book. But I pray, can you read anything you see? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Ay, if I know the letters and the language. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Ye say honestly. Rest you merry. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Stay, fellow, I can read.<br>He reads the letter<br>‘Signor Martino and his wife and daughters,<br>County Anselme and his beauteous sisters,<br>The lady widow of Vitruvio,<br>Signor Placentio and his lovely nieces,<br>[[Mercutio]] and his brother [[Valentine]],<br>Mine uncle [[Capulet]], his wife and daughters,<br>My fair niece [[Rosaline]] and Livia,<br>Signor Valentio and his cousin [[Tybalt]],<br>[[Lucio]] and the lively [[Helena]].’<br>A fair assembly. Whither should they come? |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Up. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Whither? |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER To supper to our [[house]]. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Whose [[house]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER My master’s. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Indeed, I should have asked thee that before. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Now I’ll tell you without asking. My master is |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | the great rich [[Capulet]], and if you be not of the [[house]]<br>of Montagues, I pray come and crush a cup of wine.<br>Rest you merry. Exit |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | At this same ancient feast of [[Capulet]]’s<br>Sups the fair [[Rosaline]], whom thou so loves,<br>With all the admirèd beauties of [[Verona]].<br>Go thither, and with unattainted eye<br>Compare her face with some that I shall show,<br>And I will make thee think thy swan a crow. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | When the devout religion of mine eye<br>Maintains such falsehood, then turn [[Tears|tears]] to fires;<br>And these who, often drowned, could never die,<br>Transparent heretics, be burnt for liars.<br>One fairer than my [[Love|love]] !—the all-seeing sun<br>Ne’er saw her match since first the world begun. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | Tut, you saw her fair, none else being by,<br>Herself poised with herself in either eye;<br>But in that crystal scales let there be weighed<br>Your lady’s [[Love|love]] against some other maid<br>That I will show you shining at this feast,<br>And she shall scant show [[Well|well]] that now seems best. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | I’ll go along, no such sight to be shown,<br>But to rejoice in splendour of mine own. Exeunt |
