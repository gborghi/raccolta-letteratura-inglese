| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Romeo]] and [[Juliet]] aloft with the ladder of cords |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Wilt thou be gone? It is not yet near day.<br>It was the [[Nightingale|nightingale]], and not the lark,<br>That pierced the [[Fear|fear]]-full hollow of thine ear.<br>Nightly she sings on yon pom’granate tree.<br>Believe me, [[Love|love]], it was the [[Nightingale|nightingale]]. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | It was the lark, the herald of the morn,<br>No [[Nightingale|nightingale]]. Look, [[Love|love]], what envious streaks<br>Do lace the severing clouds in yonder east.<br>[[Night]]’s candles are burnt out, and jocund day<br>Stands tiptoe on the misty mountain tops.<br>I must be gone and live, or stay and die. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Yon light is not daylight; I know it, I.<br>It is some meteor that the sun exhaled<br>To be to thee this [[Night|night]] a torchbearer<br>And light thee on thy way to Mantua.<br>Therefore stay yet. Thou need’st not to be gone. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Let me be ta‘en, let me be put to [[Death|death]].<br>I am content, so thou wilt have it so.<br>I’ll say yon grey is not the morning’s eye,<br>’Tis but the pale reflex of [[Cynthia]]’s brow;<br>Nor that is not the lark whose notes do beat<br>The vaulty heaven so high above our heads.<br>I have more care to stay than will to go.<br>Come, [[Death|death]], and welcome; [[Juliet]] wills it so.<br>How is’t, my [[Soul|soul]]? Let’s talk. It is not day. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | It is, it is. Hie hence, be gone, away.<br>It is the lark that sings so out of tune,<br>Straining harsh discords and unpleasing sharps.<br>Some say the lark makes sweet division;<br>This doth not so, for she divideth us.<br>Some say the lark and loathed toad changed eyes.<br>O, now I would they had changed voices, too,<br>Since arm from arm that voice doth us affray,<br>Hunting thee hence with hunt’s-up to the day.<br>O, now be gone! More light and light it grows. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | More light and light, more dark and dark our woes. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Nurse]] hastily |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Madam. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | [[Nurse]]. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Your lady mother is coming to your chamber.<br>The day is broke; be wary, look about. Exit |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Then, [[Window|window]], let day in, and let life out. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Farewell, farewell! One kiss, and I’ll descend. |
| *(didascalia)* | He lets down the ladder of cords and goes down |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Art thou gone so, [[Love|love]], lord, my husband, friend?<br>I must hear from thee every day in the hour,<br>For in a minute there are many days.<br>O, by this count I shall be much in years<br>Ere I again behold my [[Romeo]]. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Farewell.<br>I will omit no opportunity<br>That may convey my greetings, [[Love|love]], to thee. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | O, think’st thou we shall ever meet again? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | I [[Doubt|doubt]] it not, and all these woes shall serve<br>For sweet discourses in our times to come. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Juliet|JULIET]] |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | O God, I have an ill-divining [[Soul|soul]]!<br>Methinks I see thee, now thou art so low,<br>As one dead in the [[Bottom|bottom]] of a tomb.<br>Either my eyesight fails, or thou look’st pale. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | And trust me, [[Love|love]], in my eye so do you.<br>Dry sorrow drinks our [[Blood|blood]]. Adieu, adieu. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Juliet|JULIET]] pulling up the ladder and weeping |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | O fortune, fortune, all men call thee fickle.<br>If thou art fickle, what dost thou with him<br>That is renowned for [[Faith|faith]]? Be fickle, fortune,<br>For then I [[Hope|hope]] thou wilt not keep him long,<br>But send him back. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]]’s Wife below |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Ho, daughter, are you up? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Who is’t that calls? It is my lady mother.<br>Is she not down so late, or up so early?<br>What unaccustomed cause procures her hither? |
| *(didascalia)* | She goes down and enters below |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Why, how now, [[Juliet]]? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Madam, I am not [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Evermore weeping for your cousin’s [[Death|death]]?<br>What, wilt thou wash him from his [[Grave|grave]] with [[Tears|tears]]?<br>An if thou couldst, thou couldst not make him live,<br>Therefore have done. Some grief shows much of [[Love|love]],<br>But much of grief shows still some want of wit. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Yet let me weep for such a feeling loss. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | So shall you feel the loss, but not the friend<br>Which you so weep for. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Feeling so the loss,<br>I cannot choose but ever weep the friend. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | [[Well]], girl, thou weep’st not so much for his [[Death|death]]<br>As that the [[Villain|villain]] lives which slaughtered him. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | What [[Villain|villain]], madam? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | That same [[Villain|villain]] [[Romeo]]. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | (aside)<br>[[Villain]] and he be many miles asunder.<br>(To her mother) God pardon him—I do, with all my<br>heart,<br>And yet no man like he doth grieve my heart. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | That is because the traitor murderer lives. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Ay, madam, from the reach of these my hands.<br>Would none but I might venge my cousin’s [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | We will have vengeance for it, [[Fear|fear]] thou not.<br>Then weep no more. I’ll send to one in Mantua,<br>Where that same banished runagate doth live,<br>Shall give him such an unaccustomed dram<br>That he shall soon keep [[Tybalt]] company;<br>And then I [[Hope|hope]] thou wilt be satisfied. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Indeed, I never shall be satisfied<br>With [[Romeo]] till I behold him, dead,<br>Is my poor heart so for a kinsman vexed.<br>Madam, if you could find out but a man<br>To bear a [[Poison|poison]], I would temper it<br>That [[Romeo]] should, upon receipt thereof,<br>Soon [[Sleep|sleep]] in quiet. O, how my heart abhors<br>To hear him named and cannot come to him<br>To wreak the [[Love|love]] I bore my cousin<br>Upon his [[Body|body]] that hath slaughtered him! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Find thou the means, and I’ll find such a man.<br>But now I’ll tell thee joyful tidings, girl. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | And joy comes [[Well|well]] in such a needy [[Time|time]].<br>What are they, I beseech your ladyship? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | [[Well]], [[Well|well]], thou hast a careful father, [[Child|child]];<br>One who, to put thee from thy heaviness,<br>Hath sorted out a sudden day of joy<br>That thou expect’st not, nor I looked not for. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Madam, in happy [[Time|time]]. What day is that? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Marry, my [[Child|child]], early next [[thursday|Thursday]] morn<br>The gallant, young, and noble gentleman<br>The County [[Paris]] at Saint Peter’s Church<br>Shall happily make thee there a joyful bride. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Now, by Saint Peter’s Church, and Peter too,<br>He shall not make me there a joyful bride.<br>I [[Wonder|wonder]] at this haste, that I must wed<br>Ere he that should be husband comes to woo.<br>I pray you, tell my lord and father, madam,<br>I will not marry yet; and when I do, I swear<br>It shall be [[Romeo]]—whom you know I hate—<br>Rather than [[Paris]]. These are news indeed. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]] and the [[Nurse]] |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Here comes your father. Tell him so yourself,<br>And see how he will take it at your hands. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | When the sun sets, the earth doth drizzle dew,<br>But for the sunset of my brother’s son<br>It rains downright.<br>How now, a conduit, girl? What, still in [[Tears|tears]]?<br>Evermore show‘ring? In one little [[Body|body]]<br>Thou counterfeit’st a barque, a sea, a [[Wind|wind]],<br>For still thy eyes—which I may call the sea—<br>Do ebb and flow with [[Tears|tears]]. The barque thy [[Body|body]] is,<br>Sailing in this salt flood; the winds thy sighs,<br>Who, raging with thy [[Tears|tears]] and they with them,<br>Without a sudden calm will overset<br>Thy tempest-tossed [[Body|body]].—How now, wife?<br>Have you delivered to her our decree? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Ay, sir, but she will none, she gives you thanks.<br>I would the [[Fool|fool]] were married to her [[Grave|grave]]. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Soft, take me with you, take me with you, wife.<br>How, will she none? Doth she not give us thanks?<br>Is she not proud? Doth she not count her blest,<br>Unworthy as she is, that we have wrought<br>So worthy a gentleman to be her bride? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Not proud you have, but thankful that you have.<br>Proud can I never be of what I hate,<br>But thankful even for hate that is meant [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | How, how, how, how—chopped [[logic]]? What is this?<br>‘Proud’, and ‘I thank you’, and ’I thank you not’,<br>And yet ‘not proud’ ? Mistress minion, you,<br>Thank me no thankings, nor proud me no prouds,<br>But fettle your fine joints ’gainst [[thursday|Thursday]] next<br>To go with [[Paris]] to Saint Peter’s Church,<br>Or I will drag thee on a hurdle thither.<br>Out, you green-sickness carrion! Out, you baggage,<br>You tallow-face! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Fie, fie, what, are you mad? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | (kneeling)<br>Good father, I beseech you on my knees,<br>Hear me with patience but to speak a word. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Hang thee, young baggage, disobedient wretch!<br>I tell thee what: get thee to church o’ [[thursday|Thursday]],<br>Or never after look me in the face.<br>Speak not, reply not, do not answer me. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Juliet]] rises |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | My fingers itch. Wife, we scarce thought us blest<br>That God had lent us but this only [[Child|child]],<br>But now I see this one is one too much,<br>And that we have a curse in having her.<br>Out on her, hilding! |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | God in heaven bless her!<br>You are to blame, my lord, to rate her so. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | And why, my lady Wisdom? Hold your tongue,<br>Good Prudence. Smatter with your gossips, go! |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | I speak no treason. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Capulet|CAPULET]] O, God-i’-good-e’en! |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Nurse|NURSE]] |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | May not one speak? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Peace, you mumbling [[Fool|fool]],<br>Utter your gravity o’er a gossip’s bowl,<br>For here we need it not. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | You are too hot. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | God’s bread, it makes me mad. Day, [[Night|night]]; work, play;<br>[[Alone]], in company, still my care hath been<br>To have her matched; and having now provided<br>A gentleman of noble parentage,<br>Of fair demesnes, youthful, and nobly lined,<br>Stuffed, as they say, with honourable parts,<br>Proportioned as one’s thought would wish a man—<br>And then to have a wretched puling [[Fool|fool]],<br>A whining maumet, in her fortune’s tender,<br>To answer ‘I’ll not wed, I cannot [[Love|love]];<br>I am too young, I pray you pardon me’!<br>But an you will not wed, I’ll pardon you!<br>Graze where you will, you shall not [[house]] with me.<br>Look to‘t, think on’t. I do not use to jest.<br>[[thursday|Thursday]] is near. Lay hand on heart. Advise.<br>An you be mine, I’ll give you to my friend.<br>An you be not, hang, beg, starve, die in the streets,<br>For, by my [[Soul|soul]], I’ll ne’er acknowledge thee,<br>Nor what is mine shall never do thee good.<br>Trust to’t. Bethink you. I’ll not be forsworn. Exit |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Is there no pity sitting in the clouds<br>That sees into the [[Bottom|bottom]] of my grief’<br>O sweet my mother, cast me not away!<br>Delay this [[Marriage|marriage]] for a month, a week;<br>Or if you do not, make the bridal bed<br>In that dim monument where [[Tybalt]] lies. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Talk not to me, for I’ll not speak a word.<br>Do as thou wilt, for I have done with thee. Exit |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | O, God—ONurse, how shall this be prevented?<br>My husband is on earth, my [[Faith|faith]] in heaven.<br>How shall that [[Faith|faith]] return again to earth<br>Unless that husband send it me from heaven<br>By leaving earth? Comfort me, counsel me.<br>Alack, alack, that heaven should practise stratagems<br>Upon so soft a subject as myself!<br>What sayst thou? Hast thou not a word of joy?<br>Some comfort, [[Nurse]]. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | [[Faith]], here it is: [[Romeo]]<br>Is banished, and all the world to nothing<br>That he dares ne‘er come back to challenge you,<br>Or if he do, it needs must be by stealth.<br>Then, since the case so stands as now it doth,<br>I think it best you married with the County.<br>O, he’s a lovely gentleman!<br>[[Romeo]]’s a dishclout to him. An eagle, madam,<br>Hath not so green, so quick, so fair an eye<br>As [[Paris]] hath. Beshrew my very heart,<br>I think you are happy in this second match,<br>For it excels your first; or if it did not,<br>Your first is dead, or ’twere as good he were<br>As living hence and you no use of him. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Speak’st thou from thy heart? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | And from my [[Soul|soul]], too, else beshrew them both. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Amen. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | What? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | [[Well]], thou hast comforted me marvellous much.<br>Go in; and tell my lady I am gone,<br>Having displeased my father, to Laurence’ cell<br>To make confession and to be absolved. |
| *(didascalia)* | Marry, I will; and this is wisely done. Exit |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | (watching her go)<br>Ancient damnation! O most wicked fiend!<br>Is it more sin to wish me thus forsworn,<br>Or to dispraise my lord with that same tongue<br>Which she hath praised him with above compare<br>So many thousand times? Go, counsellor!<br>Thou and my bosom henceforth shall be twain.<br>I’ll to the friar, to know his remedy.<br>If all else fail, myself have power to die. Exit |
