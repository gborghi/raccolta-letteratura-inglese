| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Friar Laurence]] |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | [[Romeo]], come forth, come forth, thou [[Fear|fear]]-full man.<br>Affliction is enamoured of thy parts,<br>And thou art wedded to calamity. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Romeo]] |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Father, what news? What is the Prince’s doom?<br>What sorrow craves acquaintance at my hand<br>That I yet know not? |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Too familiar<br>Is my dear son with such sour company.<br>I bring thee tidings of the Prince’s doom. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | What less than doomsday is the Prince’s doom? |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | A gentler judgement vanished from his lips:<br>Not [[Body|body]]’s [[Death|death]], but [[Body|body]]’s banishment. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Ha, banishment? Be merciful, say ‘[[Death|death]]’,<br>For [[Exile|exile]] hath more terror in his look,<br>Much more than [[Death|death]]. Do not say ‘banishment’. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Hence from [[Verona]] art thou banished.<br>Be patient, for the world is broad and wide. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | There is no world without [[Verona]] walls<br>But purgatory, torture, hell itself.<br>Hence banished is banished from the world,<br>And world’s [[Exile|exile]] is [[Death|death]]. Then ‘banishèd’<br>Is [[Death|death]] mistermed. Calling [[Death|death]] ‘banishèd’<br>Thou cutt‘st my head off with a golden axe,<br>And smil’st upon the stroke that murders me. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | O deadly sin, O rude unthankfulness!<br>Thy fault our law calls [[Death|death]], but the kind Prince,<br>Taking thy part, hath rushed aside the law<br>And turned that black word ’[[Death|death]]’ to banishment.<br>This is dear [[Mercy|mercy]], and thou seest it not. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | ‘Tis torture, and not [[Mercy|mercy]]. Heaven is here<br>Where [[Juliet]] lives, and every cat and dog<br>And little mouse, every unworthy thing,<br>Live here in heaven and may look on her,<br>But [[Romeo]] may not. More validity,<br>More honourable state, more courtship lives<br>In carrion flies than [[Romeo]]. They may seize<br>On the white [[Wonder|wonder]] of dear [[Juliet]]’s hand,<br>And steal immortal blessing from her lips,<br>Who, even in pure and vestal modesty,<br>Still blush, as thinking their own kisses sin.<br>But [[Romeo]] may not, he is banished.<br>Flies may do this, but I from this must fly.<br>They are free men, but I am banished.<br>And sayst thou yet that [[Exile|exile]] is not [[Death|death]]?<br>Hadst thou no [[Poison|poison]] mixed, no sharp-ground knife,<br>No sudden mean of [[Death|death]], though ne’er so mean,<br>But ‘banishèd’ to kill me—‘banishèd’?<br>O friar, the damned use that word in hell.<br>Howling attends it. How hast thou the heart,<br>Being a divine, a ghostly confessor,<br>A sin-absolver and my friend professed,<br>To mangle me with that word ‘banishèd’? |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Thou fond mad man, hear me a little speak. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | O, thou wilt speak again of banishment. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | I’ll give thee armour to keep off that word—<br>Adversity’s sweet milk, [[philosophy]],<br>To comfort thee though thou art banished. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Yet ‘banishèd’? Hang up [[philosophy]]!<br>Unless [[philosophy]] can make a [[Juliet]],<br>Displant a town, reverse a prince’s doom,<br>It helps not, it prevails not. Talk no more. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | O, then I see that madmen have no ears. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | How should they, when that wise men have no eyes? |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Let me dispute with thee of thy estate. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Thou canst not speak of that thou dost not feel.<br>Wert thou as young as I, [[Juliet]] thy [[Love|love]],<br>An hour but married, [[Tybalt]] murdered,<br>Doting like me, and like me banished,<br>Then mightst thou speak, then mightst thou tear thy<br>hair,<br>And fall upon the ground, as I do now,<br>He falls upon the ground<br><br>Taking the measure of an unmade [[Grave|grave]].<br>Knock within |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Arise, one knocks. Good [[Romeo]], hide thyself. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Not I, unless the breath of heartsick groans<br>Mist-like enfold me from the search of eyes. |
| *(didascalia)* | Knocking within |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Hark, how they knock!—Who’s there?—[[Romeo]], arise.<br>Thou wilt be taken.—Stay a white.—Stand up.<br>Still knock within<br>Run to my study.—By and by!—God’s will,<br>What simpleness is this?<br>Knock within<br>I come, I come.<br>Who knocks so hard? Whence come you? What’s your<br>will? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | (within)<br>Let me come in, and you shall know my errand.<br>I come from Lady [[Juliet]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] opening the door Welcome then. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Nurse]] |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O holy friar, O tell me, holy friar,<br>Where is my lady’s lord? Where’s [[Romeo]]? |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | There on the ground, with his own [[Tears|tears]] made drunk. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O, he is even in my mistress’ case,<br>Just in her case! O woeful sympathy,<br>Piteous predicament! Even so lies she,<br>Blubb’ring and weeping, weeping and blubb’ring.<br>(To [[Romeo]]) Stand up, stand up, stand an you be a man,<br>For [[Juliet]]’s sake, for her sake, rise and stand.<br>Why should you fall into so deep an O? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | (rising)<br>[[Nurse]]. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Ah sir, ah sir, [[Death|death]]’s the end of all. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Spak’st thou of [[Juliet]]? How is it with her?<br>Doth not she think me an old murderer,<br>Now I have stained the [[Childhood|childhood]] of our joy<br>With [[Blood|blood]] removed but little from her own?<br>Where is she, and how doth she, and what says<br>My concealed lady to our cancelled [[Love|love]]? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O, she says nothing, sir, but weeps and weeps,<br>And now falls on her bed, and then starts up,<br>And ’[[Tybalt]]’ calls, and then on [[Romeo]] cries,<br>And then down falls again. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | As if that name<br>Shot from the deadly level of a gun<br>Did murder her as that name’s cursed hand<br>Murdered her kinsman. O tell me, friar, tell me,<br>In what vile part of this anatomy<br>Doth my name lodge? Tell me, that I may sack<br>The hateful mansion. |
| *(didascalia)* | He offers to stab himself, and the [[Nurse]] snatches the dagger away |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Hold thy desperate hand.<br>Art thou a man? Thy form cries out thou art.<br>Thy [[Tears|tears]] are womanish, thy wild acts denote<br>The unreasonable fury of a beast.<br>Unseemly woman in a seeming man,<br>And ill-beseeming beast in seeming both!<br>Thou hast amazed me. By my holy order,<br>I thought thy disposition better tempered.<br>Hast thou slain [[Tybalt]]? Wilt thou slay thyself,<br>And slay thy lady that in thy life lives<br>By doing damned hate upon thyself?<br>Why rail‘st thou on thy birth, the heaven, and earth,<br>Since birth and heaven and earth, all three, do meet<br>In thee at once, which thou at once wouldst lose?<br>Fie, fie, thou sham’st thy shape, thy [[Love|love]], thy wit,<br>Which like a usurer abound‘st in all,<br>And usest none in that true use indeed<br>Which should bedeck thy shape, thy [[Love|love]], thy wit.<br>Thy noble shape is but a form of wax,<br>Digressing from the valour of a man;<br>Thy dear [[Love|love]] sworn but hollow perjury,<br>Killing that [[Love|love]] which thou hast vowed to cherish;<br>Thy wit, that ornament to shape and [[Love|love]],<br>Misshapen in the conduct of them both,<br>Like powder in a skilless soldier’s flask<br>Is set afire by thine own ignorance,<br>And thou dismembered with thine own defence.<br>What, rouse thee, man! Thy [[Juliet]] is alive,<br>For whose dear sake thou wast but lately dead:<br>There art thou happy. [[Tybalt]] would kill thee,<br>But thou slewest [[Tybalt]]: there art thou happy.<br>The law that threatened [[Death|death]] becomes thy friend,<br>And turns it to [[Exile|exile]]: there art thou happy.<br>A pack of blessings light upon thy back,<br>Happiness courts thee in her best array,<br>But, like a mishavèd and sullen wench,<br>Thou pout’st upon thy fortune and thy [[Love|love]].<br>Take heed, take heed, for such die miserable.<br>Go, get thee to thy [[Love|love]], as was decreed.<br>Ascend her chamber; hence and comfort her.<br>But look thou stay not till the watch be set,<br>For then thou canst not pass to Mantua,<br>Where thou shalt live till we can find a [[Time|time]]<br>To blaze your [[Marriage|marriage]], reconcile your friends,<br>Beg pardon of the Prince, and call thee back<br>With twenty hundred thousand times more joy<br>Than thou went’st forth in lamentation.<br>Go before, [[Nurse]]. Commend me to thy lady,<br>And bid her hasten all the [[house]] to bed,<br>Which heavy sorrow makes them apt unto.<br>[[Romeo]] is coming. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O Lord, I could have stayed here all the [[Night|night]]<br>To hear good counsel! O, what learning is!<br>My lord, I’ll tell my lady you will come. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Do so, and bid my sweet prepare to chide. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Nurse]] offers to go in, and turns again |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | (giving the ring)<br>Here, sir, a ring she bid me give you, sir.<br>Hie you, make haste, for it grows very late. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | How [[Well|well]] my comfort is revived by this. Exit [[Nurse]] |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Go hence, good [[Night|night]], and here stands all your state.<br>Either be gone before the watch be set,<br>Or by the break of day disguised from hence.<br>Sojourn in Mantua. I’ll find out your man,<br>And he shall signify from [[Time|time]] to [[Time|time]]<br>Every good hap to you that chances here.<br>Give me thy hand. ’Tis late. Farewell. Good [[Night|night]]. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | But that a joy past joy calls out on me,<br>It were a grief so brief to part with thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Farewell. Exeunt severally |
