| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Friar Laurence]], with a basket |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | The grey-eyed morn smiles on the frowning [[Night|night]],<br>Chequ’ring the eastern clouds with streaks of light,<br>And fleckled darkness like a drunkard reels<br>From forth day’s path and Titan’s fiery wheels.<br>Now, ere the sun advance his burning eye<br>The day to cheer and [[Night|night]]’s dank dew to dry,<br>I must up-fill this osier cage of ours<br>With baleful weeds and precious-juicèd [[Flowers|flowers]].<br>The earth, that’s [[Nature|nature]]’s mother, is her tomb.<br>What is her burying [[Grave|grave]], that is her womb,<br>And from her womb children of divers kind<br>We sucking on her natural bosom find,<br>Many for many virtues excellent,<br>None but for some, and yet all different.<br>O mickle is the powerful grace that lies<br>In plants, herbs, stones, and their true qualities,<br>For naught so vile that on the earth doth live<br>But to the earth some special good doth give;<br>Nor aught so good but, strained from that fair use,<br>Revolts from true birth, stumbling on abuse.<br>Virtue itself turns vice being misapplied,<br>And vice sometime’s by action dignified. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Romeo]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Within the infant rind of this weak flower |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | [[Poison]] hath residence, and medicine power,<br>For this, being smelt, with that part cheers each part;<br>Being tasted, slays all senses with the heart.<br>Two such opposed kings encamp them still<br>In man as [[Well|well]] as herbs—grace and rude will;<br>And where the worser is predominant,<br>Full soon the canker [[Death|death]] eats up that plant. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Good morrow, father. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Benedicite.<br>What early tongue so sweet saluteth me?<br>Young son, it argues a distempered head<br>So soon to bid good morrow to thy bed.<br>Care keeps his watch in every old man’s eye,<br>And where care lodges, [[Sleep|sleep]] will never lie,<br>But where unbruisèd youth with unstuffed brain<br>Doth couch his limbs, there golden [[Sleep|sleep]] doth reign.<br>Therefore thy earliness doth me assure<br>Thou art uproused with some distemp’rature;<br>Or if not so, then here I hit it right:<br>Our [[Romeo]] hath not been in bed tonight. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | That last is true; the sweeter rest was mine. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | God pardon sin!—Wast thou with [[Rosaline]]? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | With [[Rosaline]], my ghostly father? No,<br>I have forgot that name and that name’s woe. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | That’s my good son; but where hast thou been then? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | I’ll tell thee ere thou ask it me again.<br>I have been feasting with mine enemy,<br>Where on a sudden one hath wounded me<br>That’s by me wounded. Both our remedies |
| *(didascalia)* | Within thy help and holy physic lies. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | I bear no hatred, blessed man, for lo,<br>My intercession likewise steads my foe. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Be plain, good son, and homely in thy drift.<br>Riddling confession finds but riddling shrift. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Then plainly know my heart’s dear [[Love|love]] is set<br>On the fair daughter of rich [[Capulet]].<br>As mine on hers, so hers is set on mine,<br>And all combined save what thou must combine<br>By holy [[Marriage|marriage]]. When and where and how<br>We met, we wooed, and made exchange of vow<br>I’ll tell thee as we pass; but this I pray,<br>That thou consent to marry us today. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Holy Saint Francis, what a change is here!<br>Is [[Rosaline]], that thou didst [[Love|love]] so dear,<br>So soon forsaken? Young men’s [[Love|love]] then lies<br>Not truly in their hearts, but in their eyes.<br>Jesu [[Maria]], what a deal of brine<br>Hath washed thy sallow cheeks for [[Rosaline]]!<br>How much salt [[Water|water]] thrown away in waste<br>To season [[Love|love]], that of it doth not taste!<br>The sun not yet thy sighs from heaven clears.<br>Thy old groans yet ring in mine ancient ears.<br>Lo, here upon thy cheek the stain doth sit<br>Of an old tear that is not washed off yet.<br>If e’er thou wast thyself, and these woes thine,<br>Thou and these woes were all for [[Rosaline]].<br>And art thou changed? Pronounce this sentence then:<br>Women may fall when there’s no strength in men. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Thou chidd’st me oft for loving [[Rosaline]]. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | For doting, not for loving, pupil mine. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | And bad’st me bury [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Not in a [[Grave|grave]]<br>To lay one in, another out to have. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | I pray thee, chide me not. Her I [[Love|love]] now<br>Doth grace for grace and [[Love|love]] for [[Love|love]] allow.<br>The other did not so. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | O, she knew [[Well|well]]<br>Thy [[Love|love]] did read by rote, that could not spell.<br>But come, young waverer, come, go with me.<br>In one respect I’ll thy assistant be;<br>For this alliance may so happy prove<br>To turn your households’ rancour to pure [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | O, let us hence! I stand on sudden haste. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Wisely and slow. They stumble that run fast. Exeunt |
