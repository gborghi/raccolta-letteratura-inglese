| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Romeo]] |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Can I go forward when my heart is here?<br>Turn back, dull earth, and find thy centre out. |
| *(didascalia)* | He turns back and withdraws. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Benvolio]] with [[Mercutio]] |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | (calling)<br>[[Romeo]], my cousin [[Romeo]], [[Romeo]]! |
| [[Mercutio|MERCUTIO]] | He is wise, and, on my life, hath stol’n him [[Home|home]] to bed. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | He ran this way, and leapt this orchard wall. Call, good [[Mercutio]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Mercutio|MERCUTIO]] Nay, I’ll conjure too. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | [[Romeo]]! Humours! adman! Passion! [[Lover]]!<br>Appear thou in the likeness of a sigh.<br>Speak but one rhyme and I am satisfied.<br>Cry but ‘Ay me!’ Pronounce but ‘[[Love|love]]’ and ‘dove’.<br>Speak to my gossip [[Venus]] one fair word,<br>One nickname for her purblind son and heir,<br>Young Adam Cupid, he that shot so trim<br>When [[King]] Cophetua loved the beggar maid.—<br>He heareth not, he stirreth not, he moveth not.<br>The ape is dead, and I must conjure him.—<br>I conjure thee by [[Rosaline]]’s bright eyes,<br>By her high forehead and her scarlet lip,<br>By her fine foot, straight leg, and quivering thigh,<br>And the demesnes that there adjacent lie,<br>That in thy likeness thou appear to us. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | An if he hear thee, thou wilt anger him. |
| [[Mercutio|MERCUTIO]] | This cannot anger him. ’Twould anger him<br>To raise a spirit in his mistress’ circle<br>Of some strange [[Nature|nature]], letting it there stand<br>Till she had laid it and conjured it down.<br>That were some spite. My invocation<br>Is fair and honest. In his mistress’ name,<br>I conjure only but to raise up him. |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | Come, he hath hid himself among these trees<br>To be consorted with the humorous [[Night|night]].<br>Blind is his [[Love|love]], and best befits the dark. |
| [[Mercutio|MERCUTIO]] | If [[Love|love]] be blind, [[Love|love]] cannot hit the mark.<br>Now will he sit under a medlar tree<br>And wish his mistress were that kind of fruit<br>As maids call medlars when they laugh [[Alone|alone]].<br>O [[Romeo]], that she were, O that she were<br>An open-arse, and thou a popp’rin’ pear.<br>[[Romeo]], good [[Night|night]]. I’ll to my truckle-bed.<br>This field-bed is too cold for me to [[Sleep|sleep]].<br>Come, shall we go? |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | Go then, for ’tis in vain<br>To seek him here that means not to be found. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Benvolio]] and [[Mercutio]] |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Romeo|ROMEO]] coming forward |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | He jests at scars that never felt a wound.<br>But soft, what light through yonder [[Window|window]] breaks?<br>It is the east, and [[Juliet]] is the sun.<br>Arise, fair sun, and kill the envious [[Moon|moon]],<br>Who is already sick and pale with grief<br>That thou, her maid, art far more fair than she.<br>Be not her maid, since she is envious.<br>Her vestal livery is but sick and green,<br>And none but fools do wear it; cast it off. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Juliet]] aloft |
| [[Benvolio|BENVOLIO]] | It is my lady, O, it is my [[Love|love]].<br>O that she knew she were!<br>She speaks, yet she says nothing. What of that?<br>Her eye discourses; I will answer it.<br>I am too bold. ’Tis not to me she speaks.<br>Two of the fairest [[Stars|stars]] in all the heaven,<br>Having some business, do entreat her eyes<br>To twinkle in their spheres till they return.<br>What if her eyes were there, they in her head?—<br>The brightness of her cheek would shame those [[Stars|stars]]<br>As daylight doth a lamp; her eye in heaven<br>Would through the airy region stream so bright<br>That [[Birds|birds]] would sing and think it were not [[Night|night]].<br>See how she leans her cheek upon her hand.<br>O, that I were a glove upon that hand,<br>That I might touch that cheek! |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Ay me. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | (aside) She speaks.<br>O, speak again, bright angel; for thou art<br>As glorious to this [[Night|night]], being o‘er my head,<br>As is a winged messenger of heaven<br>Unto the white upturned wond’ring eyes<br>Of mortals that fall back to gaze on him<br>When he bestrides the lazy-passing clouds<br>And sails upon the bosom of the air. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | (not knowing [[Romeo]] hears her)<br>O [[Romeo]], [[Romeo]], wherefore art thou [[Romeo]]?<br>Deny thy father and refuse thy name,<br>Or if thou wilt not, be but sworn my [[Love|love]],<br>And I’ll no longer be a [[Capulet]]. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | (aside)<br>Shall I hear more, or shall I speak at this? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | ’Tis but thy name that is my enemy.<br>Thou art thyself, though not a Montague.<br>What’s Montague? It is nor hand, nor foot,<br>Nor arm, nor face, nor any other part<br>Belonging to a man. O, be some other name I<br>What’s in a name? That which we call a [[Rose|rose]]<br>By any other word would smell as sweet.<br>So [[Romeo]] would, were he not [[Romeo]] called,<br>Retain that dear perfection which he owes<br>Without that title. [[Romeo]], doff thy name,<br>And for thy name—which is no part of thee—<br>Take all myself. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | (to [[Juliet]]) I take thee at thy word.<br>Call me but [[Love|love]] and I’ll be new baptized.<br>Henceforth I never will be [[Romeo]]. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | What man art thou that, thus bescreened in [[Night|night]],<br>So stumblest on my counsel? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | By a name<br>I know not how to tell thee who I am.<br>My name, dear saint, is hateful to myself<br>Because it is an enemy to thee.<br>Had I it written, I would tear the word. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | My ears have yet not drunk a hundred words<br>Of thy tongue’s uttering, yet I know the sound.<br>Art thou not [[Romeo]], and a Montague? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Neither, fair maid, if either thee dislike. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | How cam’st thou hither, tell me, and wherefore?<br>The orchard walls are high and hard to climb,<br>And the place [[Death|death]], considering who thou art,<br>If any of my kinsmen find thee here. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | With [[Love|love]]’s light wings did I o’erperch these walls,<br>For stony limits cannot hold [[Love|love]] out,<br>And what [[Love|love]] can do, that dares [[Love|love]] attempt.<br>Therefore thy kinsmen are no stop to me. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | If they do see thee, they will murder thee. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Alack, there lies more peril in thine eye<br>Than twenty of their swords. Look thou but sweet,<br>And I am proof against their enmity. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | I would not for the world they saw thee here. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | I have [[Night|night]]’s cloak to hide me from their eyes,<br>And but thou [[Love|love]] me, let them find me here.<br>My life were better ended by their hate<br>Than [[Death|death]] prorogued, wanting of thy [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | By whose direction found’st thou out this place? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | By [[Love|love]], that first did prompt me to enquire.<br>He lent me counsel, and I lent him eyes.<br>I am no pilot, yet wert thou as far<br>As that vast shore washed with the farthest sea,<br>I should adventure for such merchandise. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Thou knowest the mask of [[Night|night]] is on my face,<br>Else would a maiden blush bepaint my cheek<br>For that which thou hast heard me speak tonight.<br>Fain would I dwell on form, fain, fain deny<br>What I have spoke; but farewell, compliment.<br>Dost thou [[Love|love]] me? I know thou wilt say ‘Ay’,<br>And I will take thy word. Yet if thou swear‘st<br>Thou mayst prove false. At [[lovers]]’ perjuries,<br>They say, Jove laughs. O gentle [[Romeo]],<br>If thou dost [[Love|love]], pronounce it faithfully;<br>Or if thou think’st I am too quickly won,<br>I’ll frown, and be perverse, and say thee nay,<br>So thou wilt woo; but else, not for the world.<br>In [[Truth|truth]], fair Montague, I am too fond,<br>And therefore thou mayst think my ‘haviour light.<br>But trust me, gentleman, I’ll prove more true<br>Than those that have more cunning to be strange.<br>I should have been more strange, I must confess,<br>But that thou overheard’st, ere I was ware,<br>My true-[[Love|love]] passion. Therefore pardon me,<br>And not impute this yielding to light [[Love|love]],<br>Which the dark [[Night|night]] hath so discoverèd. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Lady, by yonder blessèd [[Moon|moon]] I vow,<br>That tips with silver all these fruit-tree tops— |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | O swear not by the [[Moon|moon]], th’inconstant [[Moon|moon]]<br>That monthly changes in her circled orb,<br>Lest that thy [[Love|love]] prove likewise variable. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | What shall I swear by? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Do not swear at all,<br>Or if thou wilt, swear by thy gracious [[Self|self]],<br>Which is the god of my idolatry,<br>And I’ll believe thee. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | If my heart’s dear [[Love|love]]— |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | [[Well]], do not swear. Although I joy in thee,<br>I have no joy of this contract tonight.<br>It is too rash, too unadvised, too sudden,<br>Too like the [[Lightning|lightning]] which doth cease to be<br>Ere one can say it lightens. Sweet, good [[Night|night]].<br>This bud of [[Love|love]] by [[Summer|summer]]’s ripening breath<br>May prove a beauteous flower when next we meet.<br>Good [[Night|night]], good [[Night|night]]. As sweet repose and rest<br>Come to thy heart as that within my breast. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | O, wilt thou leave me so unsatisfied? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | What satisfaction canst thou have tonight? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Th’exchange of thy [[Love|love]]’s faithful vow for mine. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | I gave thee mine before thou didst request it,<br>And yet I would it were to give again. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Wouldst thou withdraw it? For what purpose, [[Love|love]]? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | But to be frank and give it thee again.<br>And yet I wish but for the thing I have.<br>My bounty is as boundless as the sea,<br>My [[Love|love]] as deep. The more I give to thee<br>The more I have, for both are infinite.<br>[[Nurse]] calls within<br>I hear some noise within. Dear [[Love|love]], adieu.—<br>Anon, good [[Nurse]]!—Sweet Montague, be true.<br>Stay but a little; I will come again. Exit |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | O blessed, blessèd [[Night|night]]! I am afeard,<br>Being in [[Night|night]], all this is but [[A Dream|a dream]],<br>Too flattering-sweet to be substantial. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Juliet]] aloft |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Three words, dear [[Romeo]], and good [[Night|night]] indeed.<br>If that thy bent of [[Love|love]] be honourable,<br>Thy purpose [[Marriage|marriage]], send me word tomorrow,<br>By one that I’ll procure to come to thee,<br>Where and what [[Time|time]] thou wilt perform the rite,<br>And all my fortunes at thy foot I’ll lay,<br>And follow thee, my lord, throughout the world. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Nurse|NURSE]] (within) |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Madam! |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | I come, anon. (To [[Romeo]]) But if thou mean’st not [[Well|well]],<br>I do beseech thee— |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Nurse|NURSE]] (within) Madam! |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | By and by I come.—<br>To cease thy strife and leave me to my grief.<br>Tomorrow will I send. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | So thrive my [[Soul|soul]]— |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | A thousand times good [[Night|night]]. Exit |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | A thousand times the worse to want thy light.<br>[[Love]] goes toward [[Love|love]] as schoolboys from their books,<br>But [[Love|love]] from [[Love|love]], toward school with heavy looks. |
| *(didascalia)* | He is going. Enter [[Juliet]] aloft again |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Hist, [[Romeo]]! Hist! O for a falconer’s voice<br>To lure this tassel-gentle back again.<br>Bondage is hoarse, and may not speak aloud,<br>Else would I tear the [[Cave|cave]] where Echo lies,<br>And make her airy tongue more hoarse than mine<br>With repetition of my [[Romeo]]’s name. [[Romeo]]! |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | It is my [[Soul|soul]] that calls upon my name.<br>How silver-sweet sound [[lovers]]’ tongues by [[Night|night]],<br>Like softest music to attending ears! |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | [[Romeo]]! |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | My nyas? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | What o’[[Clock|clock]] tomorrow<br>Shall I send to thee? |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | By the hour of nine. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | I will not fail; ’tis twenty year till then.<br>I have forgot why I did call thee back. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | Let me stand here till thou remember it. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | I shall forget, to have thee still stand there,<br>Rememb’ring how I [[Love|love]] thy company. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | And I’ll still stay, to have thee still forget,<br>Forgetting any other [[Home|home]] but this. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | ’Tis almost morning. I would have thee gone—<br>And yet no farther than a wanton’s bird,<br>That lets it hop a little from his hand,<br>Like a poor prisoner in his twisted gyves,<br>And with a silk thread plucks it back again,<br>So loving-jealous of his liberty. |
| [[Romeo|ROMEO]] | I would I were thy bird. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Sweet, so would I.<br>Yet I should kill thee with much cherishing.<br>Good [[Night|night]], good [[Night|night]]. Parting is such sweet sorrow<br>That I shall say good [[Night|night]] till it be morrow. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Romeo|ROMEO]] |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | [[Sleep]] dwell upon thine eyes, peace in thy breast. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Juliet]] |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Would I were [[Sleep|sleep]] and peace, so sweet to rest.<br>Hence will I to my ghostly sire’s close cell,<br>His help to crave, and my dear hap to tell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
