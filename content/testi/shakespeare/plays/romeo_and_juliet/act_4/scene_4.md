| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]]’s Wife, and the [[Nurse]] With herbs |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Hold, take these keys, and fetch more spices, [[Nurse]]. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | They call for dates and quinces in the pastry. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]] |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Come, stir, stir, stir! The second cock hath crowed.<br>The curfew bell hath rung. ’Tis three o’[[Clock|clock]].<br>Look to the baked meats, good Angelica.<br>Spare not for cost. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Go, you cot-quean, go.<br>Get you to bed. [[Faith]], you’ll be sick tomorrow<br>For this [[Night|night]]’s watching. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | No, not a whit. What, I have watched ere now<br>All [[Night|night]] for lesser cause, and ne’er been sick. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Ay, you have been a mouse-hunt in your [[Time|time]],<br>But I will watch you from such watching now. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Capulet]]’s Wife and [[Nurse]] |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | A jealous-hood, a jealous-hood! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter three or four Servingmen, with spits and logs and baskets |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Now, fellow, what is there? |
| FIRST SERVINGMAN | Things for the cook, sir, but I know not what. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Make haste, make haste. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit First Servingman and one or two others |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Sirrah, fetch drier logs.<br>Call Peter. He will show thee where they are. |
| SECOND SERVINGMAN | I have a head, sir, that will find out logs<br>And never trouble Peter for the matter. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Mass, and [[Well|well]] said! A merry whoreson, ha!<br>Thou shalt be loggerhead. Exit Second Servingman<br><br>Good [[Faith|faith]], ’tis day.<br>The County will be here with music straight,<br>For so he said he would. |
| *(didascalia)* | Music plays within |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | I hear him near.<br>[[Nurse]]! Wife! What ho, what, [[Nurse]], I say! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Nurse]] |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Go waken [[Juliet]]. Go and trim her up.<br>I’ll go and chat with [[Paris]]. Hie, make haste,<br>Make haste, the bridegroom he is come already.<br>Make haste, I say. Exit |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Mistress, what, mistress! [[Juliet]]! Fast, I warrant her, she.<br>Why, [[Lamb|lamb]], why, lady! Fie, you slug-abed!<br>Why, [[Love|love]], I say, madam, sweetheart, why, bride!<br>What, not a word? You take your pennyworths now.<br>[[Sleep]] for a week, for the next [[Night|night]], I warrant,<br>The County [[Paris]] hath set up his rest<br>That you shall rest but little. God forgive me!<br>Marry, and amen. How sound is she asleep!<br>I needs must wake her. Madam, madam, madam!<br>Ay, let the County take you in your bed.<br>He’ll fright you up, i’[[Faith|faith]]. Will it not be? |
| *(didascalia)* | She draws back the curtains |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | What, dressed and in your clothes, and down again?<br>I must needs wake you. Lady, lady, lady!<br>Alas, alas! Help, help! My lady’s dead.<br>O welladay, that ever I was born!<br>Some aqua-vitae, ho! My lord, my lady! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]]’s Wife |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | What noise is here? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O lamentable day! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | What is the matter? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Look, look. O heavy day! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | O me, O me, my [[Child|child]], my only life!<br>Revive, look up, or I will die with thee.<br>Help, help, call help! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]] |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | For shame, bring [[Juliet]] forth. Her lord is come. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | She’s dead, deceased. She’s dead, alack the day! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Alack the day, she’s dead, she’s dead, she’s dead! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Ha, let me see her! Out, alas, she’s cold.<br>Her [[Blood|blood]] is settled, and her joints are stiff.<br>Life and these lips have long been separated.<br>[[Death]] lies on her like an untimely [[Frost|frost]]<br>Upon the sweetest flower of all the field. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O lamentable day! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | O woeful [[Time|time]]! |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | [[Death]], that hath ta’en her hence to make me wail,<br>Ties up my tongue, and will not let me speak. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Friar Laurence]] and [[Paris]], with Musicians |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Come, is the bride ready to go to church? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Ready to go, but never to return.<br>(To [[Paris]]) O son, the [[Night|night]] before thy wedding day<br>Hath [[Death|death]] lain with thy wife. See, there she lies,<br>Flower as she was, deflowered by him.<br>[[Death]] is my son-in-law, [[Death|death]] is my heir.<br>My daughter he hath wedded. I will die,<br>And leave him all. Life, living, all is [[Death|death]]’s. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Paris]], [[Capulet]] and his Wife, and the [[Nurse]] all at once wring their hands and cry out together: |
| [[Paris|PARIS]] | Have I thought long to see this morning’s face,<br>And doth it give me such a sight as this?<br>Beguiled, divorced, wronged, spited, slain!<br>Most detestable [[Death|death]], by thee beguiled,<br>By cruel, cruel thee quite overthrown.<br>O [[Love|love]], O life: not life, but [[Love|love]] in [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Accursed, unhappy, wretched, hateful day!<br>Most miserable hour that e’er [[Time|time]] saw<br>In lasting labour of his pilgrimage!<br>But one, poor one, one poor and loving [[Child|child]],<br>But one thing to rejoice and solace in,<br>And cruel [[Death|death]] hath catched it from my sight! |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O woe! O woeful, woeful, woeful day!<br>Most lamentable day! Most woeful day<br>That ever, ever, I did yet behold!<br>O day, O day, O day, O hateful day,<br>Never was seen so black a day as this I<br>O woeful day, O woeful day! 85 |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | Despised, distressed, hated, martyred, killed!<br>Uncomfortable [[Time|time]], why cam’st thou now<br>To murder, murder our solemnity?<br>O [[Child|child]], O [[Child|child]], my [[Soul|soul]] and not my [[Child|child]]!<br>Dead art thou, alack, my [[Child|child]] is dead,<br>And with my [[Child|child]] my joys are buried. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Peace, ho, for shame! Confusion’s cure lives not<br>In these confusions. Heaven and yourself<br>Had part in this fair maid. Now heaven hath all,<br>And all the better is it for the maid.<br>Your part in her you could not keep from [[Death|death]],<br>But heaven keeps his part in eternal life.<br>The most you sought was her promotion,<br>For ’twas your heaven she should be advanced,<br>And weep ye now, seeing she is advanced<br>Above the clouds as high as heaven itself?<br>O, in this [[Love|love]] you [[Love|love]] your [[Child|child]] so ill<br>That you run mad, seeing that she is [[Well|well]].<br>She’s not [[Well|well]] married that lives married long,<br>But she’s best married that dies married young.<br>Dry up your [[Tears|tears]], and stick your rosemary<br>On this fair corpse, and, as the custom is,<br>All in her best array bear her to church;<br>For though fond [[Nature|nature]] bids us all lament,<br>Yet [[Nature|nature]]’s [[Tears|tears]] are reason’s merriment. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]] | All things that we ordained festival<br>Turn from their office to black funeral.<br>Our instruments to [[Melancholy|melancholy]] [[Bells|bells]],<br>Our wedding cheer to a sad burial feast,<br>Our solemn hymns to sullen dirges change;<br>Our bridal [[Flowers|flowers]] serve for a buried corpse,<br>And all things change them to the contrary. |
| [[Friar Laurence|FRIAR LAURENCE]] | Sir, go you in; and madam, go with him,<br>And go, Sir [[Paris]]. Everyone prepare<br>To follow this fair corpse unto her [[Grave|grave]].<br>The heavens do lour upon you for some ill.<br>Move them no more by crossing their high will. |
| *(didascalia)* | They cast rosemary on [[Juliet]], and shut the curtains. Exeunt all but the [[Nurse]] and Musicians |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST MUSICIAN [[Faith]], we may put up our pipes and be gone. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Honest good fellows, ah, put up, put up,<br>For [[Well|well]] you know this is a pitiful case. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST MUSICIAN |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Ay, by my troth, the case may be amended. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Nurse]] Enter Peter |
| PETER | Musicians, O, musicians! ‘Heart’s ease’, ‘Heart’s ease’; O,an you will have me live, play ‘Heart’s ease’. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST MUSICIAN Why ‘Heart’s ease’? |
| PETER | O, musicians, because my heart itself plays ‘My heart is full of woe’. O, play me some merry dump to comfort me. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST MUSICIAN Not a dump, we. ’Tis no [[Time|time]] to play now. |
| PETER | You will not then? |
| FIRST MUSICIAN | No. |
| PETER | I will then give it you soundly. |
| FIRST MUSICIAN | What will you give us? |
| PETER | No money, on my [[Faith|faith]], but the gleek. I will give you the minstrel. |
| FIRST MUSICIAN | Then will I give you the serving-creature. |
| PETER | (drawing his dagger) Then will I lay the serving-creature’s dagger on your pate. I will carry no crochets. I’ll re you, I’ll fa you. Do you note me? |
| FIRST MUSICIAN | An you re us and fa us, you note us. |
| SECOND MUSICIAN | Pray you, put up your dagger and put out your wit. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Then have at you with my wit. I will dry-beat you with an iron wit, and put up my iron dagger. Answer me like men. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| SECOND MUSICIAN | When griping grief the heart doth wound,<br>And doleful dumps the mind oppress,<br>Then music with her silver sound—<br>Why ‘silver sound’, why ‘music with her silver sound’?<br>What say you, Matthew Minikin? |
| FIRST MUSICIAN | Marry, sir, because silver hath a sweet sound. |
| PETER | Prates! What say you, Hugh Rebec? |
| SECOND MUSICIAN | I say ’silver sound’ because musicians sound for silver. |
| PETER | Prates too! What say you, Simon Soundpost? |
| THIRD MUSICIAN | [[Faith]], I know not what to say. |
| PETER | O, I cry you [[Mercy|mercy]], you are the singer. I will say for you. It is ’music with her silver sound’ because musicians have no gold for sounding. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| PETER | Then music with her silver sound<br>With speedy help doth lend redress. Exit |
| FIRST MUSICIAN | What a pestilent knave is this same! |
| SECOND MUSICIAN | Hang him, jack! Come, we’ll in here, tarry for the mourners, and stay dinner. Exeunt |
