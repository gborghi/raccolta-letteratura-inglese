| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Juliet]] and the [[Nurse]] with garments |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Ay, those attires are best. But, gentle [[Nurse]],<br>I pray thee leave me to myself tonight,<br>For I have need of many orisons<br>To move the heavens to smile upon my state,<br>Which—[[Well|well]] thou knowest—is [[Cross|cross]] and full of sin. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]]’s Wife |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | What, are you busy, ho? Need you my help? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | No, madam, we have culled such necessaries<br>As are behoveful for our state tomorrow.<br>So please you, let me now be left [[Alone|alone]],<br>And let the [[Nurse]] this [[Night|night]] sit up with you,<br>For I am sure you have your hands full all<br>In this so sudden business. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Good [[Night|night]].<br>Get thee to bed, and rest, for thou hast need. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Capulet]]’s Wife and [[Nurse]] |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Farewell. God knows when we shall meet again.<br>I have a faint cold [[Fear|fear]] thrills through my veins<br>That almost freezes up the heat of life.<br>I’ll call them back again to comfort me.<br>[[Nurse]]!—What should she do here? |
| *(didascalia)* | She opens curtains, behind which is seen her bed |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | My dismal scene I needs must act [[Alone|alone]].<br>Come, vial. What if this mixture do not work at all?<br>Shall I be married then tomorrow morning?<br>No, no, this shall forbid it. Lie thou there.<br>She lays down a knife<br>What if it be a [[Poison|poison]] which the friar<br>Subtly hath ministered to have me dead,<br>Lest in this [[Marriage|marriage]] he should be dishonoured<br>Because he married me before to [[Romeo]]?<br>I [[Fear|fear]] it is—and yet methinks it should not,<br>For he hath still been tried a holy man.<br>How if, when I am laid into the tomb,<br>I wake before the [[Time|time]] that [[Romeo]]<br>Come to redeem me? There’s a fearful point.<br>Shall I not then be stifled in the vault,<br>To whose foul mouth no healthsome air breathes in,<br>And there die strangled ere my [[Romeo]] comes?<br>Or, if I live, is it not very like<br>The horrible conceit of [[Death|death]] and [[Night|night]],<br>Together with the terror of the place—<br>As in a vault, an ancient receptacle<br>Where for this many hundred years the bones<br>Of all my buried ancestors are packed;<br>Where bloody [[Tybalt]], yet but green in earth,<br>Lies fest‘ring in his shroud; where, as they say,<br>At some hours in the [[Night|night]] spirits resort—<br>Alack, alack, is it not like that I,<br>So early waking—what with loathsome smells,<br>And shrieks like mandrakes torn out of the earth,<br>That living mortals, hearing them, run mad—<br>O, if I wake, shall I not be distraught,<br>Environèd with all these hideous fears,<br>And madly play with my forefathers’ joints,<br>And pluck the mangled [[Tybalt]] from his shroud,<br>And, in this rage, with some great kinsman’s bone<br>As with a club dash out my desp’rate brains?<br>O, look! Methinks I see my cousin’s [[Ghost|ghost]]<br>Seeking out [[Romeo]] that did spit his [[Body|body]]<br>Upon a rapier’s point. Stay, [[Tybalt]], stay!<br>[[Romeo]], [[Romeo]], [[Romeo]]! Here’s drink. I drink to thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | She drinks from the vial and falls upon the bed, pulling closed the curtains |
