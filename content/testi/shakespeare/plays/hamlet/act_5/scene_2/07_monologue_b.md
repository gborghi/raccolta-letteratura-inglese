| *(didascalia)* | B. Just before the entrance of the [[Ghost]] in 1.4 (l. 18.1), Q2 has these additional lines continuing [[Hamlet]]’s speech: This heavy-headed revel east and west Makes us traduced and taxed of other nations. They clepe us drunkards, and with swinish phrase Soil our addition; and indeed it takes From our achievements, though performed at height, So, oft it chances in particular men That, for some vicious mole of [[Nature|nature]] in them— As in their birth, wherein they are not guilty, Since [[Nature|nature]] cannot choose his origin, By the o‘ergrowth of some complexion, Oft breaking down the pales and forts of reason, Or by some habit that too much o’erleavens The form of plausive manners—that these men, Carrying, I say, the stamp of one defect, Being [[Nature|nature]]’s livery or fortune’s star, His virtues else be they as pure as grace, As infinite as man may undergo, Shall in the general censure take corruption From that particular fault. The dram of evil Doth all the noble substance over-daub To his own scandal. |
