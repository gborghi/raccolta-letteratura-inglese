| *(didascalia)* | Enter two Clowns carrying a spade and a pickaxe |
| FIRST CLOWN | Is she to be buried in Christian burial that wilfully seeks her own salvation? |
| SECOND CLOWN | I tell thee she is, and therefore make her [[Grave|grave]] straight. The coroner hath sat on her, and finds it Christian burial. |
| FIRST CLOWN | How can that be unless she drowned herself in her own defence? |
| SECOND CLOWN | Why, ’tis found so. |
| FIRST CLOWN | It must be se offendendo, it cannot be else; for here lies the point: if I drown myself wittingly, it argues an act; and an act hath three branches: it is to act, to do, and to perform. Argal she drowned herself wittingly. |
| SECOND CLOWN | Nay, but hear you, Goodman Delver. |
| FIRST CLOWN | Give me leave. Here lies the [[Water|water]]—good. Here stands the man—good. If the man go to this [[Water|water]] and drown himself, it is, will he nill he, he goes. Mark you that. But if the [[Water|water]] come to him and drown him, he drowns not himself; argal he that is not guilty of his own [[Death|death]] shortens not his own life. |
| SECOND CLOWN | But is this law? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Ay, marry, is’t: coroner’s [[Quest|quest]] law. |
| SECOND CLOWN | Will you ha’ the [[Truth|truth]] on’t? If this had not been a gentlewoman, she should have been buried out o’ Christian burial. |
| FIRST CLOWN | Why, there thou sayst, and the more pity that great folk should have count’nance in this world to drown or hang themselves more than their even Christian. Come, my spade. There is no ancient gentlemen but gardeners, ditchers, and gravemakers; they hold up Adam’s profession. |
| *(didascalia)* | First Clown digs |
| SECOND CLOWN | Was he a gentleman? |
| FIRST CLOWN | A was the first that ever bore arms. |
| SECOND CLOWN | Why, he had none. |
| FIRST CLOWN | What, art a heathen? How dost thou understand the Scripture? The Scripture says Adam digged. Could he dig without arms? I’ll put another question to thee. If thou answerest me not to the purpose, confess thyself— |
| SECOND CLOWN | Go to. |
| FIRST CLOWN | What is he that builds stronger than either the mason, the shipwright, or the carpenter? |
| SECOND CLOWN | The gallows-maker; for that frame outlives a thousand tenants. |
| FIRST CLOWN | I like thy wit [[Well|well]], in good [[Faith|faith]]. The gallows does [[Well|well]]. But how does it [[Well|well]]? It does [[Well|well]] to those that do ill. Now thou dost ill to say the gallows is built stronger than the church, argal the gallows may do [[Well|well]] to thee. To’t again, come. |
| SECOND CLOWN | ‘Who builds stronger than a mason, a shipwright, or a carpenter?’ |
| FIRST CLOWN | Ay, tell me that, and unyoke. |
| SECOND CLOWN | Marry, now I can tell. |
| FIRST CLOWN | To’t. |
| SECOND CLOWN | Mass, I cannot tell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Prince [[Hamlet]] and [[Horatio]] afar off |
| FIRST CLOWN | Cudgel thy brains no more about it, for your dull ass will not mend his pace with beating; and when you are asked this question next, say ‘a [[Grave|grave]]-maker’; the houses that he makes lasts till doomsday. Go, get thee to Johan. Fetch me a stoup of liquor. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Second Clown |
| FIRST CLOWN | (Sings)<br>In youth when I did [[Love|love]], did [[Love|love]],<br>Methought it was very sweet<br>To contract-O-the [[Time|time]] for-a-my behove,<br>O methought there-a-was nothing-a-meet. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Has this fellow no feeling of his business that a sings at [[Grave|grave]]-making? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Custom hath made it in him a property of easiness. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | ‘Tis e’en so; the hand of little employment hath the daintier sense. |
| FIRST CLOWN | (sings)<br>But age with his stealing steps<br>Hath caught me in his clutch,<br>And hath shipped me intil the land,<br>As if I had never been such. |
| *(didascalia)* | He throws up a skull |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | That skull had a tongue in it and could sing once. How the knave jowls it to th’ ground as if ‘twere [[Cain]]’s jawbone, that did the first murder! This might be the pate of a politician which this ass o’er-offices, one that would circumvent God, might it not? [[Horatio|HORATIO]] It might, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Or of a courtier, which could say ‘Good morrow, sweet lord. How dost thou, good lord?’ This might be my lord such a one, that praised my lord such a one’s [[Horse|horse]] when a meant to beg it, might it not? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Ay, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why, e‘en so, and now my lady Worm’s, chapless, and knocked about the mazard with a sexton’s spade. Here’s fine revolution, an we had the trick to see’t. Did these bones cost no more the breeding but to play at loggats with ’em? Mine ache to think on’t. |
| FIRST CLOWN | (sings)<br>A pickaxe and a spade, a spade,<br>For and a shrouding-sheet;<br>O, a pit of clay for to be made<br>For such a guest is meet. |
| *(didascalia)* | He throws up another skull |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | There’s another. Why might not that be the skull of a lawyer? Where be his quiddits now, his quillets, his cases, his tenures, and his tricks? Why does he suffer this rude knave now to knock him about the sconce with a dirty shovel, and will not tell him of his action of battery? H‘m! This fellow might be in ’s [[Time|time]] a great buyer of land, with his statutes, his recognizances, his fines, his [[Double|double]] vouchers, his recoveries. Is this the fine of his fines and the recovery of his recoveries, to have his fine pate full of fine dirt? Will his vouchers vouch him no more of his purchases, and [[Double|double]] ones too, than the length and breadth of a pair of indentures? The very conveyances of his lands will hardly lie in this box; and must th’inheritor himself have no more, ha? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Not a jot more, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Is not parchment made of sheepskins? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Ay, my lord, and of calf-skins too. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | They are sheep and calves that seek out assurance in that. I will speak to this fellow. (To the First Clown) Whose [[Grave|grave]]’s this, sirrah? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Mine, sir.<br>(Sings)<br>O, a pit of clay for to be made<br>For such a guest is meet. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I think it be thine indeed, for thou liest in’t. |
| FIRST CLOWN | You lie out on‘t, sir, and therefore it is not yours. For my part, I do not lie in’t, and yet it is mine. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Thou dost lie in‘t, to be in’t and say ’tis thine. ’Tis for the dead, not for the quick; therefore thou liest. |
| FIRST CLOWN | ‘Tis a quick lie, sir, ’twill away again from me to you. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | What man dost thou dig it for? |
| FIRST CLOWN | For no man, sir. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | What woman, then? |
| FIRST CLOWN | For none, neither. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Who is to be buried in’t? |
| FIRST CLOWN | One that was a woman, sir; but, rest her [[Soul|soul]], she’s dead. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How absolute the knave is! We must speak by the card, or equivocation will undo us. By the Lord, [[Horatio]], these three years I have taken note of it. The age is grown so picked that the toe of the peasant comes so near the heel of the courtier he galls his kibe. (To the First Clown) How long hast thou been a [[Grave|grave]]-maker? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Of all the days i‘th’ year I came to’t that day that our last [[King]] [[Hamlet]] o’ercame Fortinbras. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How long is that since? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Cannot you tell that? Every [[Fool|fool]] can tell that. It was the very day that young [[Hamlet]] was born—he that was mad and sent into England. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Ay, marry, why was he sent into England? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Why, because a was mad. A shall recover his wits there; or if a do not, ’tis no great matter there. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why? |
| FIRST CLOWN | ’Twill not be seen in him there. There the men are as mad as he. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How came he mad? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Very strangely, they say. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How strangely? |
| FIRST CLOWN | [[Faith]], e’en with losing his wits. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Upon what ground? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Why, here in [[Denmark]]. I have been sexton here, man and boy, thirty years. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How long will a man lie i’th’ earth ere he rot? |
| FIRST CLOWN | I’[[Faith|faith]], if a be not rotten before a die—as we have many pocky corpses nowadays, that will scarce hold the laying in—a will last you some eight year or nine year. A tanner will last you nine year. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why he more than another? |
| FIRST CLOWN | Why, sir, his hide is so tanned with his trade that a will keep out [[Water|water]] a great while, and your [[Water|water]] is a sore decayer of your whoreson dead [[Body|body]]. Here’s a skull, now. This skull has lain in the earth three-and-twenty years. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Whose was it? |
| FIRST CLOWN | A whoreson mad fellow’s it was. Whose do you think it was? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Nay, I know not. |
| FIRST CLOWN | A pestilence on him for a mad rogue—a poured a flagon of Rhenish on my head once! This same skull, sir, was Yorick’s skull, the [[King]]’s jester. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | This? |
| FIRST CLOWN | E’en that. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Let me see.<br>He takes the skull<br>Alas, poor Yorick. I knew him, [[Horatio]]—a fellow of infinite jest, of most excellent fancy. He hath borne me on his back a thousand times; and now, how abhorred my [[Imagination|imagination]] is! My gorge rises at it. Here hung those lips that I have kissed I know not how oft. Where be your gibes now, your gambols, your [[songs]], your flashes of merriment that were wont to set the table on a roar? Not one now to mock your own grinning? Quite chop-fallen? Now get you to my lady’s chamber and tell her, let her paint an inch thick, to this favour she must come. Make her laugh at that. Prithee, [[Horatio]], tell me one thing. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | What’s that, my lord? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Dost thou think Alexander looked o’ this fashion i’th’ earth? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | E’en so. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | And smelt so? Pah! |
| *(didascalia)* | He throws the skull down |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | E’en so, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | To what base uses we may return, [[Horatio]]! Why may not [[Imagination|imagination]] trace the noble [[Dust|dust]] of Alexander till a find it stopping a bung-hole? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | ’Twere to consider too curiously to consider so. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | No, [[Faith|faith]], not a jot; but to follow him thither with modesty enough, and likelihood to lead it, as thus: Alexander died, Alexander was buried, Alexander returneth into [[Dust|dust]], the [[Dust|dust]] is earth, of earth we make loam, and why of that loam whereto he was converted might they not stop a beer-barrel?<br>Imperial Caesar, dead and turned to clay,<br>Might stop a hole to keep the [[Wind|wind]] away.<br>O, that that earth which kept the world in awe<br>Should patch a wall t’expel the [[Winter|winter]]’s flaw!<br>But soft, but soft; aside.<br>[[Hamlet]] and [[Horatio]] stand aside. Enter [[King]]<br><br>[[Claudius]], [[Queen]] [[Gertrude]], [[Laertes]], and a coffin,<br><br>with a Priest and lords attendant<br>Here comes the [[King]],<br>The [[Queen]], the courtiers—who is that they follow,<br>And with such maimed rites? This doth betoken<br>The corpse they follow did with desp‘rate hand<br>Fordo it own life. ’Twas of some estate.<br>Couch we a while, and mark. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | What ceremony else? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (aside to [[Horatio]])<br>That is [[Laertes]], a very noble youth. Mark. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | What ceremony else? |
| PRIEST | Her obsequies have been as far enlarged<br>As we have warrantise. Her [[Death|death]] was doubtful,<br>And but that great command o’ersways the order<br>She should in ground unsanctified have lodged<br>Till the last trumpet. For charitable prayers,<br>Shards, flints, and pebbles should be thrown on her,<br>Yet here she is allowed her virgin rites,<br>Her maiden strewments, and the bringing [[Home|home]]<br>Of bell and burial. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | Must there no more be done? |
| PRIEST | No more be done.<br>We should profane the service of the dead<br>To sing sage requiem and such rest to her<br>As to peace-parted souls. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | Lay her i’th’ earth,<br>And from her fair and unpolluted flesh<br>May violets [[spring]]. I tell thee, churlish priest,<br>A minist’ring angel shall my sister be<br>When thou liest howling. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (aside) What, the fair [[Ophelia]]! |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | (scattering [[Flowers|flowers]])<br>Sweets to the sweet. Farewell.<br>I hoped thou shouldst have been my [[Hamlet]]’s wife.<br>I thought thy bride-bed to have decked, sweet maid,<br>And not t’have strewed thy [[Grave|grave]]. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | O, treble woe<br>Fall ten times treble on that cursed head<br>Whose wicked deed thy most ingenious sense<br>Deprived thee of!—Hold off the earth a while,<br>Till I have caught her once more in mine arms.<br>He leaps into the [[Grave|grave]]<br>Now pile your [[Dust|dust]] upon the quick and dead<br>Till of this flat a mountain you have made<br>To o’ertop old Pelion, or the skyish head<br>Of blue Olympus. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (coming forward) What is he whose grief<br>Bears such an emphasis, whose phrase of sorrow<br>Conjures the wand’ring [[Stars|stars]] and makes them stand<br>Like [[Wonder|wonder]]-wounded hearers? This is I,<br>[[Hamlet]] the Dane. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Hamlet]] leaps in after [[Laertes]] |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | The devil take thy [[Soul|soul]]. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Thou pray’st not [[Well|well]].<br>I prithee take thy fingers from my throat,<br>For though I am not splenative and rash,<br>Yet have I something in me dangerous,<br>Which let thy wiseness [[Fear|fear]]. Away thy hand. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | (to Lords)<br>Pluck them asunder. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | [[Hamlet]], [[Hamlet]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | ALL THE LORDS |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | Gentlemen! |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | (to [[Hamlet]]) Good my lord, be quiet. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why, I will fight with him upon this theme<br>Until my eyelids will no longer wag. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | O my son, what theme? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I loved [[Ophelia]]. Forty thousand brothers<br>Could not, with all their quantity of [[Love|love]],<br>Make up my sum.—What wilt thou do for her? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | O, he is mad, [[Laertes]]. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | (to [[Laertes]]) For [[Love|love]] of God, forbear him. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (to [[Laertes]]) ‘Swounds, show me what thou’lt do.<br>Woot weep, woot fight, woot fast, woot tear thyself,<br>Woot drink up eisel, eat a crocodile?<br>I’ll do’t. Dost thou come here to whine,<br>To outface me with leaping in her [[Grave|grave]]?<br>Be buried quick with her, and so will I.<br>And if thou prate of [[Mountains|mountains]], let them throw<br>Millions of acres on us, till our ground,<br>Singeing his pate against the burning zone,<br>Make Ossa like a wart. Nay, an thou’lt mouth,<br>I’ll rant as [[Well|well]] as thou. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] to [[Laertes]] This is mere [[Madness|madness]], |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | And thus a while the fit will work on him.<br>Anon, as patient as the female dove<br>When that her golden couplets are disclosed,<br>His [[Silence|silence]] will sit drooping. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (to [[Laertes]])<br>Hear you, sir,<br>What is the reason that you use me thus?<br>I loved you ever. But it is no matter.<br>Let Hercules himself do what he may,<br>The cat will mew, and dog will have his day. Exit |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | I pray you, good [[Horatio]], wait upon him. Exit [[Horatio]]<br><br>(To [[Laertes]]).Strengthen your patience in our last<br>[[Night|night]]’s speech.<br>We’ll put the matter to the present push.—<br>Good [[Gertrude]], set some watch over your son.—<br>This [[Grave|grave]] shall have a living monument.<br>An hour of quiet shortly shall we see;<br>Till then, in patience our proceeding be. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
