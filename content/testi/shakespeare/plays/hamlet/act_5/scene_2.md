| *(didascalia)* | Enter Prince [[Hamlet]] and [[Horatio]] |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | So much for this, sir. Now, let me see, the other.<br>You do remember all the circumstance? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Remember it, my lord! |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Sir, in my heart there was a kind of fighting<br>That would not let me [[Sleep|sleep]]. Methought I lay<br>Worse than the mutines in the bilboes. Rashly—<br>And praised be rashness for it: let us know<br>Our indiscretion sometime serves us [[Well|well]]<br>When our dear plots do pall, and that should teach us<br>There’s a divinity that shapes our ends,<br>Rough-hew them how we will— |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | That is most certain. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Up from my cabin,<br>My sea-gown scarfed about me in the dark,<br>Groped I to find out them, had my [[Desire|desire]],<br>Fingered their packet, and in fine withdrew<br>To mine own room again, making so bold,<br>My fears forgetting manners, to unseal<br>Their grand commission; where I found, [[Horatio]]—<br>O royal knavery!—an exact command,<br>Larded with many several sorts of reasons<br>Importing [[Denmark]]’s health, and England’s, too,<br>With ho! such bugs and goblins in my life,<br>That on the supervise, no leisure bated,<br>No, not to stay the grinding of the axe,<br>My head should be struck off. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Is’t possible? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (giving it to him)<br>Here’s the commission. Read it at more leisure.<br>But wilt thou hear me how I did proceed? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | I beseech you. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Being thus benetted round with villainies—<br>Ere I could make a prologue to my brains,<br>They had begun the play—sat me down,<br>Devised a new commission, wrote it fair.<br>I once did hold it, as our statists do,<br>A baseness to write fair, and laboured much<br>How to forget that learning; but, sir, now<br>It did me yeoman’s service. Wilt thou know<br>Th’effect of what I wrote? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Ay, good my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | An earnest conjuration from the [[King]],<br>As England was his faithful tributary,<br>As [[Love|love]] between them like the palm should flourish,<br>As peace should still her wheaten garland wear<br>And stand a comma ‘tween their amities,<br>And many such like ‘as’es of great charge,<br>That on the view and know of these contents,<br>Without debatement further more or less,<br>He should the bearers put to sudden [[Death|death]],<br>Not shriving-[[Time|time]] allowed. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | How was this sealed? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why, even in that was heaven ordinant.<br>I had my father’s signet in my purse,<br>Which was the model of that Danish seal;<br>Folded the writ up in the form of th‘other,<br>Subscribed it, gave’t th’impression, placed it safely,<br>The changeling never known. Now the next day<br>Was our sea-fight; and what to this was sequent<br>Thou know’st already. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | So [[Guildenstern]] and [[Rosencrantz]] go to’t. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why, man, they did make [[Love|love]] to this employment.<br>They are not near my [[Conscience|conscience]]. Their defeat<br>Doth by their own insinuation grow.<br>’Tis dangerous when the baser [[Nature|nature]] comes<br>Between the pass and fell incensed points<br>Of mighty opposites. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Why, what a [[King|king]] is this! |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Does it not, think‘st thee, stand me now upon—<br>He that hath killed my [[King|king]] and whored my mother,<br>Popped in between th’election and my hopes,<br>Thrown out his angle for my proper life,<br>And with such coz’nage—is’t not perfect [[Conscience|conscience]]<br>To quit him with this arm? And is’t not to be damned<br>To let this canker of our [[Nature|nature]] come<br>In further evil? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | It must be shortly known to him from England<br>What is the issue of the business there. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | It will be short. The interim’s mine,<br>And a man’s life’s no more than to say ‘one’.<br>But I am very sorry, good [[Horatio]],<br>That to [[Laertes]] I forgot myself;<br>For by the image of my cause I see<br>The portraiture of his. I’ll [[Court|court]] his favours.<br>But sure, the bravery of his grief did put me<br>Into a tow’ring passion. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Peace, who comes here? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter young Osric, a courtier, taking off his hat |
| OSRIC | Your lordship is right welcome back to [[Denmark]]. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I humbly thank you, sir. (To [[Horatio]]) Dost know this [[Water|water]]-fly? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | No, my good lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Thy state is the more gracious, for ‘tis a vice to know him. He hath much land, and fertile. Let a beast be lord of beasts, and his crib shall stand at the [[King|king]]’s mess. ’Tis a chuff, but, as I say, spacious in the possession of dirt. |
| OSRIC | Sweet lord, if your [[Friendship|friendship]] were at leisure I should impart a thing to you from his majesty. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I will receive it, sir, with all diligence of spirit.<br>Put your bonnet to his right use; ’tis for the head. |
| OSRIC | I thank your lordship, ’tis very hot. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | No, believe me, ’tis very cold. The [[Wind|wind]] is northerly. |
| OSRIC | It is indifferent cold, my lord, indeed. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Methinks it is very sultry and hot for my complexion. |
| OSRIC | Exceedingly, my lord. It is very sultry, as ’twere—I cannot tell how. But, my lord, his majesty bade me signify to you that a has laid a great wager on your head. Sir, this is the matter. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I beseech you, remember. |
| OSRIC | Nay, good my lord, for mine ease, in good [[Faith|faith]]. Sir, you are not ignorant of what excellence [[Laertes]] is at his weapon. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | What’s his weapon? |
| OSRIC | Rapier and dagger. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | That’s two of his weapons. But [[Well|well]]. |
| OSRIC | The [[King]], sir, hath wagered with him six Barbary horses, against the which he imponed, as I take it, six French rapiers and poniards, with their assigns as girdle, hanger, or so. Three of the carriages, in [[Faith|faith]], are very dear to fancy, very responsive to the hilts, most delicate carriages, and of very liberal conceit. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | What call you the carriages? |
| OSRIC | The carriages, sir, are the hangers. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | The phrase would be more germane to the matter if we could carry cannon by our sides. I would it might be hangers till then. But on: six Barbary horses against six French swords, their assigns, and three liberal-conceited carriages—that’s the French bet against the Danish. Why is this ‘imponed’, as you call it? |
| OSRIC | The [[King]], sir, hath laid, sir, that in a dozen passes between you and him he shall not exceed you three hits. He hath on’t twelve for nine, and it would come to immediate trial if your lordship would vouchsafe the answer. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How if I answer no? |
| OSRIC | I mean, my lord, the opposition of your person in trial. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Sir, I will walk here in the hall. If it please his majesty, ‘tis the breathing [[Time|time]] of day with me. Let the foils be brought; the gentleman willing, an the [[King]] hold his purpose, I will win for him an I can. If not, I’ll gain nothing but my shame and the odd hits. OSRIC Shall I re-deliver you e’en so? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | To this effect, sir; after what flourish your [[Nature|nature]] will. |
| OSRIC | I commend my duty to your lordship. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Yours, yours. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Osric |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | He does [[Well|well]] to commend it himself; there are no tongues else for ’s turn. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | This lapwing runs away with the shell on his head. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | A did comply with his dug before a sucked it. Thus has he—and many more of the same bevy that I know the drossy age dotes on—only got the tune of the [[Time|time]] and outward habit of encounter, a kind of yeasty collection which carries them through and through the most fanned and winnowed opinions; and do but blow them to their trial, the bubbles are out. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | You will lose this wager, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I do not think so. Since he went into France, I have been in continual practice. I shall win at the odds. But thou wouldst not think how all here about my heart—but it is no matter. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Nay, good my lord— |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | It is but foolery, but it is such a kind of gain-giving as would perhaps trouble a woman. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | If your mind dislike anything, obey it. I will forestall their repair hither, and say you are not fit. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Not a whit. We defy augury. There’s a special [[Providence|providence]] in the fall of a sparrow. If it be now, ’tis not to come. If it be not to come, it will be now. If it be not now, yet it will come. The readiness is all. Since no man has aught of what he [[Leaves|leaves]], what is’t to leave betimes? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] [[Claudius]], [[Queen]] [[Gertrude]], [[Laertes]], and |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | lords, with Osric and other attendants with |
| *(didascalia)* | trumpets, drums, cushions, foils, and gauntlets; a |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | table, and flagons of wine on it |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | Come, [[Hamlet]], come, and take this hand from me. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (to [[Laertes]])<br>Give me your pardon, sir. I’ve done you wrong;<br>But pardon’t as you are a gentleman.<br>This presence knows,<br>And you must needs have heard, how I am punished<br>With sore distraction. What I have done<br>That might your [[Nature|nature]], [[Honour|honour]], and exception<br>Roughly awake, I here proclaim was [[Madness|madness]].<br>Was’t [[Hamlet]] wronged [[Laertes]]? Never [[Hamlet]].<br>If [[Hamlet]] from himself be ta‘en away,<br>And when he’s not himself does wrong [[Laertes]],<br>Then [[Hamlet]] does it not, [[Hamlet]] denies it.<br>Who does it then? His [[Madness|madness]]. If’t be so,<br>[[Hamlet]] is of the faction that is wronged.<br>His [[Madness|madness]] is poor [[Hamlet]]’s enemy.<br>Sir, in this audience<br>Let my disclaiming from a purposed evil<br>Free me so far in your most generous thoughts<br>That I have shot mine arrow o’er the [[house]]<br>And hurt my brother. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | I am satisfied in [[Nature|nature]],<br>Whose motive in this case should stir me most<br>To my [[Revenge|revenge]]. But in my terms of [[Honour|honour]]<br>I stand aloof, and will no reconcilement<br>Till by some elder masters of known [[Honour|honour]]<br>I have a voice and precedent of peace<br>To keep my name ungored; but till that [[Time|time]]<br>I do receive your offered [[Love|love]] like [[Love|love]],<br>And will not wrong it. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I do embrace it freely,<br>And will this brothers’ wager frankly play.—<br>(To attendants) Give us the foils. Come on. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | (to attendants)<br>Come, one for me. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I’ll be your foil, [[Laertes]]. In mine ignorance<br>Your skill shall, like a star i’th’ darkest [[Night|night]],<br>Stick fiery off indeed. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | You mock me, sir. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | No, by this hand. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | Give them the foils, young Osric. Cousin [[Hamlet]],<br>You know the wager? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Very [[Well|well]], my lord.<br>Your grace hath laid the odds o’th’ weaker side. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | I do not [[Fear|fear]] it; I have seen you both.<br>But since he is bettered, we have therefore odds. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | (taking a foil)<br>This is too heavy; let me see another. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (taking a foil)<br>This likes me [[Well|well]]. These foils have all a length? |
| OSRIC | Ay, my good lord.<br>[[Hamlet]] and [[Laertes]] prepare to play |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | (to attendants)<br>Set me the stoups of wine upon that table.<br>If [[Hamlet]] give the first or second hit,<br>Or quit in answer of the third exchange,<br>Let all the battlements their ordnance [[Fire|fire]].<br>The [[King]] shall drink to [[Hamlet]]’s better breath,<br>And in the cup an [[Union|union]] shall he throw<br>Richer than that which four successive kings<br>In [[Denmark]]’s [[Crown|crown]] have worn. Give me the cups,<br>And let the kettle to the trumpet speak,<br>The trumpet to the cannoneer without,<br>The cannons to the heavens, the heaven to earth,<br><br>‘Now the [[King]] drinks to [[Hamlet]]’. |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets the while he drinks |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | Come, begin.<br>And you, the judges, bear a wary eye. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (to [[Laertes]]) Come on, sir. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | Come, my lord.<br>They play |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | One. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | No. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (to Osric) Judgement. |
| OSRIC | A hit, a very palpable hit. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | [[Well]], again. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | Stay. Give me drink. [[Hamlet]], this pearl is thine.<br>Here’s to thy health.— |
| *(didascalia)* | Drum and trumpets sound, and shot goes off Give him the cup. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I’ll play this bout first. Set it by a while.—<br>Come.<br>They play again<br>Another hit. What say you? |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | A touch, a touch, I do confess. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | Our son shall win. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | He’s fat and scant of breath.—<br>Here, [[Hamlet]], take my napkin. Rub thy brows.<br>The [[Queen]] carouses to thy fortune, [[Hamlet]]. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Good madam. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | [[Gertrude]], do not drink. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | I will, my lord, I pray you pardon me.<br>She drinks, then offers the cup to [[Hamlet]] |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | (aside)<br>It is the poisoned cup; it is too late. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I dare not drink yet, madam; by and by. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | (to [[Hamlet]]) Come, let me wipe thy face. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | (aside to [[Claudius]]) My lord, I’ll hit him now. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | (aside to [[Laertes]]) I do not think’t. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | (aside)<br>And yet ‘tis almost ’gainst my [[Conscience|conscience]]. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Come for the third, [[Laertes]], you but dally.<br>I pray you pass with your best violence.<br>I am afeard you make a wanton of me. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | Say you so? Come on.<br>They play |
| OSRIC | Nothing neither way. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | (to [[Hamlet]])<br>Have at you now! |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Laertes]] wounds [[Hamlet]]. In scuffling, they change |
| *(didascalia)* | rapiers, and [[Hamlet]] wounds [[Laertes]] |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | (to attendants)<br>Part them, they are incensed. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | (to [[Laertes]])<br>Nay, come again. |
| *(didascalia)* | The [[Queen]] falls down |
| OSRIC | Look to the [[Queen]] there, ho! |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | They bleed on both sides. (To [[Hamlet]]) How is’t, my lord? |
| OSRIC | How is’t, [[Laertes]]? |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | Why, as a woodcock to mine own springe, Osric.<br><br>I am justly killed with mine own treachery. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How does the [[Queen]]? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | She swoons to see them bleed. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] [[Gertrude|GERTRUDE]] | No, no, the drink, the drink! O my dear [[Hamlet]], |
| *(didascalia)* | The drink, the drink—I am poisoned. She dies |
| *(didascalia)* | O villainy! Ho! Let the door be locked! Exit Osric |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Treachery, seek it out. |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | It is here, [[Hamlet]]. [[Hamlet]], thou art slain.<br>No med’cine in the world can do thee good.<br>In thee there is not half an hour of life.<br>The treacherous instrument is in thy hand,<br>Unbated and envenomed. The foul practice<br>Hath turned itself on me. Lo, here I lie,<br>Never to rise again. Thy mother’s poisoned.<br>I can no more. The [[King]], the [[King]]’s to blame. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | The point envenomed too? Then, venom, to thy work.<br>He hurts [[King]] [[Claudius]] |
| ALL THE COURTIERS | Treason, treason! |
| [[King|KING]] [[Claudius|CLAUDIUS]] | O yet defend me, friends! I am but hurt. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Here, thou incestuous, murd’rous, damned Dane,<br>Drink off this potion. Is thy [[Union|union]] here?<br>Follow my mother. [[King]] [[Claudius]] dies |
| [[Laertes|LAERTES]] | He is justly served.<br>It is a [[Poison|poison]] tempered by himself.<br>Exchange [[Forgiveness|forgiveness]] with me, noble [[Hamlet]].<br>Mine and my father’s [[Death|death]] come not upon thee,<br>Nor thine on me. He dies |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Heaven make thee free of it! I follow thee.<br>I am dead, [[Horatio]]. Wretched [[Queen]], adieu!<br>You that look pale and tremble at this chance,<br>That are but mutes or audience to this act,<br>Had I but [[Time|time]]—as this fell sergeant [[Death]]<br>Is strict in his arrest—O, I could tell you—<br>But let it be. [[Horatio]], I am dead,<br>Thou liv’st. Report me and my cause aright<br>To the unsatisfied. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Never believe it.<br>I am more an antique Roman than a Dane.<br>Here’s yet some liquor left. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | As thou’rt a man,<br>Give me the cup. Let go. By heaven, I’ll ha’t.<br>O God, [[Horatio]], what a wounded name,<br>Things standing thus unknown, shall live behind me!<br>If thou didst ever hold me in thy heart,<br>Absent thee from felicity a while,<br>And in this harsh world draw thy breath in [[Pain|pain]]<br>To tell my story.<br>March afar off, and shout within<br>What warlike noise is this? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Osric |
| OSRIC | Young Fortinbras, with conquest come from Poland,<br>To th’ambassadors of England gives<br>This warlike volley. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | O, I die, [[Horatio]]!<br>The potent [[Poison|poison]] quite o‘ercrows my spirit.<br>I cannot live to hear the news from England,<br>But I do prophesy th’election lights<br>On Fortinbras. He has my dying voice.<br>So tell him, with th’occurrents, more and less,<br>Which have solicited. The rest is [[Silence|silence]].<br>O, O, O, O!<br>He dies |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Now cracks a noble heart. Good [[Night|night]], sweet prince,<br>And flights of angels sing thee to thy rest.—<br>Why does the drum come hither? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Fortinbras with the English Ambassadors, with a drummer, colours, and attendants |
| FORTINBRAS | Where is this sight? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | What is it ye would see?<br>If aught of woe or [[Wonder|wonder]], cease your search. |
| FORTINBRAS | This quarry cries on havoc. O proud [[Death|death]],<br>What feast is toward in thine eternal cell<br>That thou so many princes at a shot<br>So bloodily hast struck! |
| AMBASSADOR | The sight is dismal,<br>And our affairs from England come too late.<br>The ears are senseless that should give us hearing<br>To tell him his commandment is fulfilled,<br>That [[Rosencrantz]] and [[Guildenstern]] are dead.<br>Where should we have our thanks? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Not from his mouth,<br>Had it th‘ability of life to thank you.<br>He never gave commandment for their [[Death|death]].<br>But since so jump upon this bloody question<br>You from the Polack wars, and you from England,<br>Are here arrived, give order that these bodies<br>High on a stage be placed to the view;<br>And let me speak to th’ yet unknowing world<br>How these things came about. So shall you hear<br>Of carnal, bloody, and unnatural acts,<br>Of accidental judgements, casual slaughters,<br>Of deaths put on by cunning and forced cause;<br>And, in this upshot, purposes mistook<br>Fall’n on th’inventors’ heads. All this can I<br>Truly deliver. |
| FORTINBRAS | Let us haste to hear it,<br>And call the noblest to the audience.<br>For me, with sorrow I embrace my fortune.<br>I have some rights of [[Memory|memory]] in this kingdom,<br>Which now to claim my vantage doth invite me. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Of that I shall have also cause to speak,<br>And from his mouth whose voice will draw on more.<br>But let this same be presently performed,<br>Even whiles men’s minds are wild, lest more<br>mischance<br>On plots and errors happen. |
| FORTINBRAS | Let four captains<br>Bear [[Hamlet]] like a soldier to the stage,<br>For he was likely, had he been put on,<br>To have proved most royally; and for his passage,<br>The soldiers’ music and the rites of war<br>Speak loudly for him.<br>Take up the [[Body|body]]. Such a sight as this<br>Becomes the field, but here shows much amiss.<br>Go, bid the soldiers shoot. |

| TWELFTH | [[Night|NIGHT]], the end of the Christmas season, was traditionally a [[Time|time]] of revelry and topsy-turvydom; Shakespeare’s title for a play in which a servant aspires to his mistress’s hand has no more specific reference. It was thought appropriate to the festive occasion of Candlemas (2 February) 1602 when, in the first known allusion to it, John Manningham, a law student of the Middle Temple in [[London]], noted ‘at our feast we had a play called [[Twelfth Night]], or What You Will’. References to ‘the Sophy’—the Shah of Persia (2.5.174; 3.4.271)—probably post-date Sir Robert [[Shirley]]’s return from Persia, in a [[Ship|ship]] named The Sophy, in 1599; and ‘the new map with the augmentation of the Indies’ (3.2.75) appears to be one published in 1599 and reissued in 1600. Shakespeare may have picked up the name [[Orsino]] for his young duke from a Tuscan nobleman whom [[Queen Elizabeth]] entertained at Whitehall with a play performed by Shakespeare’s company on [[Twelfth Night]] 1601. Probably he wrote [[Twelfth Night]] during that year.<br>[[Twelfth Night]]’s romantic setting is Illyria, the Greek and Roman name for Adriatic territory roughly corresponding to the former Yugoslavia. Manningham had noted that the play was ‘much like The [[Comedy]] of Errors or Menaechmi in Plautus’, thinking no [[Doubt|doubt]] of the confusions created by identical twins. Shakespeare may also have known an anonymous Italian [[Comedy|comedy]], GI’Ingannati (The Deceived Ones), acted in 1531 and first printed in 1537, which influenced a number of other plays and prose tales including Barnaby Riche’s story of Apolonius and Silla printed as part of Riche’s Farewell to Military Profession (1581). Riche gave Shakespeare his main plot of a shipwrecked girl ([[Viola]]) who, disguised as a boy (Cesario), serves a young Duke ([[Orsino]]) and undertakes [[Love|love]]-errands on his behalf to a noble lady ([[Olivia]]) who falls in [[Love|love]] with her but mistakenly marries her twin brother ([[Sebastian]]). Shakespeare idealizes Riche’s characters and purges the story of some of its explicit [[Sexuality|sexuality]]: Riche’s [[Olivia]], for example, is pregnant before [[Marriage|marriage]], and his [[Viola]] reveals her [[Identity|identity]], in a manner impractical for a boy actor, by stripping to the waist. Shakespeare complicates the plot by giving [[Olivia]] a reprobate uncle, [[Sir Toby Belch]], and two additional suitors, the asinine [[Sir Andrew Aguecheek]] and her steward, [[Malvolio]], tricked by members of her household into believing that she loves him. More important to the play than to the plot is the entirely Shakespearian clown, [[Feste]], a wry and oblique commentator whose wit in folly is opposed to [[Malvolio]]’s folly in wit.<br>[[Twelfth Night]] is the consummation of Shakespeare’s romantic [[Comedy|comedy]], a play of wide emotional range, extending from the robust, brilliantly orchestrated [[Humour|humour]] of the scene of midnight revelry (2.2) to the rapt [[Wonder|wonder]] of the antiphon of recognition (5.1.224-56) between the reunited twins. In performance the balance shifts, favouring sometimes the exposure and celebration of folly, at other times the poignancy of unattained [[Love|love]] and of unheeded wisdom; but few other plays have so consistently provided theatrical pleasure of so high an order. |
