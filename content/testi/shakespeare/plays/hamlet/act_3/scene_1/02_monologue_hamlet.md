| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | To be, or not to be; that is the question: Whether ‘tis nobler in the mind to suffer The slings and arrows of outrageous fortune, Or to take arms against a sea of troubles, And, by opposing, end them. To die, to [[Sleep|sleep]]— No more, and by a [[Sleep|sleep]] to say we end The heartache and the thousand natural shocks That flesh is heir to—’tis a consummation Devoutly to be wished. To die, to [[Sleep|sleep]]. To [[Sleep|sleep]], perchance to dream. Ay, there’s the rub, For in that [[Sleep|sleep]] of [[Death|death]] what [[Dreams|dreams]] may come When we have shuffled off this mortal coil Must give us pause. There’s the respect That makes calamity of so long life, For who would bear the whips and scorns of [[Time|time]], Th‘oppressor’s wrong, the proud man’s contumely, The pangs of disprized [[Love|love]], the law’s delay, The insolence of office, and the spurns That patient merit of th’unworthy takes, When he himself might his quietus make With a bare bodkin? Who would these fardels bear, To grunt and sweat under a weary life, But that the dread of something after [[Death|death]], The undiscovered country from whose bourn No traveller returns, puzzles the will, And makes us rather bear those ills we have Than fly to others that we know not of? Thus [[Conscience|conscience]] does make cowards of us all, And thus the native hue of resolution Is sicklied o’er with the pale cast of thought, And enterprises of great pith and moment With this regard their currents turn awry, And lose the name of action. Soft you, now, The fair [[Ophelia]]!—Nymph, in thy orisons Be all my sins remembered. |
