| *(didascalia)* | Enter Barnardo and Francisco, two sentinels, at several doors |
| BARNARDO | Who’s there? |
| FRANCISCO | Nay, answer me. Stand and unfold yourself. |
| BARNARDO | Long live the [[King]]! |
| FRANCISCO | Barnardo? |
| BARNARDO | He. |
| FRANCISCO | You come most carefully upon your hour. |
| BARNARDO | ’Tis now struck twelve. Get thee to bed, Francisco. |
| FRANCISCO | For this relief much thanks. ’Tis bitter cold,<br>And I am sick at heart. |
| BARNARDO | Have you had quiet guard? |
| FRANCISCO | Not a mouse stirring. |
| BARNARDO | [[Well]], good [[Night|night]].<br>If you do meet [[Horatio]] and Marcellus,<br>The rivals of my watch, bid them make haste. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Horatio]] and Marcellus |
| FRANCISCO | I think I hear them.—Stand! Who’s there? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Friends to this ground. |
| MARCELLUS | And liegemen to the Dane. |
| FRANCISCO | Give you good [[Night|night]]. |
| MARCELLUS | O farewell, honest soldier. Who hath relieved you? |
| FRANCISCO | Barnardo has my place. Give you good [[Night|night]]. Exit |
| MARCELLUS | Holla, Barnardo! |
| BARNARDO | Say—what, is [[Horatio]] there? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | A piece of him. |
| BARNARDO | Welcome, [[Horatio]]. Welcome, good Marcellus. |
| MARCELLUS | What, has this thing appeared again tonight? |
| BARNARDO | I have seen nothing. |
| MARCELLUS | [[Horatio]] says ’tis but our fantasy,<br>And will not let belief take hold of him<br>Touching this dreaded sight twice seen of us.<br>Therefore I have entreated him along<br>With us to watch the minutes of this [[Night|night]],<br>That if again this apparition come<br>He may approve our eyes and speak to it. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Tush, tush, ’twill not appear. |
| BARNARDO | Sit down a while,<br>And let us once again assail your ears,<br>That are so fortified against our story,<br>What we two nights have seen. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | [[Well]], sit we down,<br>And let us hear Barnardo speak of this. |
| BARNARDO | Last [[Night|night]] of all,<br>When yon same star that’s westward from the pole<br>Had made his course t’illume that part of heaven<br>Where now it burns, Marcellus and myself,<br>The bell then beating one–– |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Ghost]] in complete armour, holding a truncheon, with his beaver up |
| MARCELLUS | Peace, break thee off. Look where it comes again. |
| BARNARDO | In the same figure like the [[King]] that’s dead. |
| MARCELLUS | (to [[Horatio]])<br>Thou art a scholar—speak to it, [[Horatio]]. |
| BARNARDO | Looks it not like the [[King]]?—Mark it, [[Horatio]]. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Most like. It harrows me with [[Fear|fear]] and [[Wonder|wonder]]. |
| BARNARDO | It would be spoke to. |
| MARCELLUS | Question it, [[Horatio]]. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | (to the [[Ghost]])<br>What art thou that usurp’st this [[Time|time]] of [[Night|night]],<br>Together with that fair and warlike form<br>In which the majesty of buried [[Denmark]]<br>Did sometimes march? By heaven, I charge thee speak. |
| MARCELLUS | It is offended. |
| BARNARDO | See, it stalks away. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | (to the [[Ghost]])<br>Stay, speak, speak, I charge thee speak. Exit [[Ghost]] |
| MARCELLUS | ’Tis gone, and will not answer. |
| BARNARDO | How now, [[Horatio]]? You tremble and look pale.<br>Is not this something more than fantasy?<br>What think you on’t? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Before my God, I might not this believe<br>Without the sensible and true avouch<br>Of mine own eyes. |
| MARCELLUS | Is it not like the [[King]]? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | As thou art to thyself.<br>Such was the very armour he had on<br>When he th‘ambitious Norway combated.<br>So frowned he once when in an angry parley<br>He smote the sledded Polacks on the ice.<br>’Tis strange. |
| MARCELLUS | Thus twice before, and just at this dead hour,<br>With martial stalk hath he gone by our watch. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | In what particular thought to work I know not,<br>But in the gross and scope of my opinion<br>This bodes some strange eruption to our state. |
| MARCELLUS | Good now, sit down, and tell me, he that knows,<br>Why this same strict and most observant watch<br>So nightly toils the subject of the land,<br>And why such daily cast of brazen cannon,<br>And foreign mart for implements of war,<br>Why such impress of shipwrights, whose sore task<br>Does not divide the [[Sunday]] from the week:<br>What might be toward that this sweaty haste<br>Doth make the [[Night|night]] joint-labourer with the day,<br>Who is’t that can inform me? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | That can I—<br>At least the whisper goes so: our last [[King|king]],<br>Whose image even but now appeared to us,<br>Was as you know by Fortinbras of Norway,<br>Thereto pricked on by a most emulate [[Pride|pride]],<br>Dared to the combat; in which our valiant [[Hamlet]]—<br>For so this side of our known world esteemed him—<br>Did slay this Fortinbras, who by a sealed compact<br>[[Well]] ratified by law and heraldry<br>Did forfeit with his life all those his lands<br>Which he stood seized on to the conqueror;<br>Against the which a moiety competent<br>Was gaged by our [[King]], which had returned<br>To the inheritance of Fortinbras<br>Had he been vanquisher, as by the same cov‘nant<br>And carriage of the article designed<br>His fell to [[Hamlet]]. Now sir, young Fortinbras,<br>Of unimproved mettle hot and full,<br>Hath in the skirts of Norway here and there<br>Sharked up a list of landless resolutes<br>For food and diet to some enterprise<br>That hath a stomach in’t, which is no other—<br>And it doth [[Well|well]] appear unto our state—<br>But to recover of us by strong hand<br>And terms compulsative those foresaid lands<br>So by his father lost. And this, I take it,<br>Is the main motive of our preparations,<br>The source of this our watch, and the chief head<br>Of this post-haste and rummage in the land. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Ghost]], as before |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | But soft, behold—lo where it comes again!<br>I’ll [[Cross|cross]] it though it blast me.—Stay, illusion.<br>The [[Ghost]] spreads his arms<br>If thou hast any sound or use of voice,<br>Speak to me.<br>If there be any good thing to be done<br>That may to thee do ease and grace to me,<br>Speak to me.<br>If thou art privy to thy country’s fate<br>Which happily foreknowing may avoid,<br>O speak!<br>Or if thou hast uphoarded in thy life<br>Extorted treasure in the womb of earth—<br>For which, they say, you spirits oft walk in [[Death|death]]—<br>The cock crows<br>Speak of it, stay and speak.—Stop it, Marcellus. |
| MARCELLUS | Shall I strike at it with my partisan? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Do, if it will not stand. |
| BARNARDO | ’Tis here. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | ’Tis here. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Ghost]] |
| MARCELLUS | ’Tis gone.<br>We do it wrong, being so majestical,<br>To offer it the show of violence,<br>For it is as the air invulnerable,<br>And our vain blows malicious mockery. |
| BARNARDO | It was about to speak when the cock crew. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | And then it started like a guilty thing<br>Upon a fearful summons. I have heard<br>The cock, that is the trumpet to the morn,<br>Doth with his lofty and shrill-sounding throat<br>Awake the god of day, and at his warning,<br>Whether in sea or [[Fire|fire]], in earth or air,<br>Th’extravagant and erring spirit hies<br>To his confine; and of the [[Truth|truth]] herein<br>This present object made probation. |
| MARCELLUS | It faded on the crowing of the cock.<br>Some say that ever ’gainst that season comes<br>Wherein our saviour’s birth is celebrated<br>The bird of dawning singeth all [[Night|night]] long;<br>And then, they say, no spirit can walk abroad,<br>The nights are wholesome; then no planets strike,<br>No fairy takes, nor [[Witch|witch]] hath power to charm,<br>So hallowed and so gracious is the [[Time|time]]. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | So have I heard, and do in part believe it.<br>But look, the morn in russet mantle clad<br>Walks o’er the dew of yon high eastern [[Hill|hill]].<br>Break we our watch up, and by my advice<br>Let us impart what we have seen tonight<br>Unto young [[Hamlet]]; for upon my life,<br>This spirit, dumb to us, will speak to him.<br>Do you consent we shall acquaint him with it,<br>As needful in our loves, fitting our duty? |
| MARCELLUS | Let’s do’t, I pray; and I this morning know<br>Where we shall find him most conveniently. Exeunt |
