| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | O all you host of heaven! Oearth! What else? And shall I couple hell? O fie! Hold, hold, my heart, And you, my sinews, grow not instant old, But bear me stiffly up. Remember thee? Ay, thou poor [[Ghost|ghost]], while [[Memory|memory]] holds a seat In this distracted globe. Remember thee? Yea, from the table of my [[Memory|memory]] I’ll wipe away all trivial fond records, All saws of books, all forms, all pressures past, That youth and observation copied there, And thy commandment all [[Alone|alone]] shall live Within the book and volume of my brain Unmixed with baser matter. Yes, yes, by heaven. O most pernicious woman! O [[Villain|villain]], [[Villain|villain]], smiling, damned [[Villain|villain]]! My tables, My tables—meet it is I set it down That one may smile and smile and be a [[Villain|villain]]. At least I’m sure it may be so in [[Denmark]]. He writes So, uncle, there you are. Now to my word: It is ‘Adieu, adieu, remember me’. I have sworn’t. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | and MARCELLUS (within) My lord, my lord. Enter [[Horatio]] and Marcellus |
| MARCELLUS | (calling) Lord [[Hamlet]]! 115 |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Heaven secure him. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | So be it. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | (calling) Illo, ho, ho, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Hillo, ho, ho, boy; come, bird, come. |
| MARCELLUS | How is’t, my noble lord? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | (to [[Hamlet]]) What news, my lord? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | O wonderful! |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Good my lord, tell it. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | No, you’ll reveal it. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Not I, my lord, by heaven. |
| MARCELLUS | Nor I, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How say you then, would heart of man once think it? But you’ll be secret? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | and MARCELLUS Ay, by heav’n, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | There’s ne’er a [[Villain|villain]] dwelling in all [[Denmark]] But he’s an arrant knave. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | There needs no [[Ghost|ghost]], my lord, come from the [[Grave|grave]] To tell us this. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why, right, you are i’th’ right, And so without more circumstance at all I hold it fit that we shake hands and part, You as your business and desires shall point you— For every man has business and [[Desire|desire]], Such as it is—and for mine own poor part, Look you, I’ll go pray. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | These are but wild and whirling words, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I’m sorry they offend you, heartily, Yes, [[Faith|faith]], heartily. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | There’s no offence, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Yes, by Saint Patrick, but there is, [[Horatio]], And much offence, too. Touching this vision here, It is an honest [[Ghost|ghost]], that let me tell you. For your [[Desire|desire]] to know what is between us, O’ermaster’t as you may. And now, good friends, As you are friends, scholars, and soldiers, Give me one poor request. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | What is’t, my lord? We will. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Never make known what you have seen tonight. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | and MARCELLUS My lord, we will not. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Nay, but swear’t. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | In [[Faith|faith]], my lord, not I. |
| MARCELLUS | Nor I, my lord, in [[Faith|faith]]. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Upon my [[Sword|sword]]. |
| MARCELLUS | We have sworn, my lord, already. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Indeed, upon my [[Sword|sword]], indeed. The [[Ghost]] cries under the stage |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Swear. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Ah ha, boy, sayst thou so? Art thou there, truepenny?— Come on. You hear this fellow in the cellarage. Consent to swear. |
