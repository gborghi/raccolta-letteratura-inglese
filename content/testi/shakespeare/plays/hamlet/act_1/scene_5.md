| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Ghost]], and Prince [[Hamlet]] following |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Whither wilt thou lead me? Speak. I’ll go no further. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Mark me. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I will. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | My hour is almost come<br>When I to sulph’rous and tormenting flames<br>Must render up myself. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Alas, poor [[Ghost|ghost]]! |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Pity me not, but lend thy serious hearing<br>To what I shall unfold. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | So art thou to [[Revenge|revenge]] when thou shalt hear. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | What? |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | I am thy father’s spirit,<br>Doomed for a certain term to walk the [[Night|night]],<br>And for the day confined to fast in fires<br>Till the foul crimes done in my days of [[Nature|nature]]<br>Are burnt and purged away. But that I am forbid<br>To tell the [[Secrets|secrets]] of my [[Prison|prison]]-[[house]]<br>I could a tale unfold whose lightest word<br>Would harrow up thy [[Soul|soul]], freeze thy young [[Blood|blood]],<br>Make thy two eyes like [[Stars|stars]] start from their spheres,<br>Thy knotty and combined locks to part,<br>And each particular hair to stand on end<br>Like quills upon the fretful porcupine.<br>But this eternal blazon must not be<br>To ears of flesh and [[Blood|blood]]. List, [[Hamlet]], list, O list!<br>If thou didst ever thy dear father [[Love|love]]— |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | O God! |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | [[Revenge]] his foul and most unnatural murder. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Murder? |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Murder most foul, as in the best it is,<br>But this most foul, strange, and unnatural. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Haste, haste me to know it, that with wings as swift<br>As meditation or the thoughts of [[Love|love]]<br>May sweep to my [[Revenge|revenge]]. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | I find thee apt,<br>And duller shouldst thou be than the fat weed<br>That rots itself in ease on Lethe wharf<br>Wouldst thou not stir in this. Now, [[Hamlet]], hear.<br>’Tis given out that, sleeping in mine orchard,<br>A serpent stung me. So the whole ear of [[Denmark]]<br>Is by a forged process of my [[Death|death]]<br>Rankly abused. But know, thou noble youth,<br>The serpent that did sting thy father’s life<br>Now wears his [[Crown|crown]]. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | O my prophetic [[Soul|soul]]! Mine uncle? |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Ay, that incestuous, that adulterate beast,<br>With witchcraft of his wit, with traitorous gifts—<br>O wicked wit and gifts, that have the power<br>So to seduce!—won to his shameful lust<br>The will of my most seeming-virtuous [[Queen|queen]].<br>O [[Hamlet]], what a falling off was there!—<br>From me, whose [[Love|love]] was of that dignity<br>That it went hand-in-hand even with the vow<br>I made to her in [[Marriage|marriage]], and to decline<br>Upon a wretch whose natural gifts were poor<br>To those of mine.<br>But virtue, as it never will be moved,<br>Though lewdness [[Court|court]] it in a shape of heaven,<br>So lust, though to a radiant angel linked,<br>Will sate itself in a celestial bed,<br>And prey on garbage.<br>But soft, methinks I scent the morning’s air.<br>Brief let me be. Sleeping within mine orchard,<br>My custom always in the afternoon,<br>Upon my secure hour thy uncle stole<br>With juice of cursed hebenon in a vial,<br>And in the porches of mine ears did pour<br>The leperous distilment, whose effect<br>Holds such an enmity with [[Blood|blood]] of man<br>That swift as quicksilver it courses through<br>The natural gates and alleys of the [[Body|body]],<br>And with a sudden vigour it doth posset<br>And curd, like eager droppings into milk,<br>The thin and wholesome [[Blood|blood]]. So did it mine;<br>And a most instant tetter barked about,<br>Most lazar-like, with vile and loathsome crust,<br>All my smooth [[Body|body]].<br>Thus was I, sleeping, by a brother’s hand<br>Of life, of [[Crown|crown]], of [[Queen|queen]] at once dispatched,<br>Cut off even in the blossoms of my sin,<br>Unhouseled, dis-appointed, unaneled,<br>No reck’ning made, but sent to my account<br>With all my imperfections on my head.<br>O horrible, O horrible, most horrible!<br>If thou hast [[Nature|nature]] in thee, bear it not.<br>Let not the royal bed of [[Denmark]] be<br>A couch for luxury and damned incest.<br>But howsoever thou pursuest this act,<br>Taint not thy mind, nor let thy [[Soul|soul]] contrive<br>Against thy mother aught. Leave her to heaven,<br>And to those thorns that in her bosom lodge<br>To prick and sting her. Fare thee [[Well|well]] at once.<br>The glow-worm shows the matin to be near,<br>And gins to pale his uneffectual [[Fire|fire]].<br>Adieu, adieu, [[Hamlet]]. Remember me. Exit |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | O all you host of heaven! Oearth! What else?<br>And shall I couple hell? O fie! Hold, hold, my heart,<br>And you, my sinews, grow not instant old,<br>But bear me stiffly up. Remember thee?<br>Ay, thou poor [[Ghost|ghost]], while [[Memory|memory]] holds a seat<br>In this distracted globe. Remember thee?<br>Yea, from the table of my [[Memory|memory]]<br>I’ll wipe away all trivial fond records,<br>All saws of books, all forms, all pressures past,<br>That youth and observation copied there,<br>And thy commandment all [[Alone|alone]] shall live |
| *(didascalia)* | Within the book and volume of my brain |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Unmixed with baser matter. Yes, yes, by heaven.<br>O most pernicious woman!<br>O [[Villain|villain]], [[Villain|villain]], smiling, damned [[Villain|villain]]!<br>My tables,<br>My tables—meet it is I set it down<br>That one may smile and smile and be a [[Villain|villain]].<br>At least I’m sure it may be so in [[Denmark]].<br>He writes<br>So, uncle, there you are. Now to my word:<br>It is ‘Adieu, adieu, remember me’.<br>I have sworn’t. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | and MARCELLUS (within) My lord, my lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Horatio]] and Marcellus |
| MARCELLUS | (calling) Lord [[Hamlet]]! 115 |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Heaven secure him. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | So be it. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | (calling) Illo, ho, ho, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Hillo, ho, ho, boy; come, bird, come. |
| MARCELLUS | How is’t, my noble lord? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | (to [[Hamlet]]) What news, my lord? |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | O wonderful! |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Good my lord, tell it. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | No, you’ll reveal it. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Not I, my lord, by heaven. |
| MARCELLUS | Nor I, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | How say you then, would heart of man once think it?<br>But you’ll be secret? |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | and MARCELLUS Ay, by heav’n, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | There’s ne’er a [[Villain|villain]] dwelling in all [[Denmark]]<br>But he’s an arrant knave. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | There needs no [[Ghost|ghost]], my lord, come from the [[Grave|grave]]<br>To tell us this. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Why, right, you are i’th’ right,<br>And so without more circumstance at all<br>I hold it fit that we shake hands and part,<br>You as your business and desires shall point you—<br>For every man has business and [[Desire|desire]],<br>Such as it is—and for mine own poor part,<br>Look you, I’ll go pray. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | These are but wild and whirling words, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | I’m sorry they offend you, heartily,<br>Yes, [[Faith|faith]], heartily. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | There’s no offence, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Yes, by Saint Patrick, but there is, [[Horatio]],<br>And much offence, too. Touching this vision here,<br>It is an honest [[Ghost|ghost]], that let me tell you.<br>For your [[Desire|desire]] to know what is between us,<br>O’ermaster’t as you may. And now, good friends,<br>As you are friends, scholars, and soldiers,<br>Give me one poor request. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | What is’t, my lord? We will. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Never make known what you have seen tonight. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | and MARCELLUS<br>My lord, we will not. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Nay, but swear’t. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | In [[Faith|faith]], my lord, not I. |
| MARCELLUS | Nor I, my lord, in [[Faith|faith]]. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Upon my [[Sword|sword]]. |
| MARCELLUS | We have sworn, my lord, already. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Indeed, upon my [[Sword|sword]], indeed.<br>The [[Ghost]] cries under the stage |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Swear. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Ah ha, boy, sayst thou so? Art thou there, truepenny?—<br>Come on. You hear this fellow in the cellarage.<br>Consent to swear. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | Propose the oath, my lord. |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Never to speak of this that you have seen,<br>Swear by my [[Sword|sword]]. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | (under the stage) Swear. |
| *(didascalia)* | They swear |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Hic et ubique? Then we’ll shift our ground.—<br>Come hither, gentlemen,<br>And lay your hands again upon my [[Sword|sword]].<br>Never to speak of this that you have heard,<br>Swear by my [[Sword|sword]]. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | (under the stage) Swear. |
| *(didascalia)* | They swear |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | [[Well]] said, old mole. Canst work i’th’ earth so fast?<br>A worthy pioneer.—Once more remove, good friends. |
| [[Horatio|HORATIO]] | O day and [[Night|night]], but this is wondrous strange! |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | And therefore as a stranger give it welcome.<br>There are more things in heaven and earth, [[Horatio]],<br>Than are dreamt of in our [[philosophy]]. But come,<br>Here as before, never, so help you [[Mercy|mercy]],<br>How strange or odd soe‘er I bear myself—<br>As I perchance hereafter shall think meet<br>To put an antic disposition on—<br>That you at such [[Time|time]] seeing me never shall,<br>With arms encumbered thus, or this headshake,<br>Or by pronouncing of some doubtful phrase<br>As ‘[[Well]], we know’ or ‘We could an if we would’,<br>Or ‘If we list to speak’, or ‘There be, an if they might’,<br>Or such ambiguous giving out, to note<br>That you know aught of me—this not to do,<br>So grace and [[Mercy|mercy]] at your most need help you, swear. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | (under the stage) Swear. |
| *(didascalia)* | They swear |
| [[Hamlet|HAMLET]] | Rest, rest, perturbed spirit.—So, gentlemen,<br>With all my [[Love|love]] I do commend me to you,<br>And what so poor a man as [[Hamlet]] is<br>May do t’express his [[Love|love]] and friending to you,<br>God willing, shall not lack. Let us go in together,<br>And still your fingers on your lips, I pray.<br>The [[Time|time]] is out of joint. O cursed spite<br>That ever I was born to set it right!<br>Nay, come, let’s go together. Exeunt |
