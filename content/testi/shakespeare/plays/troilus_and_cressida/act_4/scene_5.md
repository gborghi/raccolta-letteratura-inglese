| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Pandarus]] and [[Cressida]] |
| [[Pandarus|PANDARUS]] | Be moderate, be moderate. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | Why tell you me of moderation?<br>The grief is fine, full, perfect that I taste,<br>And violenteth in a sense as strong<br>As that which causeth it. How can I moderate it?<br>If I could temporize with my affection<br>Or brew it to a weak and colder palate,<br>The like allayment could I give my grief.<br>My [[Love|love]] admits no qualifying dross;<br>No more my grief, in such a precious loss. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Troilus]] |
| [[Pandarus|PANDARUS]] | Here, here, here he comes. Ah, sweet ducks! |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | (embracing him) O [[Troilus]], [[Troilus]]! |
| [[Pandarus|PANDARUS]] | What a pair of spectacles is here! Let me<br>embrace you too. ‘O heart‘, as the goodly saying is,<br>‘O heart, heavy heart,<br>Why sigh’st thou without breaking?’<br>where he answers again<br>‘Because thou canst not ease thy smart<br>By [[Friendship|friendship]] nor by speaking.’<br>There was never a truer rhyme. Let us cast away<br>nothing, for we may live to have need of such a verse.<br>We see it, we see it. How now, lambs? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Cressid, I [[Love|love]] thee in so strained a purity<br>That the blest gods, as angry with my fancy—<br>More bright in zeal than the devotion which<br>Cold lips blow to their deities—take thee from me. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | Have the gods envy? |
| [[Pandarus|PANDARUS]] | Ay, ay, ay, ay, ’tis too plain a case. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | And is it true that I must go from Troy? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | A hateful [[Truth|truth]]. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | What, and from [[Troilus]] too? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | From Troy and [[Troilus]]. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | Is’t possible? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | And suddenty—where injury of chance<br>Puts back leave-taking, jostles roughly by<br>All [[Time|time]] of pause, rudely beguiles our lips<br>Of all rejoindure, forcibly prevents<br>Our locked embrasures, strangles our dear vows<br>Even in the birth of our own labouring breath.<br>We two, that with so many thousand sighs<br>Did buy each other, must poorly sell ourselves<br>With the rude brevity and discharge of one.<br>Injurious [[Time]] now with a robber’s haste<br>Crams his rich thiev’ry up, he knows not how.<br>As many farewells as be [[Stars|stars]] in heaven,<br>With distinct breath and consigned kisses to them,<br>He fumbles up into a loose adieu<br>And scants us with a single famished kiss,<br>Distasted with the salt of broken [[Tears|tears]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Aeneas |
| AENEAS | My lord, is the lady ready? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | (to [[Cressida]])<br>Hark, you are called. Some say the genius so<br>Cries ‘Come!’ to him that instantly must die. |
| *(didascalia)* | To [[Pandarus]] Bid them have patience. She shall come |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | anon. |
| [[Pandarus|PANDARUS]] | Where are my [[Tears|tears]]? Rain, to lay this [[Wind|wind]], or my heart will be blown up by the root. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with Aeneas |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | I must then to the Grecians. |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | No remedy. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | A woeful Cressid ’mongst the merry Greeks!<br>When shall we see again? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Hear me, my [[Love|love]]: be thou but true of heart— |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | I true? How now! What wicked deem is this? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Nay, we must use expostulation kindly,<br>For it is parting from us.<br>I speak not ‘Be thou true’ as fearing thee—<br>For I will throw my glove to [[Death]] himself<br>That there’s no maculation in thy heart—<br>But ‘Be thou true’ say I, to fashion in<br>My sequent protestation: ‘Be thou true,<br>And I will see thee’. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | O you shall be exposed, my lord, to dangers<br>As infinite as imminent. But I’ll be true. |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | And I’ll grow friend with danger. Wear this sleeve. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | And you this glove. When shall I see you? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | I will corrupt the Grecian sentinels<br>To give thee nightly visitation.<br>But yet, be true. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | O heavens! ’Be true’ again! |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Hear why I speak it, [[Love|love]].<br>The Grecian youths are full of quality,<br>Their loving [[Well|well]] composed, with gifts of [[Nature|nature]]<br>flowing,<br>And swelling o’er with arts and exercise.<br>How novelty may move, and parts with person,<br>Alas, a kind of godly [[Jealousy|jealousy]]—<br>Which I beseech you call a virtuous sin—<br>Makes me afeard. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | O heavens, you [[Love|love]] me not! |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Die I a [[Villain|villain]] then!<br>In this I do not call your [[Faith|faith]] in question<br>So mainly as my merit. I cannot sing,<br>Nor heel the high lavolt, nor sweeten talk,<br>Nor play at subtle games—fair virtues all,<br>To which the Grecians are most prompt and<br>pregnant.<br>But I can tell that in each grace of these<br>There lurks a still and dumb-discoursive devil<br>That tempts most cunningly. But be not tempted. |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | Do you think I will? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | No, but something may be done that we will not,<br>And sometimes we are devils to ourselves,<br>When we will tempt the frailty of our powers,<br>Presuming on their changeful potency. |
| AENEAS | (within)<br>Nay, good my lord! |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Come, kiss, and let us part. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Paris|PARIS]] art the door |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Brother [[Troilus]]? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Good brother, come you hither,<br>And bring Aeneas and the Grecian with you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Paris]] |
| [[Cressida|CRESSIDA]] | My lord, will you be true? |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Who, I? Alas, it is my vice, my fault.<br>Whiles others fish with craft for great opinion,<br>I with great [[Truth|truth]] catch mere simplicity;<br>Whilst some with cunning gild their copper crowns,<br>With [[Truth|truth]] and plainness I do wear mine bare. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Paris]], Aeneas, Antenor, Deiphobus, and Diomedes |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | [[Fear]] not my [[Truth|truth]]. The moral of my wit<br>Is ‘plain and true!’; there’s all the reach of it.—<br>Welcome, Sir Diomed. Here is the lady<br>Which for Antenor we deliver you.<br>At the port, lord, I’ll give her to thy hand,<br>And by the way possess thee what she is.<br>Entreat her fair, and by my [[Soul|soul]], fair Greek,<br>If e’er thou stand at [[Mercy|mercy]] of my [[Sword|sword]],<br>Name Cressid, and thy life shall be as safe<br>As Priam is in Ilium. |
| DIOMEDES | Fair Lady Cressid,<br>So please you, save the thanks this prince expects.<br>The lustre in your eye, heaven in your cheek,<br>Pleads your fair usage; and to Diomed<br>You shall be mistress, and command him wholly. |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Grecian, thou dost not use me courteously,<br>To shame the zeal of my petition towards thee<br>In praising her. I tell thee, lord of Greece,<br>She is as far high-soaring o’er thy praises<br>As thou unworthy to be called her servant.<br>I charge thee use her [[Well|well]], even for my charge;<br>For, by the dreadful Pluto, if thou dost not,<br>Though the great bulk [[Achilles]] be thy guard<br>I’ll cut thy throat. |
| DIOMEDES | O be not moved, Prince [[Troilus]].<br>Let me be privileged by my place and message<br>To be a speaker free. When I am hence<br>I’ll answer to my lust. And know you, lord,<br>I’ll nothing do on charge. To her own worth<br>She shall be prized; but that you say ‘Be’t so’,<br>I’ll speak it in my spirit and [[Honour|honour]] ‘No!’ |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | Come, to the port.—I’ll tell thee, Diomed,<br>This brave shall oft make thee to hide thy head.—<br>Lady, give me your hand, and as we walk<br>To our own selves bend we our needful talk. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Troilus]], [[Cressida]], and Diomedes |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | A trumpet sounds |
| [[Paris|PARIS]] | Hark, [[Hector]]’s trumpet. |
| AENEAS | How have we spent this morning?<br>The Prince must think me tardy and remiss,<br>That swore to ride before him in the field. |
| [[Paris|PARIS]] | ‘Tis [[Troilus]]’ fault. Come, come to field with him. |
| DEIPHOBUS | Let us make ready straight. |
| AENEAS | Yea, with a bridegroom’s fresh alacrity<br>Let us address to tend on [[Hector]]’s heels.<br>The glory of our Troy doth this day lie<br>On his fair worth and single chivalry. Exeunt |
