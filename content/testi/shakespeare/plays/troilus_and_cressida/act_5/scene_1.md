| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Achilles]] and Patroclus |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | I’ll heat his [[Blood|blood]] with Greekish wine tonight,<br>Which with my scimitar I’ll cool tomorrow.<br>Patroclus, let us feast him to the height. |
| PATROCLUS | Here comes [[Thersites]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Thersites]] |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | How now, thou core of envy,<br>Thou crusty botch of [[Nature|nature]], what’s the news? |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Why, thou picture of what thou seemest, and idol of idiot-worshippers, here’s a letter for thee. |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | From whence, [[Fragment|fragment]]? |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Why, thou full dish of [[Fool|fool]], from Troy.<br>[[Achilles]] reads the letter |
| PATROCLUS | Who keeps the tent now? |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | The surgeon’s box or the patient’s wound. |
| PATROCLUS | [[Well]] said, adversity. And what need these tricks? |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Prithee be silent, boy. I profit not by thy talk.<br>Thou art thought to be [[Achilles]]’ male varlet. |
| PATROCLUS | ‘Male varlet’, you rogue? What’s that? |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Why, his masculine whore. Now the rotten diseases of the south, guts-griping, ruptures, catarrhs, loads o’ gravel i’th’ back, lethargies, cold palsies, and the like, take and take again such preposterous discoveries! |
| PATROCLUS | Why, thou damnable box of envy thou, what mean’st thou to curse thus? |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Do I curse thee? |
| PATROCLUS | Why, no, you ruinous butt, you whoreson indistinguishable cur, no. |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | No? Why art thou then exasperate? Thou idle immaterial skein of sleave-silk, thou green sarsenet flap for a sore eye, thou tassel of a prodigal’s purse, thou! Ah, how the poor world is pestered with such waterflies! Diminutives of [[Nature|nature]]. |
| PATROCLUS | Out, gall! |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Finch egg! |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | My sweet Patroclus, I am thwarted quite<br>From my great purpose in tomorrow’s battle.<br>Here is a letter from [[Queen]] Hecuba,<br>A token from her daughter, my fair [[Love|love]],<br>Both taxing me, and gaging me to keep<br>An oath that I have sworn. I will not break it.<br>Fall, Greeks; fail, fame; [[Honour|honour]], or go or stay.<br>My major vow lies here; this I’ll obey.—<br>Come, come, [[Thersites]], help to trim my tent.<br>This [[Night|night]] in banqueting must all be spent.—<br>Away, Patroclus. Exeunt [[Achilles]] and Patroclus |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | With too much [[Blood|blood]] and too little brain these two may run mad, but if with too much brain and too little [[Blood|blood]] they do, I’ll be a curer of madmen. Here’s [[Agamemnon]]: an honest fellow enough, and one that loves quails, but he has not so much brain as ear-wax. And the goodly transformation of Jupiter there, his brother the bull, the primitive statue and oblique memorial of cuckolds, a thrifty shoeing-horn in a chain, hanging at his brother’s leg: to what form but that he is should wit larded with malice and malice farced with wit turn him to? To an ass were nothing: he is both ass and ox. To an ox were nothing: he is both ox and ass. To be a dog, a mule, a cat, a fitchew, a toad, a lizard, an owl, a puttock, or a herring without a roe, I would not care; but to be Menetaus!—I would conspire against destiny. Ask me not what I would be if I were not [[Thersites]], for I care not to be the louse of a lazar, so I were not Menetaus.—Hey-day, sprites and fires. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Hector]], [[Ajax]], [[Agamemnon]], [[Ulysses]], Nestor, |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Menelaus, [[Troilus]], and Diomedes, with lights |
| [[Agamemnon|AGAMEMNON]] | We go wrong, we go wrong. |
| [[Ajax|AJAX]] | No, yonder ’tis:<br>There, where we see the light. |
| [[Hector|HECTOR]] | I trouble you. |
| [[Ajax|AJAX]] | No, not a whit. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Achilles]] |
| [[Ulysses|ULYSSES]] | Here comes himself to guide you. |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | Welcome, brave [[Hector]]. Welcome, princes all. |
| [[Agamemnon|AGAMEMNON]] | (to [[Hector]])<br>So now, fair prince of Troy, I bid good [[Night|night]].<br>[[Ajax]] commands the guard to tend on you. |
| [[Hector|HECTOR]] | Thanks and good [[Night|night]] to the Greeks’ general. |
| MENELAUS | Good [[Night|night]], my lord. |
| [[Hector|HECTOR]] | Good [[Night|night]], sweet Lord Menelaus. |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | (aside) Sweet draught! ‘Sweet’, quoth a? Sweet sink, sweet sewer. |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | Good [[Night|night]] and welcome both at once, to those<br>That go or tarry. |
| [[Agamemnon|AGAMEMNON]] | Good [[Night|night]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Agamemnon]] and Menelaus |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | Old Nestor tarries, and you too, Diomed.<br>Keep [[Hector]] company an hour or two. |
| DIOMEDES | I cannot, lord. I have important business<br>The tide whereof is now.—Good [[Night|night]], great [[Hector]]. |
| [[Hector|HECTOR]] | Give me your hand. |
| [[Ulysses|ULYSSES]] | (aside to [[Troilus]])<br>Follow his torch, he goes to Calchas’ tent.<br>I’ll keep you company. |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | (aside) Sweet sir, you [[Honour|honour]] me. |
| [[Hector|HECTOR]] | (to Diomedes)<br>And so good [[Night|night]]. |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | Come, come, enter my tent. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Diomedes, followed by [[Ulysses]] and |
| [[Achilles|ACHILLES]] | [[Troilus]], at one door; and [[Achilles]], [[Hector]],<br>[[Ajax]], and Nestor at another door |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | That same Diomed’s a false-hearted rogue, a most unjust knave. I will no more trust him when he leers than I will a serpent when he hisses. He will spend his mouth and promise like Brabbler the hound, but when he performs astronomers foretell it: that is prodigious, there will come some change. The sun borrows of the [[Moon|moon]] when Diomed keeps his word. I will rather leave to see [[Hector]] than not to dog him. They say he keeps a Trojan drab, and uses the traitor Calchas his tent. I’ll after.—Nothing but lechery! All incontinent varlets! Exit |
