| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | (to [[Polixenes]]) Sir, welcome. It is my father’s will I should take on me The hostess-[[Ship|ship]] o’th’ day. (To [[Camillo]]) You’re welcome, sir. Give me those [[Flowers|flowers]] there, Dorcas. Reverend sirs, For you there’s rosemary and rue. These keep Seeming and savour all the [[Winter|winter]] long. Grace and remembrance be to you both, And welcome to our shearing. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Shepherdess, A fair one are you. [[Well]] you fit our ages With [[Flowers|flowers]] of [[Winter|winter]]. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Sir, the year growing ancient, Not yet on [[Summer|summer]]’s [[Death|death]], nor on the birth Of trembling [[Winter|winter]], the fairest [[Flowers|flowers]] o’th’ season Are our carnations and streaked gillyvors, Which some call [[Nature|nature]]’s bastards. Of that kind Our rustic [[Garden|garden]]’s barren, and I care not To get slips of them. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Wherefore, gentle maiden, Do you neglect them? |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | For I have heard it said There is an art which in their piedness shares With great creating [[Nature|nature]]. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Say there be, Yet [[Nature|nature]] is made better by no mean But [[Nature|nature]] makes that mean. So over that art Which you say adds to [[Nature|nature]] is an art That [[Nature|nature]] makes. You see, sweet maid, we marry A gentler scion to the wildest stock, And make conceive a bark of baser kind By bud of nobler race. This is an art Which does mend [[Nature|nature]]—change it rather; but The art itself is [[Nature|nature]]. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | So it is. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Then make your [[Garden|garden]] rich in gillyvors, And do not call them bastards. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | I’ll not put The dibble in earth to set one slip of them, No more than, were I painted, I would wish This youth should say ‘twere [[Well|well]], and only therefore [[Desire]] to breed by me. Here’s [[Flowers|flowers]] for you: Hot lavender, mints, savory, marjoram, The marigold, that goes to bed wi’th’ sun, And with him rises, weeping. These are [[Flowers|flowers]] Of middle [[Summer|summer]], and I think they are given To men of middle age. You’re very welcome. She gives them [[Flowers|flowers]] |
| [[Camillo|CAMILLO]] | I should leave grazing were I of your flock, And only live by gazing. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Out, alas, You’d be so lean that blasts of January Would blow you through and through. (To [[Florizel]]) Now, my fair‘st friend, I would I had some [[Flowers|flowers]] o’th’ [[Spring|spring]] that might Become your [[Time|time]] of day; (to Mopsa and Dorcas) and yours, and yours, That wear upon your virgin branches yet Your maidenheads growing. O Proserpina, For the [[Flowers|flowers]] now that, frighted, thou letst fall From Dis’s wagon!-daffodils, That come before the [[Swallow|swallow]] dares, and take The winds of March with [[Beauty|beauty]]; violets, dim, But sweeter than the lids of Juno’s eyes Or Cytherea’s breath; pale primroses, That die unmarried ere they can behold Bright Phoebus in his strength—a malady Most incident to maids; bold oxlips, and The [[Crown|crown]] imperial; lilies of all kinds, The flower-de-luce being one. O, these I lack, To make you garlands of, and my sweet friend, To strew him o‘er and o’er. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | What, like a corpse? |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | No, like a bank, for [[Love|love]] to lie and play on, Not like a corpse—or if, not to be buried, But quick and in mine arms. Come, take your [[Flowers|flowers]]. Methinks I play as I have seen them do In Whitsun pastorals. Sure this robe of mine Does change my disposition. |
