| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Florizel]] dressed as Doricles a countryman, and [[Perdita]] as [[Queen]] of the Feast |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | These your unusual weeds to each part of you<br>Does give a life; no shepherdess, but Flora<br>Peering in April’s front. This your sheep-shearing<br>Is as a meeting of the petty gods,<br>And you the [[Queen|queen]] on’t. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Sir, my gracious lord,<br>To chide at your extremes it not becomes me—<br>O, pardon that I name them! Your high [[Self|self]],<br>The gracious mark o’th’ land, you have obscured<br>With a swain’s wearing, and me, poor lowly maid,<br>Most goddess-like pranked up. But that our feasts<br>In every mess have folly, and the feeders<br>Digest it with a custom, I should blush<br>To see you so attired; swoon, I think,<br>To show myself a glass. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | I bless the [[Time|time]]<br>When my good falcon made her flight across<br>Thy father’s ground. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Now Jove afford you cause!<br>To me the difference forges dread; your [[Greatness|greatness]]<br>Hath not been used to [[Fear|fear]]. Even now I tremble<br>To think your father by some accident<br>Should pass this way, as you did. O, the fates!<br>How would he look to see his work, so noble,<br>Vilely bound up? What would he say? Or how<br>Should I, in these my borrowed flaunts, behold<br>The sternness of his presence? |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | Apprehend<br>Nothing but jollity. The gods themselves,<br>Humbling their deities to [[Love|love]], have taken<br>The shapes of beasts upon them. Jupiter<br>Became a bull, and bellowed; the green Neptune<br>A ram, and bleated; and the [[Fire|fire]]-robed god,<br>Golden [[Apollo]], a poor humble swain,<br>As I seem now. Their transformations<br>Were never for a piece of [[Beauty|beauty]] rarer,<br>Nor in a way so chaste, since my desires<br>Run not before mine [[Honour|honour]], nor my lusts<br>Burn hotter than my [[Faith|faith]]. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | O, but sir,<br>Your resolution cannot hold when ’tis<br>Opposed, as it must be, by th’ power of the [[King]].<br>One of these two must be necessities,<br>Which then will speak that you must change this<br>purpose,<br>Or I my life. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | Thou dearest [[Perdita]],<br>With these forced thoughts I prithee darken not<br>The mirth o’th’ feast. Or I’ll be thine, my fair,<br>Or not my father’s. For I cannot be<br>Mine own, nor anything to any, if<br>I be not thine. To this I am most constant,<br>Though destiny say no. Be merry, gentle;<br>Strangle such thoughts as these with anything<br>That you behold the while. Your guests are coming.<br>Lift up your countenance as it were the day<br>Of celebration of that nuptial which<br>We two have sworn shall come. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | O Lady Fortune,<br>Stand you auspicious! |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | See, your guests approach.<br>Address yourself to entertain them sprightly,<br>And let’s be red with mirth. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Old Shepherd, with [[Polixenes]] and [[Camillo]], |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | disguised, the Clown, Mopsa, Dorcas, and others |
| OLD SHEPHERD | (to [[Perdita]])<br>Fie, daughter, when my old wife lived, upon<br>This day she was both pantler, butler, cook,<br>Both dame and servant, welcomed all, served all,<br>Would sing her [[Song|song]] and [[Dance|dance]] her turn, now here<br>At upper end o‘th’ table, now i’th’ middle,<br>On his shoulder, and his, her face afire<br>With labour, and the thing she took to quench it<br>She would to each one sip. You are retired<br>As if you were a feasted one and not<br>The hostess of the meeting. Pray you bid<br>These unknown friends to’s welcome, for it is<br>A way to make us better friends, more known.<br>Come, quench your blushes, and present yourself<br>That which you are, mistress o’th’ feast. Come on,<br>And bid us welcome to your sheep-shearing,<br>As your good flock shall prosper. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | (to [[Polixenes]]) Sir, welcome.<br>It is my father’s will I should take on me<br>The hostess-[[Ship|ship]] o’th’ day.<br>(To [[Camillo]]) You’re welcome, sir.<br>Give me those [[Flowers|flowers]] there, Dorcas. Reverend sirs,<br>For you there’s rosemary and rue. These keep<br>Seeming and savour all the [[Winter|winter]] long.<br>Grace and remembrance be to you both,<br>And welcome to our shearing. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Shepherdess,<br>A fair one are you. [[Well]] you fit our ages<br>With [[Flowers|flowers]] of [[Winter|winter]]. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Sir, the year growing ancient,<br>Not yet on [[Summer|summer]]’s [[Death|death]], nor on the birth<br>Of trembling [[Winter|winter]], the fairest [[Flowers|flowers]] o’th’ season<br>Are our carnations and streaked gillyvors,<br>Which some call [[Nature|nature]]’s bastards. Of that kind<br>Our rustic [[Garden|garden]]’s barren, and I care not<br>To get slips of them. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Wherefore, gentle maiden,<br>Do you neglect them? |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | For I have heard it said<br>There is an art which in their piedness shares<br>With great creating [[Nature|nature]]. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Say there be,<br>Yet [[Nature|nature]] is made better by no mean<br>But [[Nature|nature]] makes that mean. So over that art<br>Which you say adds to [[Nature|nature]] is an art<br>That [[Nature|nature]] makes. You see, sweet maid, we marry<br>A gentler scion to the wildest stock,<br>And make conceive a bark of baser kind<br>By bud of nobler race. This is an art<br>Which does mend [[Nature|nature]]—change it rather; but<br>The art itself is [[Nature|nature]]. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | So it is. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Then make your [[Garden|garden]] rich in gillyvors,<br>And do not call them bastards. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | I’ll not put<br>The dibble in earth to set one slip of them,<br>No more than, were I painted, I would wish<br>This youth should say ‘twere [[Well|well]], and only therefore<br>[[Desire]] to breed by me. Here’s [[Flowers|flowers]] for you:<br>Hot lavender, mints, savory, marjoram,<br>The marigold, that goes to bed wi’th’ sun,<br>And with him rises, weeping. These are [[Flowers|flowers]]<br>Of middle [[Summer|summer]], and I think they are given<br>To men of middle age. You’re very welcome.<br>She gives them [[Flowers|flowers]] |
| [[Camillo|CAMILLO]] | I should leave grazing were I of your flock,<br>And only live by gazing. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Out, alas,<br>You’d be so lean that blasts of January<br>Would blow you through and through.<br>(To [[Florizel]]) Now, my fair‘st friend,<br>I would I had some [[Flowers|flowers]] o’th’ [[Spring|spring]] that might<br>Become your [[Time|time]] of day; (to Mopsa and Dorcas) and<br>yours, and yours,<br>That wear upon your virgin branches yet<br>Your maidenheads growing. O Proserpina,<br>For the [[Flowers|flowers]] now that, frighted, thou letst fall<br>From Dis’s wagon!-daffodils,<br>That come before the [[Swallow|swallow]] dares, and take<br>The winds of March with [[Beauty|beauty]]; violets, dim,<br>But sweeter than the lids of Juno’s eyes<br>Or Cytherea’s breath; pale primroses,<br>That die unmarried ere they can behold<br>Bright Phoebus in his strength—a malady<br>Most incident to maids; bold oxlips, and<br>The [[Crown|crown]] imperial; lilies of all kinds,<br>The flower-de-luce being one. O, these I lack,<br>To make you garlands of, and my sweet friend,<br>To strew him o‘er and o’er. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | What, like a corpse? |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | No, like a bank, for [[Love|love]] to lie and play on,<br>Not like a corpse—or if, not to be buried,<br>But quick and in mine arms. Come, take your [[Flowers|flowers]].<br>Methinks I play as I have seen them do<br>In Whitsun pastorals. Sure this robe of mine<br>Does change my disposition. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | What you do<br>Still betters what is done. When you speak, sweet,<br>I’d have you do it ever; when you sing,<br>I’d have you buy and sell so, so give alms,<br>Pray so; and for the ord‘ring your affairs,<br>To sing them too. When you do [[Dance|dance]], I wish you<br>A wave o’th’ sea, that you might ever do<br>Nothing but that, move still, still so,<br>And own no other function. Each your doing,<br>So singular in each particular,<br>Crowns what you are doing in the present deeds,<br>That all your acts are queens. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | O Doricles,<br>Your praises are too large. But that your youth<br>And the true [[Blood|blood]] which peeps so fairly through’t<br>Do plainly give you out an unstained shepherd,<br>With wisdom I might [[Fear|fear]], my Doricles,<br>You wooed me the false way. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | I think you have<br>As little skill to [[Fear|fear]] as I have purpose<br>To put you to’t. But come, our [[Dance|dance]], I pray;<br>Your hand, my [[Perdita]]. So turtles pair,<br>That never mean to part. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | I’ll swear for ’em. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | (to [[Camillo]])<br>This is the prettiest low-born lass that ever<br>Ran on the greensward. Nothing she does or seems<br>But smacks of something greater than herself,<br>Too noble for this place. |
| [[Camillo|CAMILLO]] | He tells her something<br>That makes her [[Blood|blood]] look out. Good sooth, she is<br>The [[Queen|queen]] of curds and cream. |
| CLOWN | Come on, strike up! |
| DORCAS | Mopsa must be your mistress. Marry, garlic to mend her kissing with! |
| MOPSA | Now, in good [[Time|time]]! |
| CLOWN | Not a word, a word, we stand upon our manners.<br>Come, strike up! |
| *(didascalia)* | Music. Here a [[Dance|dance]] of shepherds and shepherdesses |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Pray, good shepherd, what fair swain is this<br>Which dances with your daughter? |
| OLD SHEPHERD | They call him Doricles, and boasts himself<br>To have a worthy feeding; but I have it<br>Upon his own report, and I believe it.<br>He looks like sooth. He says he loves my daughter.<br>I think so, too, for never gazed the [[Moon|moon]]<br>Upon the [[Water|water]] as he’ll stand and read,<br>As ’twere, my daughter’s eyes; and to be plain,<br>I think there is not half a kiss to choose<br>Who loves another best. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | She dances featly. |
| OLD SHEPHERD | So she does anything, though I report it<br>That should be silent. If young Doricles<br>Do light upon her, she shall bring him that<br>Which he not [[Dreams|dreams]] of. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servant |
| SERVANT | O, master, if you did but hear the pedlar at the door, you would never [[Dance|dance]] again after a tabor and pipe. No, the bagpipe could not move you. He sings several tunes faster than you’ll tell money. He utters them as he had eaten ballads, and all men’s ears grew to his tunes. |
| CLOWN | He could never come better. He shall come in. I [[Love|love]] a ballad but even too [[Well|well]], if it be doleful matter merrily set down, or a very pleasant thing indeed, and sung lamentably. |
| SERVANT | He hath [[songs]] for man or woman, of all sizes. No milliner can so fit his customers with gloves. He has the prettiest [[Love|love]] [[songs]] for maids, so without bawdry, which is strange, with such delicate burdens of dildos and fadings, ‘Jump her, and thump her’; and where some stretch-mouthed rascal would, as it were, mean mischief and break a foul gap into the matter, he makes the maid to answer, ‘Whoop, do me no harm, good man’; puts him off, slights him, with ‘Whoop, do me no harm, good man!’ |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | This is a brave fellow. |
| CLOWN | Believe me, thou talkest of an admirable conceited fellow. Has he any unbraided wares? |
| SERVANT | He hath ribbons of all the colours i‘th’ rainbow; points more than all the lawyers in Bohemia can learnedly handle, though they come to him by th’ gross; inkles, caddises, cambrics, lawns—why, he sings ’em over as they were gods or goddesses. You would think a smock were a she-angel, he so chants to the sleeve-hand and the work about the square on’t. |
| CLOWN | Prithee bring him in, and let him approach singing. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Forewarn him that he use no scurrilous words in’s tunes. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Servant |
| CLOWN | You have of these pedlars that have more in them than you’d think, sister. |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | Ay, good brother, or go about to think. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Autolycus]], wearing a false beard, carrying his pack, and singing |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | Lawn as white as driven [[Snow|snow]],<br>Cypress black as e’er was crow,<br>Gloves as sweet as damask roses,<br>Masks for faces, and for noses;<br>Bugle-bracelet, necklace amber,<br>Perfume for a lady’s chamber;<br>Golden coifs, and stomachers<br>For my lads to give their dears;<br>Pins and poking-sticks of steel,<br>What maids lack from head to heel<br>Come buy of me, come, come buy, come buy,<br>Buy, lads, or else your lasses cry. Come buy! |
| CLOWN | If I were not in [[Love|love]] with Mopsa thou shouldst take no money of me, but being enthralled as I am, it will also be the bondage of certain ribbons and gloves. |
| MOPSA | I was promised them against the feast, but they come not too late now. |
| DORCAS | He hath promised you more than that, or there be liars. |
| MOPSA | He hath paid you all he promised you. Maybe he has paid you more, which will shame you to give him again. |
| CLOWN | Is there no manners left among maids? Will they wear their plackets where they should bear their faces? Is there not milking-[[Time|time]], when you are going to bed, or kiln-hole, to whistle of these [[Secrets|secrets]], but you must be tittle-tattling before all our guests? ’Tis [[Well|well]] they are whispering. Clammer your tongues, and not a word more. |
| MOPSA | I have done. Come, you promised me a tawdry-lace and a pair of sweet gloves. |
| CLOWN | Have I not told thee how I was cozened by the way, and lost all my money? |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | And indeed, sir, there are cozeners abroad, therefore it behoves men to be wary. |
| CLOWN | [[Fear]] not thou, man, thou shalt lose nothing here. |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | I [[Hope|hope]] so, sir, for I have about me many parcels of charge. |
| CLOWN | What hast here? Ballads? |
| MOPSA | Pray now, buy some. I [[Love|love]] a ballad in print, alife, for then we are sure they are true. |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | Here’s one to a very doleful tune, how a usurer’s wife was brought to bed of twenty money-bags at a burden, and how she longed to eat adders’ heads and toads carbonadoed. |
| MOPSA | Is it true, think you? |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | Very true, and but a month old. |
| DORCAS | Bless me from marrying a usurer! |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | Here’s the midwife’s name to’t, one Mistress Tail-Porter, and five or six honest wives’ that were present. Why should I carry lies abroad? |
| MOPSA | (to Clown) Pray you now, buy it. |
| CLOWN | Come on, lay it by, and let’s first see more ballads. We’ll buy the other things anon. |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | Here’s another ballad, of a fish that appeared upon the coast on Wednesday the fourscore of April, forty thousand fathom above [[Water|water]], and sung this ballad against the hard hearts of maids. It was thought she was a woman, and was turned into a cold fish for she would not exchange flesh with one that loved her. The ballad is very pitiful, and as true. |
| DORCAS | Is it true too, think you? |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | Five justices’ hands at it, and witnesses more than my pack will hold. |
| CLOWN | Lay it by, too. Another. |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | This is a merry ballad, but a very pretty one. MOPSA Let’s have some merry ones. |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | Why, this is a passing merry one, and goes to the tune of ‘Two Maids Wooing a Man’. There’s scarce a maid westward but she sings it. ’Tis in request, I can tell you. |
| MOPSA | We can both sing it. If thou‘lt bear a part thou shalt hear; ’tis in three parts. |
| DORCAS | We had the tune on’t a month ago. |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | I can bear my part, you must know, ’tis my occupation. Have at it with you. |
| CLOWN | We’ll have this [[Song|song]] out anon by ourselves. My father and the gentlemen are in sad talk, and we’ll not trouble them. Come, bring away thy pack after me. Wenches, I’ll buy for you both. Pedlar, let’s have the first choice. Follow me, girls. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with Dorcas and Mopsa |
| [[Autolycus|AUTOLYCUS]] | And you shall pay [[Well|well]] for ’em. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Servant |
| SERVANT | Master, there is three carters, three shepherds, three neatherds, three swineherds that have made themselves all men of hair. They call themselves saultiers, and they have a [[Dance|dance]] which the wenches say is a gallimaufry of gambols, because they are not in’t. But they themselves are o’th’ mind, if it be not too rough for some that know little but bowling, it will please plentifully. |
| OLD SHEPHERD | Away. We’ll none on’t. Here has been too much homely foolery already. (To [[Polixenes]]) I know, sir, we weary you. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | You weary those that refresh us. Pray, let’s see these four threes of herdsmen. |
| SERVANT | One three of them, by their own report, sir, hath danced before the [[King]], and not the worst of the three but jumps twelve foot and a half by th’ square. |
| OLD SHEPHERD | Leave your prating. Since these good men are pleased, let them come in—but quickly, now. |
| SERVANT | Why, they stay at door, sir.<br>Here a [[Dance|dance]] of twelve satyrs |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | (to the Old Shepherd)<br>O, father, you’ll know more of that hereafter.<br>(To [[Camillo]]) Is it not too far gone? ’Tis [[Time|time]] to part<br>them.<br>He’s simple, and tells much.<br>(To [[Florizel]]) How now, fair shepherd,<br>Your heart is full of something that does take<br>Your mind from feasting. Sooth, when I was young<br>And handed [[Love|love]] as you do, I was wont<br>To load my she with knacks. I would have ransacked<br>The pedlar’s silken treasury, and have poured it<br>To her acceptance. You have let him go,<br>And nothing marted with him. If your lass<br>Interpretation should abuse, and call this<br>Your lack of [[Love|love]] or bounty, you were straited<br>For a reply, at least if you make a care<br>Of happy holding her. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | Old sir, I know<br>She prizes not such trifles as these are.<br>The gifts she looks from me are packed and locked<br>Up in my heart, which I have given already,<br>But not delivered.<br>(To [[Perdita]]) O, hear me breathe my life<br>Before this ancient sir, who, it should seem,<br>Hath sometime loved. I take thy hand, this hand<br>As soft as dove’s down, and as white as it,<br>Or Ethiopian’s tooth, or the fanned [[Snow|snow]] that’s bolted<br>By th’ northern blasts twice o’er. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | What follows this?<br>How prettily the young swain seems to wash<br>The hand was fair before! I have put you out.<br>But to your protestation. Let me hear<br>What you profess. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | Do, and be witness to’t. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | And this my neighbour too? |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | And he, and more<br>Than he; and men, the earth, the heavens, and all,<br>That were I crowned the most imperial monarch,<br>Thereof most worthy, were I the fairest youth<br>That ever made eye swerve, had force and knowledge<br>More than was ever man’s, I would not prize them<br>Without her [[Love|love]]; for her employ them all,<br>Commend them and condemn them to her service<br>Or to their own perdition. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Fairly offered. |
| [[Camillo|CAMILLO]] | This shows a sound affection. |
| OLD SHEPHERD | But, my daughter,<br>Say you the like to him? |
| [[Perdita|PERDITA]] | I cannot speak<br>So [[Well|well]], nothing so [[Well|well]], no, nor mean better.<br>By th’ pattern of mine own thoughts I cut out<br>The purity of his. |
| OLD SHEPHERD | Take hands, a bargain;<br>And, friends unknown, you shall bear witness to’t.<br>I give my daughter to him, and will make<br>Her portion equal his. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | O, that must be<br>I’th’ virtue of your daughter. One being dead,<br>I shall have more than you can dream of yet,<br>Enough then for your [[Wonder|wonder]]. But come on,<br>Contract us fore these witnesses. |
| OLD SHEPHERD | Come, your hand;<br>And, daughter, yours. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Soft, swain, a while, beseech you.<br>Have you a father? |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | I have. But what of him? |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Knows he of this? |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | He neither does nor shall. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Methinks a father<br>Is at the nuptial of his son a guest<br>That best becomes the table. Pray you once more,<br>Is not your father grown incapable<br>Of reasonable affairs? Is he not stupid<br>With age and alt’ring rheums? Can he speak, hear,<br>Know man from man? Dispute his own estate?<br>Lies he not bed-rid, and again does nothing<br>But what he did being childish? |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | No, good sir.<br>He has his health, and ampler strength indeed<br>Than most have of his age. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | By my white beard,<br>You offer him, if this be so, a wrong<br>Something unfilial. Reason my son<br>Should choose himself a wife, but as good reason<br>The father, all whose joy is nothing else<br>But fair posterity, should hold some counsel<br>In such a business. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | I yield all this;<br>But for some other reasons, my [[Grave|grave]] sir,<br>Which ’tis not fit you know, I not acquaint<br>My father of this business. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Let him know’t. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | He shall not. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | Prithee let him. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | No, he must not. |
| OLD SHEPHERD | Let him, my son. He shall not need to grieve<br>At knowing of thy choice. |
| [[Florizel|FLORIZEL]] | Come, come, he must not.<br>Mark our contract. |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | (removing his disguise)<br>Mark your [[divorce]], young sir,<br>Whom son I dare not call. Thou art too base<br>To be acknowledged. Thou a sceptre’s heir,<br>That thus affects a sheep-hook?<br>(To the Old Shepherd) Thou, old traitor,<br>I am sorry that by hanging thee I can but<br>Shorten thy life one week.<br>(To [[Perdita]]) And thou, fresh piece<br>Of excellent witchcraft, who of force must know<br>The royal [[Fool|fool]] thou cop’st with— |
| OLD SHEPHERD | O, my heart! |
| [[Polixenes|POLIXENES]] | I’ll have thy [[Beauty|beauty]] scratched with briers and made<br>More homely than thy state.<br>(To [[Florizel]]) For thee, fond boy,<br>If I may ever know thou dost but sigh<br>That thou no more shalt see this knack, as never<br>I mean thou shalt, we’ll bar thee from succession,<br>Not hold thee of our [[Blood|blood]], no, not our kin,<br>Farre than Deucalion off.
