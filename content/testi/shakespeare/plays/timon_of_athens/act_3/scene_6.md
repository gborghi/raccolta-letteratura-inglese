| *(didascalia)* | Enter three Senators at one door |
| FIRST SENATOR | My lords, you have my voice to’t. The fault’s bloody.<br>’Tis necessary he should die.<br>Nothing emboldens sin so much as [[Mercy|mercy]]. |
| SECOND SENATOR | Most true; the law shall bruise ’im. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Alcibiades]] at another door, with attendants |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | [[Honour]], health, and compassion to the senate! |
| FIRST SENATOR | Now, captain. |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | I am an humble suitor to your virtues;<br>For pity is the virtue of the law,<br>And none but tyrants use it cruelly.<br>It pleases [[Time|time]] and fortune to lie heavy<br>Upon a friend of mine, who in hot [[Blood|blood]]<br>Hath stepped into the law, which is past depth<br>To those that without heed do plunge into’t.<br>He is a man, setting his feat aside,<br>Of comely virtues;<br>Nor did he soil the fact with cowardice—<br>An [[Honour|honour]] in him which buys out his fault—<br>But with a noble fury and fair spirit,<br>Seeing his reputation touched to [[Death|death]],<br>He did oppose his foe;<br>And with such sober and unnoted passion<br>He did behave his anger, ere ’twas spent,<br>As if he had but proved an argument. |
| FIRST SENATOR | You undergo too strict a [[Paradox|paradox]],<br>Striving to make an ugly deed look fair.<br>Your words have took such pains as if they laboured<br>To bring manslaughter into form, and set quarrelling<br>Upon the head of valour—which indeed<br>Is valour misbegot, and came into the world<br>When sects and factions were newly born.<br>He’s truly valiant that can wisely suffer<br>The worst that man can breathe, and make his<br>wrongs his outsides<br>To wear them like his raiment carelessly,<br>And ne‘er prefer his injuries to his heart<br>To bring it into danger.<br>If wrongs be evils and enforce us kill,<br>What folly ’tis to hazard life for ill! |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | My lord— |
| FIRST SENATOR | You cannot make gross sins look clear.<br>To [[Revenge|revenge]] is no valour, but to bear. |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | My lords, then, under favour, pardon me<br>If I speak like a captain.<br>Why do fond men expose themselves to battle,<br>And not endure all threats, [[Sleep|sleep]] upon‘t,<br>And let the foes quietly cut their throats<br>Without repugnancy? If there be<br>Such valour in the bearing, what make we<br>Abroad? Why then, women are more valiant<br>That stay at [[Home|home]] if bearing carry it,<br>And the ass more captain than the [[Lion|lion]], the felon<br>Loaden with irons wiser than the judge,<br>If wisdom be in [[Suffering|suffering]]. O my lords,<br>As you are great, be pitifully good.<br>Who cannot condemn rashness in cold [[Blood|blood]]?<br>To kill, I grant, is sin’s extremest gust,<br>But in defence, by [[Mercy|mercy]], ’tis most just.<br>To be in anger is impiety,<br>But who is man that is not angry?<br>Weigh but the crime with this. |
| SECOND SENATOR | You breathe in vain. |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | In vain?<br>His service done at Lacedaemon and Byzantium<br>Were a sufficient briber for his life. |
| FIRST SENATOR | What’s that? |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | Why, I say, my lords, he’s done fair service,<br>And slain in fight many of your enemies.<br>How full of valour did he bear himself<br>In the last conflict, and made plenteous wounds! |
| SECOND SENATOR | He has made too much plenty with ‘em.<br>He’s a sworn rioter; he has a sin<br>That often drowns him and takes his valour prisoner.<br>If there were no foes, that were enough<br>To overcome him. In that beastly fury<br>He has been known to commit outrages<br>And cherish factions. ’Tis inferred to us<br>His days are foul and his drink dangerous. |
| FIRST SENATOR | He dies. |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | Hard fate! He might have died in war.<br>My lords, if not for any parts in him—<br>Though his right arm might purchase his own [[Time|time]]<br>And be in debt to none—yet more to move you,<br>Take my deserts to his and join ’em both.<br>And for I know<br>Your reverend ages [[Love|love]] security,<br>I’ll pawn my victories, all my [[Honour|honour]] to you<br>Upon his good returns.<br>If by this crime he owes the law his life,<br>Why, let the war receive’t in valiant gore,<br>For law is strict, and war is nothing more. |
| FIRST SENATOR | We are for law; he dies. Urge it no more,<br>On height of our displeasure. Friend or brother,<br>He forfeits his own [[Blood|blood]] that spills another. |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | Must it be so? It must not be.<br>My lords, I do beseech you know me. |
| SECOND SENATOR | How? |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | Call me to your remembrances. |
| THIRD SENATOR | What? |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | I cannot think but your age has forgot me.<br>It could not else be I should prove so base<br>To sue and be denied such common grace.<br>My wounds ache at you. |
| FIRST SENATOR | Do you dare our anger?<br>’Tis in few words, but spacious in effect:<br>We banish thee for ever. |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | Banish me?<br>Banish your dotage, banish usury<br>That makes the senate ugly. |
| FIRST SENATOR | If after two days’ shine<br>Athens contain thee, attend our weightier judgement;<br>And, not to swell your spirit, he shall be<br>Executed presently. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Senators and attendants |
| [[Alcibiades|ALCIBIADES]] | Now the gods keep you old enough that you may live<br>Only in bone, that none may look on you!<br>I’m worse than mad. I have kept back their foes<br>While they have told their money and let out<br>Their coin upon large interest—I myself,<br>Rich only in large hurts. All those for this?<br>Is this the balsam that the usuring senate<br>Pours into captains’ wounds? Banishment!<br>It comes not ill; I hate not to be banished.<br>It is a cause worthy my spleen and fury,<br>That I may strike at Athens. I’ll cheer up<br>My discontented troops, and lay for hearts.<br>’Tis [[Honour|honour]] with most lands to be at odds.<br>Soldiers should brook as little wrongs as gods. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
