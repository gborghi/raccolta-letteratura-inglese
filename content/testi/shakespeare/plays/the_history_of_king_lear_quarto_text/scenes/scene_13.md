| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of Gloucester]] and [[King Lear]], the Earl of [[Kent]] disguised, Lear’s [[Fool]], and [[Edgar]] as a Bedlam beggar |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Here is better than the open air; take it thankfully. I will piece out the comfort with what addition I can. I will not be long from you. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Kent|KENT]] All the power of his wits have given way to impatience; the gods discern your kindness! Exit [[Gloucester]] |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Frateretto calls me, and tells me Nero is an angler in the lake of darkness. Pray, [[Innocent|innocent]]; beware the foul fiend. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | (to Lear) Prithee, nuncle, tell me whether a madman be a gentleman or a yeoman. |
| LEAR | A [[King|king]], a [[King|king]]! To have a thousand With red burning spits come hissing in upon them! |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | The foul fiend bites my back. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | (to Lear) He’s mad that trusts in the tameness of a wolf, a [[Horse|horse]]’s health, a boy’s [[Love|love]], or a whore’s oath. |
| LEAR | It shall be done. I will arraign them straight. |
| *(didascalia)* | To [[Edgar]] Come, sit thou here, most learned justicer. |
| *(didascalia)* | To [[Fool]] Thou sapient sir, sit here.—No, you she- |
| LEAR | foxes— |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Look where he stands and glares. Want’st thou eyes at troll-madam? |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings Come o’er the burn, Bessy, to me. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Fool|FOOL]] sings |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Her boat hath a leak,<br>And she must not speak<br>Why she dares not come over to thee. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | The foul fiend haunts Poor Tom in the voice of a [[Nightingale|nightingale]]. Hoppedance cries in Tom’s belly for two white herring. Croak not, black angel: I have no food for thee. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Lear)<br>How do you, sir? Stand you not so amazed.<br>Will you lie down and rest upon the cushions? |
| LEAR | I’ll see their trial first. Bring in the evidence. |
| *(didascalia)* | To [[Edgar]] Thou robed man of justice, take thy place; |
| *(didascalia)* | To [[Fool]] And thou, his yokefellow of equity, |
| *(didascalia)* | Bench by his side. To [[Kent]] You are o’th’ |
| LEAR | commission,<br>Sit you, too. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Let us deal justly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Sleepest or wakest thou, jolly shepherd?<br>Thy sheep be in the corn,<br>And for one blast of thy minikin mouth<br>Thy sheep shall take no harm.<br>Purr, the cat is grey. |
| LEAR | Arraign her first. ’Tis Gonoril. I here take my oath before this honourable assembly she kicked the poor [[King]] her father. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Come hither, mistress. Is your name Gonoril? |
| LEAR | She cannot deny it. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Cry you [[Mercy|mercy]], I took you for a join-stool. |
| LEAR | And here’s another, whose warped looks proclaim<br>What store her heart is made on. Stop her there.<br>Arms, arms, [[Sword|sword]], [[Fire|fire]], corruption in the place!<br>False justicer, why hast thou let her scape? |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Bless thy five wits. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Lear)<br>O pity! Sir, where is the patience now<br>That you so oft have boasted to retain? |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | (aside)<br>My [[Tears|tears]] begin to take his part so much<br>They’ll mar my counterfeiting. |
| LEAR | The little [[dogs]] and all,<br>Tray, Blanch, and Sweetheart—see, they bark at me. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Tom will throw his head at them.—Avaunt, you curs!<br>Be thy mouth or black or white,<br>Tooth that poisons if it bite,<br>Mastiff, greyhound, mongrel grim,<br>Hound or spaniel, brach or him,<br>Bobtail tyke or trundle-tail,<br>Tom will make them weep and wail;<br>For with throwing thus my head,<br>[[dogs|Dogs]] leap the hatch, and all are fled.<br>Loudla, doodla! Come, march to wakes and fairs<br>And market towns. Poor Tom, thy horn is dry. |
| LEAR | Then let them anatomize [[Regan]]; see what breeds about her heart. Is there any cause in [[Nature|nature]] that makes this hardness? (To [[Edgar]]) You, sir, I entertain you for one of my hundred, only I do not like the fashion of your garments. You’ll say they are Persian attire; but let them be changed. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Now, good my lord, lie here a while. |
| LEAR | Make no noise, make no noise. Draw the curtains.<br>So, so, so. We’ll go to supper i’th’ morning. So, so, so.<br>He sleeps. Enter the [[Duke of Gloucester]] |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to [[Kent]])<br>Come hither, friend. Where is the [[King]] my master? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Here, sir, but trouble him not; his wits are gone. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Good friend, I prithee take him in thy arms.<br>I have o’erheard a plot of [[Death|death]] upon him.<br>There is a litter ready. Lay him in’t<br>And drive towards Dover, friend, where thou shalt meet<br>Both welcome and protection. Take up thy master.<br>If thou shouldst dally half an hour, his life,<br>With thine and all that offer to defend him,<br>Stand in assured loss. Take up, take up,<br>And follow me, that will to some provision<br>Give thee quick conduct. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Lear) Oppressed [[Nature|nature]] sleeps.<br>This rest might yet have balmed thy broken sinews<br>Which, if convenience will not allow,<br>Stand in hard cure. (To [[Fool]]) Come, help to bear thy<br>master.<br>Thou must not stay behind. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Come, come away. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Edgar]] |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | When we our betters see bearing our woes,<br>We scarcely think our miseries our foes.<br>Who [[Alone|alone]] suffers, suffers most i‘th’ mind,<br>Leaving free things and happy shows behind.<br>But then the mind much sufferance doth o’erskip<br>When grief hath mates, and bearing fellowship.<br>How light and portable my [[Pain|pain]] seems now,<br>When that which makes me bend, makes the [[King]]<br>bow.<br>He childed as I fathered. Tom, away.<br>Mark the high noises, and thyself bewray<br>When false opinion, whose wrong thoughts defile thee,<br>In thy just proof repeals and reconciles thee.<br>What will hap more tonight, safe scape the [[King]]!<br>Lurk, lurk. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
