| *(didascalia)* | [[Storm]]. Enter [[King Lear]] and his [[Fool]] |
| LEAR | Blow, [[Wind|wind]], and crack your cheeks! Rage, blow,<br>You cataracts and hurricanoes, spout<br>Till you have drenched the steeples, drowned the<br>cocks!<br>You sulphurous and thought-executing fires,<br>Vaunt-couriers to oak-cleaving thunderbolts,<br>Singe my white head; and thou all-shaking thunder,<br>Smite flat the thick rotundity of the world,<br>Crack [[Nature|nature]]’s mould, all germens spill at once<br>That make ingrateful man. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | O nuncle, [[Court|court]] holy [[Water|water]] in a dry [[house]] is better than this rain-[[Water|water]] out o’ door. Good nuncle, in, and ask thy daughters blessing. Here’s a [[Night|night]] pities neither wise man nor [[Fool|fool]]. |
| LEAR | Rumble thy bellyful; spit, [[Fire|fire]]; spout, rain.<br>Nor rain, [[Wind|wind]], thunder, [[Fire|fire]] are my daughters.<br>I tax not you, you elements, with unkindness.<br>I never gave you kingdom, called you children.<br>You owe me no subscription. Why then, let fall<br>Your horrible pleasure. Here I stand your slave,<br>A poor, infirm, weak and despised old man,<br>But yet I call you servile ministers,<br>That have with two pernicious daughters joined<br>Your high engendered battle ‘gainst a head<br>So old and white as this. O, ’tis foul! |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | He that has a [[house]] to put his head in has a good headpiece. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | The codpiece that will [[house]] Before the head has any,<br>The head and he shall louse,<br>So beggars marry many.<br><br>The man that makes his toe<br>What he his heart should make<br>Shall have a corn cry woe,<br>And turn his [[Sleep|sleep]] to wake—<br>for there was never yet fair woman but she made mouths in a glass. |
| LEAR | No, I will be the pattern of all patience. |
| *(didascalia)* | He sits. Enter the Earl of [[Kent]] disguised |
| LEAR | I will say nothing. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Who’s there? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Marry, here’s grace and a codpiece—that’s a wise man and a [[Fool|fool]]. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Lear)<br>Alas, sir, sit you here? Things that [[Love|love]] [[Night|night]]<br>[[Love]] not such nights as these. The wrathful skies<br>Gallow the very wanderers of the dark<br>And makes them keep their caves. Since I was man<br>Such sheets of [[Fire|fire]], such bursts of horrid thunder,<br>Such groans of roaring [[Wind|wind]] and rain I ne’er<br>Remember to have heard. Man’s [[Nature|nature]] cannot carry<br>The affliction nor the force. |
| LEAR | Let the great gods,<br>That keep this dreadful pother o’er our heads,<br>Find out their enemies now. Tremble, thou wretch<br>That hast within thee undivulgèd crimes<br>Unwhipped of justice; hide thee, thou bloody hand,<br>Thou perjured and thou simular man of virtue<br>That art incestuous; caitiff, in pieces shake,<br>That under covert and convenient seeming<br>Hast practised on man’s life;<br>Close pent-up guilts, rive your concealed centres<br>And cry these dreadful summoners grace.<br>I am a man more sinned against than sinning. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Alack, bare-headed?<br>Gracious my lord, hard by here is a hovel.<br>Some [[Friendship|friendship]] will it lend you ‘gainst the tempest.<br>Repose you there whilst I to this hard [[house]]-<br>More hard than is the stone whereof ’tis raised,<br>Which even but now, demanding after you,<br>Denied me to come in—return and force<br>Their scanted courtesy. |
| LEAR | My wit begins to turn.<br>(To [[Fool]]) Come on, my boy. How dost, my boy? Art<br>cold?<br>I am cold myself.—Where is this straw, my fellow?<br>The art of our necessities is strange,<br>That can make vile things precious. Come, your<br>hovel.—<br>Poor [[Fool|fool]] and knave, I have one part of my heart<br>That sorrows yet for thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Fool|FOOL]] sings |
| LEAR | He that has a little tiny wit,<br>With heigh-ho, the [[Wind|wind]] and the rain,<br>Must make content with his fortunes fit,<br>For the rain it raineth every day. |
| LEAR | True, my good boy. (To [[Kent]]) Come, bring us to this hovel. Exeunt |
