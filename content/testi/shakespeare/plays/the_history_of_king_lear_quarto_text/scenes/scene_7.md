| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of [[Kent]], disguised, at one door, and Oswald the steward, at another door |
| OSWALD | Good even to thee, friend. Art of the [[house]]? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Ay. |
| OSWALD | Where may we set our horses? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I’th’ mire. |
| OSWALD | Prithee, if thou [[Love|love]] me, tell me. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I [[Love|love]] thee not. |
| OSWALD | Why then, I care not for thee. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | If I had thee in Lipsbury pinfold I would make thee care for me. |
| OSWALD | Why dost thou use me thus? I know thee not. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Fellow, I know thee. |
| OSWALD | What dost thou know me for? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | A knave, a rascal, an eater of broken meats, a base, proud, shallow, beggarly, three-suited, hundred-pound, filthy worsted-stocking knave; a lily-livered, action-taking knave; a whoreson, glass-gazing, superfinical rogue; one-trunk-inheriting slave; one that wouldst be a bawd in way of good service, and art nothing but the composition of a knave, beggar, coward, pander, and the son and heir of a mongrel bitch, whom I will beat into clamorous whining if thou deny the least syllable of the addition. |
| OSWALD | What a monstrous fellow art thou, thus to rail on one that’s neither known of thee nor knows thee! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | What a brazen-faced varlet art thou, to deny thou knowest me! Is it two days ago since I beat thee and tripped up thy heels before the [[King]]? Draw, you rogue; for though it be [[Night|night]], the [[Moon|moon]] shines. |
| *(didascalia)* | He draws his [[Sword|sword]] |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I’ll make a sop of the moonshine o’ you. Draw, you whoreson, cullionly barber-monger, draw! |
| OSWALD | Away. I have nothing to do with thee. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Draw, you rascal. You bring letters against the [[King]], and take [[Vanity]] the puppet’s part against the royalty of her father. Draw, you rogue, or I’ll so carbonado your shanks—draw, you rascal, come your ways! |
| OSWALD | Help, ho, murder, help! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Strike, you slave! Stand, rogue! Stand, you neat slave, strike! |
| OSWALD | Help, ho, murder, help! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Edmund]] the bastard with his rapier drawn, then the [[Duke of Gloucester]], then the Duke of Cornwall and [[Regan]] the Duchess |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Edmund|EDMUND]] parting them How now, what’s the matter? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | With you, goodman boy. An you please come, I’ll flesh you. Come on, young master. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Weapons? Arms? What’s the matter here? |
| CORNWALL | Keep peace, upon your lives. He dies that strikes again. What’s the matter? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | The messengers from our sister and the [[King]]. |
| CORNWALL | (to [[Kent]] and Oswald) What’s your difference? Speak. |
| OSWALD | I am scarce in breath, my lord. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | No marvel, you have so bestirred your valour, you cowardly rascal. [[Nature]] disclaims in thee; a tailor made thee. |
| CORNWALL | Thou art a strange fellow—a tailor make a man? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Ay, a tailor, sir. A stone-cutter or a painter could not have made him so ill though he had been but two hours at the trade. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Speak yet; how grew your quarrel? |
| OSWALD | This ancient ruffian, sir, whose life I have spared at suit of his grey beard— |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Thou whoreson Z, thou unnecessary letter—(to Cornwall) my lord, if you’ll give me leave I will tread this unboulted [[Villain|villain]] into mortar and daub the walls of a jakes with him. (To Oswald) Spare my grey beard, you wagtail? |
| CORNWALL | Peace, sir. You beastly knave, have you no reverence? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Yes, sir, but anger has a privilege. |
| CORNWALL | Why art thou angry? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | That such a slave as this should wear a [[Sword|sword]],<br>That wears no honesty. Such smiling rogues<br>As these, like rats, oft bite those cords in twain<br>Which are too entrenched to unloose, smooth every<br>passion<br>That in the natures of their lords [[Rebel|rebel]],<br>Bring oil to [[Fire|fire]], [[Snow|snow]] to their colder moods,<br>Renege, affirm, and turn their halcyon beaks<br>With every gale and vary of their masters,<br>Knowing naught, like [[dogs]], but following.<br>(To Oswald) A plague upon your epileptic visage!<br>Smile you my speeches as I were a [[Fool|fool]]?<br>Goose, an I had you upon Sarum Plain<br>I’d send you cackling [[Home|home]] to Camelot. |
| CORNWALL | What, art thou mad, old fellow? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] to [[Kent]] How fell you out? Say that. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | No contraries hold more antipathy<br>Than I and such a knave. |
| CORNWALL | Why dost thou call him knave?<br>What’s his offence? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | His countenance likes me not. |
| CORNWALL | No more perchance does mine, or his, or hers. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Sir, ’tis my occupation to be plain:<br>I have seen better faces in my [[Time|time]]<br>Than stands on any shoulder that I see<br>Before me at this instant. |
| CORNWALL | This is a fellow<br>Who, having been praised for bluntness, doth affect<br>A saucy roughness, and constrains the garb<br>Quite from his [[Nature|nature]]. He cannot flatter, he.<br>He must be plain, he must speak [[Truth|truth]].<br>An they will take’t, so; if not, he’s plain.<br>These kind of knaves I know, which in this plainness<br>Harbour more craft and more corrupter ends<br>Than twenty silly-ducking observants<br>That stretch their duties nicely. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Sir, in good sooth, or in sincere verity,<br>Under the allowance of your grand aspect,<br>Whose influence, like the wreath of radiant [[Fire|fire]]<br>In flickering Phoebus’ front— |
| CORNWALL | What mean’st thou by this? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | To go out of my dialect, which you discommend so much. I know, sir, I am no flatterer. He that beguiled you in a plain accent was a plain knave, which for my part I will not be, though I should win your displeasure to entreat me to’t. |
| CORNWALL | (to Oswald)<br>What’s the offence you gave him? |
| OSWALD | I never gave him any.<br>It pleased the [[King]] his master very late<br>To strike at me upon his misconstruction,<br>When he, conjunct, and flattering his displeasure,<br>Tripped me behind; being down, insulted, railed,<br>And put upon him such a deal of man that<br>That worthied him, got praises of the [[King]]<br>For him attempting who was [[Self|self]]-subdued,<br>And in the fleshment of this dread exploit<br>Drew on me here again. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | None of these rogues and cowards<br>But [[Ajax]] is their [[Fool|fool]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | CORNWALL calling Bring forth the stocks, ho!— |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | You stubborn, ancient knave, you reverend braggart,<br>We’ll teach you. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I am too old to learn.<br>Call not your stocks for me. I serve the [[King]],<br>On whose employments I was sent to you.<br>You should do small respect, show too bold malice<br>Against the grace and person of my master,<br>Stocking his messenger. |
| *(didascalia)* | CORNWALL calling Fetch forth the stocks!— |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | As I have life and [[Honour|honour]], there shall he sit till [[Noon|noon]]. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Till [[Noon|noon]]?—till [[Night|night]], my lord, and all [[Night|night]] too. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Why, madam, if I were your father’s dog<br>You could not use me so. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Sir, being his knave, I will. |
| *(didascalia)* | Stocks brought out |
| CORNWALL | This is a fellow of the selfsame [[Nature|nature]]<br>Our sister speaks of.—Come, bring away the stocks. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Let me beseech your grace not to do so.<br>His fault is much, and the good [[King]] his master<br>Will check him for’t. Your purposed low correction<br>Is such as basest and contemnèd wretches<br>For pilf’rings and most common trespasses<br>Are punished with. The [[King]] must take it ill<br>That he’s so slightly valued in his messenger,<br>Should have him thus restrained. |
| CORNWALL | I’ll answer that. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | My sister may receive it much more worse<br>To have her gentlemen abused, assaulted,<br>For following her affairs. Put in his legs.<br>They put [[Kent]] in the stocks<br>Come, my good lord, away! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Gloucester]] and [[Kent]] |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I am sorry for thee, friend. ’Tis the Duke’s pleasure,<br>Whose disposition, all the world [[Well|well]] knows,<br>Will not be rubbed nor stopped. I’ll entreat for thee. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Pray you, do not, sir. I have watched and travelled<br>hard.<br>Some [[Time|time]] I shall [[Sleep|sleep]] out; the rest I’ll whistle.<br>A good man’s fortune may grow out at heels.<br>Give you good morrow. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | The Duke’s to blame in this; ’twill be ill took. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Good [[King]], that must approve the common say:<br>Thou out of heaven’s benediction com’st<br>To the warm sun. |
| *(didascalia)* | He takes out a letter |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Approach, thou beacon to this under globe,<br>That by thy comfortable beams I may<br>Peruse this letter. Nothing almost sees miracles<br>But misery. I know ’tis from [[Cordelia]],<br>Who hath now fortunately been informed<br>Of my obscured course, and shall find [[Time|time]]<br>For this enormous state, seeking to give<br>Losses their remedies. All weary and overwatched,<br>Take vantage, heavy eyes, not to behold<br>This shameful lodging. Fortune, good [[Night|night]];<br>Smile; once more turn thy [[Wheel|wheel]].<br>He sleeps |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Edgar]] |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | I heard myself proclaimed,<br>And by the happy hollow of a tree<br>Escaped the hunt. No port is free, no place<br>That guard and most unusual vigilance<br>Does not attend my taking. While I may scape<br>I will preserve myself, and am bethought<br>To take the basest and most poorest shape<br>That ever penury in contempt of man<br>Brought near to beast. My face I’ll grime with filth,<br>Blanket my loins, elf all my hair with knots,<br>And with presented [[Nakedness|nakedness]] outface<br>The [[Wind|wind]] and persecution of the sky.<br>The country gives me proof and precedent<br>Of Bedlam beggars who with roaring voices<br>Strike in their numbed and mortified bare arms<br>Pins, wooden pricks, nails, sprigs of rosemary,<br>And with this horrible object from low farms,<br>Poor pelting villages, sheep-cotes and mills<br>Sometime with lunatic bans, sometime with prayers<br>Enforce their [[Charity|charity]]. ‘Poor Tuelygod, Poor Tom!’<br>That’s something yet. [[Edgar]] I nothing am. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King Lear]], his [[Fool]], and a Knight |
| LEAR | ’Tis strange that they should so depart from [[Home|home]]<br>And not send back my messenger. |
| KNIGHT | As I learned,<br>The [[Night|night]] before there was no purpose<br>Of his remove. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (waking) Hail to thee, noble master. |
| LEAR | How! Mak’st thou this shame thy pastime? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Ha, ha, look, he wears cruel garters! Horses are tied by the heads, [[dogs]] and bears by th’ neck, monkeys by th’ loins, and men by th’ legs. When a man’s over-lusty at legs, then he wears wooden nether-stocks. |
| LEAR | (to [[Kent]])<br>What’s he that hath so much thy place mistook<br>To set thee here? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | It is both he and she:<br>Your son and daughter. |
| LEAR | No. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Yes. |
| LEAR | No, I say. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I say yea. |
| LEAR | No, no, they would not. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Yes, they have. |
| LEAR | By Jupiter, I swear no. They durst not do‘t,<br>They would not, could not do’t. ’Tis worse than murder,<br>To do upon respect such violent outrage.<br>Resolve me with all modest haste which way<br>Thou mayst deserve or they propose this usage,<br>Coming from us. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | My lord, when at their [[Home|home]]<br>I did commend your highness’ letters to them,<br>Ere I was risen from the place that showed<br>My duty kneeling, came there a reeking post<br>Stewed in his haste, half breathless, panting forth<br>From Gonoril, his mistress, salutations,<br>Delivered letters spite of intermission,<br>Which presently they read, on whose contents<br>They summoned up their meiny, straight took [[Horse|horse]],<br>Commanded me to follow and attend<br>The leisure of their answer, gave me cold looks;<br>And meeting here the other messenger,<br>Whose welcome I perceived had poisoned mine—<br>Being the very fellow that of late<br>Displayed so saucily against your highness—<br>Having more man than wit about me, drew.<br>He raised the [[house]] with loud and coward cries.<br>Your son and daughter found this trespass worth<br>This shame which here it suffers. |
| LEAR | O, how this mother swells up toward my heart!<br>Histerica passio, down, thou climbing sorrow;<br>Thy element’s betow.—Where is this daughter? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | With the Earl, sir, within. |
| LEAR | Follow me not; stay there. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| KNIGHT | (to [[Kent]])<br>Made you no more offence than what you speak of? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | No. How chance the [[King]] comes with so small a train? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | An thou hadst been set in the stocks for that question, thou hadst [[Well|well]] deserved it. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Why, [[Fool|fool]]? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | We’ll set thee to school to an ant, to teach thee there’s no labouring in the [[Winter|winter]]. All that follow their noses are led by their eyes but blind men, and there’s not a nose among a hundred but can smell him that’s stinking. Let go thy hold when a great [[Wheel|wheel]] runs down a [[Hill|hill]], lest it break thy neck with following it; but the great one that goes up the [[Hill|hill]], let him draw thee after. When a wise man gives thee better counsel, give me mine again. I would have none but knaves follow it, since a [[Fool|fool]] gives it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | That sir that serves for gain And follows but for form,<br>Will pack when it begin to rain,<br>And leave thee in the [[Storm|storm]].<br><br>But I will tarry, the [[Fool|fool]] will stay,<br>And let the wise man fly.<br>The knave turns [[Fool|fool]] that runs away,<br>The [[Fool|fool]] no knave, pardie. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Where learnt you this, [[Fool|fool]]? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Not in the stocks. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King Lear]] and the [[Duke of Gloucester]] |
| LEAR | Deny to speak with me? They’re sick, they’re weary?<br>They travelled hard tonight?—mere insolence,<br>Ay, the images of revolt and flying off.<br>Fetch me a better answer. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | My dear lord,<br>You know the fiery quality of the Duke,<br>How unremovable and fixed he is<br>In his own course. |
| LEAR | Vengeance, [[Death|death]], plague, confusion! What ‘fiery quality’? Why, [[Gloucester]], [[Gloucester]], I’d Speak with the Duke of Cornwall and his wife. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Ay, my good lord. |
| LEAR | The [[King]] would speak with Cornwall; the dear father<br>Would with his daughter speak, commands, tends<br>service.<br>‘Fiery’? The Duke?—tell the hot Duke that Lear—<br>No, but not yet. Maybe he is not [[Well|well]].<br>Infirmity doth still neglect all office<br>Whereto our health is bound. We are not ourselves<br>When [[Nature|nature]], being oppressed, commands the mind<br>To suffer with the [[Body|body]]. I’ll forbear,<br>And am fallen out with my more headier will,<br>To take the indisposed and sickly fit<br>For the sound man.—[[Death]] on my state,<br>Wherefore should he sit here? This act persuades me<br>That this remotion of the Duke and her<br>Is practice only. Give me my servant forth.<br>Tell the Duke and ’s wife I’ll speak with them,<br>Now, presently. Bid them come forth and hear me,<br>Or at their chamber door I’ll beat the drum<br>Till it cry [[Sleep|sleep]] to [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I would have all [[Well|well]]<br>Betwixt you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| LEAR | O, my heart, my heart! |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Cry to it, nuncle, as the cockney did to the eels when she put ‘em i’th’ paste alive. She rapped ‘em o’th’ coxcombs with a stick, and cried ‘Down, wantons, down!’ ’Twas her brother that, in pure kindness to his [[Horse|horse]], buttered his hay. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Duke of Cornwall and [[Regan]], the [[Duke of Gloucester]], and others |
| LEAR | Good morrow to you both. |
| CORNWALL | Hail to your grace. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Kent]] here set at liberty |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | I am glad to see your highness. |
| LEAR | [[Regan]], I think you are. I know what reason<br>I have to think so. If thou shouldst not be glad<br>I would [[divorce]] me from thy mother’s shrine,<br>Sepulchring an adultress. (To [[Kent]]) Yea, are you free?<br>Some other [[Time|time]] for that.—Belovèd [[Regan]],<br>Thy sister is naught. O, [[Regan]], she hath tied<br>Sharp-toothed unkindness like a vulture here.<br>I can scarce speak to thee. Thou’lt not believe<br>Of how deplored a quality—O, [[Regan]]! |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | I pray you, sir, take patience. I have [[Hope|hope]]<br>You less know how to value her [[Desert|desert]]<br>Than she to slack her duty. |
| LEAR | My curses on her. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | O sir, you are old.<br>[[Nature]] in you stands on the very verge<br>Of her confine. You should be ruled and led<br>By some discretion that discerns your state<br>Better than you yourself. Therefore I pray<br>That to our sister you do make return;<br>Say you have wronged her, sir. |
| LEAR | Ask her [[Forgiveness|forgiveness]]?<br>Do you mark how this becomes the [[house]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | Kneeling ‘Dear daughter, I confess that I am old. |
| LEAR | Age is unnecessary. On my knees I beg<br>That you’ll vouchsafe me raiment, bed, and food.’ |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Good sir, no more. These are unsightly tricks.<br>Return you to my sister. |
| *(didascalia)* | LEAR rising No, [[Regan]]. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | She hath abated me of half my train,<br>Looked black upon me, struck me with her tongue<br>Most serpent-like upon the very heart.<br>All the stored vengeances of heaven fall<br>On her ungrateful top! Strike her young bones,<br>You taking airs, with lameness! |
| CORNWALL | Fie, fie, sir. |
| LEAR | You nimble lightnings, dart your blinding flames<br>Into her scornful eyes. Infect her [[Beauty|beauty]],<br>You fen-sucked fogs drawn by the pow’rful sun<br>To fall and blast her [[Pride|pride]]. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | O, the blest gods!<br>So will you wish on me when the rash mood— |
| LEAR | No, [[Regan]]. Thou shalt never have my curse.<br>Thy tender-hested [[Nature|nature]] shall not give<br>Thee o‘er to harshness. Her eyes are fierce, but thine<br>Do comfort and not burn. ’Tis not in thee<br>To grudge my pleasures, to cut off my train,<br>To bandy hasty words, to scant my sizes,<br>And, in conclusion, to oppose the bolt<br>Against my coming in. Thou better know’st<br>The offices of [[Nature|nature]], bond of [[Childhood|childhood]],<br>Effects of courtesy, dues of gratitude.<br>Thy half of the kingdom hast thou not forgot,<br>Wherein I thee endowed. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Good sir, to th’ purpose. |
| LEAR | Who put my man i’th’ stocks? |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets within |
| CORNWALL | What trumpet’s that? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Oswald the steward |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | I know’t, my sister’s. This approves her letters<br>That she would soon be here. (To Oswald) Is your lady<br>come? |
| LEAR | This is a slave whose easy-borrowed [[Pride|pride]]<br>Dwells in the fickle grace of her a follows. |
| *(didascalia)* | He strikes Oswald |
| LEAR | Out, varlet, from my sight! |
| CORNWALL | What means your grace? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Gonoril |
| GONORIL | Who struck my servant? [[Regan]], I have good [[Hope|hope]]<br>Thou didst not know on’t. |
| LEAR | Who comes here? O heavens,<br>If you do [[Love|love]] old men, if your sweet sway<br>Allow obedience, if yourselves are old,<br>Make it your cause! Send down and take my part.<br>(To Gonoril) Art not ashamed to look upon this<br>beard?<br>O [[Regan]], wilt thou take her by the hand? |
| GONORIL | Why not by the hand, sir? How have I offended?<br>All’s not offence that indiscretion finds<br>And dotage terms so. |
| LEAR | O sides, you are too tough!<br>Will you yet hold?—How came my man i’th’ stocks? |
| CORNWALL | I set him there, sir; but his own disorders<br>Deserved much less advancement. |
| LEAR | You? Did you? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | I pray you, father, being weak, seem so.<br>If till the expiration of your month<br>You will return and sojourn with my sister,<br>Dismissing half your train, come then to me.<br>I am now from [[Home|home]], and out of that provision<br>Which shall be needful for your entertainment. |
| LEAR | Return to her, and fifty men dismissed?<br>No, rather I abjure all roofs, and choose<br>To be a comrade with the wolf and owl,<br>To wage against the enmity of the air<br>Necessity’s sharp pinch. Return with her?<br>Why, the hot-[[Blood|blood]] in France that dowerless took<br>Our youngest born—I could as [[Well|well]] be brought<br>To knee his throne and, squire-like, pension beg<br>To keep base life afoot. Return with her?<br>Persuade me rather to be slave and sumpter<br>To this detested groom. |
| GONORIL | At your choice, sir. |
| LEAR | Now I prithee, daughter, do not make me mad.<br>I will not trouble thee, my [[Child|child]]. Farewell.<br>We’ll no more meet, no more see one another.<br>But yet thou art my flesh, my [[Blood|blood]], my daughter—<br>Or rather a disease that lies within my flesh,<br>Which I must needs call mine. Thou art a boil,<br>A plague-sore, an embossed carbuncle<br>In my corrupted [[Blood|blood]]. But I’ll not chide thee.<br>Let shame come when it will, I do not call it.<br>I do not bid the thunder-bearer shoot,<br>Nor tell tales of thee to high-judging Jove.<br>Mend when thou canst; be better at thy leisure.<br>I can be patient, I can stay with [[Regan]],<br>I and my hundred knights. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Not altogether so, sir.<br>I look not for you yet, nor am provided<br>For your fit welcome. Give ear, sir, to my sister;<br>For those that mingle reason with your passion<br>Must be content to think you are old, and so—<br>But she knows what she does. |
| LEAR | Is this [[Well|well]] spoken now? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | I dare avouch it, sir. What, fifty followers?<br>Is it not [[Well|well]]? What should you need of more,<br>Yea, or so many, sith that both charge and danger<br>Speaks ‘gainst so great a number? How in a [[house]]<br>Should many people under two commands<br>Hold amity? ’Tis hard, almost impossible. |
| GONORIL | Why might not you, my lord, receive attendance<br>From those that she calls servants, or from mine? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Why not, my lord? If then they chanced to slack you,<br>We could control them. If you will come to me—<br>For now I spy a danger—I entreat you<br>To bring but five-and-twenty; to no more<br>Will I give place or notice. |
| LEAR | I gave you all. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | And in good [[Time|time]] you gave it. |
| LEAR | Made you my guardians, my depositaries,<br>But kept a reservation to be followed<br>With such a number. What, must I come to you<br>With five-and-twenty, [[Regan]]? Said you so? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | And speak’t again, my lord. No more with me. |
| LEAR | Those wicked creatures yet do seem [[Well|well]] favoured<br>When others are more wicked. Not being the worst<br>Stands in some rank of praise. (To Gonoril) I’ll go with<br>thee.<br>Thy fifty yet doth [[Double|double]] five-and-twenty,<br>And thou art twice her [[Love|love]]. |
| GONORIL | Hear me, my lord.<br>What need you five-and-twenty, ten, or five,<br>To follow in a [[house]] where twice so many<br>Have a command to tend you? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | What needs one? |
| LEAR | O, reason not the need! Our basest beggars<br>Are in the poorest thing superfluous.<br>Allow not [[Nature|nature]] more than [[Nature|nature]] needs,<br>Man’s life is cheap as beast’s. Thou art a lady.<br>If only to go warm were gorgeous,<br>Why, [[Nature|nature]] needs not what thou, gorgeous, wearest,<br>Which scarcely keeps thee warm. But for true need—<br>You heavens, give me that patience, patience I need.<br>You see me here, you gods, a poor old fellow,<br>As full of grief as age, wretched in both.<br>If it be you that stirs these daughters’ hearts<br>Against their father, [[Fool|fool]] me not so much<br>To bear it tamely. Touch me with noble anger.<br>O, let not women’s weapons, [[Water|water]]-drops,<br>Stain my man’s cheeks! No, you unnatural hags,<br>I will have such revenges on you both<br>That all the world shall—I will do such things—<br>What they are, yet I know not; but they shall be<br>The terrors of the earth. You think I’ll weep.<br>No, I’ll not weep. |

| LEAR | I have full cause of weeping, but this heart<br>Shall break into a hundred thousand flaws<br>Or ere I’ll weep.—O [[Fool|fool]], I shall go mad! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Lear, [[Gloucester]], [[Kent]], Knight, and [[Fool]] |
| CORNWALL | Let us withdraw. ’Twill be a [[Storm|storm]]. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | This [[house]] is little. The old man and his people<br>Cannot be [[Well|well]] bestowed. |
| GONORIL | ’Tis his own blame;<br>Hath put himself from rest, and must needs taste his folly. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | For his particular I’ll receive him gladly,<br>But not one follower. |
| CORNWALL | So am I purposed. Where is my lord of [[Gloucester]]? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Followed the old man forth. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of Gloucester]] |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | He is returned. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | The [[King]] is in high rage, and will I know not whither. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | ’Tis good to give him way. He leads himself. |
| GONORIL | (to [[Gloucester]])<br>My lord, entreat him by no means to stay. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Alack, the [[Night|night]] comes on, and the bleak winds<br>Do sorely rustle. For many miles about<br>There’s not a bush. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | O sir, to wilful men<br>The injuries that they themselves procure<br>Must be their schoolmasters. Shut up your doors.<br>He is attended with a desperate train,<br>And what they may incense him to, being apt<br>To have his ear abused, wisdom bids [[Fear|fear]]. |
| CORNWALL | Shut up your doors, my lord. ‘Tis a wild [[Night|night]].<br>My [[Regan]] counsels [[Well|well]]. Come out o’th’ [[Storm|storm]]. Exeunt |
