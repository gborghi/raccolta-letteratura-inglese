| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of [[Kent]], the [[Duke of Gloucester]], and [[Edmund]] the bastard |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I thought the [[King]] had more affected the Duke of Albany than Cornwall. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | It did always seem so to us, but now in the division of the kingdoms it appears not which of the Dukes he values most; for equalities are so weighed that curiosity in neither can make choice of either’s moiety. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Is not this your son, my lord? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | His breeding, sir, hath been at my charge. I have so often blushed to acknowledge him that now I am brazed to it. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I cannot conceive you. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Sir, this young fellow’s mother could, whereupon she grew round-wombed and had indeed, sir, a son for her cradle ere she had a husband for her bed. Do you smell a fault? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I cannot wish the fault undone, the issue of it being so proper. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | But I have, sir, a son by order of law, some year elder than this, who yet is no dearer in my account. Though this knave came something saucily into the world before he was sent for, yet was his mother fair, there was good sport at his making, and the whoreson must be acknowledged. (To [[Edmund]]) Do you know this noble gentleman, [[Edmund]]? |
| [[Edmund|EDMUND]] | No, my lord. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to [[Edmund]]) My lord of [[Kent]]. Remember him hereafter as my honourable friend. |
| [[Edmund|EDMUND]] | (to [[Kent]]) My services to your lordship. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I must [[Love|love]] you, and sue to know you better. |
| [[Edmund|EDMUND]] | Sir, I shall study deserving. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to [[Kent]]) He hath been out nine years, and away he shall again. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound a sennet |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | The [[King]] is coming. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter one bearing a coronet, then [[King Lear]], then the Dukes of Albany and Cornwall; next Gonoril, [[Regan]], [[Cordelia]], with followers |
| LEAR | Attend my lords of France and Burgundy, [[Gloucester]]. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I shall, my liege. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| LEAR | Meantime we will express our darker purposes.<br>The map there. Know we have divided<br>In three our kingdom, and ’tis our first intent<br>To shake all cares and business off our state,<br>Confirming them on younger years.<br>The two great princes, France and Burgundy—<br>Great rivals in our youngest daughter’s [[Love|love]]—<br>Long in our [[Court|court]] have made their amorous sojourn,<br>And here are to be answered. Tell me, my daughters,<br>Which of you shall we say doth [[Love|love]] us most,<br>That we our largest bounty may extend<br>Where merit doth most challenge it?<br>Gonoril, our eldest born, speak first. |
| GONORIL | Sir, I do [[Love|love]] you more than words can wield the<br>matter;<br>Dearer than eyesight, space, or liberty;<br>Beyond what can be valued, rich or rare;<br>No less than life; with grace, health, [[Beauty|beauty]], [[Honour|honour]];<br>As much as [[Child|child]] e’er loved, or father, friend;<br>A [[Love|love]] that makes breath poor and speech unable.<br>Beyond all manner of so much I [[Love|love]] you. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | (aside)<br>What shall [[Cordelia]] do? [[Love]] and be silent. |
| LEAR | (to Gonoril)<br>Of all these bounds even from this line to this,<br>With shady forests and wide skirted meads,<br>We make thee lady. To thine and Albany’s issue<br>Be this perpetual.—What says our second daughter?<br>Our dearest [[Regan]], wife to Cornwall, speak. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Sir, I am made<br>Of the [[Self|self]]-same mettle that my sister is,<br>And prize me at her worth. In my true heart<br>I find she names my very deed of [[Love|love]]—<br>Only she came short, that I profess<br>Myself an enemy to all other joys<br>Which the most precious square of sense possesses,<br>And find I am [[Alone|alone]] felicitate<br>In your dear highness’ [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | (aside) Then poor [[Cordelia]]—<br>And yet not so, since I am sure my [[Love|love]]’s<br>More richer than my tongue. |
| LEAR | (to [[Regan]])<br>To thee and thine hereditary ever<br>Remain this ample third of our fair kingdom, No less in space, validity, and pleasure<br>Than that confirmed on Gonoril. (To [[Cordelia]]) But<br>now our joy,<br>Although the last, not least in our dear [[Love|love]]:<br>What can you say to win a third more opulent<br>Than your sisters? |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Nothing, my lord. |
| LEAR | How? Nothing can come of nothing. Speak again. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Unhappy that I am, I cannot heave<br>My heart into my mouth. I [[Love|love]] your majesty<br>According to my bond, nor more nor less. |
| LEAR | Go to, go to, mend your speech a little<br>Lest it may mar your fortunes. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Good my lord,<br>You have begot me, bred me, loved me.<br>I return those duties back as are right fit—<br>Obey you, [[Love|love]] you, and most [[Honour|honour]] you.<br>Why have my sisters husbands if they say<br>They [[Love|love]] you all? Haply when I shall wed<br>That lord whose hand must take my plight shall carry<br>Half my [[Love|love]] with him, half my care and duty.<br>Sure, I shall never marry like my sisters,<br>To [[Love|love]] my father all. |
| LEAR | But goes this with thy heart? |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Ay, good my lord. |
| LEAR | So young and so untender? |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | So young, my lord, and true. |
| LEAR | [[Well]], let it be so. Thy [[Truth|truth]] then he thy dower;<br>For by the sacred radiance of the sun,<br>The mysteries of Hecate and the [[Night|night]],<br>By all the operation of the orbs<br>From whom we do exist and cease to be,<br>Here I disclaim all my paternal care,<br>Propinquity, and property of [[Blood|blood]],<br>And as a stranger to my heart and me<br>Hold thee from this for ever. The barbarous Scythian,<br>Or he that makes his generation<br>Messes to gorge his appetite,<br>Shall be as [[Well|well]] neighboured, pitied, and relieved<br>As thou, my sometime daughter. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Good my +liege- |
| LEAR | Peace, [[Kent]]. Come not between the dragon and his<br>wrath.<br>I loved her most, and thought to set my rest |
| *(didascalia)* | On her kind nursery. To [[Cordelia]] Hence, and avoid |
| LEAR | my sight!—<br>So be my [[Grave|grave]] my peace as here I give<br>Her father’s heart from her. Call France. Who stirs?<br>Call Burgundy. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one or more |
| LEAR | Cornwall and Albany,<br>With my two daughters’ dowers digest this third.<br>Let [[Pride|pride]], which she calls plainness, marry her.<br>I do invest you jointly in my power,<br>Pre-eminence, and all the large effects<br>That troop with majesty. Ourself by monthly course,<br>With reservation of an hundred knights<br>By you to be sustained, shall our abode<br>Make with you by due turns. Only we still retain<br>The name and all the additions to a [[King|king]].<br>The sway, revenue, execution of the rest,<br>Beloved sons, be yours; which to confirm,<br>This crownet part betwixt you. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Royal Lear,<br>Whom I have ever honoured as my [[King|king]],<br>Loved as my father, as my master followed,<br>As my great patron thought on in my prayers— |
| LEAR | The bow is bent and drawn; make from the shaft. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Let it fall rather, though the fork invade<br>The region of my heart. Be [[Kent]] unmannerly<br>When Lear is mad. What wilt thou do, old man?<br>Think’st thou that duty shall have dread to speak<br>When power to flattery bows? To plainness [[Honour|honour]]’s<br>bound<br>When majesty stoops to folly. Reverse thy doom,<br>And in thy best consideration check<br>This hideous rashness. Answer my life my judgement,<br>Thy youngest daughter does not [[Love|love]] thee least,<br>Nor are those empty-hearted whose low sound<br>Reverbs no hollowness. |
| LEAR | [[Kent]], on thy life, no more! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | My life I never held but as a pawn<br>To wage against thy enemies, nor [[Fear|fear]] to lose it,<br>Thy safety being the motive. |
| LEAR | Out of my sight! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | See better, Lear, and let me still remain<br>The true blank of thine eye. |
| LEAR | Now, by [[Apollo]]- |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Now, by [[Apollo]], [[King]], thou swear’st thy gods in vain. |
| *(didascalia)* | LEAR making to strike himl |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Vassal, recreant! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Do, kill thy physician,<br>And the fee bestow upon the foul disease.<br>Revoke thy doom, or whilst I can vent clamour<br>From my throat I’ll tell thee thou dost evil. |
| LEAR | Hear me; on thy allegiance hear me!<br>Since thou hast sought to make us break our vow,<br>Which we durst never yet, and with strayed [[Pride|pride]]<br>To come between our sentence and our power,<br>Which nor our [[Nature|nature]] nor our place can bear,<br>Our potency made good take thy reward:<br>Four days we do allot thee for provision<br>To shield thee from dis-eases of the world,<br>And on the fifth to turn thy hated back<br>Upon our kingdom. If on the next day following<br>Thy banished trunk be found in our dominions,<br>The moment is thy [[Death|death]]. Away! By Jupiter,<br>This shall not be revoked. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Why, fare thee [[Well|well]], [[King]]; since thus thou wilt<br>appear,<br>[[Friendship]] lives hence, and banishment is here.<br>(To [[Cordelia]]) The gods to their protection take thee,<br>maid,<br>That rightly thinks, and hast most justly said.<br>(To Gonoril and [[Regan]])<br>And your large speeches may your deeds approve,<br>That good effects may [[Spring|spring]] from words of [[Love|love]].<br>Thus [[Kent]], O princes, bids you all adieu;<br>He’ll shape his old course in a country new. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[King of France]] and the Duke of |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Burgundy, with the [[Duke of Gloucester]] |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Here’s France and Burgundy, my noble lord. |
| LEAR | My lord of Burgundy,<br>We first address towards you, who with a [[King|king]]<br>Hath rivalled for our daughter: what in the least<br>Will you require in present dower with her<br>Or cease your [[Quest|quest]] of [[Love|love]]? |
| BURGUNDY | Royal majesty,<br>I crave no more than what your highness offered;<br>Nor will you tender less. |
| LEAR | Right noble Burgundy,<br>When she was dear to us we did hold her so;<br>But now her price is fallen. Sir, there she stands.<br>If aught within that little seeming substance,<br>Or all of it, with our displeasure pieced,<br>And nothing else, may fitly like your grace,<br>She’s there, and she is yours. |
| BURGUNDY | I know no answer. |
| LEAR | Sir, will you with those infirmities she owes,<br>Unfriended, new-adopted to our hate,<br>Covered with our curse and strangered with our oath,<br>Take her or leave her? |
| BURGUNDY | Pardon me, royal sir.<br>Election makes not up on such conditions. |
| LEAR | Then leave her, sir; for by the power that made me,<br>I tell you all her wealth. (To France) For you, great<br>[[King]],<br>I would not from your [[Love|love]] make such a stray<br>To match you where I hate, therefore beseech you<br>To avert your liking a more worthier way<br>Than on a wretch whom [[Nature|nature]] is ashamed<br>Almost to acknowledge hers. |
| FRANCE | This is most strange, that she that even but now<br>Was your best object, the argument of your praise,<br>Balm of your age, most best, most dearest,<br>Should in this trice of [[Time|time]] commit a thing<br>So monstrous to dismantle<br>So many folds of favour. Sure, her offence<br>Must be of such unnatural degree<br>That monsters it, or your fore-vouched affections<br>Fall’n into taint; which to believe of her<br>Must be a [[Faith|faith]] that reason without miracle<br>Could never plant in me. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | (to Lear)<br>I yet beseech your majestyIf for I want that glib and oily art<br>To speak and purpose not—since what I [[Well|well]] intend,<br>I’ll do’t before I speak—that you acknow<br>It is no vicious blot, murder, or foulness,<br>No unclean action or dishonoured step<br>That hath deprived me of your grace and favour,<br>But even the want of that for which I am rich—<br>A still-soliciting eye, and such a tongue<br>As I am glad I have not, though not to have it<br>Hath lost me in your liking. |
| LEAR | Go to, go to.<br>Better thou hadst not been born than not to have<br>pleased me better. |
| FRANCE | Is it no more but this—a tardiness in [[Nature|nature]],<br>That often [[Leaves|leaves]] the [[history]] unspoke<br>That it intends to do?—My lord of Burgundy,<br>What say you to the lady? [[Love]] is not [[Love|love]]<br>When it is mingled with respects that stands<br>Aloof from the entire point. Will you have her?<br>She is herself a dower. |
| BURGUNDY | Royal Lear,<br>Give but that portion which yourself proposed,<br>And here I take [[Cordelia]] by the hand,<br>Duchess of Burgundy— |
| LEAR | Nothing. I have sworn. |
| BURGUNDY | (to [[Cordelia]])<br>I am sorry, then, you have so lost a father<br>That you must lose a husband. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Peace be with Burgundy; since that respects<br>Of fortune are his [[Love|love]], I shall not be his wife. |
| FRANCE | Fairest [[Cordelia]], that art most rich, being poor;<br>Most choice, forsaken; and most loved, despised:<br>Thee and thy virtues here I seize upon.<br>Be it lawful, I take up what’s cast away.<br>Gods, gods! ‘Tis strange that from their cold’st neglect<br>My [[Love|love]] should kindle to inflamed respect.—<br>Thy dowerless daughter, [[King]], thrown to my chance,<br>Is [[Queen|queen]] of us, of ours, and our fair France.<br>Not all the dukes in wat’rish Burgundy<br>Shall buy this unprized precious maid of me.—<br>Bid them farewell, [[Cordelia]], though unkind.<br>Thou losest here, a better where to find. |
| LEAR | Thou hast her, France. Let her be thine, for we<br>Have no such daughter, nor shall ever see<br>That face of hers again. Therefore be gone,<br>Without our grace, our [[Love|love]], our benison.—<br>Come, noble Burgundy.<br>[Flourish.! Exeunt Lear and Burgundy, then |
| *(didascalia)* | Albany, Cornwall, [[Gloucester]], [[Edmund]], |
| LEAR | and followers |
| FRANCE | (to [[Cordelia]]) Bid farewell to your sisters. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Ye jewels of our father, with washed eyes<br>[[Cordelia]] [[Leaves|leaves]] you. I know you what you are,<br>And like a sister am most loath to call<br>Your faults as they are named. Use [[Well|well]] our father.<br>To your professed bosoms I commit him.<br>But yet, alas, stood I within his grace<br>I would prefer him to a better place.<br>So farewell to you both. |
| GONORIL | Prescribe not us our duties. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Let your study<br>Be to content your lord, who hath received you<br>At fortune’s alms. You have obedience scanted,<br>And [[Well|well]] are worth the worst that you have wanted. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | [[Time]] shall unfold what pleated cunning hides.<br>Who covers faults, at last shame them derides.<br>[[Well]] may you prosper. |
| FRANCE | Come, fair [[Cordelia]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt France and [[Cordelia]] |
| GONORIL | Sister, it is not a little I have to say of what most nearly appertains to us both. I think our father will hence tonight. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | That’s most certain, and with you. Next month with us. |
| GONORIL | You see how full of changes his age is. The observation we have made of it hath not been little. He always loved our sister most, and with what poor judgement he hath now cast her off appears too gross. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | ’Tis the infirmity of his age; yet he hath ever but slenderly known himself. |
| GONORIL | The best and soundest of his [[Time|time]] hath been but rash; then must we look to receive from his age not [[Alone|alone]] the imperfection of long-engrafted condition, but therewithal unruly waywardness that infirm and choleric years bring with them. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Such unconstant starts are we like to have from him as this of [[Kent]]’s banishment. |
| GONORIL | There is further compliment of leave-taking between France and him. Pray, let’s hit together. If our father carry authority with such dispositions as he bears, this last surrender of his will but offend us. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | We shall further think on’t. |
| GONORIL | We must do something, and l’th’ heat. Exeunt |
