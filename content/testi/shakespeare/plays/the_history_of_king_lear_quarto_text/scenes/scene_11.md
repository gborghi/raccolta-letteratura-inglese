| *(didascalia)* | [[Storm]]. Enter [[King Lear]], the Earl of [[Kent]] disguised, and Lear’s [[Fool]] |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Here is the place, my lord. Good my lord, enter.<br>The tyranny of the open [[Night|night]]’s too rough<br>For [[Nature|nature]] to endure. |
| LEAR | Let me [[Alone|alone]]. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Good my lord, enter here. |
| LEAR | Wilt break my heart? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I had rather break mine own. Good my lord, enter. |
| LEAR | Thou think‘st ’tis much that this contentious [[Storm|storm]]<br>Invades us to the skin. So ‘tis to thee;<br>But where the greater malady is fixed,<br>The lesser is scarce felt. Thou’dst shun a bear,<br>But if thy flight lay toward the roaring sea<br>Thou‘dst meet the bear i’th’ mouth. When the mind’s<br>free,<br>The [[Body|body]]’s delicate. This tempest in my mind<br>Doth from my senses take all feeling else<br>Save what beats there: filial ingratitude.<br>Is it not as this mouth should tear this hand<br>For lifting food to’t? But I will punish sure.<br>No, I will weep no more.—<br>In such a [[Night|night]] as this! O [[Regan]], Gonoril,<br>Your old kind father, whose frank heart gave you all—<br>O, that way [[Madness|madness]] lies. Let me shun that.<br>No more of that. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Good my lord, enter. |
| LEAR | Prithee, go in thyself. Seek thy own ease.<br>This tempest will not give me leave to ponder<br>On things would hurt me more; but I’ll go in. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Fool]] |
| LEAR | Poor naked wretches, wheresoe‘er you are,<br>That bide the pelting of this pitiless [[Night|night]],<br>How shall your houseless heads and unfed sides,<br>Your looped and windowed raggedness, defend you<br>From [[Seasons|seasons]] such as these? O, I have ta’en<br>Too little care of this. Take physic, pomp,<br>Expose thyself to feel what wretches feel,<br>That thou mayst shake the superflux to them<br>And show the heavens more just. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lear’s [[Fool]] |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Come not in here, nuncle; here’s a spirit. Help me, help me! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Give me thy hand. Who’s there? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | A spirit. He says his name’s Poor Tom. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | What art thou that dost grumble there in the straw?<br>Come forth. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Edgar]] as a Bedlam beggar |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Away, the foul fiend follows me. Through the sharp hawthorn blows the cold [[Wind|wind]]. Go to thy cold bed and warm thee. |
| LEAR | Hast thou given all to thy two daughters,<br>And art thou come to this? |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Who gives anything to Poor Tom, whom the foul fiend hath led through [[Fire|fire]] and through ford and whirlypool, o’er bog and quagmire; that has laid knives under his pillow and halters in his pew, set ratsbane by his potage, made him proud of heart to ride on a bay trotting-[[Horse|horse]] over four-inched bridges, to course his own [[Shadow|shadow]] for a traitor. Bless thy five wits, Tom’s a-cold! Bless thee from whirlwinds, star-blasting, and taking. Do Poor Tom some [[Charity|charity]], whom the foul fiend vexes. There could I have him, now, and there, and there again. |
| LEAR | What, has his daughters brought him to this pass?<br>(To [[Edgar]]) Couldst thou save nothing? Didst thou give<br>them all? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Nay, he reserved a blanket, else we had been all shamed. |
| LEAR | (to [[Edgar]])<br>Now all the plagues that in the pendulous air<br>Hang fated o’er men’s faults fall on thy daughters! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | He hath no daughters, sir. |
| LEAR | [[Death]], traitor! Nothing could have subdued [[Nature|nature]]<br>To such a lowness but his unkind daughters.<br>(To [[Edgar]]) Is it the fashion that discarded fathers<br>Should have thus little [[Mercy|mercy]] on their flesh?<br>Judicious punishment: ’twas this flesh begot<br>Those pelican daughters. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Pillicock sat on pillicock’s [[Hill|hill]]; a lo, lo, lo. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | This cold [[Night|night]] will turn us all to fools and madmen. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Take heed o’th’ foul fiend; obey thy parents; keep thy word justly; swear not; commit not with man’s sworn spouse: set not thy sweet heart on proud array. Tom’s a-cold. |
| LEAR | What hast thou been? |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | A servingman, proud in heart and mind, that curled my hair, wore gloves in my cap, served the lust of my mistress’ heart, and did the act of darkness with her; swore as many oaths as I spake words, and broke them in the sweet face of heaven; one that slept in the contriving of lust, and waked to do it. Wine loved I deeply, dice dearly, and in woman out-paramoured the Turk. False of heart, light of ear, bloody of hand; hog in sloth, fox in stealth, wolf in greediness, dog in [[Madness|madness]], [[Lion|lion]] in prey. Let not the creaking of shoes nor the rustlings of silks betray thy poor heart to women. Keep thy foot out of brothel, thy hand out of placket, thy pen from lender’s book, and defy the foul fiend. Still through the hawthorn blows the cold [[Wind|wind]]. Heigh no nonny. Dolphin, my boy, my boy! Cease, let him trot by. |
| LEAR | Why, thou wert better in thy [[Grave|grave]] than to answer with thy uncovered [[Body|body]] this extremity of the skies. Is man no more but this? Consider him [[Well|well]]. Thou owest the worm no silk, the beast no hide, the sheep no wool, the cat no perfume. Here’s three on ’s are sophisticated; thou art the thing itself. Unaccommodated man is no more but such a poor, bare, forked animal as thou art. Off, off, you lendings! Come on, be true. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Prithee, nuncle, be content. This is a naughty [[Night|night]] to swim in. Now a little [[Fire|fire]] in a wild field were like an old lecher’s heart—a small spark, all the rest on ’s [[Body|body]] cold. Look, here comes a walking [[Fire|fire]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of Gloucester]] with a torch |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | This is the foul fiend Flibbertigibbet. He begins at curfew and walks till the first cock. He gives the web and the pin, squinies the eye, and makes the harelip; mildews the white wheat, and hurts the poor creature of earth. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Swithin footed thrice the wold,<br>A met the [[Night|night]] mare and her nine foal;<br>Bid her alight<br>And her troth plight,<br>And aroint thee, [[Witch|witch]], aroint thee! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Lear)<br>How fares your grace? |
| LEAR | What’s he? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to [[Gloucester]]) Who’s there? What is’t you seek? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | What are you there? Your names? |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Poor Tom, that eats the swimming frog, the toad, the tadpole, the wall-newt and the [[Water|water]]; that in the fury of his heart, when the foul fiend rages, eats cowdung for salads, swallows the old rat and the ditch-dog, drinks the green mantle of the standing pool; who is whipped from tithing to tithing, and stock-punished, and imprisoned; who hath had three suits to his back, six shirts to his [[Body|body]],<br>[[Horse]] to ride, and weapon to wear.<br>But mice and rats and such small deer<br>Hath been Tom’s food for seven long year-<br>Beware my follower. Peace, Smolking; peace, thou<br>fiend! |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to<br>Lear)<br>What, hath your grace no better company? |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | The Prince of Darkness is a gentleman;<br>Modo he’s called, and Mahu— |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to Lear)<br>Our flesh and [[Blood|blood]] is grown so vile, my lord,<br>That it doth hate what gets it. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Poor Tom’s a-cold. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to Lear)<br>Go in with me. My duty cannot suffer<br>To obey in all your daughters’ hard commands.<br>Though their injunction be to bar my doors<br>And let this tyrannous [[Night|night]] take hold upon you,<br>Yet have I ventured to come seek you out<br>And bring you where both food and [[Fire|fire]] is ready. |
| LEAR | First let me talk with this philosopher.<br>(To [[Edgar]]) What is the cause of thunder? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | My good lord,<br>Take his offer; go into the [[house]]. |
| LEAR | I’ll talk a word with this most learnèd Theban.<br>(To [[Edgar]]) What is your study? |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | How to prevent the fiend, and to kill vermin. |
| LEAR | Let me ask you one word in private.<br>They converse apart |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to [[Gloucester]])<br>Importune him to go, my lord.<br>His wits begin to unsettle. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Canst thou blame him?<br>His daughters seek his [[Death|death]]. O, that good [[Kent]],<br>He said it would be thus, poor banished man!<br>Thou sayst the [[King]] grows mad; I’ll tell thee, friend,<br>I am almost mad myself. I had a son,<br>Now outlawed from my [[Blood|blood]]; a sought my life<br>But lately, very late. I loved him, friend;<br>No father his son dearer. True to tell thee,<br>The grief hath crazed my wits. What a [[Night|night]]’s this!<br>(To Lear) I do beseech your grace— |
| LEAR | O, cry you [[Mercy|mercy]].<br>(To [[Edgar]]) Noble philosopher, your company. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | Tom’s a-cold. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | In, fellow, there in t’hovel; keep thee warm. |
| LEAR | Come, let’s in all. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | This way, my lord. |
| LEAR | With him!<br>I will keep still with my philosopher. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to [[Gloucester]])<br>Good my lord, soothe him; let him take the fellow. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Take him you on. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Kent|KENT]] to [[Edgar]] |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Sirrah, come on. Go along with us. |
| LEAR | (to [[Edgar]])<br>Come, good Athenian. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | No words, no words. Hush. |
| [[Edgar|EDGAR]] | [[Child]] Roland to the dark tower come, His word was still ‘Fie, fo, and fum; I smell the [[Blood|blood]] of a British man.’ |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
