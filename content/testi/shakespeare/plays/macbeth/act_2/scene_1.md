| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Banquo]] and Fleance, with a torch before him |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | How goes the [[Night|night]], boy? |
| FLEANCE | The [[Moon|moon]] is down. I have not heard the [[Clock|clock]]. |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | And she goes down at twelve. |
| FLEANCE | I take’t ’tis later, sir. |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | (giving Fleance his [[Sword|sword]])<br>Hold, take my [[Sword|sword]]. There’s husbandry in heaven,<br><br>Their candles are all out. Take thee that, too.<br>A heavy summons lies like lead upon me,<br>And yet I would not [[Sleep|sleep]]. Merciful powers,<br>Restrain in me the cursed thoughts that [[Nature|nature]]<br>Gives way to in repose. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Macbeth]], and a servant with a torch |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | Give me my [[Sword|sword]]. Who’s there? |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | A friend. |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | What, sir, not yet at rest? The [[King]]’s a-bed.<br>He hath been in unusual pleasure, and<br>Sent forth great largesse to your offices.<br>This diamond he greets your wife withal<br>By the name of most kind hostess, and shut up<br>In measureless content. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Being unprepared<br>Our will became the servant to defect,<br>Which else should free have wrought. |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | All’s [[Well|well]].<br>I dreamt last [[Night|night]] of the three weird sisters.<br>To you they have showed some [[Truth|truth]]. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | I think not of them;<br>Yet, when we can entreat an hour to serve,<br>We would spend it in some words upon that business<br>If you would grant the [[Time|time]]. |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | At your kind’st leisure. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | If you shall cleave to my consent when ’tis,<br>It shall make [[Honour|honour]] for you. |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | So I lose none<br>In seeking to augment it, but still keep<br>My bosom franchised and allegiance clear,<br>I shall be counselled. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Good repose the while. |
| [[Banquo|BANQUO]] | Thanks, sir. The like to you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Banquo]] and Fleance |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | (to the Servant)<br>Go bid thy mistress, when my drink is ready,<br>She strike upon the bell. Get thee to bed. Exit Servant<br>Is this a dagger which I see before me,<br>The handle toward my hand? Come, let me clutch thee.<br>I have thee not, and yet I see thee still.<br>Art thou not, fatal vision, sensible<br>To feeling as to sight? Or art thou but<br>A dagger of the mind, a false creation<br>Proceeding from the heat-oppressed brain?<br>I see thee yet, in form as palpable<br>As this which now I draw.<br>Thou marshall‘st me the way that I was going,<br>And such an instrument I was to use.<br>Mine eyes are made the fools o’th’ other senses,<br>Or else worth all the rest. I see thee still,<br>And on thy blade and dudgeon gouts of [[Blood|blood]],<br>Which was not so before. There’s no such thing.<br>It is the bloody business which informs<br>Thus to mine eyes. Now o’er the one half-world<br>[[Nature]] seems dead, and wicked [[Dreams|dreams]] abuse<br>The curtained [[Sleep|sleep]]. Witchcraft celebrates<br>Pale Hecate’s offerings, and withered murder,<br>Alarumed by his sentinel the wolf,<br>Whose howl’s his watch, thus with his stealthy pace,<br>With [[Tarquin]]’s ravishing strides, towards his design<br>Moves like a [[Ghost|ghost]]. Thou sure and firm-set earth,<br>Hear not my steps which way they walk, for [[Fear|fear]]<br>Thy very stones prate of my whereabout,<br>And take the present horror from the [[Time|time]],<br>Which now suits with it. Whiles I threat, he lives.<br>Words to the heat of deeds too cold breath gives.<br>A bell rings<br><br>I go, and it is done. The bell invites me.<br>Hear it not, [[Duncan]]; for it is a knell<br>That summons thee to heaven or to hell. Exit |
