| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Macbeth]], the Doctor of Physic, and attendants |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Bring me no more reports. Let them fly all.<br>Till Birnam Wood remove to Dunsinane<br>I cannot taint with [[Fear|fear]]. What’s the boy [[Malcolm]]?<br>Was he not born of woman? The spirits that know<br>All mortal consequences have pronounced me thus:<br>‘[[Fear]] not, [[Macbeth]]. No man that’s born of woman<br>Shall e’er have power upon thee.’ Then fly, false thanes,<br>And mingle with the English epicures.<br>The mind I sway by and the heart I bear<br>Shall never sag with [[Doubt|doubt]] nor shake with [[Fear|fear]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Servant |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | The devil damn thee black, thou cream-faced loon!<br>Where gott’st thou that goose look? |
| SERVANT | There is ten thousand- |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Geese, [[Villain|villain]]? |
| SERVANT | Soldiers, sir. 15 |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Go prick thy face and over-red thy [[Fear|fear]],<br>Thou lily-livered boy. What soldiers, patch?<br>[[Death]] of thy [[Soul|soul]], those linen cheeks of thine<br>Are counsellors to [[Fear|fear]]. What soldiers, whey-face? |
| SERVANT | The English force, so please you. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Take thy face hence. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Servant |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Seyton!-I am sick at heart<br>When I behold-Seyton, I say!—This push<br>Will cheer me ever or disseat me now.<br>I have lived long enough. My way of life<br>Is fall’n into the sere, the yellow leaf,<br>And that which should accompany old age,<br>As [[Honour|honour]], [[Love|love]], obedience, troops of friends,<br>I must not look to have, but in their stead<br>Curses, not loud but deep, mouth-[[Honour|honour]], breath<br>Which the poor heart would fain deny and dare not.<br>Seyton! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Seyton |
| SEYTON | What’s your gracious pleasure? |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | What news more? |
| SEYTON | All is confirmed, my lord, which was reported. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | I’ll fight till from my bones my flesh be hacked.<br>Give me my armour. |
| SEYTON | ’Tis not needed yet. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | I’ll put it on.<br>Send out more horses. Skirr the country round.<br>Hang those that talk of [[Fear|fear]]. Give me mine armour.<br>How does your patient, doctor? |
| DOCTOR | Not so sick, my lord,<br>As she is troubled with thick-coming fancies<br>That keep her from her rest. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Cure her of that.<br>Canst thou not minister to a mind diseased,<br>Pluck from the [[Memory|memory]] a rooted sorrow,<br>Raze out the written troubles of the brain,<br>And with some sweet oblivious antidote<br>Cleanse the fraught bosom of that perilous stuff<br>Which weighs upon the heart? |
| DOCTOR | Therein the patient<br>Must minister to himself. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | Throw physic to the [[dogs]]; I’ll none of it.<br>(To an attendant) Come, put mine armour on. Give me<br>my staff.<br>Seyton, send out. Doctor, the thanes fly from me.<br>(To an attendant) Come, sir, dispatch.—If thou couldst,<br>doctor, cast<br>The [[Water|water]] of my land, find her disease,<br>And purge it to a sound and pristine health,<br>I would applaud thee to the very echo,<br>That should applaud again. (To an attendant) Pull’t off,<br>I say.<br>(To the Doctor) What rhubarb, cyme, or what<br>purgative drug<br>Would scour these English hence? Hear’st thou of<br>them? |
| DOCTOR | Ay, my good lord. Your royal preparation<br>Makes us hear something. |
| [[Macbeth|MACBETH]] | (To an attendant) Bring it after me.<br>I will not be afraid of [[Death|death]] and bane<br>Till Birnam Forest come to Dunsinane. |
| DOCTOR | (aside)<br>Were I from Dunsinane away and clear,<br>Profit again should hardly draw me here. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
