| YORK | To heave the traitor Somerset from hence, And fight against that monstrous [[Rebel|rebel]] Cade, Who since I heard to be discomfited. Enter Iden with Cade’s head |
| IDEN | If one so rude and of so mean condition May pass into the presence of a [[King|king]], Kneeling Lo, I present your grace a traitor’s head, The head of Cade, whom I in combat slew. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | The head of Cade? Great God, how just art thou! O let me view his visage, being dead, That living wrought me such exceeding trouble. Tell me, my friend, art thou the man that slew him? |
| IDEN | rising Iwis, an’t like your majesty. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How art thou called? And what is thy degree? |
| IDEN | Alexander Iden, that’s my name; A poor esquire of [[Kent]] that loves his [[King|king]]. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | (to [[King]] Henry) So please it you, my lord, ’twere not amiss He were created knight for his good service. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Iden, kneel down. Iden kneels and [[King]] Henry knights him |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Rise up a knight. Iden rises We give thee for reward a thousand marks, And will that thou henceforth attend on us. |
| IDEN | May Iden live to merit such a bounty, And never live but true unto his liege. Exit Enter [[Queen Margaret]] and the Duke of Somerset |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | See, [[Buckingham]], Somerset comes wi’th’ [[Queen]]. Go bid her hide him quickly from the Duke. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | For thousand Yorks he shall not hide his head, But boldly stand and front him to his face. |
| YORK | How now? Is Somerset at liberty? Then, York, unloose thy long imprisoned thoughts, And let thy tongue be equal with thy heart. Shall I endure the sight of Somerset? False [[King]], why hast thou broken [[Faith|faith]] with me, Knowing how hardly I can brook abuse? ‘[[King]]’ did I call thee? No, thou art not [[King|king]]; Not fit to govern and rule multitudes, Which dar’st not—no, nor canst not—rule a traitor. That head of thine doth not become a [[Crown|crown]]; Thy hand is made to grasp a palmer’s staff, And not to grace an aweful princely sceptre. That gold must round engird these brows of mine, Whose smile and frown, like to [[Achilles]]’ spear, Is able with the change to kill and cure. Here is a hand to hold a sceptre up, And with the same to act controlling laws. Give place! By heaven, thou shalt rule no more O’er him whom heaven created for thy ruler. |
| SOMERSET | O monstrous traitor! I arrest thee, York, Of capital treason ’gainst the [[King]] and [[Crown|crown]]. Obey, audacious traitor; kneel for grace. |
| YORK | (to an attendant) Sirrah, call in my sons to be my bail. Exit attendant |
| YORK | I know, ere they will have me go to ward, They’ll pawn their swords for my enfranchisement. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | to [[Buckingham]] Call hither [[Clifford]]; bid him come amain, To say if that the bastard boys of York Shall be the surety for their traitor father. Exit [[Buckingham]] |
| YORK | O [[Blood|blood]]-bespotted Neapolitan, Outcast of Naples, England’s bloody scourge! The sons of York, thy betters in their birth, Shall be their father’s bail, and bane to those That for my surety will refuse the boys. Enter at one door York’s sons Edward and crookback [[Richard]] with a drummer and soldiers See where they come. I’ll warrant they’ll make it good. Enter at the other door [[Clifford]] and his son, with a drummer and soldiers |
