| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of York]] and his army of Irish with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| YORK | From Ireland thus comes York to claim his right, And pluck the [[Crown|crown]] from feeble Henry’s head. Ring, [[Bells|bells]], aloud; burn, bonfires, clear and bright, To entertain great England’s lawful [[King|king]]. Ah, sancta maiestas! Who would not buy thee dear? Let them obey that knows not how to rule; This hand was made to handle naught but gold. I cannot give due action to my words, Except a [[Sword|sword]] or sceptre balance it. A sceptre shall it have, have I as word, On which I’ll toss the fleur-de-lis of France. Enter the [[Duke of Buckingham]] |
| YORK | (Aside) Whom have we here? [[Buckingham]] to disturb me? The [[King]] hath sent him sure—I must dissemble. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | York, if thou meanest [[Well|well]], I greet thee [[Well|well]]. |
| YORK | Humphrey of [[Buckingham]], I accept thy greeting. Art thou a messenger, or come of pleasure? |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | A messenger from Henry, our dread liege, To know the reason of these arms in peace; Or why thou, being a subject as I am, Against thy oath and true allegiance sworn, Should raise so great a power without his leave, Or dare to bring thy force so near the [[Court|court]]? |
| YORK | (aside) Scarce can I speak, my choler is so great. O, I could hew up rocks and fight with flint, I am so angry at these abject terms; And now, like [[Ajax]] Telamonius, On sheep or oxen could I spend my fury. I am far better born than is the [[King]], More like a [[King|king]], more kingly in my thoughts; But I must make fair weather yet a while, Till Henry be more weak and I more strong. (Aloud) [[Buckingham]], I prithee pardon me, That I have given no answer all this while; My mind was troubled with deep [[Melancholy|melancholy]]. The cause why I have brought this army hither Is to remove proud Somerset from the [[King]], Seditious to his grace and to the state. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | That is too much presumption on thy part; But if thy arms be to no other end, The [[King]] hath yielded unto thy demand: The Duke of Somerset is in the Tower. |
| YORK | Upon thine [[Honour|honour]], is he prisoner? |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | Upon mine [[Honour|honour]], he is prisoner. |
| YORK | Then, [[Buckingham]], I do dismiss my powers. Soldiers, I thank you all; disperse yourselves; Meet me tomorrow in Saint George’s field. You shall have pay and everything you wish. Exeunt soldiers (To [[Buckingham]]) And let my sovereign, virtuous Henry, Command my eldest son—nay, all my sons— As pledges of my fealty and [[Love|love]]. I’ll send them all as willing as I live. Lands, goods, [[Horse|horse]], armour, anything I have Is his to use, so Somerset may die. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | York, I commend this kind submission. We twain will go into his highness’ tent. Enter [[King]] Henry and attendants |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | [[Buckingham]], doth York intend no harm to us, That thus he marcheth with thee arm in arm? |
| YORK | In all submission and [[Humility|humility]] York doth present himself unto your highness. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Then what intends these forces thou dost bring? |
