| WARWICK | (to [[King]] Henry) It is reported, mighty sovereign, That good Duke Humphrey traitorously is murdered By Suffolk and the [[Cardinal Beaufort]]’s means. The commons, like an angry hive of [[Bees|bees]] That want their leader, scatter up and down And care not who they sting in his [[Revenge|revenge]]. Myself have calmed their spleenful mutiny, Until they hear the order of his [[Death|death]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | That he is dead, good Warwick, ’tis too true. But how he died God knows, not Henry. Enter his chamber, view his breathless corpse, And comment then upon his sudden [[Death|death]]. |
| WARWICK | That shall I do, my liege.—Stay, Salisbury, With the rude multitude till I return. Exeunt Warwick at one door, Salisbury and |
| *(didascalia)* | commons at another |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O thou that judgest all things, stay my thoughts, My thoughts that labour to persuade my [[Soul|soul]] Some violent hands were laid on Humphrey’s life. If my suspect be false, forgive me God, For judgement only doth belong to thee. Fain would I go to chafe his paly lips With twenty thousand kisses, and to drain Upon his face an ocean of salt [[Tears|tears]], To tell my [[Love|love]] unto his dumb, deaf trunk, And with my fingers feel his hand unfeeling. But all in vain are these mean obsequies, Enter Warwick who draws apart the curtains and |
| *(didascalia)* | shows [[Gloucester]] dead in his bed. Bed put forth And to survey his dead and earthy image, What were it but to make my sorrow greater? |
| WARWICK | Come hither, gracious sovereign, view this [[Body|body]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | That is to see how deep my [[Grave|grave]] is made: For with his [[Soul|soul]] fled all my worldly solace, For seeing him I see my life in [[Death|death]]. |
| WARWICK | As surely as my [[Soul|soul]] intends to live With that dread [[King]] that took our state upon Him To free us from his Father’s wrathful curse, I do believe that violent hands were laid Upon the life of this thrice-famed Duke. |
| SUFFOLK | A dreadful oath, sworn with a solemn tongue! What instance gives Lord Warwick for his vow? |
| WARWICK | See how the [[Blood|blood]] is settled in his face. Oft have I seen a timely-parted [[Ghost|ghost]] Of ashy semblance, meagre, pale, and bloodless, Being all descended to the labouring heart; Who, in the conflict that it holds with [[Death|death]], Attracts the same for aidance ‘gainst the enemy; Which, with the heart, there cools, and ne’er returneth To blush and beautify the cheek again. But see, his face is black and full of [[Blood|blood]]; His eyeballs further out than when he lived, Staring full ghastly like a strangled man; His hair upreared; his nostrils stretched with struggling; His hands abroad displayed, as one that grasped And tugged for life and was by strength subdued. Look on the sheets. His hair, you see, is sticking; His [[Well|well]]-proportioned beard made rough and rugged, Like to the [[Summer|summer]]’s corn by tempest lodged. It cannot be but he was murdered here. The least of all these signs were probable. |
| SUFFOLK | Why, Warwick, who should do the Duke to [[Death|death]]? Myself and Beaufort had him in protection, And we, I [[Hope|hope]], sir, are no murderers. |
