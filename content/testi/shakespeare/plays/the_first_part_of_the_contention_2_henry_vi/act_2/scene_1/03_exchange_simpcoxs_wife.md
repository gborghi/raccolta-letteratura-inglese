| SIMPCOX’S WIFE | (to [[Gloucester]]) Too true—and bought his climbing very dear. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to Simpcox) Mass, thou loved’st plums [[Well|well]] that wouldst venture so. |
| SIMPCOX | Alas, good master, my wife desired some damsons, And made me climb with danger of my life. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | aside A subtle knave, but yet it shall not serve. (To Simpcox) Let me see thine eyes: wink now, now open them. In my opinion yet thou seest not [[Well|well]]. |
| SIMPCOX | Yes, master, clear as day, I thank God and Saint Alban. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Sayst thou me so? (Pointing) What colour is this cloak of? |
| SIMPCOX | Red, master; red as [[Blood|blood]]. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Why, that’s [[Well|well]] said. (Pointing) And his cloak? |
| SIMPCOX | Why, that’s green. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (pointing) And what colour’s His hose? |
| SIMPCOX | Yellow, master; yellow as gold. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | And what colour’s my gown? |
| SIMPCOX | Black, sir; coal-black, as jet. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why, then, thou know’st what colour jet is of? |
| UFFOLK | And yet I think jet did he never see. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | But cloaks and gowns before this day, a many. |
| SIMPCOX’S WIFE | Never before this day in all his life. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Tell me, sirrah, what’s my name? |
| SIMPCOX | Alas, master, I know not. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (pointing) What’s his name? |
| SIMPCOX | I know not. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (pointing) Nor his? |
| SIMPCOX | No, truly, sir. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (pointing) Nor his name? |
| SIMPCOX | No indeed, master. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | What’s thine own name? |
| SIMPCOX | Simon Simpcox, an it please you, master. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Then, Simon, sit thou there the lying’st knave In Christendom. If thou hadst been born blind Thou mightst as [[Well|well]] have known our names as thus To name the several colours we do wear. Sight may distinguish colours, but suddenly To nominate them all—it is impossible. Saint Alban here hath done a miracle. Would you not think his cunning to be great That could restore this cripple to his legs again? |
| SIMPCOX | O master, that you could! |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to the Mayor and aldermen) My masters of Saint Albans, have you not Beadles in your town, and things called whips? |
| MAYOR | We have, my lord, an if it please your grace. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Then send for one presently. |
| MAYOR | (to a townsman) Sirrah, go fetch the beadle hither straight. Exit one |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Bring me a stool. A stool is brought (To Simpcox) Now, sirrah, if you mean To save yourself from whipping, leap me o’er This stool and run away. |
| SIMPCOX | Alas, master, I am not able even to stand [[Alone|alone]]. You go about to torture me in vain. Enter a Beadle with whips |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | [[Well]], sirrah, we must have you find your legs. (To the Beadle) Whip him till he leap over that same stool. |
| BEADLE | I will, my lord. (To Simpcox) Come on, sirrah, off with your doublet quickly. |
| SIMPCOX | Alas, master, what shall I do ? I am not able to stand. After the Beadle hath hit him once, he leaps over the stool and runs away. Some of the townsmen follow and cry, ‘A miracle ! A miracle !’ |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O God, seest thou this and bear’st so long? |
