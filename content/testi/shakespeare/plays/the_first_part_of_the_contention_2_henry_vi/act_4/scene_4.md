| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Henry reading a supplication, [[Queen Margaret]] carrying Suffolk’s head, the [[Duke of Buckingham]], and the Lord Saye, with others |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] aside |
| *(didascalia)* | Oft have I heard that grief softens the mind,<br>And makes it fearful and degenerate;<br>Think, therefore, on [[Revenge|revenge]], and cease to weep.<br>But who can cease to weep and look on this?<br>Here may his head lie on my throbbing breast,<br>But where’s the [[Body|body]] that I should embrace? |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>What answer makes your grace to the rebels’ supplication? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | I’ll send some holy bishop to entreat,<br>For God forbid so many simple souls<br>Should perish by the [[Sword|sword]]. And I myself,<br>Rather than bloody war shall cut them short,<br>Will parley with [[Jack Cade]] their general.<br>But stay, I’ll read it over once again.<br>He reads |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (to Suffolk’s head)<br>Ah, barbarous villains! Hath this lovely face<br>Ruled like a wandering planet over me,<br>And could it not enforce them to relent,<br>That were unworthy to behold the same? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Lord Saye, [[Jack Cade]] hath sworn to have thy head. |
| SAYE | Ay, but I [[Hope|hope]] your highness shall have his. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to [[Queen Margaret]])<br>How now, madam? Still lamenting and mourning<br>Suffolk’s [[Death|death]]?<br>I [[Fear|fear]] me, [[Love|love]], if that I had been dead,<br>Thou wouldest not have mourned so much for me. |
| *(didascalia)* | No, my [[Love|love]], I should not mourn, but die for thee. Enter a Messenger, Fin haste |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How now? What news? Why com’st thou in such haste? |
| MESSENGER | The rebels are in Southwark—fly, my lord!<br>[[Jack Cade]] proclaims himself Lord Mortimer,<br>Descended from the Duke of [[Clarence]]’ [[house]],<br>And calls your grace [[Usurper|usurper]], openly,<br>And vows to [[Crown|crown]] himself in Westminster.<br><br>His army is a ragged multitude<br>Of hinds and peasants, rude and merciless.<br>Sir Humphrey Stafford and his brother’s [[Death|death]]<br>Hath given them heart and [[Courage|courage]] to proceed.<br>All scholars, lawyers, courtiers, gentlemen,<br>They call false caterpillars and intend their [[Death|death]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O, graceless men; they know not what they do. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | My gracious lord, retire to Kenilworth<br>Until a power be raised to put them down. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Ah, were the [[Duke of Suffolk]] now alive<br>These Kentish rebels would be soon appeased! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Lord Saye, the trait’rous rabble hateth thee—<br>Therefore away with us to Kenilworth. |
| SAYE | So might your grace’s person be in danger.<br>The sight of me is odious in their eyes,<br>And therefore in this [[City|city]] will I stay<br>And live [[Alone|alone]] as secret as I may. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger |
| SECOND MESSENGER | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>[[Jack Cade]] hath almost gotten [[London]] Bridge;<br>The citizens fly and forsake their houses;<br>The rascal people, thirsting after prey,<br>Join with the traitor; and they jointly swear<br>To spoil the [[City|city]] and your royal [[Court|court]]. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | (to [[King]] Henry)<br>Then linger not, my lord; away, take [[Horse|horse]]! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Come, Margaret. God, our [[Hope|hope]], will succour us. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] aside |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | My [[Hope|hope]] is gone, now Suffolk is deceased. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to Saye)<br>Farewell, my lord. Trust not the Kentish rebels. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | (to Saye)<br>Trust nobody, for [[Fear|fear]] you be betrayed. |
| SAYE | The trust I have is in mine [[innocence]],<br>And therefore am I bold and resolute. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Saye at one door, the rest at another |
