| SUFFOLK | O that I were a god, to shoot forth thunder Upon these paltry, servile, abject drudges. Small things make base men proud. This [[Villain|villain]] here, Being captain of a pinnace, threatens more Than Bargulus, the strong Illyrian pirate. Drones suck not eagles’ [[Blood|blood]], but rob beehives. It is impossible that I should die By such a lowly vassal as thyself. Thy words move rage, and not remorse in me. CAPTAIN But my deeds, Suffolk, soon shall stay thy rage. |
| SUFFOLK | I go of message from the [[Queen]] to France— I charge thee, waft me safely [[Cross|cross]] the Channel! |
| CAPTAIN | Walter— |
| WHITMORE | Come, Suffolk, I must waft thee to thy [[Death|death]]. |
| SUFFOLK | Paene gelidus timor occupat artus— |
| SUFFOLK | It is thee I [[Fear|fear]]. |
| WHITMORE | Thou shalt have cause to [[Fear|fear]] before I leave thee. What, are ye daunted now? Now will ye stoop? |
| FIRST GENTLEMAN | (to Suffolk) My gracious lord, entreat him—speak him fair. |
| SUFFOLK | Suffolk’s imperial tongue is stern and rough, Used to command, untaught to plead for favour. Far be it we should [[Honour|honour]] such as these With humble suit. No, rather let my head Stoop to the block than these knees bow to any Save to the God of heaven and to my [[King|king]]; And sooner [[Dance|dance]] upon a bloody pole Than stand uncovered to the vulgar groom. True nobility is exempt from [[Fear|fear]]; More can I bear than you dare execute. |
| CAPTAIN | Hale him away, and let him talk no more. |
| SUFFOLK | Come, ‘soldiers’, show what cruelty ye can, That this my [[Death|death]] may never be forgot. Great men oft die by vile Besonians; A Roman sworder and banditto slave Murdered sweet Tully ; [[Brutus]]’ bastard hand Stabbed [[Julius Caesar]]; savage islanders [[Pompey]] the Great; and Suffolk dies by pirates. Exit Whitmore with Suffolk |
| CAPTAIN | And as for these whose ransom we have set, It is our pleasure one of them depart. (To the Second Gentleman) Therefore, come you with us and (to his men, pointing to the First Gentleman) let him go. Exeunt all but the First Gentleman Enter Whitmore with Suffolk’s head and [[Body|body]] |
| WHITMORE | There let his head and lifeless [[Body|body]] lie, Until the [[Queen]] his mistress bury it. Exit |
| FIRST GENTLEMAN | O barbarous and bloody spectacle! His [[Body|body]] will I bear unto the [[King]]. If he [[Revenge|revenge]] it not, yet will his friends; So will the [[Queen]], that living held him dear. Exit with Suffolk’s head and [[Body|body]] |
