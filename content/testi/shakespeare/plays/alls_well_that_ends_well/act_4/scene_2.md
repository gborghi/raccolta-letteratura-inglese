| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Bertram]] and the maid called [[Diana]] |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | They told me that your name was Fontibel. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | No, my good lord, [[Diana]]. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | Titled goddess,<br>And worth it, with addition. But, fair [[Soul|soul]],<br>In your fine frame hath [[Love|love]] no quality?<br>If the quick [[Fire|fire]] of youth light not your mind,<br>You are no maiden but a monument.<br>When you are dead you should be such a one<br>As you are now, for you are cold and stern,<br>And now you should be as your mother was<br>When your sweet [[Self|self]] was got. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | She then was honest. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | So should you be. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | No.<br>My mother did but duty; such, my lord,<br>As you owe to your wife.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]])<br>No more o’ that.<br>I prithee do not strive against my vows.<br>I was compelled to her, but I [[Love|love]] thee<br>By [[Love|love]]’s own sweet constraint, and will for ever<br>Do thee all rights of service. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | Ay, so you serve us<br>Till we serve you. But when you have our roses,<br>You barely leave our thorns to prick ourselves,<br>And mock us with our bareness.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]])<br>How have I sworn! |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | ‘Tis not the many oaths that makes the [[Truth|truth]],<br>But the plain single vow that is vowed true.<br>What is not holy, that we swear not by,<br>But take the high’st to witness; then pray you, tell me,<br>If I should swear by Jove’s great attributes<br>I loved you dearly, would you believe my oaths<br>When I did [[Love|love]] you ill? This has no holding,<br>To swear by him whom I protest to [[Love|love]]<br>That I will work against him. Therefore your oaths<br>Are words and poor conditions but unsealed,<br>At least in my opinion.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]])<br>Change it, change it.<br>Be not so holy-cruel. [[Love]] is holy,<br>And my integrity ne’er knew the crafts<br>That you do charge men with. Stand no more off,<br>But give thyself unto my sick desires,<br>Who then recovers. Say thou art mine, and ever<br>My [[Love|love]] as it begins shall so persever. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | I see that men make toys e’en such a surance<br>That we’ll forsake ourselves. Give me that ring. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | I’ll lend it thee, my dear, but have no power<br>To give it from me. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | Will you not, my lord? |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | It is an [[Honour|honour]] ‘[[Longing|longing]] to our [[house]],<br>Bequeathed down from many ancestors,<br>Which were the greatest obloquy i’th’ world<br>In me to lose. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | Mine [[Honour|honour]]’s such a ring.<br>My chastity’s the [[Jewel|jewel]] of our [[house]],<br>Bequeathed down from many ancestors,<br>Which were the greatest obloquy i’th’ world<br>In me to lose. Thus your own proper wisdom<br>Brings in the champion [[Honour]] on my part<br>Against your vain assault.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]])<br>Here, take my ring.<br>My [[house]], mine [[Honour|honour]], yea my life be thine,<br>And I’ll be bid by thee. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | When midnight comes, knock at my chamber [[Window|window]].<br>I’ll order take my mother shall not hear.<br>Now will I charge you in the bond of [[Truth|truth]],<br>When you have conquered my yet maiden bed,<br>Remain there but an hour, nor speak to me—<br>My reasons are most strong, and you shall know them<br>When back again this ring shall be delivered—<br>And on your finger in the [[Night|night]] I’ll put<br>Another ring that, what in [[Time|time]] proceeds,<br>May token to the future our past deeds.<br>Adieu till then; then, fail not. You have won<br>A wife of me, though there my [[Hope|hope]] be done. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | A heaven on earth I have won by wooing thee. |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | For which live long to thank both heaven and me. |
| *(didascalia)* | You may so in the end. Exit [[Bertram]] |
| [[Diana|DIANA]] | My mother told me just how he would woo,<br>As if she sat in’s heart. She says all men<br>Have the like oaths. He had sworn to marry me<br>When his wife’s dead; therefore I’ll lie with him<br>When I am buried. Since Frenchmen are so braid,<br>Marry that will; I live and die a maid.<br>Only, in this disguise I think’t no sin<br>To cozen him that would unjustly win. Exit |
