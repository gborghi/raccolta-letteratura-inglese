| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lafeu and [[Bertram]] |
| LAFEU | But I [[Hope|hope]] your lordship thinks not him a soldier.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]]) Yes, my lord, and of very valiant approof. |
| LAFEU | You have it from his own deliverance. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | And by other warranted testimony. |
| LAFEU | Then my dial goes not true. I took this lark for a bunting. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | I do assure you, my lord, he is very great in knowledge, and accordingly valiant. |
| LAFEU | I have then sinned against his experience and transgressed against his valour—and my state that way is dangerous, since I cannot yet find in my heart to repent. Here he comes. I pray you make us friends. I will pursue the amity. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Paroles |
| PAROLES | (to [[Bertram]]) These things shall be done, sir. |
| LAFEU | (to [[Bertram]]) Pray you, sir, who’s his tailor? 15 |
| PAROLES | Sir! |
| LAFEU | O, I know him [[Well|well]]. Ay, ‘Sir’, he; ‘Sir’ ’s a good workman, a very good tailor.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]]) (aside to Paroles) Is she gone to the [[King]]? |
| PAROLES | She is.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]]) Will she away tonight? |
| PAROLES | As you’ll have her. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | I have writ my letters, casketed my treasure,<br>Given order for our horses, and tonight,<br>When I should take possession of the bride,<br>End ere I do begin. |
| LAFEU | (aside) A good traveller is something at the latter end of a dinner, but one that lies three-thirds and uses a known [[Truth|truth]] to pass a thousand nothings with, should be once heard and thrice beaten. (To Paroles) God save you, captain.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]]) (to Paroles) Is there any unkindness between my lord and you, monsieur? |
| PAROLES | I know not how I have deserved to run into my lord’s displeasure. 35 |
| LAFEU | You have made shift to run into’t, boots and spurs and all, like him that leaped into the custard, and out of it you’ll run again, rather than suffer question for your residence.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]]) It may be you have mistaken him, my lord. |
| LAFEU | And shall do so ever, though I took him at’s prayers. Fare you [[Well|well]], my lord, and believe this of me: there can be no kernel in this light nut. The [[Soul|soul]] of this man is his clothes. Trust him not in matter of heavy consequence. I have kept of them tame, and know their natures.—Farewell, monsieur. I have spoken better of you than you have wit or will to deserve at my hand, but we must do good against evil. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| PAROLES | An idle lord, I swear. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | I think not so. |
| PAROLES | Why, do you not know him? |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | Yes, I do know him [[Well|well]], and common speech<br>Gives him a worthy pass. Here comes my clog. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Helen]], attended |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | I have, sir, as I was commanded from you,<br>Spoke with the [[King]], and have procured his leave<br>For present parting; only he desires<br>Some private speech with you.<br>[[Bertram|BERTRAM]])<br>I shall obey his will.<br>You must not marvel, [[Helen]], at my course,<br>Which holds not colour with the [[Time|time]], nor does<br>The ministration and required office<br>On my particular. Prepared I was not<br>For such a business, therefore am I found<br>So much unsettled. This drives me to entreat you<br>That presently you take your way for [[Home|home]],<br>And rather muse than ask why I entreat you,<br>For my respects are better than they seem,<br>And my appointments have in them a need<br>Greater than shows itself at the first view<br>To you that know them not. This to my mother.<br>He gives her a letter<br>’Twill be two days ere I shall see you, so<br>I leave you to your wisdom. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | Sir, I can nothing say<br>But that I am your most obedient servant. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | Come, come, no more of that. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | And ever shall<br>With true observance seek to eke out that<br>Wherein toward me my homely [[Stars|stars]] have failed<br>To equal my great fortune. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | Let that go.<br>My haste is very great. Farewell. Hie [[Home|home]]. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | Pray sir, your pardon. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | [[Well]], what would you say? |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | I am not worthy of the wealth I owe,<br>Nor dare I say ’tis mine—and yet it is—<br>But like a timorous thief most fain would steal<br>What law does vouch mine own. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | What would you have? |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | Something, and scarce so much: nothing indeed.<br>I would not tell you what I would, my lord. [[Faith]],<br>yes:<br>Strangers and foes do sunder and not kiss. |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | I pray you, stay not, but in haste to [[Horse|horse]]. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | I shall not break your bidding, good my lord.—<br>Where are my other men?—Monsieur, farewell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Helen]] and attendants at one door |
| [[Bertram|BERTRAM]] | Go thou toward [[Home|home]], where I will never come<br>Whilst I can shake my [[Sword|sword]] or hear the drum.—<br>Away, and for our flight. |
| PAROLES | Bravely. Coraggio! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt at another door |
