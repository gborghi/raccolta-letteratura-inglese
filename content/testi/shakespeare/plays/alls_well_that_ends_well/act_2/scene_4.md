| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Helen]] reading a letter, and Lavatch the clown |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | My mother greets me kindly. Is she [[Well|well]]? |
| LAVATCH | She is not [[Well|well]], but yet she has her health. She’s very merry, but yet she is not [[Well|well]]. But thanks be given she’s very [[Well|well]] and wants nothing i’th’ world. But yet she is not [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | If she be very [[Well|well]], what does she ail<br>That she’s not very [[Well|well]]? |
| LAVATCH | Truly, she’s very [[Well|well]] indeed, but for two things. [[Helen|HELEN]] What two things? |
| LAVATCH | One, that she’s not in heaven, whither God send her quickly. The other, that she’s in earth, from whence God send her quickly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Paroles |
| PAROLES | Bless you, my fortunate lady. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | I [[Hope|hope]], sir, I have your good will to have<br>Mine own good fortunes. 15 |
| PAROLES | You had my prayers to lead them on, and to keep them on have them stitt.—O my knave, how does my old lady? |
| LAVATCH | So that you had her wrinkles and I her money, I would she did as you say. |
| PAROLES | Why, I say nothing. |
| LAVATCH | Marry, you are the wiser man, for many a man’s tongue shakes out his master’s undoing. To say nothing, to do nothing, to know nothing, and to have nothing, is to be a great part of your title, which is within a very little of nothing. |
| PAROLES | Away, thou’rt a knave. |
| LAVATCH | You should have said, sir, ‘Before a knave, thou’rt a knave‘—that’s ‘Before me, thou‘rt a knave’. This had been [[Truth|truth]], sir. |
| PAROLES | Go to, thou art a witty [[Fool|fool]]. I have found thee. |
| LAVATCH | Did you find me in yourself, sir, or were you taught to find me? |
| *(didascalia)* | PAROLES In myself, knave. |
| LAVATCH | The search, sir, was profitable, and much [[Fool|fool]] may you find in you, even to the world’s pleasure and the increase of laughter. |
| PAROLES | ([[To Helen|to Helen]]) A good knave, i’[[Faith|faith]], and [[Well|well]] fed.<br>Madam, my lord will go away tonight.<br>A very serious business calls on him.<br>The great prerogative and rite of [[Love|love]],<br>Which as your due [[Time|time]] claims, he does acknowledge,<br>But puts it off to a compelled restraint:<br>Whose want and whose delay is strewed with sweets,<br>Which they distil now in the curbed [[Time|time]],<br>To make the coming hour o’erflow with joy,<br>And pleasure drown the brim. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | What’s his will else? |
| PAROLES | That you will take your instant leave o’th’ [[King]],<br>And make this haste as your own good proceeding,<br>Strengthened with what apology you think<br>May make it probable need. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | What more commands he? |
| PAROLES | That having this obtained, you presently<br>Attend his further pleasure. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | In everything<br>I wait upon his will. |
| PAROLES | I shall report it so. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | I pray you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Paroles at one door] |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | Come, sirrah. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Fat another door] |
