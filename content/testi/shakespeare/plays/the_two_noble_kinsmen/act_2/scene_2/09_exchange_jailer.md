| JAILER | Banished—Prince Pirithous Obtained his liberty; but never more, Upon his oath and life, must he set foot Upon this kingdom. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | aside He’s a blessed man. He shall see Thebes again, and call to arms The bold young men that, when he bids ’em charge, Fall on like [[Fire|fire]]. [[Arcite]] shall have a fortune, If he dare make himself a worthy [[Lover|lover]], Yet in the field to strike a battle for her; And if he lose her then, he’s a cold coward. How bravely may he bear himself to win her If he be noble [[Arcite]]; thousand ways! Were I at liberty I would do things Of such a virtuous [[Greatness|greatness]] that this lady, This blushing virgin, should take manhood to her And seek to ravish me. |
| JAILER | My lord, for you I have this charge to— |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | To discharge my life. |
| JAILER | No, but from this place to remove your lordship— The windows are too open. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Devils take ’em That are so envious to me—prithee kill me. |
| JAILER | And hang for’t afterward? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | By this good light, Had I as word I would kill thee. |
| JAILER | Why, my lord? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Thou bring’st such pelting scurvy news continually, Thou art not worthy life. I will not go. |
| JAILER | Indeed you must, my lord. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | May I see the [[Garden|garden]]? |
| JAILER | No. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Then I am resolved—I will not go. |
| JAILER | I must constrain you, then; and for you are dangerous, I’ll clap more irons on you. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Do, good keeper. I’ll shake ’em so ye shall not [[Sleep|sleep]]: I’ll make ye a new morris. Must I go? |
| JAILER | There is no remedy. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Farewell, kind [[Window|window]]. May rude [[Wind|wind]] never hurt thee. O, my lady, If ever thou hast felt what sorrow was, Dream how I suffer. Come, now bury me. Exeunt [[Palamon]] and the Jailer |
