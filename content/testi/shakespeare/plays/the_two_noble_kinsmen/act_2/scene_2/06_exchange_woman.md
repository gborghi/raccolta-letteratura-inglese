| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | What think you of this [[Beauty|beauty]]? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | ’Tis a rare one. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Is’t but a rare one? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yes, a matchless [[Beauty|beauty]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Might not a man [[Well|well]] lose himself and [[Love|love]] her? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I cannot tell what you have done; I have, Beshrew mine eyes for’t. Now I feel my shackles. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | You [[Love|love]] her then? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Who would not? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | And [[Desire|desire]] her? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Before my liberty. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I saw her first. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | That’s nothing. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | But it shall be. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I saw her too. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes, but you must not [[Love|love]] her. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I will not, as you do, to worship her As she is heavenly and a blessèd goddess! I [[Love|love]] her as a woman, to enjoy her— So both may [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | You shall not [[Love|love]] at all. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Not [[Love|love]] at all—who shall deny me? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I that first saw her, I that took possession First with mine eye of all those beauties In her revealed to mankind. If thou lov’st her, Or entertain’st a [[Hope|hope]] to blast my wishes, Thou art a traitor, [[Arcite]], and a fellow False as thy title to her. [[Friendship]], [[Blood|blood]], And all the ties between us I disclaim, If thou once think upon her. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yes, I [[Love|love]] her—And if the lives of all my name lay on it, I must do so. I [[Love|love]] her with my [[Soul|soul]]— If that will lose ye, farewell, [[Palamon]]! I say again, I [[Love|love]] her, and in loving her maintain I am as worthy and as free a [[Lover|lover]], And have as just a title to her [[Beauty|beauty]], As any [[Palamon]], or any living That is a man’s son. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Have I called thee friend? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yes, and have found me so. Why are you moved thus? Let me deal coldly with you. Am not I Part of your [[Blood|blood]], part of your [[Soul|soul]]? You have told me That I was [[Palamon]] and you were [[Arcite]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Am not I liable to those affections, Those joys, griefs, angers, fears, my friend shall suffer? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Ye may be. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Why then would you deal so cunningly, So strangely, so unlike a noble kinsman, To [[Love|love]] [[Alone|alone]]? Speak truly. Do you think me Unworthy of her sight? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | No, but unjust If thou pursue that sight. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Because another First sees the enemy, shall I stand still, And let mine [[Honour|honour]] down, and never charge? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes, if he be but one. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | But say that one Had rather combat me? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Let that one say so, And use thy [[Freedom|freedom]]; else, if thou pursuest her, Be as that cursed man that hates his country, A branded [[Villain|villain]]. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | You are mad. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I must be. Till thou art worthy, [[Arcite]], it concerns me; And in this [[Madness|madness]] if I hazard thee And take thy life, I deal but truly. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Fie, sir. You play the [[Child|child]] extremely. I will [[Love|love]] her, I must, I ought to do so, and I dare— And all this justly. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | O, that now, that now Thy false [[Self|self]] and thy friend had but this fortune— To be one hour at liberty and grasp Our good swords in our hands! I would quickly teach thee What ’twere to filch affection from another. Thou art baser in it than a cutpurse. Put but thy head out of this [[Window|window]] more And, as I have a [[Soul|soul]], I’ll nail thy life to’t. |
