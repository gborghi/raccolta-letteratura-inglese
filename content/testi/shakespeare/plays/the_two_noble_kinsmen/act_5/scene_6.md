| *(didascalia)* | Enter, guarded, [[Palamon]] and his three Knights pinioned; enter with them the Jailer and an executioner with block and axe |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | There’s many a man alive that hath outlived<br>The [[Love|love]] o’th’ people; yea, i’th’ selfsame state<br>Stands many a father with his [[Child|child]]: some comfort<br>We have by so considering. We expire,<br>And not without men’s pity; to live still,<br>Have their good wishes. We prevent<br>The loathsome misery of age, beguile<br>The gout and rheum that in lag hours attend<br>For grey approachers; we come towards the gods<br>Young and unwappered, not halting under crimes<br>Many and state—that sure shall please the gods<br>Sooner than such, to give us nectar with ’em,<br>For we are more clear spirits. My dear kinsmen,<br>Whose lives for this poor comfort are laid down,<br>You have sold ’em too too cheap. |
| FIRST KNIGHT | What ending could be<br>Of more content? O’er us the victors have<br>Fortune, whose title is as momentary<br>As to us [[Death|death]] is certain—a grain of [[Honour|honour]]<br>They not o’erweigh us. |
| SECOND KNIGHT | Let us bid farewell, And with our patience anger tott’ring fortune,<br>Who at her certain’st reels. |
| THIRD KNIGHT | Come, who begins? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | E’en he that led you to this banquet shall<br>Taste to you all. (To the<br>jailer) Aha, my friend, my<br>friend,<br>Your gentle daughter gave me [[Freedom|freedom]] once;<br>You’ll see’t done now for ever. Pray, how does she?<br>I heard she was not [[Well|well]]; her kind of ill<br>Gave me some sorrow. |
| JAILER | Sir, she’s [[Well|well]] restored<br>And to be married shortly. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | By my short life,<br>I am most glad on’t. ’Tis the latest thing<br>I shall be glad of. Prithee, tell her so;<br>Commend me to her, and to piece her portion<br>Tender her this.<br>He gives his purse |
| FIRST KNIGHT | Nay, let’s be offerers all. |
| SECOND KNIGHT | Is it a maid? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Verily, I think so—<br>A right good creature more to me deserving<br>Than I can quit or speak of. |
| ALL THREE KNIGHTS | Commend us to her.<br>They give their purses |
| JAILER | The gods requite you all, and make her thankful. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Adieu, and let my life be now as short<br>As my leave-taking.<br>He lies on the block |
| FIRST KNIGHT | Lead, courageous cousin. |
| SECOND | and THIRD KNIGHTS We’ll follow cheerfully.<br>A great noise within: crying, ‘Run! Save! Hold!’ |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter in haste a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | Hold! Hold! O, hold! Hold! Hold! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pirithous in haste |
| PIRITHOUS | Hold, ho! It is a cursèd haste you made<br>If you have done so quickly! Noble [[Palamon]],<br>The gods will show their glory in a life<br>That thou art yet to lead. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Can that be,<br>When [[Venus]], I have said, is false? How do things<br>fare? |
| PIRITHOUS | Arise, great sir, and give the tidings ear<br>That are most rarely sweet and bitter. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | What<br>Hath waked us from our dream? |
| PIRITHOUS | List, then: your cousin,<br>Mounted upon a steed that Emily<br>Did first bestow on him, a black one owing<br>Not a hair-worth of white—which some will say<br>Weakens his price and many will not buy<br>His goodness with this note; which superstition<br>Here finds allowance—on this [[Horse|horse]] is [[Arcite]]<br>Trotting the stones of Athens, which the calkins<br>Did rather tell than trample; for the [[Horse|horse]]<br>Would make his length a mile, if’t pleased his rider<br>To put [[Pride|pride]] in him. As he thus went counting<br>The flinty pavement, dancing, as ‘twere, to th’ music<br>His own hooves made—for, as they say, from iron<br>Came music’s origin—what envious flint,<br>Cold as old [[Saturn]] and like him possessed<br>With [[Fire|fire]] malevolent, darted a spark,<br>Or what fierce sulphur else, to this end made,<br>I comment not—the hot [[Horse|horse]], hot as [[Fire|fire]],<br>Took toy at this and fell to what disorder<br>His power could give his will; bounds; comes on end;<br>Forgets school-doing, being therein trained<br>And of kind manège; pig-like he whines<br>At the sharp rowel, which he frets at rather<br>Than any jot obeys; seeks all foul means<br>Of boist’rous and rough jad’ry to disseat<br>His lord, that kept it bravely. When naught served,<br>When neither curb would crack, girth break, nor<br>diff’ring plunges<br>Disroot his rider whence he grew, but that<br>He kept him ‘tween his legs, on his hind hooves—<br>On end he stands—<br>That [[Arcite]]’s legs, being higher than his head,<br>Seemed with strange art to hang. His victor’s wreath<br>Even then fell off his head; and presently<br>Backward the jade comes o’er and his full poise<br>Becomes the rider’s load. Yet is he living;<br>But such a vessel ’tis that floats but for<br>The surge that next approaches. He much desires<br>To have some speech with you—lo, he appears. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Theseus]], [[Hippolyta]], [[Emilia]], and [[Arcite]] in a chair borne by attendants |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | O miserable end of our alliance!<br>The gods are mighty. [[Arcite]], if thy heart,<br>Thy worthy manly heart, be yet unbroken,<br>Give me thy last words. I am [[Palamon]],<br>One that yet loves thee dying. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Take [[Emilia]],<br>And with her all the world’s joy. Reach thy hand—<br>Farewell—I have told my last hour. I was false,<br>Yet never treacherous. Forgive me, cousin—<br>One kiss from fair [[Emilia]]—(they kiss) ’tis done.<br>Take her; I die.<br>He dies |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Thy brave [[Soul|soul]] seek Elysium. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to [[Arcite]]’s [[Body|body]])<br>I’ll close thine eyes, Prince. Blessed souls be with thee.<br>Thou art a right good man, and, while I live,<br>This day I give to [[Tears|tears]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | And I to [[Honour|honour]]. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | In this place first you fought, e’en very here<br>I sundered you. Acknowledge to the gods<br>Our thanks that you are living.<br>His part is played, and, though it were too short,<br>He did it [[Well|well]]. Your day is lengthened and<br>The blissful dew of heaven does arrouse you.<br>The powerful [[Venus]] [[Well|well]] hath graced her altar,<br>And given you your [[Love|love]]; our master, Mars,<br>Hath vouched his oracle, and to [[Arcite]] gave<br>The grace of the contention. So the deities<br>Have showed due justice.—Bear this hence. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt attendants with [[Arcite]]’s [[Body|body]] |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | cousin,<br>That we should things [[Desire|desire]] which do cost us<br>The loss of our [[Desire|desire]]! That naught could buy<br>Dear [[Love|love]], but loss of dear [[Love|love]]! |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Never fortune<br>Did play a subtler game—the conquered triumphs,<br>The victor has the loss. Yet in the passage<br>The gods have been most equal. [[Palamon]],<br>Your kinsman hath confessed the right o’th’ lady<br>Did lie in you, for you first saw her and<br>Even then proclaimed your fancy. He restored her<br>As your stol’n [[Jewel|jewel]], and desired your spirit<br>To send him hence forgiven. The gods my justice<br>Take from my hand, and they themselves become<br>The executioners. Lead your lady off,<br>And call your [[lovers]] from the stage of [[Death|death]],<br>Whom I adopt my friends. A day or two<br>Let us look sadly and give grace unto<br>The funeral of [[Arcite]], in whose end<br>The visages of bridegrooms we’ll put on<br>And smile with [[Palamon]], for whom an hour,<br>But one hour since, I was as dearly sorry<br>As glad of [[Arcite]], and am now as glad<br>As for him sorry. O you heavenly charmers,<br>What things you make of us! For what we lack<br>We laugh, for what we have, are sorry; still<br>Are children in some kind. Let us be thankful<br>For that which is, and with you leave dispute<br>That are above our question. Let’s go off<br>And bear us like the [[Time|time]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exeunt |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Epilogue |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Epilogue |
| EPILOGUE | I would now ask ye how ye like the play,<br>But, as it is with schoolboys, cannot say.<br>I am cruel fearful. Pray yet stay awhile,<br>And let me look upon ye. No man smile?<br>Then it goes hard, I see. He that has<br>Loved a young handsome wench, then, show his<br>face—<br>’Tis strange if none be here—and, if he will,<br>Against his [[Conscience|conscience]] let him hiss and kill<br>Our market. ’Tis in vain, I see, to stay ye.<br>Have at the worst can come, then! Now, what say ye?<br>And yet mistake me not—I am not bold—<br>We have no such cause. If the tale we have totd—<br>For ’tis no other—any way content ye,<br>For to that honest purpose it was meant ye,<br>We have our end; and ye shall have ere long,<br>I dare say, many a better to prolong<br>Your old loves to us. We and all our might<br>Rest at your service. Gentlemen, good [[Night|night]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exit |
