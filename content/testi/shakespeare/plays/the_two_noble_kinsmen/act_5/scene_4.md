|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Doctor, the jailer, and the Wooer in the habit of [[Palamon]] |
| DOCTOR | Has this advice I told you done any good upon her? |
| WOOER | O, very much. The maids that kept her company have half persuaded her that I am [[Palamon]]. Within this half-hour she came smiling to me, and asked me what I would eat, and when I would kiss her. I told her presently, and kissed her twice. |
| DOCTOR | ’Twas [[Well|well]] done—twenty times had been far better,<br>For there the cure lies mainly. |
| WOOER | Then she told me<br>She would watch with me tonight, for [[Well|well]] she knew<br>What hour my fit would take me. |
| DOCTOR | Let her do so, 11<br>And when your fit comes, fit her [[Home|home]],<br>And presently. |
| WOOER | She would have me sing. |
| DOCTOR | You did so? |
| WOOER | No. |
| DOCTOR | ’Twas very ill done, then. You should observe her every way. |
| WOOER | Alas,<br>I have no voice, sir, to confirm her that way. |
| DOCTOR | That’s all one, if ye make a noise.<br>If she entreat again, do anything—<br>Lie with her if she ask you. |
| JAILER | Ho there, Doctor. |
| DOCTOR | Yes, in the way of cure. |
| JAILER | But first, by your leave,<br>I’th’ way of honesty. |
| DOCTOR | That’s but a niceness—<br>Ne’er cast your [[Child|child]] away for honesty.<br>Cure her first this way, then if she will be honest,<br>She has the path before her. |
| JAILER | Thank ye, Doctor. |
| DOCTOR | Pray bring her in and let’s see how she is. |
| JAILER | I will, and tell her her [[Palamon]] stays for her.<br>But, Doctor, methinks you are i’th’ wrong still. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Jailer |
| DOCTOR | Go, go. You fathers are fine fools—her honesty?<br>An we should give her physic till we find that— |
| WOOER | Why, do you think she is not honest, sir? |
| DOCTOR | How old is she? |
| WOOER | She’s eighteen. |
| DOCTOR | She may be—<br>But that’s all one. ’Tis nothing to our purpose.<br>Whate’er her father says, if you perceive<br>Her mood inclining that way that I spoke of,<br>Videlicet, the way of flesh—you have me? |
| WOOER | Yes, very [[Well|well]], sir. |
| DOCTOR | Please her appetite,<br>And do it [[Home|home]]—it cures her, ipso facto,<br>The [[Melancholy|melancholy]] [[Humour|humour]] that infects her. |
| WOOER | I am of your mind, Doctor. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Jailer and his Daughter, |
| WOOER | mad |
| DOCTOR | You’ll find it so—she comes: pray [[Humour|humour]] her. |
| *(didascalia)* | The Doctor and the Wooer stand apart |
| JAILER | (to his Daughter)<br>Come, your [[Love|love]] [[Palamon]] stays for you, [[Child|child]],<br>And has done this long hour, to visit you. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | I thank him for his gentle patience.<br>He’s a kind gentleman, and I am much bound to<br>him.<br>Did you ne’er see the [[Horse|horse]] he gave me? |
| JAILER | Yes. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | How do you like him? |
| JAILER | He’s a very fair one. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | You never saw him [[Dance|dance]]? |
| JAILER | No. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | I have, often. He dances very finely, very comely,<br>And, for a jig, come cut and long-tail to him,<br>He turns ye like a top. |
| JAILER | That’s fine, indeed. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | He’ll [[Dance|dance]] the morris twenty mile an hour,<br>And that will founder the best hobbyhorse,<br>If I have any skill, in all the parish—<br>And gallops to the tune of ‘Light o’ [[Love|love]]’.<br>What think you of this [[Horse|horse]]? |
| JAILER | Having these virtues<br>I think he might be brought to play at tennis. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Alas, that’s nothing. |
| JAILER | Can he write and read too? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | A very fair hand, and casts himself th’accounts<br>Of all his hay and provender. That ostler<br>Must rise betime that cozens him. You know<br>The chestnut mare the Duke has? |
| JAILER | Very [[Well|well]]. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | She is horribly in [[Love|love]] with him, poor beast,<br>But he is like his master—coy and scornful. |
| JAILER | What dowry has she? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Some two hundred bottles<br>And twenty strike of oats, but he’ll ne’er have her.<br>He lisps in’s neighing, able to entice<br>A miller’s mare. He’ll be the [[Death|death]] of her. |
| DOCTOR | What stuff she utters! |
| JAILER | Make curtsy—here your [[Love|love]] comes. |
| WOOER | (coming forward) Pretty [[Soul|soul]],<br>How do ye?<br>She curtsies<br>That’s a fine maid, there’s a curtsy. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Yours to command, i’th’ way of honesty—<br>How far is’t now to th’ end o’th’ world, my masters? |
| DOCTOR | Why, a day’s [[Journey|journey]], wench. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | (to Wooer) Will you go with me? |
| WOOER | What shall we do there, wench? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Why, play at stool-ball—<br>What is there else to do? |
| WOOER | I am content<br>If we shall keep our wedding there. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | ’Tis true—<br>For there, I will assure you, we shall find<br>Some blind priest for the purpose that will venture<br>To marry us, for here they are nice, and foolish.<br>Besides, my father must be hanged tomorrow,<br>And that would be a blot i’th’ business.<br>Are not you [[Palamon]]? |
| WOOER | Do not you know me? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Yes, but you care not for me. I have nothing<br>But this poor petticoat and two coarse smocks. |
| WOOER | That’s all one—I will have you. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Will you surely? |
| WOOER | Yes, by this fair hand, will I. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | We’ll to bed then. |
| WOOER | E’en when you will.<br>He kisses her |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | (rubbing off the kiss)<br>O, sir, you would fain be nibbling. |
| WOOER | Why do you rub my kiss off? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | ’Tis a sweet one,<br>And will perfume me finely against the wedding. 90<br>(Indicating the Doctor) Is not this your cousin [[Arcite]]? |
| DOCTOR | Yes, sweetheart,<br>And I am glad my cousin [[Palamon]]<br>Has made so fair a choice. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Do you think he’ll have me? |
| DOCTOR | Yes, without [[Doubt|doubt]]. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | (to the Jailer) Do you think so too? |
| JAILER | Yes. |
| *(didascalia)* | We shall have many children. To the Doctor Lord, |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | how you’re grown!<br>My [[Palamon]], I [[Hope|hope]], will grow too, finely,<br>Now he’s at liberty. Alas, poor chicken,<br>He was kept down with hard meat and ill lodging,<br>But I’ll kiss him up again. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Messenger |
| MESSENGER | What do you here? You’ll lose the noblest sight<br>That e’er was seen. |
| JAILER | Are they i’th’ field? |
| MESSENGER | They are—<br>You bear a charge there too. |
| JAILER | I’ll away straight. |
| *(didascalia)* | To the others I must e’en leave you here. |
| DOCTOR | Nay, we’ll go with you—<br>I will not lose the sight. |
| JAILER | How did you like her? |
| DOCTOR | I’ll warrant you, within these three or four days<br>I’ll make her right again. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit the Jailer with the Messenger |
| DOCTOR | (To the Wooer) You must not from her,<br>But still preserve her in this way. |
| WOOER | I will. |
| DOCTOR | Let’s get her in. |
| WOOER | (to the Jailer’s Daughter)<br>Come, sweet, we’ll go to dinner,<br>And then we’ll play at cards. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | And shall we kiss too? |
| WOOER | A hundred times. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | And twenty. |
| WOOER | Ay, and twenty. no |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | And then we’ll [[Sleep|sleep]] together. |
| DOCTOR | (to the Wooer)<br>Take her offer. |
| WOOER | (to the Jailer’s Daughter)<br>Yes, marry, will we. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | But you shall not hurt me. |
| WOOER | I will not, sweet. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | If you do, [[Love|love]], I’ll cry. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
