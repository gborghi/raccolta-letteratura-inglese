| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | You are a right woman, sister: you have pity, But want the understanding where to use it. If you [[Desire|desire]] their lives, invent a way Safer than banishment. Can these two live, And have the agony of [[Love|love]] about ‘em, And not kill one another? Every day They’d fight about you, hourly bring your [[Honour|honour]] In public question with their swords. Be wise, then, And here forget ’em. It concerns your credit And my oath equally. I have said—they die. Better they fall by th’ law than one another. Bow not my [[Honour|honour]]. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | O my noble brother, That oath was rashly made, and in your anger. Your reason will not hold it. If such vows Stand for express will, all the world must perish. Beside, I have another oath ’gainst yours, Of more authority, I am sure more [[Love|love]]— Not made in passion, neither, but good heed. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What is it, sister? |
| PIRITHOUS | (to [[Emilia]]) Urge it [[Home|home]], brave lady. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | That you would ne’er deny me anything Fit for my modest suit and your free granting. I tie you to your word now; if ye fail in’t, Think how you maim your [[Honour|honour]]— For now I am set a-begging, sir. I am deaf To all but your compassion—how their lives Might breed the ruin of my name, opinion. Shall anything that loves me perish for me? That were a cruel wisdom: do men prune The straight young boughs that blush with thousand blossoms Because they may be rotten? O, Duke [[Theseus]], The goodly mothers that have groaned for these, And all the [[Longing|longing]] maids that ever loved, If your vow stand, shall curse me and my [[Beauty|beauty]], And in their funeral [[songs]] for these two cousins Despise my cruelty and cry woe worth me, Till I am nothing but the scorn of women. For heaven’s sake, save their lives and banish ’em. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | On what conditions? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Swear ’em never more To make me their contention, or to know me, To tread upon thy dukedom; and to be, Wherever they shall travel, ever strangers To one another. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I’ll be cut a-pieces Before I take this oath—forget I [[Love|love]] her? O all ye gods, despise me, then. Thy banishment I not mislike, so we may fairly carry Our swords and cause along—else, never trifle, But take our lives, Duke. I must [[Love|love]], and will; And for that [[Love|love]] must and dare kill this cousin On any piece the earth has. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Will you, [[Arcite]], Take these conditions? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | He’s a [[Villain|villain]] then. |
| PIRITHOUS | These are men! |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | No, never, Duke. ’Tis worse to me than begging, To take my life so basely. Though I think I never shall enjoy her, yet I’ll preserve The [[Honour|honour]] of affection and die for her, Make [[Death|death]] a devil. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What may be done? For now I feel compassion. |
| PIRITHOUS | Let it not fall again, sir. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Say, [[Emilia]], If one of them were dead—as one must—are you Content to take the other to your husband? They cannot both enjoy you. They are princes As goodly as your own eyes, and as noble As ever fame yet spoke of. Look upon ’em, And if you can [[Love|love]], end this difference. I give consent. (To [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]]) Are you content too, princes? |
