| *(didascalia)* | [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Fore God, his grace is bold to trust these traitors. |
| EXETER | They shall be apprehended by and by. |
| WESTMORLAND | How smooth and even they do bear themselves,<br>As if allegiance in their bosoms sat,<br>Crowned with [[Faith|faith]] and constant loyalty. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] |
| WESTMORLAND | The [[King]] hath note of all that they intend,<br>By interception which they dream not of. |
| EXETER | Nay, but the man that was his bedfellow,<br>Whom he hath dulled and cloyed with gracious<br>favours—<br>That he should for a foreign purse so sell<br>His sovereign’s life to [[Death|death]] and treachery. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound trumpets. Enter [[King]] Harry, Lord Scrope, the |
| EXETER | Earl of Cambridge, and Sir Thomas Grey |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Now sits the [[Wind|wind]] fair, and we will aboard.<br>My lord of Cambridge, and my kind lord of Masham,<br>And you, my gentle knight, give me your thoughts.<br>Think you not that the powers we bear with us<br>Will cut their passage through the force of France,<br>Doing the execution and the act<br>For which we have in head assembled them? |
| SCROPE | No [[Doubt|doubt]], my liege, if each man do his best. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I [[Doubt|doubt]] not that, since we are [[Well|well]] persuaded<br>We carry not a heart with us from hence<br>That grows not in a fair consent with ours,<br>Nor leave not one behind that doth not wish<br>[[success|Success]] and conquest to attend on us. |
| CAMBRIDGE | Never was monarch better feared and loved<br>Than is your majesty. There’s not, I think, a subject<br>That sits in heart-grief and uneasiness<br>Under the sweet shade of your [[government]]. |
| GREY | True. Those that were your father’s enemies<br>Have steeped their galls in honey, and do serve you<br>With hearts create of duty and of zeal. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | We therefore have great cause of thankfulness,<br>And shall forget the office of our hand<br>Sooner than quittance of [[Desert|desert]] and merit,<br>According to their weight and worthiness. |
| SCROPE | So service shall with steelèd sinews toil,<br>And labour shall refresh itself with [[Hope|hope]],<br>To do your grace incessant services. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | We judge no less.—Uncle of Exeter,<br>Enlarge the man committed yesterday<br>That railed against our person. We consider<br>It was excess of wine that set him on,<br>And on his more advice we pardon him. |
| SCROPE | That’s [[Mercy|mercy]], but too much security.<br>Let him be punished, sovereign, lest example<br>Breed, by his sufferance, more of such a kind. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | O let us yet be merciful. |
| CAMBRIDGE | So may your highness, and yet punish too. |
| GREY | Sir, you show great [[Mercy|mercy]] if you give him life,<br>After the taste of much correction. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Alas, your too much [[Love|love]] and care of me<br>Are heavy orisons ’gainst this poor wretch.<br>If little faults proceeding on distemper<br>Shall not be winked at, how shall we stretch our eye<br>When capital crimes, chewed, swallowed, and digested,<br>Appear before us? We’ll yet enlarge that man,<br>Though Cambridge, Scrope, and Grey, in their dear care<br>And tender preservation of our person,<br>Would have him punished. And now to our French causes.<br>Who are the late commissioners? |
| CAMBRIDGE | I one, my lord.<br>Your highness bade me ask for it today. |
| SCROPE | So did you me, my liege. |
| GREY | And I, my royal sovereign. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Then [[Richard]], Earl of Cambridge, there is yours;<br>There yours, Lord Scrope of Masham, and sir knight,<br>Grey of Northumberland, this same is yours.<br>Read them, and know I know your worthiness.—<br>My lord of Westmorland, and Uncle Exeter,<br>We will aboard tonight.—Why, how now, gentlemen?<br>What see you in those papers, that you lose<br>So much complexion?—Look ye how they change:<br>Their cheeks are paper.—Why, what read you there<br>That have so cowarded and chased your [[Blood|blood]]<br>Out of appearance? |
| CAMBRIDGE | I do confess my fault,<br>And do submit me to your highness’ [[Mercy|mercy]]. |
| GREY | and SCROPE To which we all appeal. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | The [[Mercy|mercy]] that was quick in us but late<br>By your own counsel is suppressed and killed.<br>You must not dare, for shame, to talk of [[Mercy|mercy]],<br>For your own reasons turn into your bosoms,<br>As [[dogs]] upon their masters, worrying you.—<br>See you, my princes and my noble peers,<br>These English monsters? My lord of Cambridge here,<br>You know how apt our [[Love|love]] was to accord<br>To furnish him with all appurtenants<br>Belonging to his [[Honour|honour]]; and this vile man<br>Hath for a few light crowns lightly conspired<br>And sworn unto the practices of France<br>To kill us here in Hampton. To the which<br>This knight, no less for bounty bound to us<br>Than Cambridge is, hath likewise sworn. But O<br>What shall I say to thee, Lord Scrope, thou cruel,<br>Ingrateful, savage, and inhuman creature?<br>Thou that didst bear the key of all my counsels,<br>That knew‘st the very [[Bottom|bottom]] of my [[Soul|soul]],<br>That almost mightst ha’ coined me into gold<br>Wouldst thou ha’ practised on me for thy use:<br>May it be possible that foreign hire<br>Could out of thee extract one spark of evil<br>That might annoy my finger? ’Tis so strange<br>That though the [[Truth|truth]] of it stands off as gross<br>As black on white, my eye will scarcely see it.<br>Treason and murder ever kept together,<br>As two yoke-devils sworn to either’s purpose,<br>Working so grossly in a natural cause<br>That admiration did not whoop at them;<br>But thou, ‘gainst all proportion, didst bring in<br>[[Wonder]] to wait on treason and on murder.<br>And whatsoever cunning fiend it was<br>That wrought upon thee so preposterously<br>Hath got the voice in hell for excellence.<br>And other devils that suggest by treasons<br>Do botch and bungle up damnation<br>With patches, colours, and with forms, being fetched<br>From glist’ring semblances of piety;<br>But he that tempered thee, bade thee stand up,<br>Gave thee no instance why thou shouldst do treason,<br>Unless to dub thee with the name of traitor.<br>If that same demon that hath gulled thee thus<br>Should with his [[Lion|lion]] gait walk the whole world,<br>He might return to vasty Tartar back<br>And tell the legions, ‘I can never win<br>A [[Soul|soul]] so easy as that Englishman’s.’<br>O how hast thou with [[Jealousy|jealousy]] infected<br>The sweetness of affiance. Show men dutiful?<br>Why so didst thou. Seem they [[Grave|grave]] and learned?<br>Why so didst thou. Come they of noble family?<br>Why so didst thou. Seem they religious?<br>Why so didst thou. Or are they spare in diet,<br>Free from gross passion, or of mirth or anger,<br>Constant in spirit, not swerving with the [[Blood|blood]],<br>Garnished and decked in modest complement,<br>Not working with the eye without the ear,<br>And but in purged judgement trusting neither?<br>Such, and so finely boulted, didst thou seem.<br>And thus thy fall hath left a kind of blot<br>To mark the full-fraught man, and best endowed,<br>With some suspicion. I will weep for thee,<br>For this revolt of thine methinks is like<br>Another fall of man.—Their faults are open.<br>Arrest them to the answer of the law,<br>And God acquit them of their practices. |
| EXETER | I arrest thee of high treason, by the name of [[Richard]], Earl of Cambridge.—I arrest thee of high treason, by the name of Henry, Lord Scrope of Masham.—I arrest thee of high treason, by the name of Thomas Grey, knight, of Northumberland. |
| SCROPE | Our purposes God justly hath discovered,<br>And I repent my fault more than my [[Death|death]],<br>Which I beseech your highness to forgive<br>Although my [[Body|body]] pay the price of it. |
| CAMBRIDGE | For me, the gold of France did not seduce,<br>Although I did admit it as a motive<br>The sooner to effect what I intended.<br>But God be thankèd for prevention,<br>Which heartily in sufferance will rejoice,<br>Beseeching God and you to pardon me. |
| GREY | Never did faithful subject more rejoice<br>At the discovery of most dangerous treason<br>Than I do at this hour joy o’er myself,<br>Prevented from a damned enterprise.<br>My fault, but not my [[Body|body]], pardon, sovereign. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | God ’quit you in his [[Mercy|mercy]]. Hear your sentence.<br>You have conspired against our royal person,<br>Joined with an enemy proclaimed and fixed,<br>And from his coffers<br>Received the golden earnest of our [[Death|death]],<br>Wherein you would have sold your [[King|king]] to slaughter,<br>His princes and his peers to servitude,<br>His subjects to oppression and contempt,<br>And his whole kingdom into desolation.<br>Touching our person seek we no [[Revenge|revenge]],<br>But we our kingdom’s safety must so tender,<br>Whose ruin you have sought, that to her laws<br>We do deliver you. Get ye therefore hence,<br>Poor miserable wretches, to your [[Death|death]];<br>The taste whereof, God of his [[Mercy|mercy]] give<br>You patience to endure, and true repentance<br>Of all your dear offences.—Bear them hence. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt the traitors, guarded |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Now lords for France, the enterprise whereof<br>Shall be to you, as us, like glorious.<br>We [[Doubt|doubt]] not of a fair and lucky war,<br>Since God so graciously hath brought to light<br>This dangerous treason lurking in our way<br>To hinder our beginnings. We [[Doubt|doubt]] not now<br>But every rub is smoothed on our way.<br>Then forth, dear countrymen. Let us deliver<br>Our puissance into the hand of God,<br>Putting it straight in expedition.<br>Cheerly to sea, the signs of war advance:<br>No [[King|king]] of England, if not [[King of France|king of France]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exeunt |
