| [[King|KING]] HARRY | If, Duke of Burgundy, you would the peace Whose want gives growth to th’imperfections Which you have cited, you must buy that peace With full accord to all our just demands, Whose tenors and particular effects You have enscheduled briefly in your hands. |
| BURGUNDY | The [[King]] hath heard them, to the which as yet There is no answer made. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | [[Well]] then, the peace, Which you before so urged, lies in his answer. |
| [[King|KING]] CHARLES | I have but with a cursitory eye O’erglanced the articles. Pleaseth your grace To appoint some of your council presently To sit with us once more, with better heed To re-survey them, we will suddenly Pass our accept and peremptory answer. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Brother, we shall.—Go, Uncle Exeter And brother [[Clarence]], and you, brother [[Gloucester]]; Warwick and Huntingdon, go with the [[King]], And take with you free power to ratify, Augment, or alter, as your wisdoms best Shall see advantageable for our dignity, Anything in or out of our demands, And we’ll consign thereto.—Will you, fair sister, Go with the princes, or stay here with us? |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Our gracious brother, I will go with them. Haply a woman’s voice may do some good When articles too nicely urged be stood on. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Yet leave our cousin Catherine here with us. She is our capital demand, comprised Within the fore-rank of our articles. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | She hath good leave. Exeunt all but [[King]] Harry, Catherine, and Alice |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Fair Catherine, and most fair, Will you vouchsafe to teach a soldier terms Such as will enter at a lady’s ear And plead his [[Love|love]]-suit to her gentle heart? |
| CATHERINE | Your majesty shall mock at me. I cannot speak your England. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | O fair Catherine, if you will [[Love|love]] me soundly with your French heart, I will be glad to hear you confess it brokenly with your English tongue. Do you like me, Kate? |
| CATHERINE | Pardonnez-moi, I cannot tell vat is ‘like me’. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | An angel is like you, Kate, and you are like an angel. |
| CATHERINE | (to Alice) Que dit-il?—que je suis semblable à les anges? |
| ALICE | Oui, vraiment—sauf votre grâce—ainsi dit-il. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I said so, dear Catherine, and I must not blush to affirm it. |
| CATHERINE | O bon Dieu! Les langues des hommes sont pleines de tromperies. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | What says she, fair one? That the tongues of men are full of deceits? |
| ALICE | Oui, dat de tongeus of de mans is be full of deceits—dat is de Princess. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | The Princess is the better Englishwoman. I‘[[Faith|faith]], Kate, my wooing is fit for thy understanding. I am glad thou canst speak no better English, for if thou couldst, thou wouldst find me such a plain [[King|king]] that thou wouldst think I had sold my farm to buy my [[Crown|crown]]. I know no ways to mince it in [[Love|love]], but directly to say, ‘I [[Love|love]] you’; then if you urge me farther than to say, ‘Do you in [[Faith|faith]]?’, I wear out my suit. Give me your answer, i’[[Faith|faith]] do, and so clap hands and a bargain. How say you, lady? |
