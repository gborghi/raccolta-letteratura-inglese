| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Archbishop of Canterbury and the Bishop of Ely |
| CANTERBURY | My lord, I’ll tell you. That [[Self|self]] bill is urged<br>Which in th’eleventh year of the last [[King|king]]’s reign<br>Was like, and had indeed against us passed,<br>But that the scrambling and unquiet [[Time|time]]<br>Did push it out of farther question. |
| ELY | But how, my lord, shall we resist it now? |
| CANTERBURY | It must be thought on. If it pass against us,<br>We lose the better half of our possession,<br>For all the temporal lands which men devout<br>By testament have given to the Church<br>Would they strip from us—being valued thus:<br>As much as would maintain, to the [[King]]’s [[Honour|honour]],<br>Full fifteen earls and fifteen hundred knights,<br>Six thousand and two hundred good esquires;<br>And, to relief of lazars and weak age,<br>Of indigent faint souls past corporal toil,<br>A hundred almshouses right [[Well|well]] supplied;<br>And to the coffers of the [[King]] beside<br>A thousand pounds by th’ year. Thus runs the bill. |
| ELY | This would drink deep. |
| CANTERBURY | ’Twould drink the cup and all. |
| ELY | But what prevention? |
| CANTERBURY | The [[King]] is full of grace and fair regard. |
| ELY | And a true [[Lover|lover]] of the holy Church. |
| CANTERBURY | The courses of his youth promised it not.<br>The breath no sooner left his father’s [[Body|body]]<br>But that his wildness, mortified in him,<br>Seemed to die too. Yea, at that very moment<br>Consideration like an angel came<br>And whipped th‘offending Adam out of him,<br>Leaving his [[Body|body]] as a paradise<br>T’envelop and contain celestial spirits.<br>Never was such a sudden scholar made;<br>Never came [[Reformation|reformation]] in a flood<br>With such a heady currance scouring faults;<br>Nor never Hydra-headed wilfulness<br>So soon did lose his seat—and all at once—<br>As in this [[King|king]]. |
| ELY | We are blessed in the change. |
| CANTERBURY | Hear him but reason in divinity<br>And, all-admiring, with an inward wish<br>You would [[Desire|desire]] the [[King]] were made a prelate;<br>Hear him [[debate]] of commonwealth affairs,<br>You would say it hath been all-in-all his study;<br>List his discourse of war, and you shall hear<br>A fearful battle rendered you in music;<br>Turn him to any cause of policy,<br>The Gordian knot of it he will unloose,<br>Familiar as his garter—that when he speaks,<br>The air, a chartered libertine, is still,<br>And the mute [[Wonder|wonder]] lurketh in men’s ears<br>To steal his sweet and honeyed sentences:<br>So that the art and practic part of life<br>Must be the mistress to this theoric.<br>Which is a [[Wonder|wonder]] how his grace should glean it,<br>Since his addiction was to courses vain,<br>His companies unlettered, rude, and shallow,<br>His hours filled up with riots, banquets, sports,<br>And never noted in him any study,<br>Any retirement, any sequestration<br>From open haunts and popularity. |
| ELY | The strawberry grows underneath the nettle,<br>And wholesome berries thrive and ripen best<br>Neighboured by fruit of baser quality;<br>And so the Prince obscured his contemplation<br>Under the veil of wildness—which, no [[Doubt|doubt]],<br>Grew like the [[Summer|summer]] grass, fastest by [[Night|night]],<br>Unseen, yet crescive in his faculty. |
| CANTERBURY | It must be so, for miracles are ceased,<br>And therefore we must needs admit the means<br>How things are perfected. |
| ELY | But, my good lord,<br>How now for mitigation of this bill<br>Urged by the Commons? Doth his majesty<br>Incline to it, or no? |
| CANTERBURY | He seems indifferent,<br>Or rather swaying more upon our part<br>Than cherishing th’exhibitors against us;<br>For I have made an offer to his majesty,<br>Upon our spiritual convocation<br>And in regard of causes now in hand,<br>Which I have opened to his grace at large:<br>As touching France, to give a greater sum<br>Than ever at one [[Time|time]] the clergy yet<br>Did to his predecessors part withal. |
| ELY | How did this offer seem received, my lord? |
| CANTERBURY | With good acceptance of his majesty,<br>Save that there was not [[Time|time]] enough to hear,<br>As I perceived his grace would fain have done,<br>The severals and unhidden passages<br>Of his true titles to some certain dukedoms,<br>And generally to the [[Crown|crown]] and seat of France,<br>Derived from Edward, his great-grandfather. |
| ELY | What was th’impediment that broke this off? |
| CANTERBURY | The French ambassador upon that instant<br>Craved audience—and the hour I think is come<br>To give him hearing. Is it four o’[[Clock|clock]]? |
| ELY | It is. 95 |
| CANTERBURY | Then go we in, to know his embassy—<br>Which I could with a ready guess declare<br>Before the Frenchman speak a word of it. |
| ELY | I’ll wait upon you, and I long to hear it. Exeunt |
