| CONSTABLE | Hark how our steeds for present service neigh. |
| *(didascalia)* | BOURBON |
| CONSTABLE | Mount them and make incision in their hides,<br>That their hot [[Blood|blood]] may spin in English eyes<br>And dout them with superfluous [[Courage|courage]]. Ha! |
| RAMBURES | What, will you have them weep our horses’ [[Blood|blood]]?<br>How shall we then behold their natural [[Tears|tears]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | The English are embattled, you French peers. |
| CONSTABLE | To [[Horse|horse]], you gallant princes, straight to [[Horse|horse]]!<br>Do but behold yon poor and starved band,<br>And your fair show shall suck away their souls,<br>Leaving them but the shells and husks of men.<br>There is not work enough for all our hands,<br>Scarce [[Blood|blood]] enough in all their sickly veins<br>To give each naked curtal-axe a stain<br>That our French gallants shall today draw out<br>And sheathe for lack of sport. Let us but blow on<br>them,<br>The vapour of our valour will o‘erturn them.<br>’Tis positive ’gainst all exceptions, lords,<br>That our superfluous lackeys and our peasants,<br>Who in unnecessary action swarm<br>About our squares of battle, were enough<br>To purge this field of such a hilding foe,<br>Though we upon this mountain’s basis by<br>Took stand for idle speculation,<br>But that our honours must not. What’s to say?<br>A very little little let us do<br>And all is done. Then let the trumpets sound<br>The tucket sonance and the note to mount,<br>For our approach shall so much dare the field<br>That England shall couch down in [[Fear|fear]] and yield. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord Grandpré |
| CONSTABLE | GRANDPRÉ<br>Why do you stay so long, my lords of France?<br>Yon island carrions, desperate of their bones,<br>Ill-favouredly become the morning field.<br>Their ragged curtains poorly are let loose<br>And our air shakes them passing scornfully.<br>Big Mars seems bankrupt in their beggared host<br>And faintly through a rusty beaver peeps.<br>The horsemen sit like fixed candlesticks<br>With torchstaves in their hands, and their poor jades<br>Lob down their heads, drooping the hides and hips,<br>The gum down-roping from their pale dead eyes,<br>And in their palled dull mouths the gimmaled bit<br>Lies foul with chewed grass, still and motionless.<br>And their executors, the knavish crows,<br>Fly o’er them all impatient for their hour.<br>Description cannot suit itself in words<br>To demonstrate the life of such a battle<br>In life so lifeless as it shows itself. |
| CONSTABLE | They have said their prayers, and they stay for [[Death|death]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | BOURBON |
| CONSTABLE | Shall we go send them dinners and fresh suits<br>And give their fasting horses provender,<br>And after fight with them? |
| CONSTABLE | I stay but for my guidon. To the field!<br>I will the banner from a trumpet take<br>And use it for my haste. Come, come away!<br>The sun is high, and we outwear the day. Exeunt |
