| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Our [[King]] is not like him in that. He never killed any of his friends. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | It is not [[Well|well]] done, mark you now, to take the tales out of my mouth ere it is made an end and finished. I speak but in the figures and comparisons of it. As Alexander killed his friend Cleitus, being in his ales and his cups, so also Harry Monmouth, being in his right wits and his good judgements, turned away the fat knight with the great-belly doublet—he was full of jests and gipes and knaveries and mocks—I have forgot his name. |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Sir John [[Falstaff]]. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | That is he. I’ll tell you, there is good men porn at Monmouth. |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Here comes his majesty. Alarum. Enter [[King]] Harry and the English army, with the Duke of Bourbon, the Duke of Orléans, and other prisoners. Flourish |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I was not angry since I came to France Until this instant. Take a trumpet, herald; Ride thou unto the horsemen on yon [[Hill|hill]]. If they will fight with us, bid them come down, Or void the field: they do offend our sight. If they’ll do neither, we will come to them, And make them skirr away as swift as stones Enforced from the old Assyrian slings. Besides, we’ll cut the throats of those we have, And not a man of them that we shall take Shall taste our [[Mercy|mercy]]. Go and tell them so. Enter Montjoy |
| EXETER | Here comes the herald of the French, my liege. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | His eyes are humbler than they used to be. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | How now, what means this, herald? Know‘st thou not That I have fined these bones of mine for ransom? Com’st thou again for ransom? |
| MONTJOY | No, great [[King]]. I come to thee for charitable licence, That we may wander o’er this bloody field To book our dead and then to bury them, To sort our nobles from our common men— For many of our princes, woe the while, Lie drowned and soaked in mercenary [[Blood|blood]]. So do our vulgar drench their peasant limbs In [[Blood|blood]] of princes, and our wounded steeds Fret fetlock-deep in gore, and with wild rage Jerk out their armed heels at their dead masters, Killing them twice. O give us leave, great [[King]], To view the field in safety, and dispose Of their dead bodies. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I tell thee truly, herald, I know not if the day be ours or no, For yet a many of your horsemen peer And gallop o’er the field. |
| MONTJOY | The day is yours. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Praised be God, and not our strength, for it. What is this [[Castle|castle]] called that stands hard by? |
| MONTJOY | They call it Agincourt. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Then call we this the field of Agincourt, Fought on the day of Crispin Crispian. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Your grandfather of famous [[Memory|memory]], an’t please your majesty, and your great-uncle Edward the Plack Prince of Wales, as I have read in the chronicles, fought a most prave pattle here in France. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | They did, [[Fluellen]]. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Your majesty says very true. If your majesties is remembered of it, the Welshmen did good service in a [[Garden|garden]] where leeks did grow, wearing leeks in their Monmouth caps, which your majesty know to this hour is an honourable badge of the service. And I do believe your majesty takes no scorn to wear the leek upon Saint Tavy’s day. |
