| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Dukes of [[Gloucester]], [[Clarence]], and Exeter, the Earls of Salisbury and Warwick, and Sir Thomas Erpingham, with all the host |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Where is the [[King]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | The [[King]] himself is rode to view their battle.<br>[WARWICK]<br>Of fighting men they have full threescore thousand. |
| EXETER | There’s five to one. Besides, they all are fresh. |
| SALISBURY | God’s arm strike with us! ‘Tis a fearful odds.<br>God b’wi’ you, princes all. I’ll to my charge.<br>If we no more meet till we meet in heaven,<br>Then joyfully, my noble Lord of [[Clarence]],<br>My dear Lord [[Gloucester]], and my good Lord Exeter,<br>And (to Warwick) my kind kinsman, warriors all,<br>adieu. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] |
| SALISBURY | Farewell, good Salisbury, and good luck go with thee. |
| EXETER | Farewell, kind lord. Fight valiantly today—<br>And yet I do thee wrong to mind thee of it,<br>For thou art framed of the firm [[Truth|truth]] of valour. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Salisbury |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] |
| EXETER | He is as full of valour as of kindness,<br>Princely in both. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Harry, behind |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK |
| EXETER | O that we now had here<br>But one ten thousand of those men in England<br>That do no work today. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | What’s he that wishes so?<br>My cousin Warwick? No, my fair cousin.<br>If we are marked to die, we are enough<br>To do our country loss; and if to live,<br>The fewer men, the greater share of [[Honour|honour]].<br>God’s will, I pray thee wish not one man more.<br>By Jove, I am not covetous for gold,<br>Nor care I who doth feed upon my cost;<br>It ernes me not if men my garments wear;<br>Such outward things dwell not in my desires.<br>But if it be a sin to covet [[Honour|honour]]<br>I am the most offending [[Soul|soul]] alive.<br>No, [[Faith|faith]], my coz, wish not a man from England.<br>God’s peace, I would not lose so great an [[Honour|honour]]<br>As one man more methinks would share from me<br>For the best [[Hope|hope]] I have. O do not wish one more.<br>Rather proclaim it presently through my host<br>That he which hath no stomach to this fight,<br>Let him depart. His passport shall be made<br>And crowns for convoy put into his purse.<br>We would not die in that man’s company<br>That fears his fellowship to die with us.<br>This day is called the Feast of Crispian.<br>He that outlives this day and comes safe [[Home|home]]<br>Will stand a-tiptoe when this day is named<br>And rouse him at the name of Crispian.<br>He that shall see this day and live t‘old age<br>Will yearly on the vigil feast his neighbours<br>And say, ’Tomorrow is Saint Crispian.’<br>Then will he strip his sleeve and show his scars<br>And say, ’These wounds I had on Crispin’s day.’<br>Old men forget; yet all shall be forgot,<br>But he’ll remember, with advantages,<br>What feats he did that day. Then shall our names,<br>Familiar in his mouth as household words—<br>Harry the [[King]], Bedford and Exeter,<br>Warwick and Talbot, Salisbury and [[Gloucester]]—<br>Be in their flowing cups freshly remembered.<br>This story shall the good man teach his son,<br>And Crispin Crispian shall ne’er go by<br>From this day to the ending of the world<br>But we in it shall be remembered,<br>We few, we happy few, we band of brothers.<br>For he today that sheds his [[Blood|blood]] with me<br>Shall be my brother; be he ne’er so vile,<br>This day shall gentle his condition.<br>And gentlemen in England now abed<br>Shall think themselves accursed they were not here,<br>And hold their manhoods cheap whiles any speaks<br>That fought with us upon Saint Crispin’s day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Earl of Salisbury]] |
| SALISBURY | My sovereign lord, bestow yourself with [[Speed|speed]].<br>The French are bravely in their battles set<br>And will with all expedience charge on us. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | All things are ready if our minds be so. |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK Perish the man whose mind is backward now. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Thou dost not wish more help from England, coz? |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | God’s will, my liege, would you and I [[Alone|alone]],<br>Without more help, could fight this royal battle. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Why now thou hast unwished five thousand men,<br>Which likes me better than to wish us one.—<br>You know your places. God be with you all. |
| *(didascalia)* | Tucket. Enter Montjoy |
| MONTJOY | Once more I come to know of thee, [[King]] Harry,<br>If for thy ransom thou wilt now compound<br>Before thy most assured overthrow.<br>For certainly thou art so near the gulf<br>Thou needs must be englutted. Besides, in [[Mercy|mercy]]<br>The Constable desires thee thou wilt mind<br>Thy followers of repentance, that their souls<br>May make a peaceful and a sweet retire<br>From off these fields where, wretches, their poor<br>bodies<br>Must lie and fester. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Who hath sent thee now? |
| MONTJOY | The Constable of France. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I pray thee bear my former answer back.<br>Bid them achieve me, and then sell my bones.<br>Good God, why should they mock poor fellows thus?<br>The man that once did sell the [[Lion|lion]]’s skin<br>While the beast lived, was killed with hunting him. 95<br>A many of our bodies shall no [[Doubt|doubt]]<br>Find native graves, upon the which, I trust,<br>Shall witness live in brass of this day’s work.<br>And those that leave their valiant bones in France,<br>Dying like men, though buried in your dunghills<br>They shall be famed. For there the sun shall greet<br>them<br>And draw their honours reeking up to heaven,<br>Leaving their earthly parts to choke your clime,<br>The smell whereof shall breed a plague in France.<br>Mark then abounding valour in our English,<br>That, being dead, like to the bullets grazing<br>Break out into a second course of mischief,<br>Killing in relapse of [[Mortality|mortality]].<br>Let me speak proudly. Tell the Constable<br>We are but warriors for the working day.<br>Our gayness and our gilt are all besmirched<br>With rainy marching in the painful field.<br>There’s not a piece of feather in our host—<br>Good argument, I [[Hope|hope]], we will not fly—<br>And [[Time|time]] hath worn us into slovenry.<br>But by the mass, our hearts are in the trim.<br>And my poor soldiers tell me, yet ere [[Night|night]]<br>They’ll be in fresher robes, as they will pluck<br>The gay new coats o‘er your French soldiers’ heads,<br>And turn them out of service. If they do this—<br>As if God please, they shall—my ransom then<br>Will soon be levied. Herald, save thou thy labour.<br>Come thou no more for ransom, gentle herald.<br>They shall have none, I swear, but these my joints—<br>Which if they have as I will leave ’em them,<br>Shall yield them little. Tell the Constable. |
| MONTJOY | I shall, [[King]] Harry. And so fare thee [[Well|well]].<br>Thou never shalt hear herald any more. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I [[Fear|fear]] thou wilt once more come for a ransom. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Montjoy |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of York]] |
| YORK | My lord, most humbly on my knee I beg<br>The leading of the vanguard. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Take it, brave York.—Now soldiers, march away,<br>And how thou pleasest, God, dispose the day. Exeunt |
