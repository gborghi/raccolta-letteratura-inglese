| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Harry and the [[Duke of Gloucester]], then the Duke of [[Clarence]] |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | [[Gloucester]], ’tis true that we are in great danger; The greater therefore should our [[Courage|courage]] be. Good morrow, brother [[Clarence]]. God Almighty! There is some [[Soul|soul]] of goodness in things evil, Would men observingly distil it out— For our bad neighbour makes us early stirrers, Which is both healthful and good husbandry. Besides, they are our outward consciences, And preachers to us all, admonishing That we should dress us fairly for our end. Thus may we gather honey from the weed And make a moral of the devil himself. Enter Sir Thomas Erpingham Good morrow, old Sir Thomas Erpingham. A good soft pillow for that good white head Were better than a churlish turf of France. |
| ERPINGHAM | Not so, my liege. This lodging likes me better, Since I may say, ‘Now lie I like a [[King|king]].’ |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | ’Tis good for men to [[Love|love]] their present pains Upon example. So the spirit is eased, And when the mind is quickened, out of [[Doubt|doubt]] The organs, though defunct and dead before, Break up their drowsy [[Grave|grave]] and newly move With casted slough and fresh legerity. Lend me thy cloak, Sir Thomas. He puts on Erpingham’s cloak Brothers both, Commend me to the princes in our camp. Do my good morrow to them, and anon [[Desire]] them all to my pavilion. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | We shall, my liege. |
| ERPINGHAM | Shall I attend your grace? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | No, my good knight. Go with my brothers to my lords of England. I and my bosom must [[debate]] awhile, And then I would no other company. |
| ERPINGHAM | The Lord in heaven bless thee, noble Harry. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | God-a-[[Mercy|mercy]], old heart, thou speak’st cheerfully. Exeunt all but [[King]] Harry Enter [[Pistol]] to him |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Qui vous là? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | A friend. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Discuss unto me: art thou officer, Or art thou base, common, and popular? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I am a gentleman of a company. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Trail’st thou the puissant pike? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Even so. What are you? |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | As good a gentleman as the Emperor. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Then you are a better than the [[King]]. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | The [[King]]’s a bawcock and a heart-of-gold, A lad of life, an imp of fame, Of parents good, of fist most valiant. I kiss his dirty shoe, and from heartstring I [[Love|love]] the lovely bully. What is thy name? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Harry le roi. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Leroi? A Cornish name. Art thou of Cornish crew? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | No, I am a Welshman. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Know’st thou [[Fluellen]]? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Yes. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Tell him I’ll knock his leek about his pate Upon Saint Davy’s day. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Do not you wear your dagger in your cap that day, lest he knock that about yours. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Art thou his friend? |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | And his kinsman too. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | The fico for thee then. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I thank you. God be with you. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | My name is [[Pistol]] called. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | It sorts [[Well|well]] with your fierceness. Exit [[Pistol]] Enter Captains [[Fluellen]] and [[Gower]] severally. [[King]] Harry stands apart |
