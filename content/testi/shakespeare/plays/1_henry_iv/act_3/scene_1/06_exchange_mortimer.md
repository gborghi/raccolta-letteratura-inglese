| MORTIMER | In [[Faith|faith]], he is a worthy gentleman, Exceedingly [[Well|well]] read, and profited In strange concealments, valiant as a [[Lion|lion]], And wondrous affable, and as bountiful As mines of India. Shall I tell you, cousin? 165 He holds your temper in a high respect, And curbs himself even of his natural scope When you come ’[[Cross|cross]] his [[Humour|humour]]; [[Faith|faith]], he does. I warrant you, that man is not alive Might so have tempted him as you have done Without the taste of danger and reproof. But do not use it oft, let me entreat you. |
| WORCESTER | (to [[Hotspur]]) In [[Faith|faith]], my lord, you are too wilful-blame, And since your coming hither have done enough To put him quite besides his patience. You must needs learn, lord, to amend this fault. Though sometimes it show [[Greatness|greatness]], [[Courage|courage]], [[Blood|blood]]— And that’s the dearest grace it renders you— Yet oftentimes it doth present harsh rage, Defect of manners, want of [[government]], [[Pride]], haughtiness, opinion, and disdain, The least of which haunting a nobleman Loseth men’s hearts, and [[Leaves|leaves]] behind a stain Upon the [[Beauty|beauty]] of all parts besides, Beguiling them of commendation. 185 |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | [[Well]], I am schooled. Good manners be your [[Speed|speed]]! Enter Glyndŵr with Lady Percy and Mortimer’s wife Here come our wives, and let us take our leave. Mortimer’s wife weeps, and speaks to him in Welsh |
| MORTIMER | This is the deadly spite that angers me: My wife can speak no English, I no Welsh. GLYNDŴR My daughter weeps she’ll not part with you. She’ll be a soldier, too; she’ll to the wars. |
| MORTIMER | Good father, tell her that she and my aunt Percy Shall follow in your conduct speedily. Glyndŵr speaks to her in Welsh, and she answers him in the same |
| GLYNDWR | She is desperate here, a peevish [[Self|self]]-willed harlotry, One that no [[Persuasion|persuasion]] can do good upon. 195 The lady speaks in Welsh |
| MORTIMER | I understand thy looks. That pretty Welsh Which thou down pourest from these swelling heavens I am too perfect in, and but for shame In such a parley should I answer thee. The lady kisses him, and speaks again in Welsh |
| MORTIMER | I understand thy kisses, and thou mine, And that’s a feeling disputation; But I will never be a truant, [[Love|love]], Till I have learnt thy language, for thy tongue Makes Welsh as sweet as ditties highly penned, Sung by a fair [[Queen|queen]] in a [[Summer|summer]]’s bower With ravishing division, to her lute. GLYNDŴR Nay, if you melt, then will she run mad. The lady sits on the rushes and speaks again in Welsh |
| MORTIMER | O, I am ignorance itself in this! GLYNDŴR She bids you on the wanton rushes lay you down And rest your gentle head upon her lap, And she will sing the [[Song|song]] that pleaseth you, And on your eyelids [[Crown|crown]] the god of [[Sleep|sleep]], Charming your [[Blood|blood]] with pleasing heaviness, Making such difference ’twixt wake and [[Sleep|sleep]] As is the difference betwixt day and [[Night|night]] 215 The hour before the heavenly-harnessed team Begins his golden progress in the east. |
