| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[King]], the Earls of Northumberland and Worcester, [[Hotspur]], Sir Walter Blunt, with other lords |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to [[Hotspur]], Northumberland, and Worcester) My [[Blood|blood]] hath been too cold and temperate, Unapt to stir at these indignities, And you have found me, for accordingly You tread upon my patience; but be sure I will from henceforth rather be myself, Mighty and to be feared, than my condition, Which hath been smooth as oil, soft as young down, And therefore lost that title of respect Which the proud [[Soul|soul]] ne’er pays but to the proud. |
| WORCESTER | Our [[house]], my sovereign liege, little deserves The scourge of [[Greatness|greatness]] to be used on it, And that same [[Greatness|greatness]] too, which our own hands Have holp to make so portly. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | (to the [[King]]) My lord— |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Worcester, get thee gone, for I do see<br>Danger and disobedience in thine eye.<br>O sir, your presence is too bold and peremptory,<br>And majesty might never yet endure<br>The moody frontier of a servant brow.<br>You have good leave to leave us. When we need<br>Your use and counsel we shall send for you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Worcester |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | You were about to speak. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Yea, my good lord.<br>Those prisoners in your highness’ name demanded,<br>Which Harry Percy here at Holmedon took,<br>Were, as he says, not with such strength denied<br>As was delivered to your majesty,<br>Who either through envy or misprision<br>Was guilty of this fault, and not my son. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | (to the [[King]])<br>My liege, I did deny no prisoners;<br>But I remember, when the fight was done,<br>When I was dry with rage and extreme toil,<br>Breathless and faint, leaning upon my [[Sword|sword]],<br>Came there a certain lord, neat and trimly dressed,<br>Fresh as a bridegroom, and his chin, new-reaped,<br>Showed like a stubble-land at harvest-[[Home|home]].<br>He was perfumed like a milliner,<br>And ‘twixt his finger and his thumb he held<br>A pouncet-box, which ever and anon<br>He gave his nose and took’t away again—<br>Who therewith angry, when it next came there<br>Took it in snuff—and still he smiled and talked;<br>And as the soldiers bore dead bodies by,<br>He called them untaught knaves, unmannerly<br>To bring a slovenly unhandsome corpse<br>Betwixt the [[Wind|wind]] and his nobility.<br>With many holiday and lady terms<br>He questioned me; amongst the rest demanded<br>My prisoners in your majesty’s behalf.<br>I then, all smarting with my wounds being cold—<br>To be so pestered with a popinjay!—<br>Out of my grief and my impatience<br>Answered neglectingly, I know not what—<br>He should, or should not—for he made me mad<br>To see him shine so brisk, and smell so sweet,<br>And talk so like a waiting gentlewoman<br>Of guns, and drums, and wounds, God save the mark!<br>And telling me the sovereign’st thing on earth<br>Was parmacity for an inward bruise,<br>And that it was great pity, so it was,<br>This villainous saltpetre should be digged<br>Out of the bowels of the harmless earth,<br>Which many a good tall fellow had destroyed<br>So cowardly, and but for these vile guns<br>He would himself have been a soldier.<br>This bald unjointed chat of his, my lord,<br>Made me to answer indirectly, as I said,<br>And I beseech you, let not his report<br>Come current for an accusation<br>Betwixt my [[Love|love]] and your high majesty. |
| BLUNT | (to the [[King]])<br>The circumstance considered, good my lord,<br>Whate’er Lord Harry Percy then had said<br>To such a person, and in such a place,<br>At such a [[Time|time]], with all the rest retold,<br>May reasonably die, and never rise<br>To do him wrong or any way impeach<br>What then he said, so he unsay it now. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why, yet he doth deny his prisoners,<br>But with proviso and exception<br>That we at our own charge shall ransom straight<br>His brother-in-law the foolish Mortimer,<br>Who, on my [[Soul|soul]], hath wilfully betrayed<br>The lives of those that he did lead to fight<br>Against that great magician, damned Glyndŵr—<br>Whose daughter, as we hear, the Earl of March<br>Hath lately married. Shall our coffers, then,<br>Be emptied to redeem a traitor [[Home|home]]?<br>Shall we buy treason, and indent with fears<br>When they have lost and forfeited themselves?<br>No, on the barren [[Mountains|mountains]] let him starve;<br>For I shall never hold that man my friend<br>Whose tongue shall ask me for one penny cost<br>To ransom [[Home|home]] revolted Mortimer— |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Revolted Mortimer?<br>He never did fall off, my sovereign liege,<br>But by the chance of war. To prove that true<br>Needs no more but one tongue for all those wounds,<br>Those mouthed wounds, which valiantly he took<br>When on the gentle Severn’s sedgy bank,<br>In single opposition, hand to hand,<br>He did confound the best part of an hour<br>In changing hardiment with great Glyndwr.<br>Three times they breathed, and three times did they<br>drink,<br>Upon agreement, of swift Severn’s flood,<br>Who, then affrighted with their bloody looks,<br>Ran fearfully among the trembling reeds,<br>And hid his crisp head in the hollow bank,<br>Bloodstainèd with these valiant combatants.<br>Never did bare and rotten policy<br>Colour her working with such deadly wounds,<br>Nor never could the noble Mortimer<br>Receive so many, and all willingly.<br>Then let not him be slandered with revolt. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Thou dost belie him, Percy, thou dost belie him.<br>He never did encounter with Glyndŵr. I tell thee,<br>He durst as [[Well|well]] have met the devil [[Alone|alone]]<br>As Owain Glyndŵr for an enemy.<br>Art thou not ashamed? But, sirrah, henceforth<br>Let me not hear you speak of Mortimer.<br>Send me your prisoners with the speediest means,<br>Or you shall hear in such a kind from me<br>As will displease you.—My lord Northumberland,<br>We license your departure with your son.<br>(To [[Hotspur]]) Send us your prisoners, or you’ll hear of it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Hotspur]] and Northumberland |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | An if the devil come and roar for them<br>I will not send them. I will after straight<br>And tell him so, for I will ease my heart,<br>Although it be with hazard of my head. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | What, drunk with choler? Stay and pause awhile. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Earl of Worcester]] |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Here comes your uncle. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Speak of Mortimer?<br>Zounds, I will speak of him, and let my [[Soul|soul]]<br>Want [[Mercy|mercy]] if I do not join with him.<br>In his behalf I’ll empty all these veins,<br>And shed my dear [[Blood|blood]] drop by drop in the [[Dust|dust]],<br>But I will lift the downfall Mortimer<br>As high in the air as this unthankful [[King]],<br>As this ingrate and cankered Bolingbroke. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | (to Worcester)<br>Brother, the [[King]] hath made your nephew mad. |
| WORCESTER | Who struck this heat up after I was gone? |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | He will forsooth have all my prisoners;<br>And when I urged the ransom once again<br>Of my wife’s brother, then his cheek looked pale,<br>And on my face he turned an eye of [[Death|death]],<br>Trembling even at the name of Mortimer. |
| WORCESTER | I cannot blame him: was not he proclaimed<br>By [[Richard]], that dead is, the next of [[Blood|blood]]? |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | He was; I heard the proclamation.<br>And then it was when the unhappy [[King]],<br>Whose wrongs in us God pardon, did set forth<br>Upon his Irish expedition,<br>From whence he, intercepted, did return<br>To be deposed, and shortly murdered. |
| WORCESTER | And for whose [[Death|death]] we in the world’s wide mouth<br>Live scandalized and foully spoken of. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | But soft, I pray you; did [[King]] [[Richard]] then<br>Proclaim my brother [[Edmund]] Mortimer<br>Heir to the [[Crown|crown]]? |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | He did; myself did hear it. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Nay, then I cannot blame his cousin [[King]]<br>That wished him on the barren [[Mountains|mountains]] starve.<br>But shall it be that you that set the [[Crown|crown]]<br>Upon the head of this forgetful man,<br>And for his sake wear the detested blot<br>Of murderous subornation, shall it be<br>That you a world of curses undergo,<br>Being the agents or base second means,<br>The cords, the ladder, or the hangman, rather?<br>O, pardon me that I descend so low<br>To show the line and the predicament<br>Wherein you range under this subtle [[King]]!<br>Shall it for shame be spoken in these days,<br>Or fill up chronicles in [[Time|time]] to come,<br>That men of your nobility and power<br>Did gage them both in an unjust behalf,<br>As both of you, God pardon it, have done:<br>To put down [[Richard]], that sweet lovely [[Rose|rose]],<br>And plant this thorn, this canker, Bolingbroke?<br>And shall it in more shame be further spoken<br>That you are fooled, discarded, and shook off<br>By him for whom these shames ye underwent?<br>No; yet [[Time|time]] serves wherein you may redeem<br>Your banished honours, and restore yourselves<br>Into the good thoughts of the world again,<br>[[Revenge]] the jeering and disdained contempt<br>Of this proud [[King]], who studies day and [[Night|night]]<br>To answer all the debt he owes to you<br>Even with the bloody payment of your deaths.<br>Therefore, I say— |
| WORCESTER | Peace, cousin, say no more.<br>And now I will unclasp a secret book,<br>And to your quick-conceiving discontents<br>I’ll read you matter deep and dangerous,<br>As full of peril and adventurous spirit<br>As to o’erwalk a current roaring loud<br>On the unsteadfast footing of a spear. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | If he fall in, good [[Night|night]], or sink or swim.<br>Send danger from the east unto the west,<br>So [[Honour|honour]] [[Cross|cross]] it from the north to south;<br>And let them grapple. O, the [[Blood|blood]] more stirs<br>To rouse a [[Lion|lion]] than to start a hare! |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | (to Worcester)<br>[[Imagination]] of some great exploit<br>Drives him beyond the bounds of patience. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | By heaven, methinks it were an easy leap<br>To pluck bright [[Honour|honour]] from the pale-faced [[Moon|moon]],<br>Or dive into the [[Bottom|bottom]] of the deep,<br>Where fathom-line could never touch the ground,<br>And pluck up drowned [[Honour|honour]] by the locks,<br>So he that doth redeem her thence might wear,<br>Without corrival, all her dignities.<br>But out upon this half-faced fellowship! |
| WORCESTER | (to Northumberland)<br>He apprehends a world of figures here,<br>But not the form of what he should attend.<br>(To [[Hotspur]]) Good cousin, give me audience for a while,<br>And list to me. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I cry you [[Mercy|mercy]]. |
| WORCESTER | Those same noble Scots<br>That are your prisoners— |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I’ll keep them all.<br>By God, he shall not have a Scot of them;<br>No, if a scot would save his [[Soul|soul]] he shall not.<br>I’ll keep them, by this hand. |
| WORCESTER | You start away,<br>And lend no ear unto my purposes.<br>Those prisoners you shall keep. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Nay, I will; that’s flat.<br>He said he would not ransom Mortimer,<br>Forbade my tongue to speak of Mortimer;<br>But I will find him when he lies asleep,<br>And in his ear I’ll hollo ‘Mortimerl’<br>Nay, I’ll have a starling shall be taught to speak<br>Nothing but ‘Mortimer’, and give it him<br>To keep his anger still in motion. |
| WORCESTER | Hear you, cousin, a word. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | All studies here I solemnly defy,<br>Save how to gall and pinch this Bolingbroke.<br>And that same [[Sword|sword]]-and-buckler Prince of Wales—<br>But that I think his father loves him not<br>And would be glad he met with some mischance—<br>I would have him poisoned with a pot of ale. |
| WORCESTER | Farewell, kinsman. I’ll talk to you<br>When you are better tempered to attend. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | (to [[Hotspur]])<br>Why, what a wasp-stung and impatient [[Fool|fool]]<br>Art thou to break into this woman’s mood,<br>Tying thine ear to no tongue but thine own I |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Why, look you, I am whipped and scourged with rods,<br>Nettled and stung with pismires, when I hear<br>Of this vile politician Bolingbroke.<br>In [[Richard]]’s [[Time|time]]—what d‘ye call the place?<br>A plague upon’t, it is in Gloucestershire.<br>‘Twas where the madcap Duke his uncle kept—<br>His uncle York—where I first bowed my knee<br>Unto this [[King|king]] of smiles, this Bolingbroke.<br>’Sblood, when you and he came back from<br>Ravenspurgh. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | At Berkeley [[Castle|castle]]. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | You say true.<br>Why, what a candy deal of courtesy<br>This fawning greyhound then did proffer me!<br>‘Look when his infant fortune came to age’,<br>And ‘gentle Harry Percy’, and ‘kind cousin’.<br>O, the devil take such cozeners!—God forgive me.<br>Good uncle, tell your tale; I have done. |
| WORCESTER | Nay, if you have not, to’t again.<br>We’ll stay your leisure. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I have done, i’[[Faith|faith]]. |
| WORCESTER | Then once more to your Scottish prisoners.<br>Deliver them up without their ransom straight;<br>And make the Douglas’ son your only mean<br>For powers in Scotland, which, for divers reasons<br>Which I shall send you written, be assured<br>Will easily be granted. (To Northumberland) You, my<br>lord,<br>Your son in Scotland being thus employed,<br>Shall secretly into the bosom creep<br>Of that same noble prelate [[Well|well]]-beloved,<br>The Archbishop. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Of York, is’t not? |
| WORCESTER | True, who bears hard<br>His brother’s [[Death|death]] at Bristol, the Lord Scrope.<br>I speak not this in estimation,<br>As what I think might be, but what I know<br>Is ruminated, plotted, and set down,<br>And only stays but to behold the face<br>Of that occasion that shall bring it on. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I smell it; upon my life, it will do [[Well|well]]! |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Before the game is afoot thou still lett’st slip. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Why, it cannot choose but be a noble plot—<br>And then the power of Scotland and of York<br>To join with Mortimer, ha? |
| WORCESTER | And so they shall. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | In [[Faith|faith]], it is exceedingly [[Well|well]] aimed. |
| WORCESTER | And ’tis no little reason bids us [[Speed|speed]]<br>To save our heads by raising of a head;<br>For, bear ourselves as even as we can,<br>The [[King]] will always think him in our debt,<br>And think we think ourselves unsatisfied<br>Till he hath found a [[Time|time]] to pay us [[Home|home]].<br>And see already how he doth begin<br>To make us strangers to his looks of [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | He does, he does. We’ll be revenged on him. |
| WORCESTER | Cousin, farewell. No further go in this<br>Than I by letters shall direct your course.<br>When [[Time|time]] is ripe, which will be suddenly,<br>I’ll steal to Glyndŵr and Lord Mortimer,<br>Where you and Douglas and our powers at once,<br>As I will fashion it, shall happily meet,<br>To bear our fortunes in our own strong arms,<br>Which now we hold at much uncertainty. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Farewell, good brother. We shall thrive, I trust. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | (to Worcester)<br>Uncle, adieu. O, let the hours be short<br>Till fields and blows and groans applaud our sport! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Worcester at one door, Northumberland and [[Hotspur]] at another door |
