| *(didascalia)* | Enter Prince Harry, Poins, Harvey, and Russell |
| POINS | Come, shelter, shelter! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Harvey and Russell at another door |
| POINS | I have removed Oldcastle’s [[Horse|horse]], and he frets like a<br>gummed velvet. |
| *(didascalia)* | PRINCE HARRY Stand close! Exit Poins |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir John Oldcastle |
| SIR JOHN | Poins ! Poins, and be hanged! Poins ! |
| PRINCE HARRY | Peace, ye fat-kidneyed rascal ! What a brawling dost thou keep ! |
| SIR JOHN | Where’s Poins, Hal? |
| *(didascalia)* | PRINCE HARRY He is walked up to the top of the [[Hill|hill]]. I’ll go seek him. Exit |
| SIR JOHN | I am accursed to rob in that thief’s company. The rascal hath removed my [[Horse|horse]] and tied him I know not where. If I travel but four foot by the square further afoot, I shall break my [[Wind|wind]]. [[Well]], I [[Doubt|doubt]] not but to die a fair [[Death|death]], for all this—if I scape hanging for killing that rogue. I have forsworn his company hourly any [[Time|time]] this two-and-twenty years, and yet I am bewitched with the rogue’s company. If the rascal have not given me medicines to make me [[Love|love]] him, I’ll be hanged. It could not be else: I have drunk medicines. Poins ! Hal ! A plague upon you both ! Russell ! Harvey ! I’ll starve ere I’ll rob a foot further. An ’twere not as good a deed as drink to turn true man and to leave these rogues, I am the veriest varlet that ever chewed with a tooth. Eight yards of uneven ground is threescore and ten miles afoot with me, and the stony-hearted villains know it [[Well|well]] enough. A plague upon’t when thieves cannot be true one to another ! |
| *(didascalia)* | They whistle. Enter Prince Harry, Poins, Harvey, and Russell |
| SIR JOHN | Whew !A plague upon you all !Give me my [[Horse|horse]], you<br>rogues, give me my [[Horse|horse]], and be hanged! |
| PRINCE HARRY | Peace, ye fat-guts. Lie down, lay thine ear close to the ground, and list if thou canst hear the tread of travellers. |
| SIR JOHN | Have you any levers to lift me up again, being down ? ’Sblood, I’ll not bear my own flesh so far afoot again for all the coin in thy father’s exchequer. What a plague mean ye to colt me thus? |
| PRINCE HARRY | Thou liest : thou art not colted, thou art uncolted. |
| SIR JOHN | I prithee, good [[Prince Hal]], help me to my [[Horse|horse]], good [[King|king]]’s son. |
| PRINCE HARRY | Out, ye rogue, shall I be your ostler? |
| SIR JOHN | Hang thyself in thine own heir-apparent garters ! If I be ta’en, I’ll peach for this. An I have not ballads made on you all and sung to filthy tunes, let a cup of sack be my [[Poison|poison]]. When a jest is so forward, and afoot too ! I hate it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Gadshill visored |
| GADSHILL | Stand! |
| SIR JOHN | So I do, against my will. |
| POINS | O, ’tis our setter, I know his voice. Gadshill, what news? |
| *(didascalia)* | GADSHILL Case ye, case ye, on with your visors ! There’s money of the [[King]]’s coming down the [[Hill|hill]]; ’tis going to the [[King]]’s exchequer. |
| SIR JOHN | You lie, ye rogue, ’tis going to the [[King]]’s tavern. |
| GADSHILL | There’s enough to make us all. |
| SIR JOHN | To be hanged. |
| *(didascalia)* | They put on visors |
| PRINCE HARRY | Sirs, you four shall front them in the narrow lane. Ned Poins and I will walk lower. If they scape from your encounter, then they light on us. |
| HARVEY | How many be there of them? |
| GADSHILL | Some eight or ten. |
| SIR JOHN | Zounds, will they not rob us? |
| PRINCE HARRY | What, a coward, Sir John Paunch? |
| SIR JOHN | Indeed I am not [[John of Gaunt]] your grandfather, but yet no coward, Hal. |
| PRINCE HARRY | [[Well]], we leave that to the proof. |
| POINS | Sirrah Jack, thy [[Horse|horse]] stands behind the hedge. When thou needest him, there thou shalt find him. Farewell, and stand fast. |
| SIR JOHN | Now cannot I strike him if I should be hanged. |
| PRINCE HARRY | (aside to Poins) Ned, where are our disguises? |
| POINS | (aside to the Prince) Here, hard by. Stand close. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt the Prince and Poins |
| SIR JOHN | Now, my masters, happy man be his dole, say I; every man to his business. |
| *(didascalia)* | They stand aside.] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Travellers, amongst them the Carriers l |
| FIRSTI TRAVELLER | Come, neighbour, the boy shall lead our horses down the [[Hill|hill]]. We’ll walk afoot a while, and ease their legs. |
| *(didascalia)* | THIEVES coming forward Stand ! |
| *(didascalia)* | SECOND TRAVELLER Jesus bless us ! |
| SIR JOHN | Strike, down with them, cut the villains’ throats ! Ah, whoreson caterpillars, bacon-fed knaves ! They hate us youth. Down with them, fleece them ! |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST TRAVELLER O, we are undone, both we and ours for ever! |
| SIR JOHN | Hang ye, gorbellied knaves, are ye undone ? No, ye fat chuffs; I would your store were here. On, bacons, on ! What, ye knaves! Young men must live. You are grand-jurors, are ye? We’ll jure ye, [[Faith|faith]].<br>Here they rob them and bind them. Exeunt the thieves with the travellers |
