| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Earl of Worcester]] and Sir [[Richard]] Vernon |
| WORCESTER | O no, my nephew must not know, Sir [[Richard]],<br>The liberal and kind offer of the [[King]]. |
| VERNON | ’Twere best he did. |
| WORCESTER | Then are we all undone.<br>It is not possible, it cannot be,<br>The [[King]] should keep his word in loving us.<br>He will suspect us still, and find a [[Time|time]]<br>To punish this offence in other faults.<br>Supposition all our lives shall be stuck full of eyes,<br>For treason is but trusted like the fox,<br>Who, ne‘er so tame, so cherished, and locked up,<br>Will have a wild trick of his ancestors.<br>Look how we can, or sad or merrily,<br>Interpretation will misquote our looks,<br>And we shall feed like oxen at a stall,<br>The better cherished still the nearer [[Death|death]].<br>My nephew’s trespass may be [[Well|well]] forgot;<br>It hath the excuse of youth and heat of [[Blood|blood]],<br>And an adopted name of privilege—<br>A hare-brained [[Hotspur]], governed by a spleen.<br>All his offences live upon my head,<br>And on his father’s. We did train him on,<br>And, his corruption being ta’en from us,<br>We as the [[spring]] of all shall pay for all.<br>Therefore, good cousin, let not Harry know<br>In any case the offer of the [[King]]. |
| VERNON | Deliver what you will; I’ll say ’tis so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Hotspur]] and the Earl of Douglas |
| VERNON | Here comes your cousin. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | My uncle is returned.<br>Deliver up my lord of Westmorland.<br>Uncle, what news? |
| WORCESTER | The [[King]] will bid you battle presently. |
| DOUGLAS | Defy him by the Lord of Westmorland. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Lord Douglas, go you and tell him so. |
| DOUGLAS | Marry, and shall, and very willingly. Exit |
| WORCESTER | There is no seeming [[Mercy|mercy]] in the [[King]]. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Did you beg any? God forbid! |
| WORCESTER | I told him gently of our grievances,<br>Of his oath-breaking, which he mended thus:<br>By now forswearing that he is forsworn.<br>He calls us ‘rebels’, ‘traitors’, and will scourge<br>With haughty arms this hateful name in us. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Douglas |
| DOUGLAS | Arm, gentlemen, to arms, for I have thrown<br>A brave defiance in [[King]] Henry’s teeth-<br>And Westmorland that was engaged did bear it—<br>Which cannot choose but bring him quickly on. |
| WORCESTER | (to [[Hotspur]])<br>The Prince of Wales stepped forth before the [[King]]<br>And, nephew, challenged you to single fight. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | O, would the quarrel lay upon our heads,<br>And that no man might draw short breath today<br>But I and Harry Monmouth ! Tell me, tell me,<br>How showed his tasking? Seemed it in contempt? |
| VERNON | No, by my [[Soul|soul]], I never in my life<br>Did hear a challenge urged more modestly,<br>Unless a brother should a brother dare<br>To gentle exercise and proof of arms.<br>He gave you all the duties of a man,<br>Trimmed up your praises with a princely tongue,<br>Spoke your deservings like a chronicle,<br>Making you ever better than his praise<br>By still dispraising praise valued with you;<br>And, which became him like a prince indeed,<br>He made a blushing cital of himself,<br>And chid his truant youth with such a grace<br>As if he mastered there a [[Double|double]] spirit<br>Of teaching and of learning instantly.<br>There did he pause; but let me tell the world,<br>If he outlive the envy of this day,<br>England did never owe so sweet a [[Hope|hope]],<br>So much misconstrued in his wantonness. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Cousin, I think thou art enamoured<br>On his follies. Never did I hear<br>Of any prince so wild a liberty.<br>But be he as he will, yet once ere [[Night|night]]<br>I will embrace him with a soldier’s arm,<br>That he shall shrink under my courtesy.<br>Arm, arm, with [[Speed|speed]]! And fellows, soldiers, friends,<br>Better consider what you have to do<br>Than I, that have not [[Well|well]] the gift of tongue,<br>Can lift your [[Blood|blood]] up with [[Persuasion|persuasion]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | My lord, here are letters for you. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I cannot read them now. [Exit Messenger]<br>O gentlemen, the [[Time|time]] of life is short.<br>To spend that shortness basely were too long<br>If life did ride upon a dial’s point,<br>Still ending at the arrival of an hour.<br>An if we live, we live to tread on kings;<br>If die, brave [[Death|death]] when princes die with us!<br>Now for our consciences: the arms are fair<br>When the intent of bearing them is just. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger |
| MESSENGER | My lord, prepare; the [[King]] comes on apace. [Exit] |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I thank him that he cuts me from my tale,<br>For I profess not talking, only this:<br>Let each man do his best. And here draw I<br>A [[Sword|sword]] whose temper I intend to stain<br>With the best [[Blood|blood]] that I can meet withal<br>In the adventure of this perilous day.<br>Now Esperance! Percy! And set on! |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound all the lofty instruments of war, |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | And by that music let us all embrace,<br>For, heaven to earth, some of us never shall<br>A second [[Time|time]] do such a courtesy.<br>The trumpets sound. Here they embrace. Exeunt |
