| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Henry, Prince Harry, Lord John of Lancaster, the Earl of Westmorland, Sir Walter Blunt, and Sir John Oldcastle |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How bloodily the sun begins to peer Above yon bulky [[Hill|hill]]! The day looks pale At his distemp’rature. |
| PRINCE HARRY | The southern [[Wind|wind]] Doth play the trumpet to his purposes, And by his hollow whistling in the [[Leaves|leaves]] 5 Foretells a tempest and a blust’ring day. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Then with the losers let it sympathize, For nothing can seem foul to those that win. The trumpet sounds a parley within. Enter the [[Earl of Worcester]] and Sir [[Richard]] Vernon How now, my lord of Worcester? ’Tis not [[Well|well]] That you and I should meet upon such terms 10 As now we meet. You have deceived our trust, And made us doff our easy robes of peace To crush our old limbs in ungentle steel. This is not [[Well|well]], my lord, this is not [[Well|well]]. What say you to it? Will you again unknit 15 This churlish knot of all-abhorred war, And move in that obedient orb again Where you did give a fair and natural light, And be no more an exhaled meteor, A prodigy of [[Fear|fear]], and a portent Of broached mischief to the [[unborn]] times? |
| WORCESTER | Hear me, my liege. For mine own part, I could be [[Well|well]] content To entertain the lag-end of my life With quiet hours; for I protest, 25 I have not sought the day of this dislike. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | You have not sought it? How comes it, then? |
| SIR JOHN | Rebellion lay in his way, and he found it. |
| PRINCE HARRY | Peace, chewet, peace! |
