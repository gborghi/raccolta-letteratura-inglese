| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Henry, Prince Harry, Lord John of Lancaster, the Earl of Westmorland, Sir Walter Blunt, and Sir John Oldcastle |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How bloodily the sun begins to peer<br>Above yon bulky [[Hill|hill]]! The day looks pale<br>At his distemp’rature. |
| PRINCE HARRY | The southern [[Wind|wind]]<br>Doth play the trumpet to his purposes,<br>And by his hollow whistling in the [[Leaves|leaves]] 5<br>Foretells a tempest and a blust’ring day. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Then with the losers let it sympathize,<br>For nothing can seem foul to those that win.<br>The trumpet sounds |
| *(didascalia)* | a parley within. Enter the [[Earl of Worcester]] |
| *(didascalia)* | and Sir [[Richard]] Vernon |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How now, my lord of Worcester? ’Tis not [[Well|well]]<br>That you and I should meet upon such terms 10<br>As now we meet. You have deceived our trust,<br>And made us doff our easy robes of peace<br>To crush our old limbs in ungentle steel.<br>This is not [[Well|well]], my lord, this is not [[Well|well]].<br>What say you to it? Will you again unknit 15<br>This churlish knot of all-abhorred war,<br>And move in that obedient orb again<br>Where you did give a fair and natural light,<br>And be no more an exhaled meteor,<br>A prodigy of [[Fear|fear]], and a portent<br>Of broached mischief to the [[unborn]] times? |
| WORCESTER | Hear me, my liege.<br>For mine own part, I could be [[Well|well]] content<br>To entertain the lag-end of my life<br>With quiet hours; for I protest, 25<br>I have not sought the day of this dislike. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | You have not sought it? How comes it, then? |
| SIR JOHN | Rebellion lay in his way, and he found it. |
| PRINCE HARRY | Peace, chewet, peace! |
| WORCESTER | (to the [[King]])<br>It pleased your majesty to turn your looks<br>Of favour from myself and all our [[house]];<br>And yet I must remember you, my lord,<br>We were the first and dearest of your friends.<br>For you my staff of office did I break<br>In [[Richard]]’s [[Time|time]], and posted day and [[Night|night]]<br>To meet you on the way and kiss your hand<br>When yet you were in place and in account<br>Nothing so strong and fortunate as I.<br>It was myself, my brother, and his son<br>That brought you [[Home|home]], and boldly did outdare<br>The dangers of the [[Time|time]]. You swore to us,<br>And you did swear that oath at Doncaster,<br>That you did nothing purpose ‘gainst the state,<br>Nor claim no further than your new-fall’n right,<br>The seat of Gaunt, dukedom of Lancaster.<br>To this we swore our aid, but in short space<br>It rained down fortune show’ring on your head,<br>And such a flood of [[Greatness|greatness]] fell on you,<br>What with our help, what with the absent [[King]],<br>What with the injuries of a wanton [[Time|time]],<br>The seeming sufferances that you had borne,<br>And the contrarious winds that held the [[King]]<br>So long in his unlucky Irish wars<br>That all in England did repute him dead;<br>And from this swarm of fair advantages<br>You took occasion to be quickly wooed<br>To gripe the general sway into your hand,<br>Forgot your oath to us at Doncaster,<br>And being fed by us, you used us so<br>As that ungentle gull, the cuckoo’s bird,<br>Useth the sparrow—did oppress our nest,<br>Grew by our feeding to so great a bulk<br>That even our [[Love|love]] durst not come near your sight<br>For [[Fear|fear]] of swallowing. But with nimble wing<br>We were enforced for safety’ sake to fly 65<br>Out of your sight, and raise this present head,<br>Whereby we stand opposed by such means<br>As you yourself have forged against yourself,<br>By unkind usage, dangerous countenance,<br>And violation of all [[Faith|faith]] and troth<br>Sworn to us in your younger enterprise. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | These things indeed you have articulate,<br>Proclaimed at market crosses, read in churches,<br>To face the garment of rebellion<br>With some fine colour that may please the eye<br>Of fickle changelings and poor discontents,<br>Which gape and rub the elbow at the news<br>Of hurly-burly innovation;<br>And never yet did insurrection want<br>Such [[Water|water]]-colours to impaint his cause,<br>Nor moody beggars starving for a [[Time|time]]<br>Of pell-mell havoc and confusion. |
| PRINCE HARRY | In both our armies there is many a [[Soul|soul]]<br>Shall pay full dearly for this encounter<br>If once they join in trial. Tell your nephew<br>The Prince of Wales doth join with all the world<br>In praise of Henry Percy. By my hopes,<br>This present enterprise set off his head,<br>I do not think a braver gentleman,<br>More active-valiant or more valiant-young,<br>More daring, or more bold, is now alive<br>To grace this latter age with noble deeds.<br>For my part, I may speak it to my shame,<br>I have a truant been to chivalry;<br>And so I hear he doth account me too.<br>Yet this, before my father’s majesty:<br>I am content that he shall take the odds<br>Of his great name and estimation,<br>And will, to save the [[Blood|blood]] on either side,<br>Try fortune with him in a single fight. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | And, Prince of Wales, so dare we venture thee,<br>Albeit considerations infinite<br>Do make against it. No, good Worcester, no.<br>We [[Love|love]] our people [[Well|well]]; even those we [[Love|love]]<br>That are misled upon your cousin’s part; 105<br>And will they take the offer of our grace,<br>Both he and they and you, yea, every man<br>Shall be my friend again, and I’ll be his.<br>So tell your cousin, and bring me word<br>What he will do. But if he will not yield,<br>Rebuke and dread correction wait on us,<br>And they shall do their office. So be gone.<br>We will not now be troubled with reply.<br>We offer fair; take it advisedly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Worcester [and Vernon] |
| PRINCE HARRY | It will not be accepted, on my life.<br>The Douglas and the [[Hotspur]] both together<br>Are confident against the world in arms. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Hence, therefore, every leader to his charge,<br>For on their answer will we set on them,<br>And God befriend us as our cause is just! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Prince Harry and Oldcastle |
| SIR JOHN | Hal, if thou see me down in the battle, and bestride me, so. ’Tis a point of [[Friendship|friendship]]. |
| PRINCE HARRY | Nothing but a colossus can do thee that [[Friendship|friendship]]. Say thy prayers, and farewell. |
| SIR JOHN | I would ’twere bed-[[Time|time]], Hal, and all [[Well|well]]. |
| PRINCE HARRY | Why, thou owest God a [[Death|death]]. Exit |
| SIR JOHN | ‘Tis not due yet. I would be loath to pay him before his day. What need I be so forward with him that calls not on me? [[Well]], ’tis no matter; [[Honour|honour]] pricks me on. Yea, but how if [[Honour|honour]] prick me off when I come on? How then? Can [[Honour|honour]] set-to a leg? No. Or an arm? No. Or take away the grief of a wound? No. [[Honour]] hath no skill in surgery, then? No. What is [[Honour|honour]]? A word. What is in that word ‘[[Honour|honour]]’? What is that ‘[[Honour|honour]]’ ? Air. A trim reckoning! Who hath it? He that died o’ Wednesday. Doth he feel it? No. Doth he hear it? No. ’Tis insensible then? Yea, to the dead. But will it not live with the living? No. Why? Detraction will not suffer it. Therefore I’ll none of it. [[Honour]] is a mere scutcheon. And so ends my catechism. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
