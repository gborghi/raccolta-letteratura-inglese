| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Hotspur]] and the Earls of,Worcester and Douglas |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | [[Well]] said, my noble Scot ! If speaking [[Truth|truth]]<br>In this fine age were not thought flattery,<br>Such attribution should the Douglas have<br>As not a soldier of this season’s stamp<br>Should go so general current through the world.<br>By God, I cannot flatter, I do defy<br>The tongues of soothers, but a braver place<br>In my heart’s [[Love|love]] hath no man than yourself.<br>Nay, task me to my word, approve me, lord. |
| DOUGLAS | Thou art the [[King|king]] of [[Honour|honour]].<br>No man so potent breathes upon the ground<br>But I will beard him. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Do so, and ’tis [[Well|well]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger with letters |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | What letters hast thou there? I can but thank you. |
| MESSENGER | These letters come from your father. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Letters from him? Why comes he not himself? 15 |
| MESSENGER | He cannot come, my lord, he is grievous sick. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Zounds, how has he the leisure to be sick<br>In such a jostling [[Time|time]]? Who leads his power?<br>Under whose [[government]] come they along? |
| MESSENGER | His letters bears his mind, not I, my lord.<br>[[Hotspur]] reads the letter |
| WORCESTER | I prithee tell me, doth he keep his bed? |
| MESSENGER | He did, my lord, four days ere I set forth;<br>And at the [[Time|time]] of my departure thence<br>He was much feared by his physicians. |
| WORCESTER | I would the state of [[Time|time]] had first been whole<br>Ere he by sickness had been visited.<br>His health was never better worth than now. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Sick now? Droop now? This sickness doth infect<br>The very life-[[Blood|blood]] of our enterprise.<br>’Tis catching hither, even to our camp.<br>He writes me here that inward sickness stays him,<br>And that his friends by deputation<br>Could not so soon be drawn; nor did he think it meet<br>To lay so dangerous and dear a trust<br>On any [[Soul|soul]] removed but on his own.<br>Yet doth he give us bold advertisement<br>That with our small conjunction we should on,<br>To see how fortune is disposed to us;<br>For, as he writes, there is no quailing now,<br>Because the [[King]] is certainly possessed 40<br>Of all our purposes. What say you to it? |
| WORCESTER | Your father’s sickness is a maim to us. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | A perilous gash, a very limb lopped off.<br>And yet, in [[Faith|faith]], it is not. His present want<br>Seems more than we shall find it. Were it good<br>To set the exact wealth of all our states<br>All at one cast, to set so rich a main<br>On the nice hazard of one doubtful hour?<br>It were not good, for therein should we read<br>The very [[Bottom|bottom]] and the sole of [[Hope|hope]],<br>The very list, the very utmost bound,<br>Of all our fortunes. |
| DOUGLAS | [[Faith]], and so we should, where now remains<br>A sweet reversion—we may boldly spend<br>Upon the [[Hope|hope]] of what is to come in.<br>A comfort of retirement lives in this. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | A rendezvous, a [[Home|home]] to fly unto,<br>If that the devil and mischance look big<br>Upon the maidenhead of our affairs. |
| WORCESTER | But yet I would your father had been here. 60<br>The quality and hair of our attempt<br>Brooks no division. It will be thought<br>By some that know not why he is away<br>That wisdom, loyalty, and mere dislike<br>Of our proceedings kept the Earl from hence;<br>And think how such an apprehension<br>May turn the tide of fearful faction,<br>And breed a kind of question in our cause.<br>For, [[Well|well]] you know, we of the off’ring side<br>Must keep aloof from strict arbitrement,<br>And stop all sight-holes, every loop from whence<br>The eye of reason may pry in upon us.<br>This absence of your father’s draws a curtain<br>That shows the ignorant a kind of [[Fear|fear]]<br>Before not dreamt of. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | You strain too far.<br>I rather of his absence make this use:<br>It lends a lustre, and more great opinion,<br>A larger dare to our great enterprise,<br>Than if the Earl were here; for men must think<br>If we without his help can make a head 80<br>To push against a kingdom, with his help<br>We shall o’erturn it topsy-turvy down.<br>Yet all goes [[Well|well]], yet all our joints are whole. |
| DOUGLAS | As heart can think, there is not such a word<br>Spoke of in Scotland as this term of [[Fear|fear]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir [[Richard]] Vernon |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | My cousin Vernon! Welcome, by my [[Soul|soul]]! |
| VERNON | Pray God my news be worth a welcome, lord.<br>The Earl of Westmorland, seven thousand strong,<br>Is marching hitherwards; with him [[Prince John]]. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | No harm. What more? |
| VERNON | And further I have learned<br>The [[King]] himself in person is set forth,<br>Or hitherwards intended speedily,<br>With strong and mighty preparation. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | He shall be welcome too. Where is his son,<br>The nimble-footed madcap Prince of Wales,<br>And his comrades that daffed the world aside<br>And bid it pass? |
| VERNON | All furnished, all in arms,<br>All plumed like ostriches, that with the [[Wind|wind]] |
| *(didascalia)* |  |
| VERNON | Baiting like eagles having lately bathed,<br>Glittering in golden coats like images,<br>As full of spirit as the month of May,<br>And gorgeous as the sun at midsummer;<br>Wanton as youthful goats, wild as young bulls.<br>I saw young Harry with his beaver on, 105<br>His cuishes on his thighs, gallantly armed,<br>Rise from the ground like feathered Mercury,<br>And vaulted with such ease into his seat<br>As if an angel dropped down from the clouds<br>To turn and [[Wind|wind]] a fiery Pegasus, 110<br>And [[Witch|witch]] the world with noble horsemanship. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | No more, no more! Worse than the sun in March,<br>This praise doth nourish agues. Let them come!<br>They come like sacrifices in their trim,<br>And to the [[Fire|fire]]-eyed maid of smoky war 115<br>All hot and bleeding will we offer them.<br>The mailed Mars shall on his altar sit<br>Up to the ears in [[Blood|blood]]. I am on [[Fire|fire]]<br>To hear this rich reprisal is so nigh,<br>And yet not ours! Come, let me taste my [[Horse|horse]],<br>Who is to bear me like a thunderbolt<br>Against the bosom of the Prince of Wales.<br>Harry to Harry shall, hot [[Horse|horse]] to [[Horse|horse]],<br>Meet and ne’er part till one drop down a corpse.<br>O, that Glyndwr were come! |
| VERNON | There is more news.<br>I learned in Worcester, as I rode along,<br>He cannot draw his power this fourteen days. |
| DOUGLAS | That’s the worst tidings that I hear of yet. |
| WORCESTER | Ay, by my [[Faith|faith]], that bears a frosty sound. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | What may the [[King]]’s whole battle reach unto? |
| VERNON | To thirty thousand. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Forty let it be.<br>My father and Glyndŵr being both away,<br>The powers of us may serve so great a day.<br>Come, let us take a muster speedily.<br>Doomsday is near: die all, die merrily. |
| DOUGLAS | Talk not of dying; I am out of [[Fear|fear]]<br>Of [[Death|death]] or [[Death|death]]’s hand for this one half year. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
