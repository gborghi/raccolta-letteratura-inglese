| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | I am come to know your pleasure. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | (aside) That you might know it would much better please me Than to demand what ’tis. (To [[Isabella]]) Your brother cannot live. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | Even so. Heaven keep your [[Honour|honour]]. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Yet may he live a while, and it may be As long as you or I. Yet he must die. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | Under your sentence? |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Yea. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | When, I beseech you?—that in his reprieve, Longer or shorter, he may be so fitted That his [[Soul|soul]] sicken not. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Ha, fie, these filthy vices! It were as good To pardon him that hath from [[Nature|nature]] stolen A man already made, as to remit Their saucy sweetness that do coin God’s image In stamps that are forbid. ’Tis all as easy Falsely to take away a life true made As to put metal in restrained moulds, To make a false one. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | ’Tis set down so in heaven, but not in earth. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Say you so? Then I shall pose you quickly. Which had you rather: that the most just law Now took your brother’s life, or, to redeem him, Give up your [[Body|body]] to such sweet uncleanness As she that he hath stained? |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | Sir, believe this. I had rather give my [[Body|body]] than my [[Soul|soul]]. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | I talk not of your [[Soul|soul]]. Our compelled sins Stand more for number than for account. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | How say you? |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Nay, I’ll not warrant that, for I can speak Against the thing I say. Answer to this. I now, the voice of the recorded law, Pronounce a sentence on your brother’s life. Might there not be a [[Charity|charity]] in sin To save this brother’s life? |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | Please you to do’t, I’ll take it as a peril to my [[Soul|soul]] It is no sin at all, but [[Charity|charity]]. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Pleased you to do’t at peril of your [[Soul|soul]] Were equal poise of sin and [[Charity|charity]]. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | That I do beg his life, if it be sin, Heaven let me bear it. You granting of my suit, If that be sin, I’ll make it my morn [[prayer]] To have it added to the faults of mine, And nothing of your answer. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Nay, but hear me. Your sense pursues not mine. Either you are ignorant, Or seem so craftily, and that’s not good. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | Let me be ignorant, and in nothing good But graciously to know I am no better. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Thus wisdom wishes to appear most bright When it doth tax itself: as these black masks Proclaim an enshield [[Beauty|beauty]] ten times louder Than [[Beauty|beauty]] could, displayed. But mark me. To be received plain, I’ll speak more gross. Your brother is to die. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | So. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | And his offence is so, as it appears, Accountant to the law upon that [[Pain|pain]]. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | True. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | Admit no other way to save his life— As I subscribe not that nor any other— But, in the loss of question, that you his sister, Finding yourself desired of such a person Whose credit with the judge, or own great place, Could fetch your brother from the manacles Of the all-binding law, and that there were No earthly mean to save him, but that either You must lay down the treasures of your [[Body|body]] To this supposed, or else to let him suffer— What would you do? |
