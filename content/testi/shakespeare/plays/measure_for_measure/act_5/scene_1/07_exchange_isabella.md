| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | O, give me pardon, That I, your vassal, have employed and pained Your unknown sovereignty. |
| DUKE | You are pardoned, Isabel. And now, dear maid, be you as free to us. Your brother’s [[Death|death]] I know sits at your heart, And you may marvel why I obscured myself, Labouring to save his life, and would not rather Make rash remonstrance of my hidden power Than let him so be lost. O most kind maid, It was the swift celerity of his [[Death|death]], Which I did think with slower foot came on, That brained my purpose. But peace be with him! That life is better life, past fearing [[Death|death]], Than that which lives to [[Fear|fear]]. Make it your comfort, So happy is your brother. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | I do, my lord. Enter [[Angelo]], [[Mariana]], Friar Peter, and the Provost |
| DUKE | For this new-married man approaching here, Whose salt [[Imagination|imagination]] yet hath wronged Your [[Well|well]]-defended [[Honour|honour]], you must pardon For [[Mariana]]’s sake; but as he adjudged your brother— Being criminal in [[Double|double]] violation Of sacred chastity and of promise-breach, Thereon dependent, for your brother’s life— The very [[Mercy|mercy]] of the law cries out Most audible, even from his proper tongue, ‘An [[Angelo]] for [[Claudio]], [[Death|death]] for [[Death|death]]’. Haste still pays haste, and leisure answers leisure; Like doth quit like, and measure still for measure. Then, [[Angelo]], thy fault’s thus manifested, Which, though thou wouldst deny, denies thee vantage. We do condemn thee to the very block Where [[Claudio]] stooped to [[Death|death]], and with like haste. Away with him. |
| [[Mariana|MARIANA]] | O my most gracious lord, I [[Hope|hope]] you will not mock me with a husband! |
| DUKE | It is your husband mocked you with a husband. Consenting to the safeguard of your [[Honour|honour]], I thought your [[Marriage|marriage]] fit; else imputation, For that he knew you, might reproach your life, And choke your good to come. For his possessions, Although by confiscation they are ours, We do enstate and widow you with all, To buy you a better husband. |
| [[Mariana|MARIANA]] | O my dear lord, I crave no other, nor no better man. |
| DUKE | Never crave him; we are definitive. |
| [[Mariana|MARIANA]] | Gentle my liege— |
| DUKE | You do but lose your labour.— Away with him to [[Death|death]]. (To [[Lucio]]) Now, sir, to you. |
| [[Mariana|MARIANA]] | (kneeling) O my good lord!—Sweet Isabel, take my part; Lend me your knees, and all my life to come I’ll lend you all my life to do you service. |
| DUKE | Against all sense you do importune her. Should she kneel down in [[Mercy|mercy]] of this fact, Her brother’s [[Ghost|ghost]] his paved bed would break, And take her hence in horror. |
| [[Mariana|MARIANA]] | Isabel, Sweet Isabel, do yet but kneel by me. Hold up your hands; say nothing; I’ll speak all. They say best men are moulded out of faults, And, for the most, become much more the better For being a little bad. So may my husband. O Isabel, will you not lend a knee? |
| DUKE | He dies for [[Claudio]]’s [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Isabella|ISABELLA]] | (kneeling) Most bounteous sir, Look, if it please you, on this man condemned As if my brother lived. I partly think A due sincerity governed his deeds, Till he did look on me. Since it is so, Let him not die. My brother had but justice, In that he did the thing for which he died. For [[Angelo]], His act did not o’ertake his bad intent, And must be buried but as an intent That perished by the way. Thoughts are no subjects, Intents but merely thoughts. |
