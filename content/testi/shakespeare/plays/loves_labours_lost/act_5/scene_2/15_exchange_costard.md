| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Fellow [[Hector]], she is gone, she is two months on her way. |
| ARMADO | What meanest thou? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | [[Faith]], unless you play the honest Trojan the poor wench is cast away. She’s quick. The [[Child|child]] brags in her belly already. ’Tis yours. |
| ARMADO | Dost thou infamonize me among potentates? Thou shalt die. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Then shall [[Hector]] be whipped for Jaquenetta that is quick by him, and hanged for [[Pompey]] that is dead by him. 675 |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Most rare [[Pompey]]! |
| BOYET | Renowned [[Pompey]]! |
| BIRON | Greater than great—great, great, great [[Pompey]], [[Pompey]] the Huge. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | [[Hector]] trembles. |
| BIRON | [[Pompey]] is moved. More Ates, more Ates—stir them on, stir them on! |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | [[Hector]] will challenge him. |
| BIRON | Ay, if a have no more man’s [[Blood|blood]] in his belly than will sup a flea. |
| ARMADO | By the North Pole, I do challenge thee. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I will not fight with a pole, like a northern man. I’ll slash, I’ll do it by the [[Sword|sword]]. I bepray you, let me borrow my arms again. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Room for the incensed Worthies. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I’ll do it in my shirt. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Most resolute [[Pompey]]. |
| MOTE | (aside to Armado) Master, let me take you a buttonhole lower. Do you not see [[Pompey]] is uncasing for the combat? What mean you? You will lose your reputation. |
| ARMADO | Gentlemen and soldiers, pardon me. I will not combat in my shirt. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | You may not deny it, [[Pompey]] hath made the challenge. |
| ARMADO | Sweet bloods, I both may and will. |
| BIRON | What reason have you for’t? |
| ARMADO | The naked [[Truth|truth]] of it is, I have no shirt. I go woolward for penance. MOTE True, and it was enjoined him in Rome for want of linen, since when I’ll be sworn he wore none but a dish-clout of Jaquenetta’s, and that a wears next his heart, for a favour. Enter a messenger, Monsieur Mercadé MERCADÉ God save you, madam. |
| PRINCESS | Welcome, Mercadé, But that thou interrupt’st our merriment. MERCADÉ I am sorry, madam, for the news I bring Is heavy in my tongue. The [[King]] your father— |
| PRINCESS | Dead, for my life. MERCADÉ Even so. My tale is told. |
| BIRON | Worthies, away. The scene begins to cloud. |
| ARMADO | For mine own part, I breathe free breath. I have seen the day of wrong through the little hole of discretion, and I will right myself like a soldier. Exeunt the Worthies |
| [[King|KING]] | How fares your majesty ? |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Boyet, prepare. I will away tonight. |
| [[King|KING]] | Madam, not so, I do beseech you stay. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Prepare, I say. I thank you, gracious lords, For all your fair endeavours, and entreat, Out of a new-sad [[Soul|soul]], that you vouchsafe In your rich wisdom to excuse or hide The liberal opposition of our spirits. If overboldly we have borne ourselves In the converse of breath, your gentleness Was guilty of it. Farewell, worthy lord. A heavy heart bears not a nimble tongue. Excuse me so coming too short of thanks, For my great suit so easily obtained. |
| [[King|KING]] | The extreme parts of [[Time|time]] extremely forms All causes to the purpose of his [[Speed|speed]], And often at his very loose decides That which long process could not arbitrate. And though the mourning brow of progeny Forbid the smiling courtesy of [[Love|love]] The holy suit which fain it would convince, Yet since [[Love|love]]’s argument was first on foot, Let not the cloud of sorrow jostle it From what it purposed, since to wail friends lost Is not by much so wholesome-profitable As to rejoice at friends but newly found. |
