| BOYET | Proud of employment, willingly I go. |
| PRINCESS | All [[Pride|pride]] is willing [[Pride|pride]], and yours is so. Exit Boyet Who are the votaries, my loving lords, That are vow-fellows with this virtuous duke? |
| *(didascalia)* | A |
| LORD | Lord [[Longueville]] is one. |
| PRINCESS | Know you the man? |
| [[Maria|MARIA]] | I know him, madam. At a [[Marriage|marriage]] feast Between Lord Perigord and the beauteous heir Of [[Jaques]] Fauconbridge solemnized In Normandy saw I this [[Longueville]]. A man of sovereign parts he is esteemed, [[Well]] fitted in arts, glorious in arms. Nothing becomes him ill that he would [[Well|well]]. The only soil of his fair virtue’s gloss- If virtue’s gloss will stain with any soil- Is a sharp wit matched with too blunt a will, Whose edge hath power to cut, whose will still wills It should none spare that come within his power. |
| PRINCESS | Some merry mocking lord, belike-is’t so? |
| [[Maria|MARIA]] | They say so most that most his humours know. |
| PRINCESS | Such short-lived wits do wither as they grow. Who are the rest? |
| CATHERINE | The young [[Dumaine]], a [[Well|well]]-accomplished youth, Of all that virtue [[Love|love]] for virtue loved. Most power to do most harm, least knowing ill, For he hath wit to make an ill shape good, And shape to win grace, though he had no wit. I saw him at the Duke Alençon’s once, And much too little of that good I saw Is my report to his great worthiness. |
| [[Rosaline|ROSALINE]] | Another of these students at that [[Time|time]] Was there with him, if I have heard a [[Truth|truth]]. Biron they call him, but a merrier man, Within the limit of becoming mirth, I never spent an hour’s talk withal. His eye begets occasion for his wit, For every object that the one doth catch The other turns to a mirth-moving jest, Which his fair tongue, conceit’s expositor, Delivers in such apt and gracious words That aged ears play truant at his tales, And younger hearings are quite ravished, So sweet and voluble is his discourse. |
| PRINCESS | God bless my ladies, are they all in [[Love|love]], That every one her own hath garnished With such bedecking ornaments of praise? |
| *(didascalia)* | A |
| LORD | Here comes Boyet. Enter Boyet |
| PRINCESS | Now, what admittance, lord? |
| BOYET | Navarre had notice of your fair approach, And he and his competitors in oath Were all addressed to meet you, gentle lady, Before I came. Marry, thus much I have learnt: He rather means to lodge you in the field, Like one that comes here to besiege his [[Court|court]], Than seek a dispensation for his oath To let you enter his unpeopled [[house]]. Enter Navarre, [[Longueville]], [[Dumaine]], and Biron Here comes Navarre. |
| [[King|KING]] | Fair Princess, welcome to the [[Court|court]] of Navarre. |
| PRINCESS | ‘Fair’ I give you back again, and welcome I have not yet. The roof of this [[Court|court]] is too high to be yours, and welcome to the wide fields too base to be mine. |
| [[King|KING]] | You shall be welcome, madam, to my [[Court|court]]. |
| PRINCESS | I will be welcome, then. Conduct me thither. |
| [[King|KING]] | Hear me, dear lady. I have sworn an oath— |
| PRINCESS | Our Lady help my lord He’ll be forsworn. |
