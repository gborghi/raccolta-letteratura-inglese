| [[King|KING]] | (reads) ‘So sweet a kiss the golden sun gives not To those fresh morning drops upon the [[Rose|rose]] As thy eyebeams when their fresh rays have smote The [[Night|night]] of dew that on my cheeks down flows. Nor shines the silver [[Moon|moon]] one-half so bright Through the transparent bosom of the deep As doth thy face through [[Tears|tears]] of mine give light. Thou shin’st in every tear that I do weep. No drop but as a coach doth carry thee, So ridest thou triumphing in my woe. Do but behold the [[Tears|tears]] that swell in me And they thy glory through my grief will show. But do not [[Love|love]] thyself; then thou wilt keep My [[Tears|tears]] for glasses, and still make me weep. O [[Queen]] of queens, how far dost thou excel, No thought can think nor tongue of mortal tell.’ How shall she know my griefs? I’ll drop the paper. Sweet [[Leaves|leaves]], shade folly. Who is he comes here? Enter [[Longueville]] with papers. The [[King]] steps aside What, [[Longueville]], and reading—listen, ear! |
