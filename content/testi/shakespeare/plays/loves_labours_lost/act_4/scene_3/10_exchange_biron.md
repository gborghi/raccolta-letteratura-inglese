| BIRON | My eyes are then no eyes, nor I Biron. O, but for my [[Love|love]], day would turn to [[Night|night]]. Of all complexions the culled sovereignty Do meet as at a fair in her fair cheek, Where several worthies make one dignity, Where nothing wants that want itself doth seek. Lend me the flourish of all gentle tongues— Fie, painted rhetoric! O, she needs it not. To things of sale a seller’s praise belongs. She passes praise—then praise too short doth blot. A withered hermit fivescore winters worn Might shake off fifty, looking in her eye. [[Beauty]] doth varnish age as if new-born, And gives the crutch the cradle’s infancy. O, ’tis the sun that maketh all things shine. |
| [[King|KING]] | By heaven, thy [[Love|love]] is black as ebony. |
| BIRON | Is ebony like her? O word divine! A wife of such wood were felicity. O, who can give an oath? Where is a book, That I may swear [[Beauty|beauty]] doth [[Beauty|beauty]] lack If that she learn not of her eye to look? No face is fair that is not full so black. |
| [[King|KING]] | O [[Paradox|paradox]] ! Black is the badge of hell, The hue of dungeons and the style of [[Night|night]], And [[Beauty|beauty]]’s crest becomes the heavens [[Well|well]]. |
| BIRON | Devils soonest tempt, resembling spirits of light. O, if in black my lady’s brows be decked, It mourns that painting and usurping hair Should ravish doters with a false aspect, And therefore is she born to make black fair. Her favour turns the fashion of the days, For native [[Blood|blood]] is counted painting now, And therefore red that would avoid dispraise Paints itself black to imitate her brow. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | To look like her are chimney-sweepers black. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | And since her [[Time|time]] are colliers counted bright. |
| [[King|KING]] | And Ethiops of their sweet complexion crack. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Dark needs no candles now, for dark is light. |
| BIRON | Your mistresses dare never come in rain, For [[Fear|fear]] their colours should be washed away. |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘Twere good yours did; for, sir, to tell you plain, I’ll find a fairer face not washed today. |
| BIRON | I’ll prove her fair, or talk till doomsday here. |
| [[King|KING]] | No devil will fright thee then so much as she. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | I never knew man hold vile stuff so dear. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | (showing his foot) Look, here’s thy [[Love|love]]—my foot and her face see. |
| BIRON | O, if the streets were paved with thine eyes Her feet were much too dainty for such tread. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | O vile! Then as she goes, what upward lies The street should see as she walked overhead. |
| [[King|KING]] | But what of this? Are we not all in [[Love|love]]? |
| BIRON | Nothing so sure, and thereby all forsworn. |
| [[King|KING]] | Then leave this chat and, good Biron, now prove Our loving lawful and our [[Faith|faith]] not torn. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Ay, marry there, some flattery for this evil. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | O, some authority how to proceed, Some tricks, some quillets how to cheat the devil. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Some salve for perjury. |
