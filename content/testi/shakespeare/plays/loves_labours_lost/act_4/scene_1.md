| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Princess, a Forester, her ladies-[[Rosaline]], [[Maria]], and Catherine—and her lords, among them Boyet |
| PRINCESS | Was that the [[King]] that spurred his [[Horse|horse]] so hard<br>Against the steep uprising of the [[Hill|hill]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | BOYET |
| PRINCESS | I know not, but I think it was not he. |
| PRINCESS | Whoe’er a was, a showed a mounting mind.<br>[[Well]], lords, today we shall have our dispatch.<br>Ere Saturday we will return to France.<br>Then, forester my friend, where is the bush<br>That we must stand and play the murderer in? |
| FORESTER | Hereby, upon the edge of yonder coppice—<br>A stand where you may make the fairest shoot. |
| PRINCESS | I thank my [[Beauty|beauty]], I am fair that shoot,<br>And thereupon thou speak’st ‘the fairest shoot’. |
| FORESTER | Pardon me, madam, for I meant not so. |
| PRINCESS | What, what? First praise me, and again say no?<br>O short-lived [[Pride|pride]]! Not fair? Alack, for woe ! |
| FORESTER | Yes, madam, fair. |
| PRINCESS | Nay, never paint me now.<br>Where fair is not, praise cannot mend the brow.<br>Here, good my glass, take this for telling true.<br>She gives him money<br>Fair payment for foul words is more than due. |
| FORESTER | Nothing but fair is that which you inherit. |
| PRINCESS | See, see, my [[Beauty|beauty]] will be saved by merit!<br>O heresy in fair, fit for these days—<br>A giving hand, though foul, shall have fair praise.<br>But come, the bow. Now [[Mercy|mercy]] goes to kill,<br>And shooting [[Well|well]] is then accounted ill.<br>Thus will I save my credit in the shoot,<br>Not wounding—pity would not let me do’t.<br>If wounding, then it was to show my skill,<br>That more for praise than purpose meant to kill.<br>And, out of question, so it is sometimes—<br>Glory grows guilty of detested crimes<br>When for fame’s sake, for praise, an outward part,<br>We bend to that the working of the heart,<br>As I for praise [[Alone|alone]] now seek to spill<br>The poor deer’s [[Blood|blood]] that my heart means no ill. |
| BOYET | Do not curst wives hold that [[Self|self]]-sovereignty<br>Only for praise’ sake when they strive to be<br>Lords o’er their lords? |
| PRINCESS | Only for praise, and praise we may afford<br>To any lady that subdues a lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Costard]] the clown |
| BOYET | Here comes a member of the commonwealth. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | God dig-you-de’en, all. Pray you, which is the<br>head lady? |
| PRINCESS | Thou shalt know her, fellow, by the rest that have no heads. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Which is the greatest lady, the highest? |
| PRINCESS | The thickest and the tallest. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | The thickest and the tallest—it is so, [[Truth|truth]] is [[Truth|truth]].<br>An your waist, mistress, were as slender as my wit<br>One o’ these maids’ girdles for your waist should be fit.<br>Are not you the chief woman? You are the thickest here. |
| PRINCESS | What’s your will, sir? What’s your will? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I have a letter from Monsieur Biron to one Lady [[Rosaline]]. |
| PRINCESS | O, thy letter, thy letter! (She takes it) He’s a good friend of mine.<br>(To [[Costard]]) Stand aside, good bearer. Boyet, you can carve.<br>Break up this capon.<br>She gives the letter to Boyet |
| BOYET | I am bound to serve.<br>This letter is mistook. It importeth none here.<br>It is writ to Jaquenetta. |
| PRINCESS | We will read it, I swear.<br>Break the neck of the wax, and everyone give ear. |
| BOYET | (reads) ‘By heaven, that thou art fair is most infallible, true that thou art beauteous, [[Truth|truth]] itself that thou art lovely. More fairer than fair, beautiful than beauteous, truer than [[Truth|truth]] itself, have commiseration on thy heroical vassal. The magnanimous and most illustrate [[King]] Cophetua set’s eye upon the penurious and indubitate beggar Zenelophon, and he it was that might rightly say “Veni, vidi, vicí”, which to annothanize in the vulgar—O base and obscure vulgar!—videlicet “He came, see, and overcame.” He came, one; see, two; overcame, three. Who came? The [[King]]. Why did he come? To see. Why did he see? To overcome. To whom came he? To the beggar. What saw he? The beggar. Who overcame he? The beggar. The conclusion is victory. On whose side? The [[King]]’s. The captive is enriched. On whose side? The beggar’s. The catastrophe is a nuptial. On whose side? The [[King]]’s—no, on both in one, or one in both. I am the [[King]]—for so stands the comparison—thou the beggar, for so witnesseth thy lowliness. Shall I command thy [[Love|love]]? I may. Shall I enforce thy [[Love|love]]? I could. Shall I entreat thy [[Love|love]]? I will. What shalt thou exchange for rags? Robes. For tittles? Titles. For thyself? Me. Thus, expecting thy reply, I profane my lips on thy foot, my eyes on thy picture, and my heart on thy every part.<br>Thine in the dearest design of industry,<br>Don Adriano de Armado.<br>Thus dost thou hear the Nemean [[Lion|lion]] roar<br>‘Gainst thee, thou [[Lamb|lamb]], that standest as his prey.<br>Submissive fall his princely feet before,<br>And he from forage will incline to play.<br>But if thou strive, poor [[Soul|soul]], what art thou then?<br>Food for his rage, repasture for his den.’ |
| PRINCESS | What plume of feathers is he that indited this letter?<br>What vane? What weathercock? Did you ever hear<br>better? |
| BOYET | I am much deceived but I remember the style. |
| PRINCESS | Else your [[Memory|memory]] is bad, going o’er it erewhile. |
| BOYET | This Armado is a Spaniard that keeps here in [[Court|court]],<br>A phantasim, a Monarcho, and one that makes sport<br>To the Prince and his bookmates. |
| PRINCESS | (to [[Costard]]) Thou, fellow, a word.<br>Who gave thee this letter? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I told you—my lord. |
| PRINCESS | To whom shouldst thou give it? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | From my lord to my lady. |
| PRINCESS | From which lord to which lady? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | From my lord Biron, a good master of mine,<br>To a lady of France that he called [[Rosaline]]. |
| PRINCESS | Thou hast mistaken his letter. Come, lords, away.<br>(To [[Rosaline]], giving her the letter)<br>Here, sweet, put up this, ‘twill be thine another day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit attended |
| BOYET | Who is the suitor? Who is the suitor? |
| [[Rosaline|ROSALINE]] | Shall I teach you to know? |
| BOYET | Ay, my continent of [[Beauty|beauty]]. |
| [[Rosaline|ROSALINE]] | Why, she that bears the bow.<br>Finely put off. |
| BOYET | My lady goes to kill horns, but if thou marry,<br>Hang me by the neck if horns that year miscarry.<br>Finely put on. |
| [[Rosaline|ROSALINE]] | [[Well]] then, I am the shooter. |
| BOYET | And who is your deer? |
| [[Rosaline|ROSALINE]] | If we choose by the horns, yourself come not near.<br>Finely put on indeed! |
| [[Maria|MARIA]] | You still wrangle with her, Boyet, and she strikes at the brow. |
| BOYET | But she herself is hit tower—have I hit her now? |
| [[Rosaline|ROSALINE]] | Shall I come upon thee with an old saying that was a man when [[King]] Pépin of France was a little boy, as touching the hit it? |
| BOYET | So I may answer thee with one as old that was a woman when [[Queen]] Guinevere of Britain was a little wench, as touching the hit it. |
| [[Rosaline|ROSALINE]] | (sings)<br>Thou canst not hit it, hit it, hit it,<br>Thou canst not hit it, my good man. |
| BOYET | (sings)<br>An I cannot, cannot, cannot,<br>An I cannot, another can. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Rosaline]] |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | By my troth, most pleasant How both did fit it! |
| [[Maria|MARIA]] | A mark marvellous [[Well|well]] shot, for they both did hit it. |
| BOYET | A mark—O mark but that mark! A mark, says my<br>lady.<br>Let the mark have a prick in’t to mete at, if it may be. |
| [[Maria|MARIA]] | Wide o’ the bow hand—i’[[Faith|faith]], your hand is out. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Indeed, a must shoot nearer, or he’ll ne’er hit the clout. |
| BOYET | An if my hand be out, then belike your hand is in. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Then will she get the upshoot by cleaving the pin. |
| [[Maria|MARIA]] | Come, come, you talk greasily, your lips grow foul. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | She’s too hard for you at pricks, sir. Challenge her to bowl. |
| BOYET | I [[Fear|fear]] too much rubbing. Goodnight, my good owl. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Boyet, [[Maria]], and Catherine |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | By my [[Soul|soul]], a swain, a most simple clown.<br>Lord, Lord, how the ladies and I have put him down!<br>O’ my troth, most sweet jests, most incony vulgar wit,<br>When it comes so smoothly off, so obscenely, as it were, so fit!<br>Armado o‘th’ t’other side—O, a most dainty man!—<br>To see him walk before a lady and to bear her fan!<br>To see him kiss his hand, and how most sweetly a will swear,<br>And his page o’ t’other side, that handful of wit—<br>Ah heavens, it is a most pathetical nit! |
| *(didascalia)* | Shout within |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Sola, sola! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
