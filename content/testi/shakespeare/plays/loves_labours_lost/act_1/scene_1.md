| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Ferdinand]], [[King]] of Navarre, Biron, [[Longueville]], and [[Dumaine]] |
| [[King|KING]] | Let fame, that all hunt after in their lives,<br>Live registered upon our brazen tombs,<br>And then grace us in the disgrace of [[Death|death]]<br>When, spite of cormorant devouring [[Time|time]],<br>Th’endeavour of this present breath may buy<br>That [[Honour|honour]] which shall bate his scythe’s keen edge<br>And make us heirs of all [[Eternity|eternity]].<br>Therefore, brave conquerors—for so you are,<br>That war against your own affections<br>And the huge army of the world’s desires—<br>Our late edict shall strongly stand in force.<br>Navarre shall be the [[Wonder|wonder]] of the world.<br>Our [[Court|court]] shall be a little academe,<br>Still and contemplative in living art.<br>You three—Biron, [[Dumaine]], and [[Longueville]]—<br>Have sworn for three years’ term to live with me<br>My fellow scholars, and to keep those statutes<br>That are recorded in this schedule here.<br>Your oaths are passed; and now subscribe your names,<br>That his own hand may strike his [[Honour|honour]] down<br>That violates the smallest branch herein.<br>If you are armed to do as sworn to do,<br>Subscribe to your deep oaths, and keep it, too. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | I am resolved. ‘Tis but a three years’ fast.<br>The mind shall banquet, though the [[Body|body]] pine.<br>Fat paunches have lean pates, and dainty bits<br>Make rich the ribs but bankrupt quite the wits.<br>He signs |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | My loving lord, [[Dumaine]] is mortified.<br>The grosser manner of these world’s delights<br>He throws upon the gross world’s baser slaves.<br>To [[Love|love]], to wealth, to pomp I pine and die,<br>With all these living in [[philosophy]].<br>He signs |
| BIRON | I can but say their protestation over.<br>So much, dear liege, I have already sworn:<br>That is, to live and study here three years.<br>But there are other strict observances,<br>As not to see a woman in that term,<br>Which I [[Hope|hope]] [[Well|well]] is not enrolled there;<br>And one day in a week to touch no food,<br>And but one meal on every day beside,<br>The which I [[Hope|hope]] is not enrolled there;<br>And then to [[Sleep|sleep]] but three hours in the [[Night|night]],<br>And not be seen to wink of all the day,<br>When I was wont to think no harm all [[Night|night]],<br>And make a dark [[Night|night]] too of half the day,<br>Which I [[Hope|hope]] [[Well|well]] is not enrolled there.<br>O, these are barren tasks, too hard to keep—<br>Not to see ladies, study, fast, not [[Sleep|sleep]]. |
| [[King|KING]] | Your oath is passed to pass away from these. |
| BIRON | Let me say no, my liege, an if you please.<br>I only swore to study with your grace,<br>And stay here in your [[Court|court]], for three years’ space. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | You swore to that, Biron, and to the rest. |
| BIRON | By yea and nay, sir, then I swore in jest.<br>What is the end of study, let me know? |
| [[King|KING]] | Why, that to know which else we should not know. |
| BIRON | Things hid and barred, you mean, from common sense. |
| [[King|KING]] | Ay, that is study’s god-like recompense. |
| BIRON | Come on, then, I will swear to study so<br>To know the thing I am forbid to know,<br>As thus: to study where I [[Well|well]] may dine<br>When I to feast expressly am forbid,<br>Or study where to meet some mistress fine<br>When mistresses from common sense are hid;<br>Or having sworn too hard a keeping oath,<br>Study to break it and not break my troth.<br>If study’s gain be thus, and this be so,<br>Study knows that which yet it doth not know.<br>Swear me to this, and I will ne’er say no. |
| [[King|KING]] | These be the stops that hinder study quite,<br>And train our intellects to vain delight. |
| BIRON | Why, all delights are vain, but that most vain<br>Which, with [[Pain|pain]] purchased, doth inherit [[Pain|pain]];<br>As painfully to pore upon a book<br>To seek the light of [[Truth|truth]] while [[Truth|truth]] the while<br>Doth falsely blind the eyesight of his look.<br>Light, seeking light, doth light of light beguile;<br>So ere you find where light in darkness lies<br>Your light grows dark by losing of your eyes.<br>Study me how to please the eye indeed<br>By fixing it upon a fairer eye,<br>Who dazzling so, that eye shall be his heed,<br>And give him light that it was blinded by.<br>Study is like the heavens’ glorious sun,<br>That will not be deep searched with saucy looks.<br>Small have continual plodders ever won<br>Save base authority from others’ books.<br>These earthly godfathers of heaven’s lights,<br>That give a name to every fixed star,<br>Have no more profit of their shining nights<br>Than those that walk and wot not what they are.<br>Too much to know is to know naught but fame,<br>And every godfather can give a name. |
| [[King|KING]] | How [[Well|well]] he’s read, to reason against reading! |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Proceeded [[Well|well]], to stop all good proceeding. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | He weeds the corn and still lets grow the weeding. |
| BIRON | The [[Spring|spring]] is near when green geese are a-breeding. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | How follows that? |
| BIRON | Fit in his place and [[Time|time]]. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | In reason nothing. |
| BIRON | Something then in rhyme. |
| [[King|KING]] | Biron is like an envious sneaping [[Frost|frost]],<br>That bites the first-born infants of the [[Spring|spring]]. |
| BIRON | [[Well]], say I am! Why should proud [[Summer|summer]] boast<br>Before the [[Birds|birds]] have any cause to sing?<br>Why should I joy in any abortive birth?<br>At Christmas I no more [[Desire|desire]] a [[Rose|rose]]<br>Than wish a [[Snow|snow]] in May’s new-fangled shows,<br>But like of each thing that in season grows.<br>So you to study, now it is too late,<br>Climb o’er the [[house]] to unlock the little gate. |
| [[King|KING]] | [[Well]], sit you out. Go [[Home|home]], Biron. Adieu. |
| BIRON | No, my good lord, I have sworn to stay with you.<br>And though I have for barbarism spoke more<br>Than for that angel knowledge you can say,<br>Yet confident I’ll keep what I have sworn,<br>And bide the penance of each three years’ day.<br>Give me the paper. Let me read the same,<br>And to the strict’st decrees I’ll write my name. |
| [[King|KING]] | (giving a paper)<br>How [[Well|well]] this yielding rescues thee from shame! |
| BIRON | (reads) ‘Item: that no woman shall come within a mile of my [[Court|court]].’ Hath this been proclaimed? |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | Four days ago. |
| BIRON | Let’s see the penalty. ‘On [[Pain|pain]] of losing her tongue.’ Who devised this penalty? |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | Marry, that did I. |
| BIRON | Sweet lord, and why? |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | To fright them hence with that dread penalty. |
| BIRON | A dangerous law against gentility.<br>‘Item: if any man be seen to talk with a woman within<br>the term of three years, he shall endure such public<br>shame as the rest of the [[Court|court]] can possible devise.’<br>This article, my liege, yourself must break;<br>For [[Well|well]] you know here comes in embassy<br>The French [[King]]’s daughter with yourself to speak—<br>A maid of grace and complete majesty—<br>About surrender-up of Aquitaine<br>To her decrepit, sick, and bedrid father.<br>Therefore this article is made in vain,<br>Or vainly comes th’admirèd Princess hither. |
| [[King|KING]] | What say you, lords? Why, this was quite forgot. |
| BIRON | So study evermore is overshot.<br>While it doth study to have what it would,<br>It doth forget to do the thing it should;<br>And when it hath the thing it hunteth most,<br>’Tis won as towns with [[Fire|fire]]—so won, so lost. |
| [[King|KING]] | We must of force dispense with this decree.<br>She must lie here, on mere necessity. |
| BIRON | Necessity will make us all forsworn<br>Three thousand times within this three years’ space;<br>For every man with his affects is born,<br>Not by might mastered, but by special grace.<br>If I break [[Faith|faith]], this word shall speak for me:<br>I am forsworn on mere necessity.<br>So to the laws at large I write my name,<br>And he that breaks them in the least degree<br>Stands in attainder of eternal shame.<br>He signs<br>Suggestions are to other as to me,<br>But I believe, although I seem so loath,<br>I am the last that will last keep his oath.<br>But is there no quick recreation granted? |
| [[King|KING]] | Ay, that there is. Our [[Court|court]], you know, is haunted<br>With a refined traveller of Spain,<br>A man in all the world’s new fashion planted,<br>That hath a mint of phrases in his brain.<br>One who the music of his own vain tongue<br>Doth ravish like enchanting harmony;<br>A man of complements, whom right and wrong<br>Have chose as umpire of their mutiny.<br>This [[Child|child]] of fancy, that Armado hight,<br>For interim to our studies shall relate<br>In high-borne words the worth of many a knight<br>From tawny Spain lost in the world’s [[debate]].<br>How you delight, my lords, I know not, I;<br>But I protest I [[Love|love]] to hear him lie,<br>And I will use him for my minstrelsy. |
| BIRON | Armado is a most illustrious wight,<br>A man of [[Fire|fire]]-new words, fashion’s own knight. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | [[Costard]] the swain and he shall be our sport,<br>And so to study three years is but short. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a constable, Anthony Dull, with [[Costard]] with a letter |
| DULL | Which is the Duke’s own person? |
| BIRON | This, fellow. What wouldst? |
| DULL | I myself reprehend his own person, for I am his grace’s farborough. But I would see his own person in flesh and [[Blood|blood]]. |
| BIRON | This is he. |
| DULL | Senor Arm—Arm—commends you. There’s villainy abroad. This letter will tell you more. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Sir, the contempts thereof are as touching me. [[King|KING]] A letter from the magnificent Armado. |
| BIRON | How low soever the matter, I [[Hope|hope]] in God for high words. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | A high [[Hope|hope]] for a low heaven. God grant us patience. |
| BIRON | To hear, or forbear laughing? |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | To hear meekly, sir, and to laugh moderately, or to forbear both. |
| BIRON | [[Well]], sir, be it as the style shall give us cause to climb in the merriness. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | The matter is to me, sir, as concerning Jaquenetta. The manner of it is, I was taken with the manner. |
| BIRON | In what manner? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | In manner and form following, sir—all those three. I was seen with her in the manor [[house]], sitting with her upon the form, and taken following her into the park; which put together is ‘in manner and form following’. Now, sir, for the manner: it is the manner of a man to speak to a woman. For the form: in some form. |
| BIRON | For the ‘following’, sir? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | As it shall follow in my correction; and God defend the right. |
| [[King|KING]] | Will you hear this letter with attention? |
| BIRON | As we would hear an oracle. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Such is the simplicity of man to hearken after the flesh. |
| [[King|KING]] | (reads) ‘Great deputy, the welkin’s vicegerent and sole dominator of Navarre, my [[Soul|soul]]’s earth’s god, and [[Body|body]]’s fostering patron’— |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Not a word of [[Costard]] yet. |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘So it is’— |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | It may be so; but if he say it is so, he is, in telling true, but so. |
| [[King|KING]] | Peace! |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Be to me and every man that dares not fight. |
| [[King|KING]] | No words! |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Of other men’s [[Secrets|secrets]], I beseech you. |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘So it is, besieged with sable-coloured [[Melancholy|melancholy]], I did commend the black-oppressing [[Humour|humour]] to the most wholesome physic of thy health-giving air, and, as I am a gentleman, betook myself to walk. The [[Time|time]] when? About the sixth hour, when beasts most graze, [[Birds|birds]] best peck, and men sit down to that nourishment which is called supper. So much for the [[Time|time]] when. Now for the ground which—which, I mean, I walked upon. It is yclept thy park. Then for the place where—where, I mean, I did encounter that obscene and most preposterous event that draweth from my [[Snow|snow]]-white pen the ebon-coloured ink which here thou viewest, beholdest, surveyest, or seest. But to the place where. It standeth north-north-east and by east from the west corner of thy curious-knotted [[Garden|garden]]. There did I see that low-spirited swain, that base minnow of thy mirth’— |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Me? |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘That unlettered, small-knowing sout’— |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Me? |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘That shallow vassal’— |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Still me? |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘Which, as I remember, hight [[Costard]]’— |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | O, me! |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘Sorted and consorted, contrary to thy established proclaimed edict and continent canon, with, with, O with—but with this I passion to say wherewith’—[[Costard|COSTARD]] With a wench. |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘With a [[Child|child]] of our grandmother Eve, a female, or for thy more sweet understanding a woman. Him I, as my ever-esteemed duty pricks me on, have sent to thee, to receive the meed of punishment, by thy sweet grace’s officer Anthony Dull, a man of good repute, carriage, bearing, and estimation.’ |
| DULL | Me, an’t shall please you. I am Anthony Dull. |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘For Jaquenetta—so is the weaker vessel called—which I apprehended with the aforesaid swain, I keep her as a vessel of thy law’s fury, and shall at the least of thy sweet notice bring her to trial. Thine in all compliments of devoted and heartburning heat of duty,<br>Don Adriano de Armado.’ |
| BIRON | This is not so [[Well|well]] as I looked for, but the best that ever I heard. |
| [[King|KING]] | Ay, the best for the worst. (To [[Costard]]) But, sirrah, what say you to this? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Sir, I confess the wench. |
| [[King|KING]] | Did you hear the proclamation? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I do confess much of the hearing it, but little of the marking of it. |
| [[King|KING]] | It was proclaimed a year’s [[Imprisonment|imprisonment]] to be taken with a wench. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I was taken with none, sir. I was taken with a [[Damsel|damsel]]. |
| [[King|KING]] | [[Well]], it was proclaimed ‘[[Damsel|damsel]]’. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | This was no [[Damsel|damsel]], neither, sir. She was a virgin. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King|KING]] It is so varied, too, for it was proclaimed ‘virgin’. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | If it were, I deny her virginity. I was taken with a maid. |
| [[King|KING]] | This ‘maid’ will not serve your turn, sir. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | This maid will serve my turn, sir. |
| [[King|KING]] | Sir, I will pronounce your sentence. You shall fast a week with bran and [[Water|water]]. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I had rather pray a month with mutton and porridge. |
| [[King|KING]] | And [[Don Armado]] shall be your keeper.<br>My lord Biron, see him delivered o’er,<br>And go we, lords, to put in practice that<br>Which each to other hath so strongly sworn. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt the [[King]], [[Longueville]], and [[Dumaine]] |
| BIRON | I’ll lay my head to any good man’s hat<br>These oaths and laws will prove an idle scorn.<br>Sirrah, come on. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | I suffer for the [[Truth|truth]], sir; for true it is I was taken with Jaquenetta, and Jaquenetta is a true girl, and therefore, welcome the sour cup of prosperity, affliction may one day smile again; and till then, sit thee down, sorrow. Exeunt |
