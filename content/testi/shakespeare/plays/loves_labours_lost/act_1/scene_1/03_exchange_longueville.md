| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | I am resolved. ‘Tis but a three years’ fast. The mind shall banquet, though the [[Body|body]] pine. Fat paunches have lean pates, and dainty bits Make rich the ribs but bankrupt quite the wits. He signs |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | My loving lord, [[Dumaine]] is mortified. The grosser manner of these world’s delights He throws upon the gross world’s baser slaves. To [[Love|love]], to wealth, to pomp I pine and die, With all these living in [[philosophy]]. He signs |
| BIRON | I can but say their protestation over. So much, dear liege, I have already sworn: That is, to live and study here three years. But there are other strict observances, As not to see a woman in that term, Which I [[Hope|hope]] [[Well|well]] is not enrolled there; And one day in a week to touch no food, And but one meal on every day beside, The which I [[Hope|hope]] is not enrolled there; And then to [[Sleep|sleep]] but three hours in the [[Night|night]], And not be seen to wink of all the day, When I was wont to think no harm all [[Night|night]], And make a dark [[Night|night]] too of half the day, Which I [[Hope|hope]] [[Well|well]] is not enrolled there. O, these are barren tasks, too hard to keep— Not to see ladies, study, fast, not [[Sleep|sleep]]. |
| [[King|KING]] | Your oath is passed to pass away from these. |
| BIRON | Let me say no, my liege, an if you please. I only swore to study with your grace, And stay here in your [[Court|court]], for three years’ space. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | You swore to that, Biron, and to the rest. |
| BIRON | By yea and nay, sir, then I swore in jest. What is the end of study, let me know? |
| [[King|KING]] | Why, that to know which else we should not know. |
| BIRON | Things hid and barred, you mean, from common sense. |
| [[King|KING]] | Ay, that is study’s god-like recompense. |
| BIRON | Come on, then, I will swear to study so To know the thing I am forbid to know, As thus: to study where I [[Well|well]] may dine When I to feast expressly am forbid, Or study where to meet some mistress fine When mistresses from common sense are hid; Or having sworn too hard a keeping oath, Study to break it and not break my troth. If study’s gain be thus, and this be so, Study knows that which yet it doth not know. Swear me to this, and I will ne’er say no. |
| [[King|KING]] | These be the stops that hinder study quite, And train our intellects to vain delight. |
