|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Here alarum. The French are beaten back by the English with great loss. Enter [[Charles the Dauphin]], the Duke of Alençon, and René Duke of Anjou |
| CHARLES | Who ever saw the like? What men have I?<br>[[dogs|Dogs]], cowards, dastards! I would ne‘er have fled,<br>But that they left me ’midst my enemies.<br>RENÉ<br>Salisbury is a desperate homicide.<br>He fighteth as one weary of his life.<br>The other lords, like lions wanting food,<br>Do rush upon us as their hungry prey.<br>ALENÇON<br>Froissart, a countryman of ours, records<br>England all Olivers and Rolands bred<br>During the [[Time|time]] Edward the Third did reign.<br>More truly now may this be verified,<br>For none but Samsons and Goliases<br>It sendeth forth to skirmish. One to ten?<br>Lean raw-boned rascals, who would e’er suppose<br>They had such [[Courage|courage]] and audacity? |
| CHARLES | Let’s leave this town, for they are hare-brained slaves,<br>And hunger will enforce them to be more eager.<br>Of old I know them: rather with their teeth<br>The walls they’ll tear down, than forsake the siege.<br>RENÉ<br>I think by some odd gimmers or device<br>Their arms are set, like clocks, still to strike on,<br>Else ne’er could they hold out so as they do.<br>By my consent we’ll even let them [[Alone|alone]].<br>ALENÇON Be it SO. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Bastard of Orléans |
| BASTARD | Where’s the Prince [[Dauphin]]? I have news for him. |
| CHARLES | Bastard of Orléans, thrice welcome to us. |
| BASTARD | Methinks your looks are sad, your cheer appalled.<br>Hath the late overthrow wrought this offence?<br>Be not dismayed, for succour is at hand.<br>A holy maid hither with me I bring,<br>Which, by a vision sent to her from heaven,<br>Ordained is to raise this tedious siege<br>And drive the English forth the bounds of France.<br>The spirit of deep prophecy she hath,<br>Exceeding the nine sibyls of old Rome.<br>What’s past and what’s to come she can descry.<br>Speak: shall I call her in? Believe my words,<br>For they are certain and unfallible. |
| CHARLES | Go call her in. Exit Bastard<br>But first, to try her skill,<br>René stand thou as [[Dauphin]] in my place.<br>Question her proudly; let thy looks be stern.<br>By this means shall we sound what skill she hath. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Bastard of Orléans with [[Joan la Pucelle]], armed |
| CHARLES | RENÉ (as Charles)<br>Fair maid, is’t thou wilt do these wondrous feats? |
| JOAN | René, is’t thou that thinkest to beguile me?<br>Where is the [[Dauphin]]? (To Charles) Come, come from behind.<br>I know thee [[Well|well]], though never seen before.<br>Be not amazed. There’s nothing hid from me.<br>In private will I talk with thee apart.<br>Stand back you lords, and give us leave awhile. |
| *(didascalia)* | René, Alençon and Bastard stand apart |
| *(didascalia)* | RENÉ to Alençon and Bastard |
| JOAN | She takes upon her bravely, at first dash. |
| JOAN | [[Dauphin]], I am by birth a shepherd’s daughter,<br>My wit untrained in any kind of art.<br>Heaven and our Lady gracious hath it pleased<br>To shine on my contemptible estate.<br>Lo, whilst I waited on my tender lambs,<br>And to sun’s parching heat displayed my cheeks,<br>God’s mother deigned to appear to me,<br>And in a vision, full of majesty,<br>Willed me to leave my base vocation<br>And free my country from calamity.<br>Her aid she promised, and assured [[success]].<br>In complete glory she revealed herself—<br>And whereas I was black and swart before,<br>With those clear rays which she infused on me<br>That [[Beauty|beauty]] am I blest with, which you may see.<br>Ask me what question thou canst possible,<br>And I will answer unpremeditated.<br>My [[Courage|courage]] try by combat, if thou dar’st,<br>And thou shalt find that I exceed my sex.<br>Resolve on this: thou shalt be fortunate,<br>If thou receive me for thy warlike mate. |
| CHARLES | Thou hast astonished me with thy high terms.<br>Only this proof I’ll of thy valour make:<br>In single combat thou shalt buckle with me.<br>An if thou vanquishest, thy words are true;<br>Otherwise, I renounce all confidence. |
| JOAN | I am prepared. Here is my keen-edged [[Sword|sword]],<br>Decked with five flower-de-luces on each side—<br>The which at Touraine, in Saint Katherine’s churchyard,<br>Out of a great deal of old iron I chose forth. |
| CHARLES | Then come a God’s name. I [[Fear|fear]] no woman. |
| JOAN | And while I live, I’ll ne’er fly from a man.<br>Here they fight and [[Joan la Pucelle]] overcomes |
| CHARLES | Stay, stay thy hands! Thou art an Amazon,<br>And fightest with the [[Sword|sword]] of Deborah. |
| JOAN | [[Christ]]’s mother helps me, else I were too weak. |
| CHARLES | Whoe‘er helps thee, ’tis thou that must help me.<br>Impatiently I burn with thy [[Desire|desire]].<br>My heart and hands thou hast at once subdued.<br>Excellent Pucelle if thy name be so,<br>Let me thy servant, and not sovereign be.<br>’Tis the French [[Dauphin]] sueth to thee thus. |
| JOAN | I must not yield to any rites of [[Love|love]],<br>For my profession’s sacred from above.<br>When I have chased all thy foes from hence,<br>Then will I think upon a recompense. |
| CHARLES | Meantime, look gracious on thy prostrate thrall. |
| *(didascalia)* | RENÉ to the other lords apart |
| CHARLES | My lord, methinks, is very long in talk.<br>ALENÇON<br>Doubtless he shrives this woman to her smock,<br>Else ne’er could he so long protract his speech.<br>RENÉ<br>Shall we disturb him, since he keeps no mean?<br>ALENÇON<br>He may mean more than we poor men do know.<br>These women are shrewd tempters with their tongues.<br>RENÉ (to Charles)<br>My lord, where are you? What devise you on?<br>Shall we give o’er Orléans, or no? |
| JOAN | Why, no, I say. Distrustful recreants,<br>Fight till the last gasp; I’ll be your guard. |
| CHARLES | What she says, I’ll confirm. We’ll fight it out. |
| JOAN | Assigned am I to be the English scourge.<br>This [[Night|night]] the siege assuredly I’ll raise.<br>Expect Saint Martin’s [[Summer|summer]], halcyon’s days,<br>Since I have entered into these wars.<br>Glory is like a circle in the [[Water|water]],<br>Which never ceaseth to enlarge itself<br>Till, by broad spreading, it disperse to naught.<br>With Henry’s [[Death|death]], the English circle ends.<br>Dispersed are the glories it included.<br>Now am I like that proud insulting [[Ship|ship]]<br>Which Caesar and his fortune bore at once. |
| CHARLES | Was Mohammed inspired with a dove?<br>Thou with an eagle art inspired then.<br>[[Helen]], the mother of great Constantine,<br>Nor yet Saint Philip’s daughters were like thee.<br>Bright star of [[Venus]], fall’n down on the earth,<br>How may I reverently worship thee enough?<br>ALENÇON<br>Leave off delays, and let us raise the siege.<br>RENÉ<br>Woman, do what thou canst to save our honours.<br>Drive them from Orléans, and be immortalized. |
| CHARLES | Presently we’ll try. Come, let’s away about it.<br>No [[Prophet|prophet]] will I trust, if she prove false. Exeunt |
