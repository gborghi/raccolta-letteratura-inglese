| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter [[Young King|young King]] Henry, the Dukes of Exeter and [[Gloucester]], the Bishop of Winchester; the Duke of Somerset and the [[Earl of Suffolk]] with red roses; the [[Earl of Warwick]] and [[Richard]] Plantagenet with white roses. [[Gloucester]] offers to put up a bill; Winchester snatches it, [[Tears|tears]] it |
| WINCHESTER | Com‘st thou with deep premeditated lines?<br>With written pamphlets studiously devised?<br>Humphrey of [[Gloucester]], if thou canst accuse,<br>Or aught intend’st to lay unto my charge,<br>Do it without invention, suddenly,<br>As I with sudden and extemporal speech<br>Purpose to answer what thou canst object. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Presumptuous priest, this place commands my<br>patience,<br>Or thou shouldst find thou hast dishonoured me.<br>Think not, although in writing I preferred<br>The manner of thy vile outrageous crimes,<br>That therefore I have forged, or am not able<br>Verbatim to rehearse the method of my pen.<br>No, prelate, such is thy audacious wickedness,<br>Thy lewd, pestiferous, and dissentious pranks,<br>As very infants prattle of thy [[Pride|pride]].<br>Thou art a most pernicious usurer,<br>Froward by [[Nature|nature]], enemy to peace,<br>Lascivious, wanton, more than [[Well|well]] beseems<br>A man of thy profession and degree.<br>And for thy treachery, what’s more manifest?—<br>In that thou laid’st a trap to take my life,<br>As [[Well|well]] at [[London]] Bridge as at the Tower.<br>Beside, I [[Fear|fear]] me, if thy thoughts were sifted,<br>The [[King]] thy sovereign is not quite exempt<br>From envious malice of thy swelling heart. |
| WINCHESTER | [[Gloucester]], I do defy thee.—Lords, vouchsafe<br>To give me hearing what I shall reply.<br>If I were covetous, ambitious, or perverse,<br>As he will have me, how am I so poor?<br>Or how haps it I seek not to advance<br>Or raise myself, but keep my wonted calling?<br>And for dissension, who preferreth peace<br>More than I do ?—except I be provoked.<br>No, my good lords, it is not that offends;<br>It is not that that hath incensed the Duke.<br>It is because no one should sway but he,<br>No one but he should be about the [[King]]—<br>And that engenders thunder in his breast<br>And makes him roar these accusations forth.<br>But he shall know I am as good— |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | As good?—<br>Thou bastard of my grandfather. |
| WINCHESTER | Ay, lordly sir; for what are you, I pray,<br>But one imperious in another’s throne? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Am I not Protector, saucy priest? |
| WINCHESTER | And am not I a prelate of the Church? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Yes—as an outlaw in a [[Castle|castle]] keeps<br>And useth it to patronage his theft. |
| WINCHESTER | Unreverent [[Gloucester]]. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Thou art reverend<br>Touching thy spiritual function, not thy life. |
| WINCHESTER | Rome shall remedy this. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] Roam thither then. |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK (to Winchester) |
| WINCHESTER | My lord, it were your duty to forbear. |
| SOMERSET | Ay, so the bishop be not overborne:<br>Methinks my lord should be religious,<br>And know the office that belongs to such. |
| WARWICK | Methinks his lordship should be humbler.<br>It fitteth not a prelate so to plead. |
| SOMERSET | Yes, when his holy state is touched so near. |
| WARWICK | State holy or unhallowed, what of that?<br>Is not his grace Protector to the [[King]]? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | (aside)<br>Plantagenet, I see, must hold his tongue,<br>Lest it be said, ‘Speak, sirrah, when you should;<br>Must your bold verdict intertalk with lords?’<br>Else would I have a fling at Winchester. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Uncles of [[Gloucester]] and of Winchester,<br>The special watchmen of our English weal,<br>I would prevail, if prayers might prevail,<br>To join your hearts in [[Love|love]] and amity.<br>O what a scandal is it to our [[Crown|crown]]<br>That two such noble peers as ye should jar!<br>Believe me, lords, my tender years can tell<br>Civil dissension is a viperous worm<br>That gnaws the bowels of the commonwealth. |
| *(didascalia)* | A noise within |
| *(didascalia)* | SERVINGMEN (within) Down with the tawny coats! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | What tumult’s this? |
| WARWICK | An uproar, I dare warrant,<br>Begun through malice of the Bishop’s men. |
| *(didascalia)* | A noise again |
| *(didascalia)* | SERVINGMEN (within) Stones, stones! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Mayor of [[London]] |
| MAYOR | O my good lords, and virtuous Henry,<br>Pity the [[City|city]] of [[London]], pity us! so<br>The Bishop and the [[Duke of Gloucester]]’s men,<br>Forbidden late to carry any weapon,<br>Have filled their pockets full of pebble stones<br>And, banding themselves in contrary parts,<br>Do pelt so fast at one another’s pate<br>That many have their giddy brains knocked out.<br>Our windows are broke down in every street,<br>And we for [[Fear|fear]] compelled to shut our shops. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter in skirmish, with bloody pates, Winchester’s Servingmen in tawny coats and [[Gloucester]]’s in blue coats |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | We charge you, on allegiance to ourself,<br>To hold your slaught’ring hands and keep the peace. |
| *(didascalia)* | The skirmish ceases |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Pray, Uncle [[Gloucester]], mitigate this strife. |
| FIRST SERVINGMAN | Nay, if we be forbidden stones, we’ll fall to it with our teeth. |
| SECOND SERVINGMAN | Do what ye dare, we are as resolute.<br>Skirmish again |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | You of my household, leave this peevish broil,<br>And set this unaccustomed fight aside. |
| THIRD SERVINGMAN | My lord, we know your grace to be a man<br>Just and upright and, for your royal birth,<br>Inferior to none but to his majesty;<br>And ere that we will suffer such a prince,<br>So kind a father of the commonweal,<br>To be disgraced by an inkhorn mate,<br>We and our wives and children all will fight<br>And have our bodies slaughtered by thy foes. |
| FIRST SERVINGMAN | Ay, and the very parings of our nails<br>Shall pitch a field when we are dead.<br>They begin to skirmish again |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Stay, stay, I say!<br>An if you [[Love|love]] me as you say you do,<br>Let me persuade you to forbear a while. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O how this discord doth afflict my [[Soul|soul]]!<br>Can you, my lord of Winchester, behold o<br>My sighs and [[Tears|tears]], and will not once relent?<br>Who should be pitiful if you be not?<br>Or who should study to prefer a peace,<br>If holy churchmen take delight in broils? |
| WARWICK | Yield, my lord Protector; yield, Winchester—<br>Except you mean with obstinate repulse<br>To slay your sovereign and destroy the realm.<br>You see what mischief-and what murder, too—<br>Hath been enacted through your enmity.<br>Then be at peace, except ye thirst for [[Blood|blood]]. |
| WINCHESTER | He shall submit, or I will never yield. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Compassion on the [[King]] commands me stoop,<br>Or I would see his heart out ere the priest<br>Should ever get that privilege of me. |
| WARWICK | Behold, my lord of Winchester, the Duke<br>Hath banished moody discontented fury,<br>As by his smoothed brows it doth appear.<br>Why look you still so stern and tragical? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Here, Winchester, I offer thee my hand. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to Winchester)<br>Fie, Uncle Beaufort! I have heard you preach<br>That malice was a great and grievous sin;<br>And will not you maintain the thing you teach,<br>But prove a chief offender in the same? |
| WARWICK | Sweet [[King]]! The Bishop hath a kindly gird.<br>For shame, my lord of Winchester, relent.<br>What, shall a [[Child|child]] instruct you what to do? |
| WINCHESTER | [[Well]], [[Duke of Gloucester]], I will yield to thee<br>[[Love]] for thy [[Love|love]], and hand for hand I give. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (aside)<br>Ay, but I [[Fear|fear]] me with a hollow heart.<br>(To the others) See here, my friends and loving<br>countrymen,<br>This token serveth for a [[Flag|flag]] of truce<br>Betwixt ourselves and all our followers.<br>So help me God, as I dissemble not. |
| WINCHESTER | So help me God (aside) as I intend it not. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O loving uncle, kind [[Duke of Gloucester]],<br>How joyful am I made by this contract!<br>(To Servingmen) Away, my masters, trouble us no<br>more,<br>But join in [[Friendship|friendship]] as your lords have done.<br>first SERVINGMAN Content. I’ll to the surgeon’s. |
| SECOND SERVINGMAN | And so will I. |
| THIRD SERVINGMAN | And I will see what physic the tavern affords. Exeunt the Mayor and Servingmen |
| WARWICK | Accept this scroll, most gracious sovereign,<br>Which in the right of [[Richard]] Plantagenet<br>We do exhibit to your majesty. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | [[Well]] urged, my lord of Warwick—for, sweet prince,<br>An if your grace mark every circumstance,<br>You have great reason to do [[Richard]] right,<br>Especially for those occasions<br>At Eltham Place I told your majesty. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | And those occasions, uncle, were of force.—<br>Therefore, my loving lords, our pleasure is<br>That [[Richard]] be restored to his [[Blood|blood]]. |
| WARWICK | Let [[Richard]] be restored to his [[Blood|blood]].<br>So shall his father’s wrongs be recompensed. |
| WINCHESTER | As will the rest, so willeth Winchester. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | If [[Richard]] will be true, not that [[Alone|alone]]<br>But all the whole inheritance I give<br>That doth belong unto the [[house]] of York,<br>From whence you [[Spring|spring]] by lineal descent. 170 |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Thy humble servant vows obedience<br>And humble service till the point of [[Death|death]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Stoop then, and set your knee against my foot.<br>[[Richard]] kneels<br>And in reguerdon of that duty done,<br>I gird thee with the valiant [[Sword|sword]] of York.<br>Rise, [[Richard]], like a true Plantagenet,<br>And rise created princely [[Duke of York]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | (rising)<br>And so thrive [[Richard]], as thy foes may fall;<br>And as my duty springs, so perish they<br>That grudge one thought against your majesty. |
| ALL BUT [[Richard|RICHARD]] AND | SOMERSET<br>Welcome, high prince, the mighty [[Duke of York]]! |
| SOMERSET | (aside)<br>Perish, base prince, ignoble [[Duke of York]]! |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Now will it best avail your majesty<br>To [[Cross|cross]] the seas and to be crowned in France.<br>The presence of a [[King|king]] engenders [[Love|love]]<br>Amongst his subjects and his loyal friends,<br>As it disanimates his enemies. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | When [[Gloucester]] says the word, [[King]] Henry goes,<br>For friendly counsel cuts off many foes. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Your ships already are in readiness. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sennet. Exeunt all but Exeter |
| EXETER | Ay, we may march in England or in France,<br>Not seeing what is likely to ensue.<br>This late dissension grown betwixt the peers<br>Burns under feigned ashes of forged [[Love|love]],<br>And will at last break out into a flame.<br>As festered members rot but by degree<br>Till bones and flesh and sinews fall away,<br>So will this base and envious discord breed.<br>And now I [[Fear|fear]] that fatal prophecy<br>Which, in the [[Time|time]] of Henry named the Fifth,<br>Was in the mouth of every sucking babe:<br>That ‘Henry born at Monmouth should win all,<br>And Henry born at Windsor should lose all’—<br>Which is so plain that Exeter doth wish<br>His days may finish, ere that hapless [[Time|time]]. Exit |
