| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Excursions. Enter old [[Lord Talbot]] led by a Servant |
| TALBOT | Where is my other life? Mine own is gone.<br>O where’s young Talbot, where is valiant John?<br>Triumphant [[Death|death]] smeared with captivity,<br>Young Talbot’s valour makes me smile at thee.<br>When he perceived me shrink and on my knee,<br>His bloody [[Sword|sword]] he brandished over me,<br>And like a hungry [[Lion|lion]] did commence<br>Rough deeds of rage and stern impatience.<br>But when my angry guardant stood [[Alone|alone]],<br>Tend‘ring my ruin and assailed of none,<br>Dizzy-eyed fury and great rage of heart<br>Suddenly made him from my side to start<br>Into the clust’ring battle of the French,<br>And in that sea of [[Blood|blood]] my boy did drench<br>His over-mounting spirit; and there died<br>My Icarus, my blossom, in his [[Pride|pride]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter English soldiers with John Talbot’s [[Body|body]], borne |
| SERVANT | O my odear lord, lo where your son is borne. |
| TALBOT | Thou antic [[Death|death]], which laugh‘st us here to scorn,<br>Anon from thy insulting tyranny,<br>Coupled in bonds of perpetuity,<br>Two Talbots winged through the lither sky<br>In thy despite shall scape [[Mortality|mortality]].<br>(To John) O thou whose wounds become hard-favoured<br>[[Death|death]],<br>Speak to thy father ere thou yield thy breath.<br>Brave [[Death|death]] by speaking, whether he will or no;<br>Imagine him a Frenchman and thy foe.—<br>Poor boy, he smiles, methinks, as who should say<br>‘Had [[Death|death]] been French, then [[Death|death]] had died today’.<br>Come, come, and lay him in his father’s arms.<br>Soldiers lay John in Talbot’s arms<br>My spirit can no longer bear these harms.<br>Soldiers, adieu. I have what I would have,<br>Now my old arms are young John Talbot’s [[Grave|grave]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | He dies. Alarum. Exeunt soldiers leaving the bodies |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Charles the Dauphin]], the dukes of Alencon and Burgundy, the Bastard of Orléans, and [[Joan la Pucelle]] |
| CHARLES | Had York and Somerset brought rescue in,<br>We should have found a bloody day of this. |
| BASTARD | How the young whelp of Talbot’s, raging wood,<br>Did flesh his puny [[Sword|sword]] in Frenchmen’s [[Blood|blood]]! |
| JOAN | Once I encountered him, and thus I said:<br>‘Thou maiden youth, be vanquished by a maid.’<br>But with a proud, majestical high scorn<br>He answered thus: ‘Young Talbot was not born<br>To be the pillage of a giglot wench.’<br>So rushing in the bowels of the French,<br>He left me proudly, as unworthy fight. |
| BURGUNDY | Doubtless he would have made a noble knight.<br>See where he lies inhearsèd in the arms<br>Of the most bloody nurser of his harms. |
| BASTARD | Hew them to pieces, hack their bones asunder,<br>Whose life was England’s glory, Gallia’s [[Wonder|wonder]]. |
| CHARLES | O no, forbear; for that which we have fled<br>During the life, let us not wrong it dead. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir William Lucy with a French herald |
| LUCY | Herald, conduct me to the [[Dauphin]]’s tent<br>To know who hath obtained the glory of the day. |
| CHARLES | On what submissive message art thou sent? |
| LUCY | Submission, [[Dauphin]]?‘Tis a mere French word.<br>We English warriors wot not what it means.<br>I come to know what prisoners thou hast ta’en,<br>And to survey the bodies of the dead. |
| CHARLES | For prisoners ask‘st thou ? Hell our [[Prison|prison]] is.<br>But tell me whom thou seek’st. |
| LUCY | But where’s the great Alcides of the field,<br>Valiant [[Lord Talbot]], Earl of Shrewsbury,<br>Created for his rare [[success]] in arms<br>Great Earl of Wexford, Waterford, and Valence,<br>[[Lord Talbot]] of Goodrich and Urchinfield,<br>Lord Strange of Blackmere, Lord Verdun of Alton,<br>Lord Cromwell of Wingfield, Lord Furnival of Sheffield,<br>The thrice victorious lord of Falconbridge,<br>Knight of the noble order of Saint George,<br>Worthy Saint [[Michael]] and the Golden Fleece,<br>Great Maréchal to Henry the Sixth<br>Of all his wars within the realm of France? |
| JOAN | Here’s a silly, stately style indeed.<br>The Turk, that two-and-fifty kingdoms hath,<br>Writes not so tedious a style as this.<br>Him that thou magnifi’st with all these titles<br>Stinking and flyblown lies here at our feet. |
| LUCY | Is Talbot slain, the Frenchmen’s only scourge,<br>Your kingdom’s terror and black Nemesis?<br>O, were mine eye-balls into bullets turned,<br>That I in rage might shoot them at your faces!<br>O, that I could but call these dead to life!—<br>It were enough to fright the realm of France.<br>Were but his picture left amongst you here<br>It would amaze the proudest of you all.<br>Give me their bodies, that I may bear them hence<br>And give them burial as beseems their worth. |
| JOAN | (to Charles)<br>I think this upstart is old Talbot’s [[Ghost|ghost]],<br>He speaks with such a proud commanding spirit.<br>For God’s sake let him have them. To keep them here<br>They would but stink and putrefy the air. |
| CHARLES | Go, take their bodies hence. |
| LUCY | I’ll bear them hence, but from their ashes shall be<br>reared<br>A phoenix that shall make all France afeard. |
| CHARLES | So we be rid of them, do with them what thou wilt. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Lucy and herald with the bodies |
| CHARLES | And now to [[Paris]] in this conquering vein.<br>All will be ours, now bloody Talbot’s slain. Exeunt |
| *(didascalia)* | 5.1 Sennet. Enter [[King]] Henry, the Dukes of [[Gloucester]] and Exeter, and others |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to [[Gloucester]])<br>Have you perused the letters from the Pope,<br>The Emperor, and the Earl of Armagnac? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I have, my lord, and their intent is this:<br>They humbly sue unto your excellence<br>To have a godly peace concluded of<br>Between the realms of England and of France. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How doth your grace affect their motion? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | [[Well]], my good lord, and as the only means<br>To stop effusion of our Christian [[Blood|blood]]<br>And ’stablish quietness on every side. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Ay, marry, uncle; for I always thought<br>It was both impious and unnatural<br>That such immanity and bloody strife<br>Should reign among professors of one [[Faith|faith]]. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Beside, my lord, the sooner to effect<br>And surer bind this knot of amity,<br>The Earl of Armagnac, near knit to Charles—<br>A man of great authority in France—<br>Proffers his only daughter to your grace<br>In [[Marriage|marriage]], with a large and sumptuous dowry. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | [[Marriage]], uncle? Alas, my years are young,<br>And fitter is my study and my books<br>Than wanton dalliance with a paramour. |
| *(didascalia)* | Yet call th’ambassadors, Exit one or more |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | and as you please,<br>So let them have their answers every one.<br>I shall be [[Well|well]] content with any choice<br>Tends to God’s glory and my country’s weal. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Bishop of Winchester, now in cardinal’s habit, and three ambassadors, one a Papal Legate |
| EXETER | (aside)<br>What, is my lord of Winchester installed<br>And called unto a cardinal’s degree?<br>Then I perceive that will be verified<br>Henry the Fifth did sometime prophesy:<br>‘If once he come to be a cardinal,<br>He’ll make his cap co-equal with the [[Crown|crown]].’ |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | My lords ambassadors, your several suits<br>Have been considered and debated on.<br>Your purpose is both good and reasonable,<br>And therefore are we certainly resolved<br>To draw conditions of a friendly peace,<br>Which by my lord of Winchester we mean<br>Shall be transported presently to France. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] to ambassadors |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | And for the proffer of my lord your master,<br>I have informed his highness so at large<br>As, liking of the lady’s virtuous gifts,<br>Her [[Beauty|beauty]], and the value of her dower,<br>He doth intend she shall be England’s [[Queen|queen]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King|KING]] HENRY to ambassadors |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | In argument and proof of which contract<br>Bear her this [[Jewel|jewel]], pledge of my affection.<br>(To [[Gloucester]]) And so, my lord Protector, see them<br>guarded<br>And safely brought to Dover, wherein shipped,<br>Commit them to the fortune of the sea. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally all but Winchester and Legate |
| WINCHESTER | Stay, my lord legate; you shall first receive<br>The sum of money which I promised<br>Should be delivered to his holiness<br>For clothing me in these [[Grave|grave]] ornaments. |
| *(didascalia)* | I will attend upon your lordship’s leisure. Exit |
| WINCHESTER | Now Winchester will not submit, I trow,<br>Or be inferior to the proudest peer.<br>Humphrey of [[Gloucester]], thou shalt [[Well|well]] perceive<br>That nor in birth or for authority<br>The Bishop will be overborne by thee.<br>I’ll either make thee stoop and bend thy knee, |
| *(didascalia)* | Or sack this country with a mutiny. Exit |
