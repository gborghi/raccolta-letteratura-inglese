| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | A [[Rose|rose]] brier. Enter [[Richard]] Plantagenet, the [[Earl of Warwick]], the Duke of Somerset, William de la Pole (the [[Earl of Suffolk]]), Vernon, and a Lawyer |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Great lords and gentlemen, what means this [[Silence|silence]]?<br>Dare no man answer in a case of [[Truth|truth]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | Within the Temple hall we were too loud. |
| SUFFOLK | The [[Garden|garden]] here is more convenient. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Then say at once if I maintained the [[Truth|truth]];<br>Or else was wrangling Somerset in th’error? |
| SUFFOLK | [[Faith]], I have been a truant in the law,<br>And never yet could frame my will to it,<br>And therefore frame the law unto my will. |
| SOMERSET | Judge you, my lord of Warwick, then between us. |
| WARWICK | Between two hawks, which flies the higher pitch,<br>Between two [[dogs]], which hath the deeper mouth,<br>Between two blades, which bears the better temper,<br>Between two horses, which doth bear him best,<br>Between two girls, which hath the merriest eye,<br>I have perhaps some shallow spirit of judgement;<br>But in these nice sharp quillets of the law,<br>Good [[Faith|faith]], I am no wiser than a daw. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Tut, tut, here is a mannerly forbearance.<br>The [[Truth|truth]] appears so naked on my side<br>That any purblind eye may find it out. |
| SOMERSET | And on my side it is so [[Well|well]] apparelled,<br>So clear, so shining, and so evident,<br>That it will glimmer through a blind man’s eye. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Since you are tongue-tied and so loath to speak,<br>In dumb significants proclaim your thoughts.<br>Let him that is a true-born gentleman<br>And stands upon the [[Honour|honour]] of his birth,<br>If he suppose that I have pleaded [[Truth|truth]],<br>From off this briar pluck a white [[Rose|rose]] with me.<br>He plucks a white [[Rose|rose]] |
| SOMERSET | Let him that is no coward nor no flatterer,<br>But dare maintain the party of the [[Truth|truth]],<br>Pluck a red [[Rose|rose]] from off this thorn with me.<br>He plucks a red [[Rose|rose]] |
| WARWICK | I [[Love|love]] no colours, and without all colour<br>Of base insinuating flattery<br>I pluck this white [[Rose|rose]] with Plantagenet. |
| SUFFOLK | I pluck this red [[Rose|rose]] with young Somerset,<br>And say withal I think he held the right. |
| VERNON | Stay, lords and gentlemen, and pluck no more<br>Till you conclude that he upon whose side<br>The fewest roses from the tree are cropped<br>Shall yield the other in the right opinion. |
| SOMERSET | Good Master Vernon, it is [[Well|well]] objected.<br>If I have fewest, I subscribe in [[Silence|silence]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | And I. |
| VERNON | Then for the [[Truth|truth]] and plainness of the case<br>I pluck this pale and maiden blossom here,<br>Giving my verdict on the white [[Rose|rose]]’ side. |
| SOMERSET | Prick not your finger as you pluck it off,<br>Lest, bleeding, you do paint the white [[Rose|rose]] red,<br>And fall on my side so against your will. |
| VERNON | If I, my lord, for my opinion bleed,<br>Opinion shall be surgeon to my hurt<br>And keep me on the side where still I am. |
| SOMERSET | [[Well]], [[Well|well]], come on! Who else? |
| LAWYER | Unless my study and my books be false,<br>The argument you held was wrong in law;<br>In sign whereof I pluck a white [[Rose|rose]] too. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Now Somerset, where is your argument? |
| SOMERSET | Here in my scabbard, meditating that<br>Shall dye your white [[Rose|rose]] in a bloody red. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Meantime your cheeks do counterfeit our roses,<br>For pale they look with [[Fear|fear]], as witnessing<br>The [[Truth|truth]] on our side. |
| SOMERSET | No, Plantagenet,<br>‘Tis not for [[Fear|fear]], but anger, that thy cheeks<br>Blush for pure shame to counterfeit our roses,<br>And yet thy tongue will not confess thy error. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Hath not thy [[Rose|rose]] a canker, Somerset? |
| SOMERSET | Hath not thy [[Rose|rose]] a thorn, Plantagenet? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Ay, sharp and piercing, to maintain his [[Truth|truth]],<br>Whiles thy consuming canker eats his falsehood. |
| SOMERSET | [[Well]], I’ll find friends to wear my bleeding roses,<br>That shall maintain what I have said is true,<br>Where false Plantagenet dare not be seen. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Now, by this maiden blossom in my hand,<br>I scorn thee and thy fashion, peevish boy. |
| SUFFOLK | Turn not thy scorns this way, Plantagenet. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Proud Pole, I will, and scorn both him and thee. |
| SUFFOLK | I’ll turn my part thereof into thy throat. |
| SOMERSET | Away, away, good William de la Pole.<br>We grace the yeoman by conversing with him. |
| WARWICK | Now, by God’s will, thou wrong’st him, Somerset.<br>His grandfather was Lionel Duke of [[Clarence]],<br>Third son to the third Edward, [[King]] of England.<br>[[Spring]] crestless yeomen from so deep a root? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | He bears him on the place’s privilege,<br>Or durst not for his craven heart say thus. |
| SOMERSET | By him that made me, I’ll maintain my words<br>On any plot of ground in Christendom.<br>Was not thy father, [[Richard]] Earl of Cambridge,<br>For treason executed in our late [[King|king]]’s days?<br>And by his treason stand’st not thou attainted,<br>Corrupted, and exempt from ancient gentry?<br>His trespass yet lives guilty in thy [[Blood|blood]],<br>And till thou be restored thou art a yeoman. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | My father was attached, not attainted;<br>Condemned to die for treason, but no traitor—<br>And that I’ll prove on better men than Somerset,<br>Were growing [[Time|time]] once ripened to my will.<br>For your partaker Pole, and you yourself,<br>I’ll note you in my book of [[Memory|memory]],<br>To scourge you for this apprehension.<br>Look to it [[Well|well]], and say you are [[Well|well]] warned. |
| SOMERSET | Ah, thou shalt find us ready for thee still,<br>And know us by these colours for thy foes,<br>For these my friends, in spite of thee, shall wear. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | And, by my [[Soul|soul]], this pale and angry [[Rose|rose]],<br>As cognizance of my [[Blood|blood]]-drinking hate,<br>Will I forever, and my faction, wear<br>Until it wither with me to my [[Grave|grave]],<br>Or flourish to the height of my degree. |
| SUFFOLK | Go forward, and be choked with thy [[Ambition|ambition]].<br>And so farewell until I meet thee next. Exit |
| SOMERSET | Have with thee, Pole.—Farewell, ambitious [[Richard]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | How I am braved, and must perforce endure it! |
| WARWICK | This blot that they object against your [[house]]<br>Shall be wiped out in the next parliament,<br>Called for the truce of Winchester and [[Gloucester]].<br>An if thou be not then created York,<br>I will not live to be accounted Warwick.<br>Meantime, in signal of my [[Love|love]] to thee.<br>Against proud Somerset and William Pole,<br>Will I upon thy party wear this [[Rose|rose]].<br>And here I prophesy: this brawl today,<br>Grown to this faction in the Temple [[Garden|garden]],<br>Shall send, between the red [[Rose|rose]] and the white,<br>A thousand souls to [[Death|death]] and deadly [[Night|night]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Good Master Vernon, I am bound to you,<br>That you on my behalf would pluck a flower. |
| VERNON | In your behalf still will I wear the same. |
| LAWYER | And so will I. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] PLANTAGENET | Thanks, gentles.<br>Come, let us four to dinner. I dare say<br>This quarrel will drink [[Blood|blood]] another day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt. The [[Rose|rose]] brier is removed |
