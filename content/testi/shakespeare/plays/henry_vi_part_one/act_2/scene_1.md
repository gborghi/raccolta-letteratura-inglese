| *(didascalia)* | Enter on the walls a French Sergeant of a band, with two Sentinels |
| SERGEANT | Sirs, take your places and be vigilant.<br>If any noise or soldier you perceive<br>Near to the walls, by some apparent sign<br>Let us have knowledge at the [[Court|court]] of guard. |
| *(didascalia)* | A SENTINEL |
| SERGEANT | Sergeant, you shall. Exit Sergeant Thus are poor servitors,<br>When others [[Sleep|sleep]] upon their quiet beds,<br>Constrained to watch in darkness, rain, and cold. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lord Talbot]], the Dukes of Bedford and Burgundy, and soldiers with scaling ladders, their drums beating a dead march |
| TALBOT | Lord regent, and redoubted Burgundy—<br>By whose approach the regions of Artois,<br>Wallon, and Picardy are friends to us—<br>This happy [[Night|night]] the Frenchmen are secure,<br>Having all day caroused and banqueted.<br>Embrace we then this opportunity,<br>As fitting best to quittance their deceit,<br>Contrived by art and baleful sorcery. |
| BEDFORD | Coward of France! How much he wrongs his fame,<br>Despairing of his own arms’ fortitude,<br>To join with witches and the help of hell. |
| BURGUNDY | Traitors have never other company.<br>But what’s that ‘Pucelle’ whom they term so pure? |
| TALBOT | A maid, they say. |
| BEDFORD | A maid? And be so martial? |
| BURGUNDY | Pray God she prove not masculine ere long.<br>If underneath the standard of the French<br>She carry armour as she hath begun— |
| TALBOT | [[Well]], let them practise and converse with spirits.<br>God is our fortress, in whose conquering name<br>Let us resolve to scale their flinty bulwarks. |
| BEDFORD | Ascend, brave Talbot. We will follow thee. |
| TALBOT | Not all together. Better far, I guess,<br>That we do make our entrance several ways—<br>That, if it chance the one of us do fail,<br>The other yet may rise against their force. |
| BEDFORD | Agreed. I’ll to yon corner. |
| BURGUNDY | And I to this. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally Bedford and Burgundy with some soldiers |
| TALBOT | And here will Talbot mount, or make his [[Grave|grave]].<br>Now, Salisbury, for thee, and for the right<br>Of English Henry, shall this [[Night|night]] appear<br>How much in duty I am bound to both. |
| *(didascalia)* | Talbot and his soldiers scale the walls |
| *(didascalia)* | SENTINELS |
| TALBOT | Arm! Arm! The enemy doth make assault! |
| ENGLISH SOLDIERS | Saint George! A Talbot! Exeunt above |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. The French soldiers leap o’er the walls in their shirts and exeunt. Enter several ways the Bastard of Orléans, the Duke of Alençon, and René Duke of Anjou, half ready and half unready |
| ENGLISH SOLDIERS | ALENÇON<br>How now, my lords? What, all unready so? |
| BASTARD | Unready? Ay, and glad we scaped so [[Well|well]].<br>RENÉ<br>‘Twas [[Time|time]], I trow, to wake and leave our beds,<br>Hearing alarums at our chamber doors.<br>ALENÇON<br>Of all exploits since first I followed arms<br>Ne’er heard I of a warlike enterprise<br>More venturous or desperate than this. |
| BASTARD | I think this Talbot be a fiend of hell.<br>RENÉ<br>If not of hell, the heavens sure favour him.<br>ALENÇON<br>Here cometh Charles. I marvel how he sped. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Charles the Dauphin]] and [[Joan la Pucelle]] |
| BASTARD | Tut, holy Joan was his defensive guard. |
| CHARLES | (to Joan)<br>Is this thy cunning, thou deceitful dame?<br>Didst thou at first, to flatter us withal,<br>Make us partakers of a little gain<br>That now our loss might be ten times so much? |
| JOAN | Wherefore is Charles impatient with his friend?<br>At all times will you have my power alike?<br>Sleeping or waking must I still prevail,<br>Or will you blame and lay the fault on me?—<br>Improvident soldiers, had your watch been good,<br>This sudden mischief never could have fall’n. |
| CHARLES | Duke of Alençon, this was your default,<br>That, being captain of the watch tonight,<br>Did look no better to that weighty charge.<br>ALENÇON<br>Had all your quarters been as safely kept<br>As that whereof I had the [[government]],<br>We had not been thus shamefully surprised. |
| BASTARD | Mine was secure.<br>RENÉ And so was mine, my lord. |
| CHARLES | And for myself, most part of all this [[Night|night]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Within her quarter and mine own precinct |
| CHARLES | I was employed in passing to and fro<br>About relieving of the sentinels.<br>Then how or which way should they first break in? |
| JOAN | Question, my lords, no further of the case,<br>How or which way. ‘Tis sure they found some place<br>But weakly guarded, where the breach was made.<br>And now there rests no other shift but this—<br>To gather our soldiers, scattered and dispersed,<br>And lay new platforms to endamage them. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter an English Soldier |
| ENGLISH SOLDIER | A Talbot! A Talbot!<br>The French fly, leaving their clothes behind |
| ENGLISH SOLDIER | I’ll be so bold to take what they have left.<br>The cry of ‘Talbot’ serves me for a [[Sword|sword]],<br>For I have loaden me with many spoils,<br>Using no other weapon but his name. Exit with spoils |
