| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lord Talbot]], the Dukes of Bedford and Burgundy, a Captain, and soldiers |
| BEDFORD | The day begins to break and [[Night|night]] is fled,<br>Whose pitchy mantle overveiled the earth.<br>Here sound retreat and cease our hot pursuit. |
| *(didascalia)* | Retreat is sounded |
| TALBOT | Bring forth the [[Body|body]] of old Salisbury<br>And here advance it in the market place,<br>The middle centre of this cursed town. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one or more |
| TALBOT | Now have I paid my vow unto his [[Soul|soul]]:<br>For every drop of [[Blood|blood]] was drawn from him<br>There hath at least five Frenchmen died tonight.<br>And that hereafter ages may behold<br>What ruin happened in [[Revenge|revenge]] of him, |
| *(didascalia)* | Within their chiefest temple I’ll erect |
| TALBOT | A tomb, wherein his corpse shall be interred—<br>Upon the which, that everyone may read,<br>Shall be engraved the sack of Orléans,<br>The treacherous manner of his mournful [[Death|death]],<br>And what a terror he had been to France.<br>But, lords, in all our bloody massacre<br>I muse we met not with the [[Dauphin]]’s grace,<br>His new-come champion, virtuous Joan of Arc,<br>Nor any of his false confederates. |
| BEDFORD | ‘Tis thought, [[Lord Talbot]], when the fight began,<br>Roused on the sudden from their drowsy beds,<br>They did amongst the troops of armed men<br>Leap o’er the walls for refuge in the field. |
| BURGUNDY | Myself, as far as I could [[Well|well]] discern<br>For smoke and dusky vapours of the [[Night|night]],<br>Am sure I scared the [[Dauphin]] and his trull,<br>When arm-in-arm they both came swiftly running,<br>Like to a pair of loving turtle-doves<br>That could not live asunder day or [[Night|night]].<br>After that things are set in order here,<br>We’ll follow them with all the power we have. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | All hail, my lords! Which of this princely train<br>Call ye the warlike Talbot, for his acts<br>So much applauded through the realm of France? |
| TALBOT | Here is the Talbot. Who would speak with him? |
| MESSENGER | The virtuous lady, Countess of Auvergne,<br>With modesty admiring thy renown,<br>By me entreats, great lord, thou wouldst vouchsafe<br>To visit her poor [[Castle|castle]] where she lies,<br>That she may boast she hath beheld the man<br>Whose glory fills the world with loud report. |
| BURGUNDY | Is it even so? Nay, then I see our wars<br>Will turn unto a peaceful comic sport,<br>When ladies crave to be encountered with.<br>You may not, my lord, despise her gentle suit. |
| TALBOT | Ne’er trust me then, for when a world of men<br>Could not prevail with all their oratory,<br>Yet hath a woman’s kindness overruled.—<br>And therefore tell her I return great thanks,<br>And in submission will attend on her.—<br>Will not your honours bear me company? |
| BEDFORD | No, truly, ‘tis more than manners will.<br>And I have heard it said, ‘Unbidden guests<br>Are often welcomest when they are gone’. |
| TALBOT | [[Well]] then, atone—since there’s no remedy—<br>I mean to prove this lady’s courtesy.<br>Come hither, captain.<br>He whispers<br>You perceive my mind? |
| CAPTAIN | I do, my lord, and mean accordingly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
