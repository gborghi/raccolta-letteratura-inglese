| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Countess of Auvergne and her Porter |
| COUNTESS | Porter, remember what I gave in charge,<br>And when you have done so, bring the keys to me. |
| PORTER | Madam, I will. Exit |
| COUNTESS | The plot is laid. If all things fall out right,<br>I shall as famous be by this exploit<br>As Scythian Tomyris by Cyrus’ [[Death|death]].<br>Great is the rumour of this dreadful knight,<br>And his achievements of no less account.<br>Fain would mine eyes be witness with mine ears,<br>To give their censure of these rare reports. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Messenger and [[Lord Talbot]] |
| MESSENGER | Madam, according as your ladyship desired,<br>By message craved, so is [[Lord Talbot]] come. |
| COUNTESS | And he is welcome. What, is this the man? |
| MESSENGER | Madam, it is. |
| COUNTESS | Is this the scourge of France?<br>Is this the Talbot, so much feared abroad<br>That with his name the mothers still their babes?<br>I see report is fabulous and false.<br>I thought I should have seen some Hercules,<br>A second [[Hector]], for his grim aspect<br>And large proportion of his strong-knit limbs.<br>Alas, this is a [[Child|child]], a seely [[Dwarf|dwarf]].<br>It cannot be this weak and writhled shrimp<br>Should strike such terror to his enemies. |
| TALBOT | Madam, I have been bold to trouble you.<br>But since your ladyship is not at leisure,<br>I’ll sort some other [[Time|time]] to visit you.<br>He is going |
| COUNTESS | (to Messenger)<br>What means he now? Go ask him whither he goes. |
| MESSENGER | Stay, my [[Lord Talbot]], for my lady craves<br>To know the cause of your abrupt departure. |
| TALBOT | Marry, for that she’s in a wrong belief,<br>I go to certify her Talbot’s here. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Porter with keys |
| COUNTESS | If thou be he, then art thou prisoner. |
| TALBOT | Prisoner? To whom? |
| COUNTESS | To me, bloodthirsty lord;<br>And for that cause I trained thee to my [[house]].<br>Long [[Time|time]] thy [[Shadow|shadow]] hath been thrall to me,<br>For in my gallery thy picture hangs;<br>But now the substance shall endure the like,<br>And I will chain these legs and arms of thine<br>That hast by tyranny these many years<br>Wasted our country, slain our citizens,<br>And sent our sons and husbands captivate— |
| TALBOT | Ha, ha, ha! |
| COUNTESS | Laughest thou, wretch? Thy mirth shall turn to moan. |
| TALBOT | I laugh to see your ladyship so fond<br>To think that you have aught but Talbot’s [[Shadow|shadow]]<br>Whereon to practise your severity. |
| COUNTESS | Why? Art not thou the man? |
| TALBOT | I am indeed. |
| COUNTESS | Then have I substance too. |
| TALBOT | No, no, I am but [[Shadow|shadow]] of myself.<br>You are deceived; my substance is not here.<br>For what you see is but the smallest part<br>And least proportion of humanity.<br>I tell you, madam, were the whole frame here,<br>It is of such a spacious lofty pitch<br>Your roof were not sufficient to contain’t. |
| COUNTESS | This is a riddling merchant for the nonce.<br>He will be here, and yet he is not here.<br>How can these contrarieties agree? |
| TALBOT | That will I show you presently.<br>He winds his horn. Within, drums strike up; a peal of ordnance. Enter English soldiers<br>How say you, madam? Are you now persuaded<br>That Talbot is but [[Shadow|shadow]] of himself?<br>These are his substance, sinews, arms, and strength,<br>With which he yoketh your rebellious necks,<br>Razeth your cities and subverts your towns,<br>And in a moment makes them desolate. |
| COUNTESS | Victorious Talbot, pardon my abuse.<br>I find thou art no less than fame hath bruited,<br>And more than may be gathered by thy shape.<br>Let my presumption not provoke thy wrath,<br>For I am sorry that with reverence<br>I did not entertain thee as thou art. |
| TALBOT | Be not dismayed, fair lady, nor misconster<br>The mind of Talbot, as you did mistake<br>The outward composition of his [[Body|body]].<br>What you have done hath not offended me;<br>Nor other satisfaction do I crave<br>But only, with your patience, that we may<br>Taste of your wine and see what cates you have:<br>For soldiers’ stomachs always serve them [[Well|well]]. |
| COUNTESS | With all my heart; and think me honoured<br>To feast so great a warrior in my [[house]]. Exeunt |
