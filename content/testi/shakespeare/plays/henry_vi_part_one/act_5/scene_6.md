| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Richard]] [[Duke of York]], the [[Earl of Warwick]], and a Shepherd |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Bring forth that sorceress condemned to burn. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Joan la Pucelle]] guarded |
| SHEPHERD | Ah, Joan, this kills thy father’s heart outright.<br>Have I sought every country far and near,<br>And now it is my chance to find thee out<br>Must I behold thy timeless cruel [[Death|death]]?<br>Ah Joan, sweet daughter Joan, I’ll die with thee. |
| JOAN | Decrepit miser, base ignoble wretch,<br>I am descended of a gentler [[Blood|blood]].<br>Thou art no father nor no friend of mine. |
| SHEPHERD | Out, out!—My lords, an’t please you, ‘tis not so.<br>I did beget her, all the parish knows.<br>Her mother liveth yet, can testify<br>She was the first fruit of my bach’lorship. |
| WARWICK | (to Joan)<br>Graceless, wilt thou deny thy parentage? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | This argues what her kind of life hath been—<br>Wicked and vile; and so her [[Death|death]] concludes. |
| SHEPHERD | Fie, Joan, that thou wilt be so obstacle.<br>God knows thou art a collop of my flesh,<br>And for thy sake have I shed many a tear.<br>Deny me not, I prithee, gentle Joan. |
| JOAN | Peasant, avaunt! (To the English) You have suborned<br>this man<br>Of purpose to obscure my noble birth. |
| SHEPHERD | (to the English)<br>‘Tis true I gave a noble to the priest<br>The morn that I was wedded to her mother.<br>(To Joan) Kneel down, and take my blessing, good my<br>girl.<br>Wilt thou not stoop? Now cursed be the [[Time|time]]<br>Of thy nativity. I would the milk<br>Thy mother gave thee when thou sucked’st her breast<br>Had been a little ratsbane for thy sake.<br>Or else, when thou didst keep my lambs afield,<br>I wish some ravenous wolf had eaten thee.<br>Dost thou deny thy father, cursed drab?<br>(To the English) O burn her, burn her! Hanging is too<br>good. Exit |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | (to guards)<br>Take her away, for she hath lived too long,<br>To fill the world with vicious qualities. |
| JOAN | First let me tell you whom you have condemned:<br>Not one begotten of a shepherd swain,<br>But issued from the progeny of kings;<br>Virtuous and holy, chosen from above<br>By inspiration of celestial grace<br>To work exceeding miracles on earth.<br>I never had to do with wicked spirits;<br>But you that are polluted with your lusts,<br>Stained with the guiltless [[Blood|blood]] of innocents,<br>Corrupt and tainted with a thousand vices—<br>Because you want the grace that others have,<br>You judge it straight a thing impossible<br>To compass wonders but by help of devils.<br>No, misconceived Joan of Arc hath been<br>A virgin from her tender infancy,<br>Chaste and immaculate in very thought,<br>Whose maiden-[[Blood|blood]] thus rigorously effused<br>Will cry for vengeance at the gates of heaven. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Ay, ay, (to guards) away with her to execution. |
| WARWICK | (to guards)<br>And hark ye, sirs: because she is a maid,<br>Spare for no faggots. Let there be enough.<br>Place barrels of pitch upon the fatal stake,<br>That so her torture may be shortened. |
| JOAN | Will nothing turn your unrelenting hearts?<br>Then Joan, discover thine infirmity,<br>That warranteth by law to be thy privilege:<br>I am with [[Child|child]], ye bloody homicides.<br>Murder not then the fruit within my womb,<br>Although ye hale me to a violent [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Now heaven forfend—the holy maid with [[Child|child]]? |
| WARWICK | (to Joan)<br>The greatest miracle that e’er ye wrought.<br>Is all your strict preciseness come to this? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | She and the [[Dauphin]] have been ingling.<br>I did imagine what would be her refuge. |
| WARWICK | [[Well]], go to, we will have no bastards live,<br>Especially since Charles must father it. |
| JOAN | You are deceived. My [[Child|child]] is none of his.<br>It was Alençon that enjoyed my [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Alençon, that notorious Machiavel?<br>It dies an if it had a thousand lives. |
| JOAN | O give me leave, I have deluded you.<br>‘Twas neither Charles nor yet the Duke I named,<br>But René [[King]] of Naples that prevailed. |
| WARWICK | A married man?—That’s most intolerable. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Why, here’s a girl; I think she knows not [[Well|well]]—<br>There were so many—whom she may accuse. |
| WARWICK | It’s sign she hath been liberal and free. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | And yet forsooth she is a virgin pure!<br>(To joan) Strumpet, thy words condemn thy brat and<br>thee.<br>Use no entreaty, for it is in vain. |
| JOAN | Then lead me hence; with whom I leave my curse.<br>May never glorious sun reflex his beams<br>Upon the country where you make abode,<br>But darkness and the gloomy shade of [[Death|death]]<br>Environ you till mischief and [[Despair|despair]]<br>Drive you to break your necks or hang yourselves. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Bishop of Winchester, now Cardinal |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | (to Joan)<br>Break thou in pieces, and consume to ashes,<br>Thou foul accursed minister of hell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Joan, guarded |
| WINCHESTER | Lord Regent, I do greet your excellence<br>With letters of commission from the [[King]].<br>For know, my lords, the states of Christendom,<br>Moved with remorse of these outrageous broils,<br>Have earnestly implored a general peace<br>Betwixt our nation and the aspiring French,<br>And here at hand the [[Dauphin]] and his train<br>Approacheth to confer about some matter. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Is all our travail turned to this effect?<br>After the slaughter of so many peers,<br>So many captains, gentlemen, and soldiers<br>That in this quarrel have been overthrown<br>And sold their bodies for their country’s benefit,<br>Shall we at last conclude effeminate peace?<br>Have we not lost most part of all the towns<br>By treason, falsehood, and by treachery,<br>Our great progenitors had conquered?<br>O Warwick, Warwick, I foresee with grief<br>The utter loss of all the realm of France! |
| WARWICK | Be patient, York. If we conclude a peace<br>It shall be with such strict and severe covenants<br>As little shall the Frenchmen gain thereby. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Charles the Dauphin]], the Duke of Alençon, the Bastard of Orléans, and René Duke of Anjou |
| CHARLES | Since, lords of England, it is thus agreed<br>That peaceful truce shall be proclaimed in France,<br>We come to be informed by yourselves<br>What the conditions of that league must be. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Speak, Winchester; for boiling choler chokes<br>The hollow passage of my poisoned voice<br>By sight of these our baleful enemies. |
| WINCHESTER | Charles and the rest, it is enacted thus:<br>That, in regard [[King]] Henry gives consent,<br>Of mere compassion and of lenity,<br>To ease your country of distressful war<br>And suffer you to breathe in fruitful peace,<br>You shall become true liegemen to his [[Crown|crown]].<br>And, Charles, upon condition thou wilt swear<br>To pay him tribute and submit thyself,<br>Thou shalt be placed as viceroy under him,<br>And still enjoy thy regal dignity.<br>ALENÇON<br>Must he be then as [[Shadow|shadow]] of himself?—<br>Adorn his temples with a coronet,<br>And yet in substance and authority<br>Retain but privilege of a private man?<br>This proffer is absurd and reasonless. |
| CHARLES | ’Tis known already that I am possessed<br>With more than half the Gallian territories,<br>And therein reverenced for their lawful [[King|king]].<br>Shall I, for lucre of the rest unvanquished,<br>Detract so much from that prerogative<br>As to be called but viceroy of the whole?<br>No, lord ambassador, I’ll rather keep<br>That which I have than, coveting for more,<br>Be cast from possibility of all. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Insulting Charles, hast thou by secret means<br>Used intercession to obtain a league<br>And, now the matter grows to compromise,<br>Stand‘st thou aloof upon comparison?<br>Either accept the title thou usurp’st,<br>Of benefit proceeding from our [[King|king]]<br>And not of any challenge of [[Desert|desert]],<br>Or we will plague thee with incessant wars.<br>RENÉ (aside to Charles)<br>My lord, you do not [[Well|well]] in obstinacy<br>To cavil in the course of this contract.<br>If once it be neglected, ten to one<br>We shall not find like opportunity.<br>ALENÇON (aside to Charles)<br>To say the [[Truth|truth]], it is your policy<br>To save your subjects from such massacre<br>And ruthless slaughters as are daily seen<br>By our proceeding in hostility;<br>And therefore take this compact of a truce,<br>Although you break it when your pleasure serves. |
| WARWICK | How sayst thou, Charles? Shall our condition stand? |
| CHARLES | It shall,<br>Only reserved you claim no interest<br>In any of our towns of garrison. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | Then swear allegiance to his majesty,<br>As thou art knight, never to disobey<br>Nor be rebellious to the [[Crown|crown]] of England,<br>Thou nor thy nobles, to the [[Crown|crown]] of England. |
| *(didascalia)* | They swear |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Duke of York|DUKE OF YORK]] | So, now dismiss your army when ye please.<br>Hang up your ensigns, let your drums be still;<br>For here we entertain a solemn peace. Exeunt |
