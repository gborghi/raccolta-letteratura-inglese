| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Charles the Dauphin]] reading a letter, the Dukes of Burgundy and Alençon, the Bastard of Orléans, René Duke of Anjou, and oan la Pucelle |
| CHARLES | These news, my lords, may cheer our drooping spirits.<br>‘Tis said the stout Parisians do revolt<br>And turn again unto the warlike French.<br>ALENÇON<br>Then march to [[Paris]], royal Charles of France,<br>And keep not back your powers in dalliance. |
| JOAN | Peace be amongst them if they turn to us;<br>Else, ruin combat with their palaces! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Scout |
| SCOUT | [[success|Success]] unto our valiant general,<br>And happiness to his accomplices. |
| CHARLES | What tidings send our scouts? I prithee speak. |
| SCOUT | The English army, that divided was<br>Into two parties, is now conjoined in one,<br>And means to give you battle presently. |
| CHARLES | Somewhat too sudden, sirs, the warning is;<br>But we will presently provide for them. |
| BURGUNDY | I trust the [[Ghost|ghost]] of Talbot is not there. |
| *(didascalia)* | JOAN |
| BURGUNDY | Now he is gone, my lord, you need not [[Fear|fear]].<br>Of all base passions, [[Fear|fear]] is most accursed.<br>Command the conquest, Charles, it shall be thine;<br>Let Henry fret and all the world repine. |
| CHARLES | Then on, my lords; and France be fortunate! Exeunt |
