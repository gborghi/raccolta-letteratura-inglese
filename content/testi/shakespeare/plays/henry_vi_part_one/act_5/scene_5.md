| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter the [[Earl of Suffolk]] with Margaret in his hand |
| SUFFOLK | Be what thou wilt, thou art my prisoner.<br>He gazes on her<br>O fairest [[Beauty|beauty]], do not [[Fear|fear]] nor fly,<br>For I will touch thee but with reverent hands,<br>And lay them gently on thy tender side.<br>I kiss these fingers for eternal peace.<br>Who art thou? Say, that I may [[Honour|honour]] thee. |
| MARGARET | Margaret my name, and daughter to a [[King|king]],<br>The [[King]] of Naples, whosoe’er thou art. |
| SUFFOLK | An earl I am, and Suffolk am I called.<br>Be not offended, [[Nature|nature]]’s miracle,<br>Thou art allotted to be ta’en by me.<br>So doth the swan his downy cygnets save,<br>Keeping them prisoner underneath his wings.<br>Yet if this servile usage once offend,<br>Go, and be free again, as Suffolk’s friend.<br>She is going<br>O stay! (Aside) I have no power to let her pass.<br>My hand would free her, but my heart says no.<br>As plays the sun upon the glassy stream,<br>Twinkling another counterfeited beam,<br>So seems this gorgeous [[Beauty|beauty]] to mine eyes.<br>Fain would I woo her, yet I dare not speak.<br>I’ll call for pen and ink, and write my mind.<br>Fie, de la Pole, disable not thyself!<br>Hast not a tongue? Is she not here to hear?<br>Wilt thou be daunted at a woman’s sight?<br>Ay, [[Beauty|beauty]]’s princely majesty is such<br>Confounds the tongue, and makes the senses rough. |
| MARGARET | Say, [[Earl of Suffolk]]—if thy name be so—<br>What ransom must I pay before I pass?<br>For I perceive I am thy prisoner. |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>How canst thou tell she will deny thy suit<br>Before thou make a trial of her [[Love|love]]? |
| MARGARET | Why speak’st thou not? What ransom must I pay? |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>She’s beautiful, and therefore to be wooed;<br>She is a woman, therefore to be won. |
| MARGARET | Wilt thou accept of ransom, yea or no? |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>Fond man, remember that thou hast a wife;<br>Then how can Margaret be thy paramour? |
| MARGARET | (aside)<br>I were best to leave him, for he will not hear. |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>There all is marred; there lies a cooling card. |
| MARGARET | (aside)<br>He talks at random; sure the man is mad. |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>And yet a dispensation may be had. |
| MARGARET | And yet I would that you would answer me. |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>I’ll win this Lady Margaret. For whom?<br>Why, for my [[King|king]]—tush, that’s a wooden thing. |
| MARGARET | (aside)<br>He talks of wood. It is some carpenter. |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>Yet so my fancy may be satisfied,<br>And peace established between these realms.<br>But there remains a scruple in that too,<br>For though her father be the [[King]] of Naples,<br>Duke of Anjou and Maine, yet is he poor,<br>And our nobility will scorn the match. |
| MARGARET | Hear ye, captain? Are you not at leisure? |
| SUFFOLK | (aside)<br>It shall be so, disdain they ne’er so much.<br>Henry is youthful, and will quickly yield.<br>(To Margaret) Madam, I have a secret to reveal. |
| MARGARET | (aside)<br>What though I be enthralled, he seems a knight<br>And will not any way dishonour me. |
| SUFFOLK | Lady, vouchsafe to listen what I say. |
| MARGARET | (aside)<br>Perhaps I shall be rescued by the French,<br>And then I need not crave his courtesy. |
| SUFFOLK | Sweet madam, give me hearing in a cause. |
| MARGARET | (aside)<br>Tush, women have been captivate ere now. |
| SUFFOLK | Lady, wherefore talk you so? |
| MARGARET | I cry you [[Mercy|mercy]], ’tis but quid for quo. |
| SUFFOLK | Say, gentle Princess, would you not suppose<br>Your bondage happy to be made a [[Queen|queen]]? |
| MARGARET | To be a [[Queen|queen]] in bondage is more vile<br>Than is a slave in base servility,<br>For princes should be free. |
| SUFFOLK | And so shall you,<br>If happy England’s royal [[King|king]] be free. |
| MARGARET | Why, what concerns his [[Freedom|freedom]] unto me? |
| SUFFOLK | I’ll undertake to make thee Henry’s [[Queen|queen]],<br>To put a golden sceptre in thy hand,<br>And set a precious [[Crown|crown]] upon thy head,<br>If thou wilt condescend to be my— |
| MARGARET | What? |
| SUFFOLK | His [[Love|love]]. |
| MARGARET | I am unworthy to be Henry’s wife. |
| SUFFOLK | No, gentle madam, I unworthy am<br>To woo so fair a dame to be his wife<br>(Aside) And have no portion in the choice myself.—<br>How say you, madam; are ye so content? |
| MARGARET | An if my father please, I am content. |
| SUFFOLK | Then call our captains and our colours forth, |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter captains, colours, and trumpeters |
| SUFFOLK | And, madam, at your father’s [[Castle|castle]] walls<br>We’ll crave a parley to confer with him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound a parley. Enter René Duke of Anjou on the walls |
| SUFFOLK | See, Rene, see thy daughter prisoner.<br>RENÉ<br>To whom ? |
| SUFFOLK | To me.<br>RENÉ Suffolk, what remedy?<br>I am a soldier, and unapt to weep<br>Or to exclaim on fortune’s fickleness. |
| SUFFOLK | Yes, there is remedy enough, my lord.<br>Assent, and for thy [[Honour|honour]] give consent<br>Thy daughter shall be wedded to my [[King|king]],<br>Whom I with [[Pain|pain]] have wooed and won thereto;<br>And this her easy-held [[Imprisonment|imprisonment]]<br>Hath gained thy daughter princely liberty.<br>RENÉ<br>Speaks Suffolk as he thinks? |
| SUFFOLK | Fair Margaret knows<br>That Suffolk doth not flatter, face or feign.<br>RENÉ<br>Upon thy princely warrant I descend<br>To give thee answer of thy just demand. |
| *(didascalia)* | And here I will expect thy coming. Exit Rene above |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets sound. Enter René |
| RENE | Welcome, brave Earl, into our territories.<br>Command in Anjou what your [[Honour|honour]] pleases. |
| SUFFOLK | Thanks, René, happy for so sweet a [[Child|child]],<br>Fit to be made companion with a [[King|king]].<br>What answer makes your grace unto my suit?<br>RENÉ<br>Since thou dost deign to woo her little worth<br>To be the princely bride of such a lord,<br>Upon condition I may quietly<br>Enjoy mine own, the countries Maine and Anjou,<br>Free from oppression or the stroke of war,<br>My daughter shall be Henry’s, if he please. |
| SUFFOLK | That is her ransom. I deliver her,<br>And those two counties I will undertake<br>Your grace shall [[Well|well]] and quietly enjoy.<br>RENÉ<br>And I again in Henry’s royal name,<br>As deputy unto that gracious [[King|king]],<br>Give thee her hand for sign of plighted [[Faith|faith]]. |
| SUFFOLK | René of France, I give thee kingly thanks,<br>Because this is in traffic of a [[King|king]].<br>(Aside) And yet methinks I could be [[Well|well]] content<br>To be mine own attorney in this case.<br>(To René) I’ll over then to England with this news,<br>And make this [[Marriage|marriage]] to be solemnized.<br>So farewell, René; set this diamond safe<br>In golden palaces, as it becomes.<br>RENÉ<br>I do embrace thee as I would embrace<br>The Christian prince [[King]] Henry, were he here. |
| MARGARET | (to Suffolk)<br>Farewell, my lord. Good wishes, praise, and prayers<br>Shall Suffolk ever have of Margaret.<br>She is going |
| SUFFOLK | Farewell, sweet madam; but hark you, Margaret—<br>No princely commendations to my [[King|king]]? |
| MARGARET | Such commendations as becomes a maid,<br>A virgin, and his servant, say to him. |
| SUFFOLK | Words sweetly placed, and modestly directed. |
| *(didascalia)* | She is going |
| SUFFOLK | But madam, I must trouble you again—<br>No loving token to his majesty? |
| MARGARET | Yes, my good lord : a pure unspotted heart,<br>Never yet taint with [[Love|love]], I send the [[King]]. |
| SUFFOLK | And this withal.<br>He kisses her |
| MARGARET | That for thyself; I will not so presume<br>To send such peevish tokens to a [[King|king]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt René and Margaret |
| *(didascalia)* | SUFFOLK aside |
| MARGARET | O, wert thou for myself!—but Suffolk, stay.<br>Thou mayst not wander in that labyrinth.<br>There Minotaurs and ugly treasons lurk.<br>Solicit Henry with her wondrous praise.<br>Bethink thee on her virtues that surmount,<br>Mad natural graces that extinguish art.<br>Repeat their semblance often on the seas,<br>That when thou com’st to kneel at Henry’s feet<br>Thou mayst bereave him of his wits with [[Wonder|wonder]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
