| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What say you, [[Hermia]]? Be advised, fair maid. To you your father should be as a god, One that composed your beauties, yea, and one To whom you are but as a form in wax, By him imprinted, and within his power To leave the figure or disfigure it. [[Demetrius]] is a worthy gentleman. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | So is [[Lysander]]. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | In himself he is, But in this kind, wanting your father’s voice, The other must be held the worthier. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | I would my father looked but with my eyes. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Rather your eyes must with his judgement look. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | I do entreat your grace to pardon me. I know not by what power I am made bold, Nor how it may concern my modesty In such a presence here to plead my thoughts, But I beseech your grace that I may know The worst that may befall me in this case If I refuse to wed [[Demetrius]]. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Either to die the [[Death|death]], or to abjure For ever the society of men. Therefore, fair [[Hermia]], question your desires. Know of your youth, examine [[Well|well]] your [[Blood|blood]], Whether, if you yield not to your father’s choice, You can endure the livery of a nun, For aye to be in shady cloister mewed, To live a barren sister all your life, Chanting faint hymns to the cold fruitless [[Moon|moon]]. Thrice blessed they that master so their [[Blood|blood]] To undergo such maiden pilgrimage; But earthlier happy is the [[Rose|rose]] distilled Than that which, withering on the virgin thorn, Grows, lives, and dies in single blessedness. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | So will I grow, so live, so die, my lord, Ere I will yield my virgin patent up Unto his lordship whose unwishèd yoke My [[Soul|soul]] consents not to give sovereignty. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Take [[Time|time]] to pause, and by the next new [[Moon|moon]]— The sealing day betwixt my [[Love|love]] and me For everlasting bond of fetlowship— Upon that day either prepare to die For disobedience to your father’s will, Or else to wed [[Demetrius]], as he would, Or on [[Diana]]’s altar to protest For aye austerity and single life. |
| [[Demetrius|DEMETRIUS]] | Relent, sweet [[Hermia]]; and, [[Lysander]], yield Thy crazed title to my certain right. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | You have her father’s [[Love|love]], [[Demetrius]]; Let me have [[Hermia]]’s. Do you marry him. |
| EGEUS | Scornful [[Lysander]]! True, he hath my [[Love|love]]; And what is mine my [[Love|love]] shall render him, And she is mine, and all my right of her I do estate unto [[Demetrius]]. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | to [[Theseus]] I am, my lord, as [[Well|well]] derived as he, As [[Well|well]] possessed. My [[Love|love]] is more than his, My fortunes every way as fairly ranked, If not with vantage, as [[Demetrius]]; And—which is more than all these boasts can be— I am beloved of beauteous [[Hermia]]. Why should not I then prosecute my right? [[Demetrius]]—I’ll avouch it to his head- Made [[Love|love]] to Nedar’s daughter, [[Helena]], And won her [[Soul|soul]], and she, sweet lady, dotes, Devoutly dotes, dotes in idolatry Upon this spotted and inconstant man. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | I must confess that I have heard so much, And with [[Demetrius]] thought to have spoke thereof; But, being over-full of [[Self|self]] affairs, My mind did lose it. But, [[Demetrius]], come; And come, Egeus. You shall go with me. I have some private schooling for you both. For you, fair [[Hermia]], look you arm yourself To fit your fancies to your father’s will, Or else the law of Athens yields you up— Which by no means we may extenuate— To [[Death|death]] or to a vow of single life. Come, my [[Hippolyta]]; what cheer, my [[Love|love]]?— [[Demetrius]] and Egeus, go along. I must employ you in some business Against our nuptial, and confer with you Of something nearly that concerns yourselves. |
