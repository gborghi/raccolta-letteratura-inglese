| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Theseus]], [[Hippolyta]], and Philostrate, with others |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Now, fair [[Hippolyta]], our nuptial hour<br>Draws on apace. Four happy days bring in<br>Another [[Moon|moon]]—but O, methinks how slow<br>This old [[Moon|moon]] wanes! She lingers my desires<br>Like to a stepdame or a dowager<br>Long withering out a young man’s revenue. |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | Four days will quickly steep themselves in [[Night|night]],<br>Four nights will quickly dream away the [[Time|time]];<br>And then the [[Moon|moon]], like to a silver bow<br>New bent in heaven, shall behold the [[Night|night]]<br>Of our solemnities. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Go, Philostrate,<br>Stir up the Athenian youth to merriments.<br>Awake the pert and nimble spirit of mirth.<br>Turn [[Melancholy|melancholy]] forth to funerals—<br>The pale companion is not for our pomp. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Philostrate |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | [[Hippolyta]], I wooed thee with my [[Sword|sword]],<br>And won thy [[Love|love]] doing thee injuries.<br>But I will wed thee in another key—<br>With pomp, with triumph, and with revelling. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Egeus and his daughter [[Hermia]], and [[Lysander]] and [[Demetrius]] |
| EGEUS | Happy be [[Theseus]], our renowned Duke. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Thanks, good Egeus. What’s the news with thee? |
| EGEUS | Full of vexation come I, with complaint<br>Against my [[Child|child]], my daughter [[Hermia]].—<br>Stand forth [[Demetrius]].—My noble lord,<br>This man hath my consent to marry her.—<br>Stand forth [[Lysander]].—And, my gracious Duke,<br>This hath bewitched the bosom of my [[Child|child]].<br>Thou, thou, [[Lysander]], thou hast given her rhymes,<br>And interchanged [[Love|love]] tokens with my [[Child|child]].<br>Thou hast by moonlight at her [[Window|window]] sung<br>With feigning voice verses of feigning [[Love|love]],<br>And stol’n the impression of her fantasy<br>With bracelets of thy hair, rings, gauds, conceits,<br>Knacks, trifles, nosegays, sweetmeats—messengers<br>Of strong prevailment in unhardened youth. 35<br>With cunning hast thou filched my daughter’s heart,<br>Turned her obedience which is due to me<br>To stubborn harshness. And, my gracious Duke,<br>Be it so she will not here before your grace<br>Consent to marry with [[Demetrius]],<br>I beg the ancient privilege of Athens:<br>As she is mine, I may dispose of her,<br>Which shall be either to this gentleman<br>Or to her [[Death|death]], according to our law<br>Immediately provided in that case. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What say you, [[Hermia]]? Be advised, fair maid.<br>To you your father should be as a god,<br>One that composed your beauties, yea, and one<br>To whom you are but as a form in wax,<br>By him imprinted, and within his power<br>To leave the figure or disfigure it.<br>[[Demetrius]] is a worthy gentleman. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | So is [[Lysander]]. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | In himself he is,<br>But in this kind, wanting your father’s voice,<br>The other must be held the worthier. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | I would my father looked but with my eyes. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Rather your eyes must with his judgement look. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | I do entreat your grace to pardon me.<br>I know not by what power I am made bold,<br>Nor how it may concern my modesty<br>In such a presence here to plead my thoughts,<br>But I beseech your grace that I may know<br>The worst that may befall me in this case<br>If I refuse to wed [[Demetrius]]. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Either to die the [[Death|death]], or to abjure<br>For ever the society of men.<br>Therefore, fair [[Hermia]], question your desires.<br>Know of your youth, examine [[Well|well]] your [[Blood|blood]],<br>Whether, if you yield not to your father’s choice,<br>You can endure the livery of a nun,<br>For aye to be in shady cloister mewed,<br>To live a barren sister all your life,<br>Chanting faint hymns to the cold fruitless [[Moon|moon]].<br>Thrice blessed they that master so their [[Blood|blood]]<br>To undergo such maiden pilgrimage;<br>But earthlier happy is the [[Rose|rose]] distilled<br>Than that which, withering on the virgin thorn,<br>Grows, lives, and dies in single blessedness. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | So will I grow, so live, so die, my lord,<br>Ere I will yield my virgin patent up<br>Unto his lordship whose unwishèd yoke<br>My [[Soul|soul]] consents not to give sovereignty. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Take [[Time|time]] to pause, and by the next new [[Moon|moon]]—<br>The sealing day betwixt my [[Love|love]] and me<br>For everlasting bond of fetlowship—<br>Upon that day either prepare to die<br>For disobedience to your father’s will,<br>Or else to wed [[Demetrius]], as he would,<br>Or on [[Diana]]’s altar to protest<br>For aye austerity and single life. |
| [[Demetrius|DEMETRIUS]] | Relent, sweet [[Hermia]]; and, [[Lysander]], yield<br>Thy crazed title to my certain right. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | You have her father’s [[Love|love]], [[Demetrius]];<br>Let me have [[Hermia]]’s. Do you marry him. |
| EGEUS | Scornful [[Lysander]]! True, he hath my [[Love|love]];<br>And what is mine my [[Love|love]] shall render him,<br>And she is mine, and all my right of her<br>I do estate unto [[Demetrius]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] to [[Theseus]] |
| EGEUS | I am, my lord, as [[Well|well]] derived as he,<br>As [[Well|well]] possessed. My [[Love|love]] is more than his,<br>My fortunes every way as fairly ranked,<br>If not with vantage, as [[Demetrius]];<br>And—which is more than all these boasts can be—<br>I am beloved of beauteous [[Hermia]].<br>Why should not I then prosecute my right?<br>[[Demetrius]]—I’ll avouch it to his head-<br>Made [[Love|love]] to Nedar’s daughter, [[Helena]],<br>And won her [[Soul|soul]], and she, sweet lady, dotes,<br>Devoutly dotes, dotes in idolatry<br>Upon this spotted and inconstant man. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | I must confess that I have heard so much,<br>And with [[Demetrius]] thought to have spoke thereof;<br>But, being over-full of [[Self|self]] affairs,<br>My mind did lose it. But, [[Demetrius]], come;<br>And come, Egeus. You shall go with me.<br>I have some private schooling for you both.<br>For you, fair [[Hermia]], look you arm yourself<br>To fit your fancies to your father’s will,<br>Or else the law of Athens yields you up—<br>Which by no means we may extenuate—<br>To [[Death|death]] or to a vow of single life.<br>Come, my [[Hippolyta]]; what cheer, my [[Love|love]]?—<br>[[Demetrius]] and Egeus, go along.<br>I must employ you in some business<br>Against our nuptial, and confer with you<br>Of something nearly that concerns yourselves. |
| EGEUS | With duty and [[Desire|desire]] we follow you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Lysander]] and [[Hermia]] |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | How now, my [[Love|love]]? Why is your cheek so pate ?<br>How chance the roses there do fade so fast? |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | Belike for want of rain, which I could [[Well|well]]<br>Beteem them from the tempest of my eyes. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | Ay me, for aught that I could ever read,<br>Could ever hear by tale or [[history]],<br>The course of true [[Love|love]] never did run smooth,<br>But either it was different in btood— |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | O [[Cross|cross]]!—too high to be enthralled to low. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | Or else misgrafted in respect of years— |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | O spite!—too old to be engaged to young. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | Or merit stood upon the choice of friends— |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | O hell!—to choose [[Love|love]] by another’s eyes. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | Or if there were a sympathy in choice,<br>War, [[Death|death]], or sickness did lay siege to it,<br>Making it momentany as a sound,<br>Swift as a [[Shadow|shadow]], short as any dream,<br>Brief as the [[Lightning|lightning]] in the collied [[Night|night]],<br>That, in a spleen, unfolds both heaven and earth,<br>And, ere a man hath power to say ‘Behold!’<br>The jaws of darkness do devour it up.<br>So quick bright things come to confusion. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | If then true [[lovers]] have been ever crossed,<br>It stands as an edict in destiny.<br>Then let us teach our trial patience,<br>Because it is a customary [[Cross|cross]],<br>As due to [[Love|love]] as thoughts, and [[Dreams|dreams]], and sighs,<br>Wishes, and [[Tears|tears]], poor fancy’s followers. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | A good [[Persuasion|persuasion]]. Therefore hear me, [[Hermia]].<br>I have a widow aunt, a dowager<br>Of great revenue, and she hath no [[Child|child]],<br>And she respects me as her only son.<br>From Athens is her [[house]] remote seven leagues.<br>There, gentle [[Hermia]], may I marry thee,<br>And to that place the sharp Athenian law<br>Cannot pursue us. If thou lov’st me then,<br>Steal forth thy father’s [[house]] tomorrow [[Night|night]],<br>And in the wood, a league without the town,<br>Where I did meet thee once with [[Helena]]<br>To do observance to a morn of May,<br>There will I stay for thee. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | My good [[Lysander]],<br>I swear to thee by Cupid’s strongest bow,<br>By his best arrow with the golden head,<br>By the simplicity of [[Venus]]’ doves,<br>By that which knitteth souls and prospers loves,<br>And by that [[Fire|fire]] which burned the Carthage [[Queen|queen]]<br>When the false Trojan under sail was seen;<br>By all the vows that ever men have broke—<br>In number more than ever women spoke—<br>In that same place thou hast appointed me<br>Tomorrow truly will I meet with thee. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | Keep promise, [[Love|love]]. Look, here comes [[Helena]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Helena]] |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | God [[Speed|speed]], fair [[Helena]]. Whither away? |
| [[Helena|HELENA]] | Call you me fair? That ’fair’ again unsay.<br>[[Demetrius]] loves your fair—O happy fair!<br>Your eyes are lodestars, and your tongue’s sweet air<br>More tuneable than lark to shepherd’s ear<br>When wheat is green, when hawthorn buds appear.<br>Sickness is catching. O, were favour so!<br>Your words I catch, fair [[Hermia]]; ere I go,<br>My ear should catch your voice, my eye your eye,<br>My tongue should catch your tongue’s sweet melody.<br>Were the world mine, [[Demetrius]] being bated,<br>The rest I’d give to be to you translated.<br>O, teach me how you look, and with what art<br>You sway the motion of [[Demetrius]]’ heart. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | I frown upon him, yet he loves me still. |
| [[Helena|HELENA]] | O that your frowns would teach my smiles such skill! |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | I give him curses, yet he gives me [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Helena|HELENA]] | O that my prayers could such affection move! |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | The more I hate, the more he follows me. |
| [[Helena|HELENA]] | The more I [[Love|love]], the more he hateth me. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | His folly, [[Helen]], is no fault of mine. |
| [[Helena|HELENA]] | None but your [[Beauty|beauty]]; would that fault were mine! |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | Take comfort. He no more shall see my face.<br>[[Lysander]] and myself will fly this place.<br>Before the [[Time|time]] I did [[Lysander]] see<br>Seemed Athens as a paradise to me.<br>O then, what graces in my [[Love|love]] do dwell,<br>That he hath turned a heaven unto a hell? |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | [[Helen]], to you our minds we will unfold.<br>Tomorrow [[Night|night]], when [[Phoebe]] doth behold<br>Her silver visage in the wat’ry glass,<br>Decking with liquid pearl the bladed grass—<br>A [[Time|time]] that [[lovers]]’ sleights doth still conceal—<br>Through Athens’ gates have we devised to steal. |
| [[Hermia|HERMIA]] | And in the wood where often you and I<br>Upon faint primrose beds were wont to lie,<br>Emptying our bosoms of their counsel sweet,<br>There my [[Lysander]] and myself shall meet,<br>And thence from Athens turn away our eyes<br>To seek new friends and stranger companies.<br>Farewell, sweet playfellow. Pray thou for us,<br>And good luck grant thee thy [[Demetrius]].—<br>Keep word, [[Lysander]]. We must starve our sight<br>From [[lovers]]’ food till morrow deep midnight. |
| [[Lysander|LYSANDER]] | I will, my [[Hermia]]. Exit [[Hermia]]<br>[[Helena]], adieu.<br>As you on him, [[Demetrius]] dote on you. Exit |
| [[Helena|HELENA]] | How happy some o’er other some can be!<br>Through Athens I am thought as fair as she.<br>But what of that? [[Demetrius]] thinks not so.<br>He will not know what all but he do know.<br>And as he errs, doting on [[Hermia]]’s eyes,<br>So I, admiring of his qualities.<br>Things base and vile, holding no quantity,<br>[[Love]] can transpose to form and dignity.<br>[[Love]] looks not with the eyes, but with the mind,<br>And therefore is winged Cupid painted blind.<br>Nor hath [[Love|love]]’s mind of any judgement taste;<br>Wings and no eyes figure unheedy haste.<br>And therefore is [[Love|love]] said to be a [[Child|child]]<br>Because in choice he is so oft beguiled.<br>As waggish boys in game themselves forswear,<br>So the boy [[Love]] is perjured everywhere.<br>For ere [[Demetrius]] looked on [[Hermia]]’s eyne<br>He hailed down oaths that he was only mine,<br>And when this hail some heat from [[Hermia]] felt,<br>So he dissolved, and showers of oaths did melt.<br>I will go tell him of fair [[Hermia]]’s flight.<br>Then to the wood will he tomorrow [[Night|night]]<br>Pursue her, and for this intelligence<br>If I have thanks it is a dear expense.<br>But herein mean I to enrich my [[Pain|pain]],<br>To have his sight thither and back again. Exit |
