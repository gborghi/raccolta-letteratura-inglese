| *(didascalia)* | Enter the old Duchess of York with the two children of [[Clarence]] |
| BOY | Good grannam, tell us, is our father dead? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | No, boy. |
| GIRL | Why do you weep so oft, and beat your breast,<br>And cry, ‛O [[Clarence]], my unhappy son’? |
| BOY | Why do you look on us and shake your head,<br>And call us orphans, wretches, castaways,<br>If that our noble father were alive? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | My pretty cousins, you mistake me both.<br>I do lament the sickness of the [[King]],<br>As loath to lose him, not your father’s [[Death|death]].<br>It were lost sorrow to wail one that’s lost. |
| BOY | Then you conclude, my grannam, he is dead.<br>The [[King]] mine uncle is to blame for this.<br>God will [[Revenge|revenge]] it—whom I will importune<br>With earnest prayers, all to that effect. |
| GIRL | And so will I. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Peace, children, peace! The [[King]] doth [[Love|love]] you [[Well|well]].<br>Incapable and shallow innocents,<br>You cannot guess who caused your father’s [[Death|death]]. |
| BOY | Grannam, we can. For my good uncle [[Gloucester]]<br>Told me the [[King]], provoked to it by the [[Queen]],<br>Devised impeachments to imprison him,<br>And when my uncle told me so he wept,<br>And pitied me, and kindly kissed my cheek,<br>Bade me rely on him as on my father,<br>And he would [[Love|love]] me dearly as his [[Child|child]]. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Ah, that deceit should steal such gentle shapes,<br>And with a virtuous visor hide deep vice!<br>He is my son, ay, and therein my shame;<br>Yet from my dugs he drew not this deceit. |
| BOY | Think you my uncle did dissemble, grannam? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Ay, boy. |
| BOY | I cannot think it. Hark, what noise is this? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Queen Elizabeth]] with her hair about her ears |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Ah, who shall hinder me to wail and weep?<br>To chide my fortune, and torment myself?<br>I’ll join with black [[Despair|despair]] against my [[Soul|soul]],<br>And to myself become an enemy. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | What means this scene of rude impatience? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | To mark an act of tragic violence.<br>Edward, my lord, thy son, our [[King|king]], is dead.<br>Why grow the branches when the root is gone?<br>Why wither not the [[Leaves|leaves]] that want their sap?<br>If you will live, lament; if die, be brief,<br>That our swift-winged souls may catch the [[King]]‘s,<br>Or like obedient subjects follow him<br>To his new kingdom of ne’er-changing [[Night|night]]. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Ah, so much interest have I in thy sorrow<br>As I had title in thy noble husband.<br>I have bewept a worthy husband’s [[Death|death]],<br>And lived with looking on his images.<br>But now two mirrors of his princely semblance<br>Are cracked in pieces by malignant [[Death|death]],<br>And I for comfort have but one false glass,<br>That grieves me when I see my shame in him.<br>Thou art a widow, yet thou art a mother,<br>And hast the comfort of thy children left.<br>But [[Death|death]] hath snatched my husband from mine arms<br>And plucked two crutches from my feeble hands,<br>[[Clarence]] and Edward. O what cause have I,<br>Thine being but a moiety of my moan,<br>To overgo thy woes, and drown thy cries? |
| BOY | (to Elizabeth)<br>Ah, aunt, you wept not for our father’s [[Death|death]].<br>How can we aid you with our kindred [[Tears|tears]]? |
| DAUGHTER | (to Elizabeth)<br>Our fatherless distress was left unmoaned;<br>Your widow-dolour likewise be unwept. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Give me no help in lamentation.<br>I am not barren to bring forth complaints.<br>All springs reduce their currents to mine eyes,<br>That I, being governed by the wat’ry [[Moon|moon]],<br>May send forth plenteous [[Tears|tears]] to drown the world.<br>Ah, for my husband, for my dear Lord Edward! |
| CHILDREN | Ah, for our father, for our dear Lord [[Clarence]]! |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Alas, for both, both mine, Edward and [[Clarence]]! |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | What stay had I but Edward, and he’s gone? |
| CHILDREN | What stay had we but [[Clarence]], and he’s gone? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | What stays had I but they, and they are gone? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Was never widow had so dear a loss! |
| CHILDREN | Were never orphans had so dear a loss! |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Was never mother had so dear a loss!<br>Alas, I am the mother of these griefs.<br>Their woes are parcelled; mine is general.<br>She for an Edward weeps, and so do I;<br>I for a [[Clarence]] weep, so doth not she.<br>These babes for [[Clarence]] weep, and so do I;<br>I for an Edward weep, so do not they.<br>Alas, you three on me, threefold distressed,<br>Pour all your [[Tears|tears]]. I am your sorrow’s [[Nurse|nurse]],<br>And I will pamper it with lamentation. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Richard]] [[Duke of Gloucester]], the Duke of |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | [[Buckingham]], Lord Stanley Earl of Derby, Lord<br><br>[[Hastings]], and Sir [[Richard]] Ratcliffe |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to Elizabeth)<br>Sister, have comfort. All of us have cause<br>To wail the dimming of our shining star,<br>But none can help our harms by wailing them.—<br>Madam, my mother, I do cry you [[Mercy|mercy]].<br>I did not see your grace. Humbly on my knee<br>I crave your blessing. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | God bless thee, and put meekness in thy breast,<br>[[Love]], [[Charity|charity]], obedience, and true duty. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Amen. (Aside) ‘And make me die a good old man.’<br>That is the butt-end of a mother’s blessing;<br>I marvel that her grace did leave it out. |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | You cloudy princes and heart-sorrowing peers<br>That bear this heavy mutual load of moan,<br>Now cheer each other in each other’s [[Love|love]].<br>Though we have spent our harvest of this [[King|king]],<br>We are to reap the harvest of his son.<br>The broken rancour of your high-swoll’n hearts<br>But lately splinted, knit, and joined together,<br>Must gently be preserved, cherished, and kept.<br>Meseemeth good that, with some little train,<br>Forthwith from Ludlow the young Prince be fet<br>Hither to [[London]] to be crowned our [[King|king]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Then be it so, and go we to determine<br>Who they shall be that straight shall post to Ludlow.—<br>Madam, and you my sister, will you go<br>To give your censures in this weighty business? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | and DUCHESS OF YORK With all our hearts. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Richard]] and [[Buckingham]] |
| [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | My lord, whoever journeys to the Prince,<br>For God’s sake let not us two stay at [[Home|home]],<br>For by the way I’ll sort occasion,<br>As index to the story we late talked of,<br>To part the [[Queen]]’s proud kindred from the Prince. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | My other [[Self|self]], my counsel’s consistory,<br>My oracle, my [[Prophet|prophet]], my dear cousin!<br>I, as a [[Child|child]], will go by thy direction.<br>Towards Ludlow then, for we’ll not stay behind. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
