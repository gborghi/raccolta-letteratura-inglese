| *(didascalia)* | Enter one Citizen at one door and another at the other |
| FIRST CITIZEN | Good morrow, neighbour. Whither away so fast? |
| SECOND CITIZEN | I promise you, I scarcely know myself.<br>Hear you the news abroad? |
| FIRST CITIZEN | Yes, that the [[King]] is dead. |
| SECOND CITIZEN | Ill news, by‘r Lady; seldom comes the better.<br>I [[Fear|fear]], I [[Fear|fear]], ’twill prove a giddy world. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Citizen |
| THIRD CITIZEN | Neighbours, God [[Speed|speed]]. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | Give you good morrow, sir. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | Doth the news hold of good [[King]] Edward’s [[Death|death]]? |
| SECOND CITIZEN | Ay, sir, it is too true. God help the while. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | Then, masters, look to see a troublous world. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | No, no, by God’s good grace his son shall reign. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | Woe to that land that’s governed by a [[Child|child]]. |
| SECOND CITIZEN | In him there is a [[Hope|hope]] of [[government]],<br>Which in his nonage council under him,<br>And in his full and ripened years himself,<br>No [[Doubt|doubt]] shall then, and till then, govern [[Well|well]]. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | So stood the state when Henry the Sixth<br>Was crowned in [[Paris]] but at nine months old. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | Stood the state so? No, no, good friends, God wot.<br>For then this land was famously enriched<br>With politic, [[Grave|grave]] counsel; then the [[King]]<br>Had virtuous uncles to protect his grace. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | Why, so hath this, both by his father and mother. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | Better it were they all came by his father,<br>Or by his father there were none at all.<br>For emulation who shall now be near’st<br>Will touch us all too near, if God prevent not.<br>O full of danger is the [[Duke of Gloucester]],<br>And the [[Queen]]’s sons and brothers haught and proud.<br>And were they to be ruled, and not to rule,<br>This sickly land might solace as before. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | Come, come, we [[Fear|fear]] the worst. All will be [[Well|well]]. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | When clouds are seen, wise men put on their cloaks;<br>When great [[Leaves|leaves]] fall, then [[Winter|winter]] is at hand;<br>When the sun sets, who doth not look for [[Night|night]]?<br>Untimely storms make men expect a dearth.<br>All may be [[Well|well]], but if God sort it so<br>’Tis more than we deserve, or I expect. |
| SECOND CITIZEN | Truly the hearts of men are full of [[Fear|fear]].<br>You cannot reason almost with a man<br>That looks not heavily and full of dread. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | Before the days of change still is it so.<br>By a divine instinct men’s minds mistrust<br>Ensuing danger, as by proof we see<br>The [[Water|water]] swell before a boist’rous [[Storm|storm]].<br>But leave it all to God. Whither away? |
| SECOND CITIZEN | Marry, we were sent for to the justices. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | And so was I. I’ll bear you company. Exeunt |
