| *(didascalia)* | A table brought in. Enter [[King]] [[Richard]], Sir [[Richard]] Ratcliffe, the Duke of Norfolk, Sir William Catesby, and others |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | What is’t o’[[Clock|clock]]? |
| CATESBY | It’s supper-[[Time|time]], my lord. It’s nine o’[[Clock|clock]]. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | I will not sup tonight. Give me some ink and paper.<br>What, is my beaver easier than it was?<br>And all my armour laid into my tent? |
| CATESBY | It is, my liege, and all things are in readiness. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Good Norfolk, hie thee to thy charge.<br>Use careful watch; choose trusty sentinels. |
| NORFOLK | I go, my lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Stir with the lark tomorrow, gentle Norfolk. |
| NORFOLK | I warrant you, my lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Catesby. |
| CATESBY | My lord? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Send out a pursuivant-at-arms<br>To Stanley’s regiment. Bid him bring his power<br>Before sun-rising, lest his son George fall<br>Into the blind [[Cave|cave]] of eternal [[Night|night]].<br>Fill me a bowl of wine. Give me a watch.<br>Saddle white Surrey for the field tomorrow.<br>Look that my staves be sound, and not too heavy.<br>Ratcliffe. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Catesby |
| RATCLIFFE | My lord? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Saw’st thou the [[Melancholy|melancholy]] Lord Northumberland? |
| RATCLIFFE | Thomas the [[Earl of Surrey]] and himself,<br>Much about cockshut [[Time|time]], from troop to troop<br>Went through the army, cheering up the soldiers. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | So, I am satisfied. Give me some wine.<br>I have not that alacrity of spirit,<br>Nor cheer of mind, that I was wont to have.<br>The wine is brought<br>Set it down. Is ink and paper ready? |
| RATCLIFFE | It is, my lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Leave me. Bid my guard watch.<br>About the mid of [[Night|night]] come to my tent,<br>Ratcliffe, and help to arm me. Leave me, I say. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Ratcliffe with others. [[Richard]] writes, and later sleeps |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord Stanley Earl of Derby to Henry [[Earl of Richmond]] and the lords in his tent |
| STANLEY | Fortune and victory sit on thy helm! |
| HENRY [[Earl of Richmond|EARL OF RICHMOND]] | All comfort that the dark [[Night|night]] can afford<br>Be to thy person, noble father-in-law.<br>Tell me, how fares our loving mother? |
| STANLEY | I, by attorney, bless thee from thy mother,<br>Who prays continually for Richmond’s good.<br>So much for that. The silent hours steal on,<br>And flaky darkness breaks within the east.<br>In brief—for so the season bids us be—<br>Prepare thy battle early in the morning,<br>And put thy fortune to th’arbitrement<br>Of bloody strokes and mortal-sharing war.<br>I, as I may—that which I would, I cannot—<br>With best advantage will deceive the [[Time|time]],<br>And aid thee in this doubtful shock of arms.<br>But on thy side I may not be too forward—<br>Lest, being seen, thy brother, tender George,<br>Be executed in his father’s sight.<br>Farewell. The leisure and the fearful [[Time|time]]<br>Cuts off the ceremonious vows of [[Love|love]]<br>And ample interchange of sweet discourse,<br>Which so long sundered friends should dwell upon.<br>God give us leisure for these rights of [[Love|love]].<br>Once more, adieu. Be valiant, and [[Speed|speed]] [[Well|well]]. |
| HENRY [[Earl of Richmond|EARL OF RICHMOND]] | Good lords, conduct him to his regiment.<br>I’ll strive with troubled thoughts to take a nap,<br>Lest leaden slumber peise me down tomorrow,<br>When I should mount with wings of victory.<br>Once more, good [[Night|night]], kind lords and gentlemen. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Stanley and the lords |
| *(didascalia)* | Richmond kneels |
| HENRY [[Earl of Richmond|EARL OF RICHMOND]] | O thou, whose captain I account myself,<br>Look on my forces with a gracious eye.<br>Put in their hands thy bruising irons of wrath,<br>That they may crush down with a heavy fall<br>Th’usurping helmets of our adversaries.<br>Make us thy ministers of chastisement,<br>That we may praise thee in the victory.<br>To thee I do commend my watchful [[Soul|soul]],<br>Ere I let fall the windows of mine eyes.<br>Sleeping and waking, O defend me still! He sleeps |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Ghost]] of young Prince Edward above |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF PRINCE EDWARD | (to [[Richard]])<br>Let me sit heavy on thy [[Soul|soul]] tomorrow,<br>Prince Edward, son to Henry the Sixth.<br>Think how thou stabbedst me in my prime of youth<br>At Tewkesbury. [[Despair]], therefore, and die.<br>(To Richmond) Be cheerful, Richmond, for the wronged<br>souls<br>Of butchered princes fight in thy behalf.<br>[[King]] Henry’s issue, Richmond, comforts thee. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter above the [[Ghost]] of [[King]] Henry the Sixth |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to [[Richard]])<br>When I was mortal, my anointed [[Body|body]]<br>By thee was punched full of deadly holes.<br>Think on the Tower and me. [[Despair]] and die.<br>Harry the Sixth bids thee [[Despair|despair]] and die.<br>(To Richmond) Virtuous and holy, be thou conqueror.<br>Harry that prophesied thou shouldst be [[King|king]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Comforts thee in thy [[Sleep|sleep]]. Live and flourish! Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter above the [[Ghost]] of George Duke of [[Clarence]] |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | (to [[Richard]])<br>Let me sit heavy on thy [[Soul|soul]] tomorrow,<br>I that was washed to [[Death|death]] with fulsome wine,<br>Poor [[Clarence]], by thy guile betrayed to [[Death|death]].<br>Tomorrow in the battle think on me,<br>And fall thy edgeless [[Sword|sword]]. [[Despair]] and die.<br>(To Richmond) Thou offspring of the [[house]] of<br>Lancaster,<br>The wronged heirs of York do pray for thee.<br>Good angels guard thy battle. Live and flourish! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter above the [[Ghosts]] of Lord Rivers, Lord Gray, and Sir Thomas Vaughan |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF RIVERS | (to [[Richard]])<br>Let me sit heavy on thy [[Soul|soul]] tomorrow,<br>Rivers that died at Pomfret. [[Despair]] and die. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF GRAY | (to [[Richard]])<br>Think upon Gray, and let thy [[Soul|soul]] [[Despair|despair]]. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF VAUGHAN | (to [[Richard]])<br>Think upon Vaughan, and with guilty [[Fear|fear]]<br>Let fall thy pointless lance. [[Despair]] and die. |
| ALL THREE | (to Richmond)<br>Awake, and think our wrongs in [[Richard]]’s bosom<br>Will conquer him. Awake, and win the day! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Ghosts]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter above the [[Ghosts]] of the two young Princes |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Ghosts|GHOSTS]] OF THE PRINCES (to [[Richard]]) |
| ALL THREE | Dream on thy cousins, smothered in the Tower.<br>Let us be lead within thy bosom, [[Richard]],<br>And weigh thee down to ruin, shame, and [[Death|death]].<br>Thy nephews’ souls bid thee [[Despair|despair]] and die.<br>(To Richmond) [[Sleep]], Richmond, [[Sleep|sleep]] in peace and wake in joy.<br>Good angels guard thee from the boar’s annoy.<br>Live, and beget a happy race of kings!<br>Edward’s unhappy sons do bid thee flourish. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Ghosts]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter above the [[Ghost]] of Lord [[Hastings]] |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF [[Hastings|HASTINGS]] | (to [[Richard]])<br>Bloody and guilty, guiltily awake,<br>And in a bloody battle end thy days.<br>Think on Lord [[Hastings]], then [[Despair|despair]] and die.<br>(To Richmond) Quiet, untroubled [[Soul|soul]], awake, awake!<br>Arm, fight, and conquer for fair England’s sake. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter above the [[Ghost]] of [[Lady Anne]] |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | (to [[Richard]])<br>[[Richard]], thy wife, that wretched Anne thy wife,<br>That never slept a quiet hour with thee,<br>Now fills thy [[Sleep|sleep]] with perturbations.<br>Tomorrow in the battle think on me,<br>And fall thy edgeless [[Sword|sword]]. [[Despair]] and die.<br>(To Richmond) Thou quiet [[Soul|soul]], [[Sleep|sleep]] thou a quiet [[Sleep|sleep]].<br>Dream of [[success]] and happy victory. |
| *(didascalia)* | Thy adversary’s wife doth pray for thee. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter above the [[Ghost]] of the [[Duke of Buckingham]] |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | (to [[Richard]])<br>The first was I that helped thee to the [[Crown|crown]];<br>The last was I that felt thy tyranny.<br>O in the battle think on [[Buckingham]],<br>And die in terror of thy guiltiness!<br>Dream on, dream on, of bloody deeds and [[Death|death]];<br>Fainting, [[Despair|despair]]; despairing, yield thy breath.<br>(To Richmond) I died for [[Hope|hope]] ere I could lend thee aid.<br>But cheer thy heart, and be thou not dismayed.<br>God and good angels fight on Richmond’s side, |
| *(didascalia)* | And [[Richard]] falls in height of all his [[Pride|pride]]. Exit |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] OF [[Buckingham|BUCKINGHAM]] | [[Richard]] starteth up out of [[A Dream|a dream]] |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Give me another [[Horse|horse]]! Bind up my wounds!<br>Have [[Mercy|mercy]], Jesu!—Soft, I did but dream.<br>O coward [[Conscience|conscience]], how dost thou afflict me?<br>The lights burn blue. It is now dead midnight.<br>Cold fearful drops stand on my trembling flesh.<br>What do I [[Fear]]? Myself? There’s none else by.<br>[[Richard]] loves [[Richard]]; that is, I am I.<br>Is there a murderer here? No. Yes, I am.<br>Then fly! What, from myself? Great reason. Why?<br>Lest I [[Revenge|revenge]]. Myself upon myself?<br>Alack, I [[Love|love]] myself. Wherefore? For any good<br>That I myself have done unto myself?<br>O no, alas, I rather hate myself<br>For hateful deeds committed by myself.<br>I am a [[Villain|villain]]. Yet I lie: I am not.<br>[[Fool]], of thyself speak [[Well|well]].—[[Fool]], do not flatter.<br>My [[Conscience|conscience]] hath a thousand several tongues,<br>And every tongue brings in a several tale,<br>And every tale condemns me for a [[Villain|villain]].<br>Perjury, perjury, in the high‘st degree!<br>Murder, stern murder, in the dir’st degree!<br>All several sins, all used in each degree,<br>Throng to the bar, crying all, ‘Guilty, guilty!’<br>I shall [[Despair|despair]]. There is no creature loves me,<br>And if I die no [[Soul|soul]] will pity me.<br>Nay, wherefore should they?—Since that I myself<br>Find in myself no pity to myself.<br>Methought the souls of all that I had murdered<br>Came to my tent, and every one did threat<br>Tomorrow’s vengeance on the head of [[Richard]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Ratcliffe |
| RATCLIFFE | My lord? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | ‘Swounds, who is there? |
| RATCLIFFE | My lord, ’tis I. The early village cock<br>Hath twice done salutation to the morn.<br>Your friends are up, and buckle on their armour. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | O Ratcliffe, I have dreamed a fearful dream.<br>What thinkest thou, will all our friends prove true? |
| RATCLIFFE | No [[Doubt|doubt]], my lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ratcliffe, I [[Fear|fear]], I [[Fear|fear]]. |
| RATCLIFFE | Nay, good my lord, be not afraid of shadows. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | By the Apostle Paul, shadows tonight<br>Have struck more terror to the [[Soul|soul]] of [[Richard]]<br>Than can the substance of ten thousand soldiers<br>Armed in proof and led by shallow Richmond.<br>’Tis not yet near day. Come, go with me.<br>Under our tents I’ll play the eavesdropper,<br>To see if any mean to shrink from me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Richard]] and Ratcliffe |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the lords to Henry [[Earl of Richmond]], sitting in his tent |
| *(didascalia)* | LORDS Good morrow, Richmond. |
| HENRY [[Earl of Richmond|EARL OF RICHMOND]] | Cry [[Mercy|mercy]], lords and watchful gentlemen,<br>That you have ta’en a tardy sluggard here. |
| *(didascalia)* | A LORDHow have you slept, my lord? |
| HENRY [[Earl of Richmond|EARL OF RICHMOND]] | The sweetest [[Sleep|sleep]] and fairest boding [[Dreams|dreams]]<br>That ever entered in a drowsy head<br>Have I since your departure had, my lords.<br>Methought their souls whose bodies [[Richard]] murdered<br>Came to my tent and cried on victory.<br>I promise you, my [[Soul|soul]] is very jocund<br>In the remembrance of so fair [[A Dream|a dream]].<br>How far into the morning is it, lords?<br>A LORD Upon the stroke of four. |
| HENRY [[Earl of Richmond|EARL OF RICHMOND]] | Why then, ’tis [[Time|time]] to arm, and give direction.<br>His oration to his soldiers<br>Much that I could say, loving countrymen,<br>The leisure and enforcement of the [[Time|time]]<br>Forbids to dwell on. Yet remember this:<br>God and our good cause fight upon our side.<br>The prayers of holy saints and wrongèd souls,<br>Like high-reared bulwarks, stand before our forces.<br>[[Richard]] except, those whom we fight against<br>Had rather have us win than him they follow.<br>For what is he they follow? Truly, friends,<br>A bloody [[Tyrant|tyrant]] and a homicide;<br>One raised in [[Blood|blood]], and one in [[Blood|blood]] established;<br>One that made means to come by what he hath,<br>And slaughtered those that were the means to help him;<br>A base, foul stone, made precious by the foil<br>Of England’s chair, where he is falsely set;<br>One that hath ever been God’s enemy.<br>Then if you fight against God’s enemy,<br>God will, in justice, ward you as his soldiers.<br>If you do sweat to put a [[Tyrant|tyrant]] down,<br>You [[Sleep|sleep]] in peace, the [[Tyrant|tyrant]] being slain.<br>If you do fight against your country’s foes,<br>Your country’s foison pays your pains the hire.<br>If you do fight in safeguard of your wives,<br>Your wives shall welcome [[Home|home]] the conquerors.<br>If you do free your children from the [[Sword|sword]],<br>Your children’s children quites it in your age.<br>Then, in the name of God and all these rights,<br>Advance your standards! Draw your willing swords!<br>For me, the ransom of this bold attempt<br>Shall be my cold corpse on the earth’s cold face;<br>But if I thrive, to gain of my attempt,<br>The least of you shall share his part thereof. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound, drums and trumpets, bold and cheerfully! |
| HENRY [[Earl of Richmond|EARL OF RICHMOND]] | God and Saint George! Richmond and victory! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt to the sound of drums and trumpets |
