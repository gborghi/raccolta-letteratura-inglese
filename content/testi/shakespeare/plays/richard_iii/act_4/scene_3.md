| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir James Tyrrell |
| TYRRELL | The tyrannous and bloody act is done—<br>The most arch deed of piteous massacre<br>That ever yet this land was guilty of.<br>Dighton and Forrest, whom I did suborn<br>To do this piece of ruthless butchery,<br>Albeit they were fleshed villains, bloody [[dogs]],<br>Melted with tenderness and mild compassion,<br>Wept like two children in their deaths’ sad story.<br>‘O thus’, quoth Dighton, ‘lay the gentle babes’;<br>‘Thus, thus’, quoth Forrest, ‘girdling one another |
| *(didascalia)* | Within their alabaster [[Innocent|innocent]] arms. |
| TYRRELL | Their lips were four red roses on a stalk,<br>And in their [[Summer|summer]] [[Beauty|beauty]] kissed each other.<br>A book of prayers on their pillow lay,<br>Which once’, quoth Forrest, ‘almost changed my mind.<br>But O, the devil’—there the [[Villain|villain]] stopped,<br>When Dighton thus told on, ‘We smothered<br>The most replenishèd sweet work of [[Nature|nature]],<br>That from the prime creation e’er she framed.’<br>Hence both are gone, with [[Conscience|conscience]] and remorse.<br>They could not speak, and so I left them both,<br>To bear this tidings to the bloody [[King|king]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] [[Richard]] |
| TYRRELL | And here he comes.—AH health, my sovereign lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Kind Tyrrell, am I happy in thy news? |
| TYRRELL | If to have done the thing you gave in charge<br>Beget your happiness, be happy then,<br>For it is done. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | But didst thou see them dead? |
| TYRRELL | I did, my lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | And buried, gentle Tyrrell? |
| TYRRELL | The chaplain of the Tower hath buried them;<br>But where, to say the [[Truth|truth]], I do not know. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Come to me, Tyrrell, soon, at after-supper,<br>When thou shalt tell the process of their [[Death|death]].<br>Meantime, but think how I may do thee good,<br>And be inheritor of thy [[Desire|desire]].<br>Farewell till then. |
| TYRRELL | I humbly take my leave. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | The son of [[Clarence]] have I pent up close.<br>His daughter meanly have I matched in [[Marriage|marriage]].<br>The sons of Edward [[Sleep|sleep]] in Abraham’s bosom,<br>And Anne, my wife, hath bid this world goodnight.<br>Now, for I know the Breton Richmond aims<br>At young Elizabeth, my brother’s daughter,<br>And by that knot looks proudly o’er the [[Crown|crown]],<br>To her go I, a jolly thriving wooer— |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir [[Richard]] Ratcliffe, running |
| RATCLIFFE | My lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Good news or bad, that thou com’st in so bluntly? |
| RATCLIFFE | Bad news, my lord. Ely is fled to Richmond,<br>And [[Buckingham]], backed with the hardy Welshmen,<br>Is in the field, and still his power increaseth. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ely with Richmond troubles me more near<br>Than [[Buckingham]]. and his rash-levied strength.<br>Come, I have learned that fearful commenting<br>Is leaden servitor to dull delay.<br>Delay leads impotent and snail-paced beggary.<br>Then fiery expedition be my wing:<br>Jove’s Mercury, an herald for a [[King|king]].<br>Go, muster men. My counsel is my shield.<br>We must be brief, when traitors brave the field. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
