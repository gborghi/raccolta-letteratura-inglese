| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | It is the cause, it is the cause, my [[Soul|soul]]. Let me not name it to you, you chaste [[Stars|stars]]. It is the cause. Yet I’ll not shed her [[Blood|blood]], Nor scar that whiter skin of hers than [[Snow|snow]], And smooth as monumental alabaster. Yet she must die, else she’ll betray more men. Put out the light, and then put out the light. If I quench thee, thou flaming minister, I can again thy former light restore Should I repent me; but once put out thy light, Thou cunning‘st pattern of excelling [[Nature|nature]], I know not where is that Promethean heat That can thy light relume. When I have plucked thy [[Rose|rose]] I cannot give it vital growth again. It needs must wither. I’ll smell thee on the tree. [He kisses her] O balmy breath, that dost almost persuade Justice to break her [[Sword|sword]]! One more, one more. Be thus when thou art dead, and I will kill thee And [[Love|love]] thee after. One more, and that’s the last. He kisses her So sweet was ne’er so fatal. I must weep, But they are cruel [[Tears|tears]]. This sorrow’s heavenly, It strikes where it doth [[Love|love]]. She wakes. |
