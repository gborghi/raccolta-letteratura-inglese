| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | My husband? |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Ay, ’twas he that told me on her first. An honest man he is, and hates the slime That sticks on filthy deeds. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | My husband? |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | What needs this iterance? Woman, I say thy husband. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | O mistress, villainy hath made mocks with [[Love|love]]. My husband say she was false? |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | He, woman. I say thy husband. Dost understand the word? My friend, thy husband, honest, honest [[Iago]]. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | If he say so, may his pernicious [[Soul|soul]] Rot half a grain a day. He lies to th’ heart. She was too fond of her most filthy bargain. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Ha? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Do thy worst. This deed of thine is no more worthy heaven Than thou wast worthy her. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Peace, you were best. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Thou hast not half that power to do me harm As I have to be hurt. O gull, O dolt, As ignorant as dirt! Thou hast done a deed— I care not for thy [[Sword|sword]], I’ll make thee known Though I lost twenty lives. Help, help, hot Help! The Moor hath killed my mistress. Murder, murder! Enter Montano, Graziano, and [[Iago]] |
| MONTANO | What is the matter? How now, general? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | O, are you come, [[Iago]]? You have done [[Well|well]], That men must lay their murders on your neck. |
| GRAZIANO | What is the matter? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to [[Iago]]) Disprove this [[Villain|villain]] if thou beest a man. He says thou told‘st him that his wife was false. I know thou didst not. Thou’rt not such a [[Villain|villain]]. Speak, for my heart is full. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | I told him what I thought, and told no more Than what he found himself was apt and true. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | But did you ever tell him she was false? |
| IACO | I did. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | You told a lie, an odious, damnèd lie, Upon my [[Soul|soul]] a lie, a wicked lie. She false with [[Cassio]]? Did you say with [[Cassio]]? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | With [[Cassio]], mistress. Go to, charm your tongue. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | I will not charm my tongue. I am bound to speak. My mistress here lies murdered in her bed. MONTANO and GRAZIANO O heavens forfend! |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to [[Iago]]) And your reports have set the murder on. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Nay, stare not, masters. It is true indeed. |
| GRAZIANO | ’Tis a strange [[Truth|truth]]. |
| MONTANO | O monstrous act! |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Villainy, villainy, villainy! I think upon’t, I think. I smell’t. O villainy! I thought so then. I’ll kill myself for grief. O villainy, villainy! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | What, are you mad? I charge you get you [[Home|home]]. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Good gentlemen, let me have leave to speak. ‘Tis proper I obey him, but not now. Perchance, [[Iago]], I will ne’er go [[Home|home]]. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | O, O, O! [[Othello]] falls on the bed |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Nay, lay thee down and roar, For thou hast killed the sweetest [[Innocent|innocent]] That e’er did lift up eye. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | rising  O, she was foul! (To Graziano) I scarce did know you, uncle. There lies your niece, Whose breath indeed these hands have newly stopped. I know this act shows horrible and grim |
| GRAZIANO | Poor Desdemon, I am glad thy father’s dead. Thy match was mortal to him, and pure grief Shore his old thread in twain. Did he live now This sight would make him do a desperate turn, Yea, curse his better angel from his side, And fall to reprobance. |
