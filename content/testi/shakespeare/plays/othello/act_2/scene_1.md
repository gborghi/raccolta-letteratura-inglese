| *(didascalia)* | Enter below Montano, Governor of Cyprus; two other gentlemen above |
| MONTANO | What from the cape can you discern at sea? |
| FIRST GENTLEMAN | Nothing at all. It is a high-wrought flood.<br>I cannot ’twixt the heaven and the main<br>Descry a sail. |
| MONTANO | Methinks the [[Wind|wind]] hath spoke aloud at land.<br>A fuller blast ne’er shook our battlements.<br>If it ha’ ruffianed so upon the sea,<br>What ribs of oak, when [[Mountains|mountains]] melt on them,<br>Can hold the mortise? What shall we hear of this? |
| SECOND GENTLEMAN | A segregation of the Turkish fleet;<br>For do but stand upon the foaming shore,<br>The chidden billow seems to pelt the clouds,<br>The [[Wind|wind]]-shaked surge with high and monstrous mane<br>Seems to cast [[Water|water]] on the burning Bear<br>And quench the guards of th’ever-fixèd Pole.<br>I never did like molestation view<br>On the enchafèd flood. |
| MONTANO | If that the Turkish fleet<br>Be not ensheltered and embayed, they are drowned.<br>It is impossible to bear it out. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a third Gentleman |
| THIRD GENTLEMAN | News, lads! Our wars are done.<br>The desperate tempest hath so banged the Turks<br>That their designment halts. A noble [[Ship|ship]] of [[Venice]]<br>Hath seen a grievous wrack and sufferance<br>On most part of their fleet. |
| MONTANO | How, is this true? |
| THIRD GENTLEMAN | The [[Ship|ship]] is here put in,<br>A Veronessa. [[Michael]] [[Cassio]],<br>Lieutenant to the warlike Moor [[Othello]],<br>Is come on shore; the Moor himself at sea,<br>And is in full commission here for Cyprus. |
| MONTANO | I am glad on’t; ’tis a worthy governor. |
| THIRD GENTLEMAN | But this same [[Cassio]], though he speak of comfort<br>Touching the Turkish loss, yet he looks sadly,<br>And prays the Moor be safe, for they were parted<br>With foul and violent tempest. |
| MONTANO | Pray heavens he be,<br>For I have served him, and the man commands<br>Like a full soldier. Let’s to the sea-side, ho!—<br>As [[Well|well]] to see the vessel that’s come in<br>As to throw out our eyes for brave [[Othello]],<br>Even till we make the main and th’aerial blue<br>An indistinct regard. |
| THIRD GENTLEMAN | Come, let’s do so,<br>For every minute is expectancy<br>Of more arrivance. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Cassio]] |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Thanks, you the valiant of this warlike isle<br>That so approve the Moor! O, let the heavens<br>Give him defence against the elements,<br>For I have lost him On a dangerous sea. |
| MONTANO | Is he [[Well|well]] shipped? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | His barque is stoutly timbered, and his pilot<br>Of very expert and approved allowance.<br>Therefore my hopes, not surfeited to [[Death|death]],<br>Stand in bold cure. |
| VOICES | (within)<br>A sail, a sail, a sail! |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | What noise?<br>A GENTLEMAN<br>The town is empty. On the brow o‘th’ sea<br>Stand ranks of people, and they cry ‘A sail!’ |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | My hopes do shape him for the governor.<br>A shot<br>A GENTLEMAN<br>They do discharge their shot of courtesy—<br>Our friends, at least. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I pray you, sir, go forth,<br>And give us [[Truth|truth]] who ’tis that is arrived.<br>A GENTLEMAN I shall. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| MONTANO | But, good lieutenant, is your general wived? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Most fortunately. He hath achieved a maid<br>That paragons description and wild fame,<br>One that excels the quirks of blazoning pens,<br>And in th’essential vesture of creation<br>Does tire the engineer. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Gentleman |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | How now, who has put in? |
| GENTLEMAN | ’Tis one [[Iago]], ensign to the general. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | He’s had most favourable and happy [[Speed|speed]].<br>Tempests themselves, high seas, and howling winds,<br>The guttered rocks and congregated sands,<br>Traitors ensteeped to enclog the guiltless keel,<br>As having sense of [[Beauty|beauty]] do omit<br>Their mortal natures, letting go safely by<br>The divine [[Desdemona]]. |
| MONTANO | What is she? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | She that I spake of, our great captain’s captain,<br>Left in the conduct of the bold [[Iago]],<br>Whose footing here anticipates our thoughts<br>A sennight’s [[Speed|speed]]. Great Jove, [[Othello]] guard,<br>And swell his sail with thine own powerful breath,<br>That he may bless this bay with his tall [[Ship|ship]],<br>Make [[Love|love]]’s quick pants in [[Desdemona]]’s arms,<br>Give renewed [[Fire|fire]] to our extincted spirits,<br>And bring all Cyprus comfort. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Desdemona]], [[Iago]], [[Emilia]], and [[Roderigo]] |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | O, behold,<br>The riches of the [[Ship|ship]] is come on shore!<br>You men of Cyprus, let her have your knees.<br>Montano and the Gentlemen make curtsy to [[Desdemona]]<br>Hail to thee, lady, and the grace of heaven<br>Before, behind thee, and on every hand<br>Enwheel thee round! |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | I thank you, valiant [[Cassio]].<br>What tidings can you tell me of my lord? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | He is not yet arrived, nor know I aught<br>But that he’s [[Well|well]] and will be shortly here. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | O, but I [[Fear|fear]]—how lost you company? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | The great contention of the sea and skies<br>Parted our fellowship. |
| VOICES | (within) A sail, a sail! |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | But hark, a sail.<br>A shot<br>A GENTLEMAN<br>They give their greeting to the citadel.<br>This likewise is a friend. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | See for the news. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Gentleman |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Good ensign, you are welcome. (Kissing [[Emilia]])<br>Welcome, mistress.<br>Let it not gall your patience, good [[Iago]],<br>That I extend my manners. ’Tis my breeding<br>That gives me this bold show of courtesy. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Sir, would she give you so much of her lips<br>As of her tongue she oft bestows on me,<br>You would have enough. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | Alas, she has no speech! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | In [[Faith|faith]], too much.<br>I find it still when I ha’ leave to [[Sleep|sleep]].<br>Marry, before your ladyship, I grant,<br>She puts her tongue a little in her heart,<br>And chides with thinking. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | You ha’ little cause to say so. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Come on, come on. You are pictures out of door,<br>[[Bells]] in your parlours; wildcats in your kitchens,<br>Saints in your injuries; devils being offended,<br>Players in your housewifery, and hussies in your beds. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | O, fie upon thee, slanderer! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Nay, it is true, or else I am a Turk.<br>You rise to play and go to bed to work. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | You shall not write my praise. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | No, let me not. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | What wouldst write of me, if thou shouldst praise me? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | O, gentle lady, do not put me to’t,<br>For I am nothing if not critical. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | Come on, [[Essay|essay]]—there’s one gone to the harbour? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Ay, madam. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | I am not merry, but I do beguile<br>The thing I am by seeming otherwise.<br>Come, how wouldst thou praise me? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | I am about it, but indeed my invention<br>Comes from my pate as birdlime does from frieze—<br>It plucks out brains and all. But my muse labours,<br>And thus she is delivered:<br>If she be fair and wise, fairness and wit,<br>The one’s for use, the other useth it. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | [[Well]] praised! How if she be black and witty? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | If she be black and thereto have a wit,<br>She’ll find a white that shall her blackness fit. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | Worse and worse. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | How if fair and foolish? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | She never yet was foolish that was fair,<br>For even her folly helped her to an heir. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | These are old fond paradoxes, to make fools laugh i’th’ alehouse.<br>What miserable praise hast thou for her<br>That’s foul and foolish? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | There’s none so foul and foolish thereunto,<br>But does foul pranks which fair and wise ones do. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | O heavy ignorance! Thou praisest the worst best. But what praise couldst thou bestow on a deserving woman indeed—one that, in the authority of her merit, did justly put on the vouch of very malice itself? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | She that was ever fair and never proud,<br>Had tongue at will and yet was never loud,<br>Never lacked gold and yet went never gay,<br>Fled from her wish, and yet said ‘Now I may’;<br>She that, being angered, her [[Revenge|revenge]] being nigh,<br>Bade her wrong stay and her displeasure fly;<br>She that in wisdom never was so frail<br>To change the cod’s head for the salmon’s tail;<br>She that could think and ne’er disclose her mind,<br>See suitors following, and not look behind—<br>She was a wight, if ever such wights were— |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | To do what? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | To suckle fools, and chronicle small beer. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | O most lame and impotent conclusion! Do not learn of him, [[Emilia]], though he be thy husband. How say you, [[Cassio]], is he not a most profane and liberal counsellor? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | He speaks [[Home|home]], madam. You may relish him more in the soldier than in the scholar.<br>[[Cassio]] and [[Desdemona]] talk apart |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | (aside) He takes her by the palm. Ay, [[Well|well]] said—whisper. With as little a web as this will I ensnare as great a fly as [[Cassio]]. Ay, smile upon her, do. I will gyve thee in thine own courtship. You say true, ‘tis so indeed. If such tricks as these strip you out of your lieutenantry, it had been better you had not kissed your three fingers so oft, which now again you are most apt to play the sir in. Very good, [[Well|well]] kissed, an excellent curtsy, ’tis so indeed; yet again your fingers to your lips? Would they were clyster-pipes for your sake. |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets within |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | (Aloud) The Moor—I know his trumpet. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | ’Tis truly so. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | Let’s meet him and receive him. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Lo where he comes! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Othello]] and attendants |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | (to [[Desdemona]])<br>O my fair warrior! |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | My dear [[Othello]]. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | It gives me [[Wonder|wonder]] great as my content<br>To see you here before me. O my [[Soul|soul]]’s joy,<br>If after every tempest come such calms,<br>May the winds blow till they have wakened [[Death|death]],<br>And let the labouring barque climb hills of seas<br>Olympus-high, and duck again as low<br>As hell’s from heaven. If it were now to die<br>’Twere now to be most happy, for I [[Fear|fear]]<br>My [[Soul|soul]] hath her content so absolute<br>That not another comfort like to this<br>Succeeds in unknown fate. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | The heavens forbid<br>But that our loves and comforts should increase<br>Even as our days do grow. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Amen to that, sweet powers!<br>I cannot speak enough of this content.<br>It stops me here, it is too much of joy.<br>And this, (they kiss) and this, the greatest discords be<br>That e’er our hearts shall make. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | (aside) O, you are [[Well|well]] tuned now,<br>But I’ll set down the pegs that make this music,<br>As honest as I am. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Come, let us to the [[Castle|castle]].<br>News, friends: our wars are done, the Turks are<br>drowned.<br>How does my old acquaintance of this isle?—<br>Honey, you shall be [[Well|well]] desired in Cyprus,<br>I have found great [[Love|love]] amongst them. O my sweet,<br>I prattle out of fashion, and I dote<br>In mine own comforts. I prithee, good [[Iago]],<br>Go to the bay and disembark my coffers.<br>Bring thou the master to the citadel.<br>He is a good one, and his worthiness<br>Does challenge much respect. Come, [[Desdemona]].—<br>Once more, [[Well|well]] met at Cyprus! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Othello]] and [[Desdemona]] with all but lago and [[Roderigo]] |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | (to an attendant as he goes out) Do thou meet me presently at the harbour. (To [[Roderigo]]) Come hither. If thou beest valiant—as they say base men being in [[Love|love]] have then a nobility in their natures more than is native to them—list me. The lieutenant tonight watches on the [[Court|court]] of guard. First, I must tell thee this: [[Desdemona]] is directly in [[Love|love]] with him. |
| RODERICO | With him? Why, ’tis not possible! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Lay thy finger thus, and let thy [[Soul|soul]] be instructed. Mark me with what violence she first loved the Moor, but for bragging and telling her fantastical lies. To [[Love|love]] him still for prating?—let not thy discreet heart think it. Her eye must be fed, and what delight shall she have to look on the devil? When the [[Blood|blood]] is made dull with the act of sport, there should be again to inflame it, and to give satiety a fresh appetite, loveliness in favour, sympathy in years, manners, and beauties, all which the Moor is defective in. Now, for want of these required conveniences, her delicate tenderness will find itself abused, begin to heave the gorge, disrelish and abhor the Moor. Very [[Nature|nature]] will instruct her in it and compel her to some second choice. Now, sir, this granted—as it is a most pregnant and unforced position—who stands so eminent in the degree of this fortune as [[Cassio]] does?—a knave very voluble, no further conscionable than in putting on the mere form of civil and humane seeming for the better compass of his salt and most hidden loose affection. Why, none; why, none—a slipper and subtle knave, a finder of occasion, that has an eye can stamp and counterfeit advantages, though true advantage never present itself, a devilish knave! Besides, the knave is handsome, young, and hath all those requisites in him that folly and green minds look after. A pestilent complete knave, and the woman hath found him already. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | I cannot believe that in her. She’s full of most blessed condition. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Blessed fig’s end! The wine she drinks is made of grapes. If she had been blessed, she would never have loved the Moor. Blessed pudding! Didst thou not see her paddle with the palm of his hand? Didst not mark that? 255 |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Yes, that I did, but that was but courtesy. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Lechery, by this hand; an index and obscure prologue to the [[history]] of lust and foul thoughts. They met so near with their lips that their breaths embraced together. Villainous thoughts, [[Roderigo]]! When these mutualities so marshal the way, hard at hand comes the master and main exercise, th’incorporate conclusion. Pish! But, sir, be you ruled by me. I have brought you from [[Venice]]. Watch you tonight. For the command, I’ll lay’t upon you. [[Cassio]] knows you not; I’ll not be far from you. Do you find some occasion to anger [[Cassio]], either by speaking too loud, or tainting his discipline, or from what other course you please, which the [[Time|time]] shall more favourably minister. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | [[Well]]. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Sir, he’s rash and very sudden in choler, and haply may strike at you. Provoke him that he may, for even out of that will I cause these of Cyprus to mutiny, whose qualification shall come into no true taste again but by the displanting of [[Cassio]]. So shall you have a shorter [[Journey|journey]] to your desires by the means I shall then have to prefer them, and the impediment most profitably removed, without the which there were no expectation of our prosperity. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | I will do this, if you can bring it to any opportunity. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | I warrant thee. Meet me by and by at the citadel. I must fetch his necessaries ashore. Farewell. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Adieu. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | That [[Cassio]] loves her, I do [[Well|well]] believe it.<br>That she loves him, ‘tis apt and of great credit.<br>The Moor—howbe’t that I endure him not—<br>Is of a constant, loving, noble [[Nature|nature]],<br>And I dare think he’ll prove to [[Desdemona]]<br>A most dear husband. Now I do [[Love|love]] her too,<br>Not out of absolute lust—though peradventure<br>I stand accountant for as great a sin—<br>But partly led to diet my [[Revenge|revenge]]<br>For that I do suspect the lusty Moor<br>Hath leapt into my seat, the thought whereof<br>Doth, like a poisonous mineral, gnaw my inwards;<br>And nothing can or shall content my [[Soul|soul]]<br>Till I am evened with him, wife for wife—<br>Or failing so, yet that I put the Moor<br>At least into a [[Jealousy|jealousy]] so strong<br>That judgement cannot cure, which thing to do,<br>If this poor trash of [[Venice]] whom I trace<br>For his quick hunting stand the putting on,<br>I’ll have our [[Michael]] [[Cassio]] on the hip,<br>Abuse him to the Moor in the rank garb—<br>For I [[Fear|fear]] [[Cassio]] with my nightcap, too—<br>Make the Moor thank me, [[Love|love]] me, and reward me<br>For making him egregiously an ass,<br>And practising upon his peace and quiet<br>Even to [[Madness|madness]]. ’Tis here, but yet confused.<br>Knavery’s plain face is never seen till used. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
