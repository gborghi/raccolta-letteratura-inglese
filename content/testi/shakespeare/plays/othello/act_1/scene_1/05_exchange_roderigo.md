| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | What a full fortune does the thick-lips owe If he can carry’t thus! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Call up her father, Rouse him, make after him, [[Poison|poison]] his delight, Proclaim him in the streets; incense her kinsmen, And, though he in a fertile climate dwell, Plague him with flies. Though that his joy be joy, Yet throw such chances of vexation on’t As it may lose some colour. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Here is her father’s [[house]]. I’ll call aloud. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Do, with like timorous accent and dire yell As when, by [[Night|night]] and negligence, the [[Fire|fire]] Is spied in populous cities. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | (calling) What ho, Brabanzio, Signor Brabanzio, ho! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | (calling) Awake, what ho, Brabanzio, thieves, thieves, thieves! Look to your [[house]], your daughter, and your bags. Thieves, thieves! Enter Brabanzio in his nightgown at a [[Window|window]] above |
| BRABANZIO | What is the reason of this terrible summons? What is the matter there? |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Signor, is all your family within? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Are your doors locked? |
| BRABANZIO | Why, wherefore ask you this? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | ’Swounds, sir, you’re robbed. For shame, put on your gown. Your heart is burst, you have lost half your [[Soul|soul]]. Even now, now, very now, an old black ram Is tupping your white ewe. Arise, arise! Awake the snorting citizens with the bell, Or else the devil will make a grandsire of you. Arise, I say. |
| BRABANZIO | What, have you lost your wits? |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Most reverend signor, do you know my voice? |
| BRABANZIO | Not I. What are you? |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | My name is [[Roderigo]]. |
| BRABANZIO | The worser welcome. I have charged thee not to haunt about my doors. In honest plainness thou hast heard me say My daughter is not for thee, and now in [[Madness|madness]], Being full of supper and distempering draughts, Upon malicious bravery dost thou come To start my quiet. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Sir, sir, sir. |
| BRABANZIO | But thou must needs be sure My spirits and my place have in their power To make this bitter to thee. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Patience, good sir. |
| BRABANZIO | What tell’st thou me of robbing? This is [[Venice]]. My [[house]] is not a grange. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Most [[Grave|grave]] Brabanzio, In simple and pure [[Soul|soul]] I come to you. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | (to Brabanzio) ’Swounds, sir, you are one of those that will not serve God if the devil bid you. Because we come to do you service and you think we are ruffians, you’ll have your daughter covered with a Barbary [[Horse|horse]], you’ll have your nephews neigh to you, you’ll have coursers for cousins and jennets for germans. |
| BRABANZIO | What profane wretch art thou? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | I am one, sir, that comes to tell you your daughter and the Moor are now making the beast with two backs. |
| BRABANZIO | Thou art a [[Villain|villain]]. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | You are a senator. |
| BRABANZIO | This thou shalt answer. I know thee, [[Roderigo]]. |
