| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir John [[Falstaff]] and [[Mistress Ford]] |
| SIR JOHN | [[Mistress Ford]], your sorrow hath eaten up my sufferance. I see you are obsequious in your [[Love|love]], and I profess requital to a hair’s breadth: not only, [[Mistress Ford]], in the simple office of [[Love|love]], but in all the accoutrement, complement, and ceremony of it. But are you sure of your husband now? |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | He’s a-birding, sweet Sir John. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | (within) What ho, gossip Ford, what ho! |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Step into th’ chamber, Sir John.<br>Sir John steps into the chamber |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Mistress Page]] |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | How now, sweetheart, who’s at [[Home|home]] besides yourself? |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Why, none but mine own people. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Indeed? |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | No, certainly. (Aside to her) Speak louder. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Truly, I am so glad you have nobody here. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Why? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Why, woman, your husband is in his old lines again. He so takes on yonder with my husband, so rails against all married mankind, so curses all Eve’s daughters of what complexion soever, and so buffets himself on the forehead, crying ‘Peer out, peer out!’, that any [[Madness|madness]] I ever yet beheld seemed but tameness, civility, and patience to this his distemper he is in now. I am glad the fat knight is not here. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Why, does he talk of him? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Of none but him; and swears he was carried out, the last [[Time|time]] he searched for him, in a basket, protests to my husband he is now here, and hath drawn him and the rest of their company from their sport to make another experiment of his suspicion. But I am glad the knight is not here. Now he shall see his own foolery. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | How near is he, [[Mistress Page]]? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Hard by at street end. He will be here anon. 35 |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | I am undone: the knight is here. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Why then, you are utterly shamed, and he’s but a dead man. What a woman are you! Away with him, away with him! Better shame than murder. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Which way should he go? How should I bestow him? Shall I put him into the basket again?<br>Sir John comes forth from the chamber |
| SIR JOHN | No, I’ll come no more i’th’ basket. May I not go out ere he come? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Alas, three of [[Master Ford]]’s brothers watch the door with pistols, that none shall issue out. Otherwise you might slip away ere he came. But what make you here? |
| SIR JOHN | What shall I do? I’ll creep up into the chimney. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | There they always use to discharge their birding-pieces. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] Creep into the kiln-hole. |
| SIR JOHN | Where is it? |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | He will seek there, on my word. Neither press, coffer, chest, trunk, [[Well|well]], vault, but he hath an abstract for the remembrance of such places, and goes to them by his note. There is no hiding you in the [[house]]. |
| SIR JOHN | I’ll go out, then. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] If you go out in your own semblance, you die, Sir John—unless you go out disguised. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | How might we disguise him? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Alas the day, I know not. There is no woman’s gown big enough for him; otherwise he might put on a hat, a muffler, and a kerchief, and so escape. |
| SIR JOHN | Good hearts, devise something. Any extremity rather than a mischief. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | My maid’s aunt, the fat woman of Brentford, has a gown above. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | On my word, it will serve him; she’s as big as he is; and there’s her thrummed hat, and her muffler too.—Run up, Sir John. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Go, go, sweet Sir John. [[Mistress Page]] and<br>I will look some linen for your head. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Quick, quick! We’ll come dress you straight. Put on the gown the while. Exit Sir John |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | I would my husband would meet him in this shape. He cannot abide the old woman of Brentford. He swears she’s a [[Witch|witch]], forbade her my [[house]], and hath threatened to beat her. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Heaven guide him to thy husband’s cudgel, and the devil guide his cudgel afterwards! |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | But is my husband coming? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Ay, in good sadness is he, and talks of the basket too, howsoever he hath had intelligence. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | We’ll try that, for I’ll appoint my men to carry the basket again, to meet him at the door with it as they did last [[Time|time]]. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Nay, but he’ll be here presently. Let’s go dress him like the [[Witch|witch]] of Brentford. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | I’ll first direct my men what they shall do with the basket. Go up; I’ll bring linen for him straight. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] Hang him, dishonest varlet ! We cannot misuse him enough. Exit [[Mistress Ford]] |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | We’ll leave a proof by that which we will do,<br>Wives may be merry, and yet honest, too.<br>We do not act that often jest and laugh.<br>’Tis old but true: ’Still swine eats all the draff’. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Mistress Ford]], with John and Robert |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Go, sirs, take the basket again on your shoulders. Your master is hard at door. If he bid you set it down, obey him. Quickly, dispatch! Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | JOHN Come, come, take it up. |
| *(didascalia)* | ROBERT Pray heaven it be not full of knight again. |
| *(didascalia)* | JOHN I [[Hope|hope]] not; I had as lief bear so much lead. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | They lift the basket. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Master Ford]], [[Master Page]], [[Doctor Caius]], Sir |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Hugh Evans, and [[Justice Shallow]] |
| FORD | Ay, but if it prove true, [[Master Page]], have you any way then to unfool me again? (To John and Robert) Set down the basket, villains.<br>John and Robert set down the basket<br>Somebody call my wife. Youth in a basket! ! O, you panderly rascals! There’s a knot, a gang, a pack, a conspiracy against me. Now shall the devil be shamed.—What, wife, I say! Come, come forth! Behold what honest clothes you send forth to bleaching. |
| PAGE | Why, this passes, [[Master Ford]]. You are not to go loose any longer; you must be pinioned. |
| EVANS | Why, this is lunatics; this is mad as a mad dog. |
| SHALLOW | Indeed, [[Master Ford]], this is not [[Well|well]], indeed. |
| FORD | So say I too, sir. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Mistress Ford]] |
| FORD | Come hither, [[Mistress Ford]] ! [[Mistress Ford]], the honest woman, the modest wife, the virtuous creature, that hath the jealous [[Fool|fool]] to her husband I suspect without cause, mistress, do I? |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | God be my witness you do, if you suspect me in any dishonesty. |
| FORD | [[Well]] said, brazen-face; hold it out.<br>He opens the basket and starts to take out clothes<br>Come forth, sirrah! |
| PAGE | This passes. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | (to Ford) Are you not ashamed? Let the clothes [[Alone|alone]]. |
| FORD | I shall find you anon. |
| EVANS | ’Tis unreasonable: will you take up your wife’s clothes? Come, away. |
| *(didascalia)* | FORD to John and Robert Empty the basket, I say. |
| *(didascalia)* | PAGE Why, man, why? |
| *(didascalia)* | FORD [[Master Page]], as I am a man, there was one conveyed out of my [[house]] yesterday in this basket. Why may not he be there again? In my [[house]] I am sure he is. My intelligence is true, my [[Jealousy|jealousy]] is reasonable. To John and Robert Pluck me out all the linen. |
| EVANS | He takes out clothes |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | If you find a man there, he shall die a flea’s [[Death|death]]. |
| PAGE | Here’s no man. |
| SHALLOW | By my fidelity, this is not [[Well|well]], [[Master Ford]].<br>This wrongs you. |
| EVANS | [[Master Ford]], you must pray, and not follow the imaginations of your own heart. This is jealousies. |
| FORD | [[Well]], he’s not here I seek for. |
| PAGE | No, nor nowhere else but in your brain. |
| *(didascalia)* | FORD Help to search my [[house]] this one [[Time|time]]. If I find not what I seek, show no colour for my extremity; let me for ever be your table-sport; let them say of me, ‘As jealous as Ford, that searched a hollow walnut for his wife’s leman’. Satisfy me once more; once more search with me. Exeunt John and Robert with the basket |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | What ho, [[Mistress Page]]! Come you and the old woman down. My husband will come into the chamber. |
| FORD | Old woman? What old woman’s that? |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Why, it is my maid’s Aunt of Brentford. |
| FORD | A [[Witch|witch]], a quean, an old, cozening quean! ! Have I not forbid her my [[house]]? She comes of errands, does she? We are simple men; we do not know what’s brought to pass under the profession of fortune-telling. She works by charms, by spells, by th’ figure, and such daubery as this is, beyond our element. We know nothing.—Come down, you [[Witch|witch]], you hag, you ! Come down, I say! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Mistress Page]], and Sir John [[Falstaff]], |
| *(didascalia)* | disguised as an old woman. |
| *(didascalia)* | Ford makes towards them |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Nay, good sweet husband!—Good gentlemen, let him not strike the old woman. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | (to Sir John) Come, Mother Prat. Come, give me your hand. |
| FORD | I’ll prat her!<br>He beats Sir John<br>Out of my door, you [[Witch|witch]], you rag, you baggage, you polecat, you runnion! Out, out! I’ll conjure you, I’ll fortune-tell you! Exit Sir John |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Are you not ashamed? I think you have killed the poor woman. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Nay, he will do it.—’Tis a goodly credit for you! |
| FORD | Hang her, [[Witch|witch]]! |
| EVANS | By Jeshu, I think the ‘oman is a [[Witch|witch]] indeed. I like not when a ’oman has a great peard. I spy a great peard under his muffler. |
| FORD | Will you follow, gentlemen? I beseech you, follow. See but the issue of my [[Jealousy|jealousy]]. If I cry out thus upon no trail, never trust me when I open again. |
| PAGE | Let’s obey his [[Humour|humour]] a little further. Come, gentlemen. Exeunt the men |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | By my troth, he beat him most pitifully. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Nay, by th’ mass, that he did not—he beat him most unpitifully, methought. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | I’ll have the cudgel hallowed and hung o’er the altar. It hath done meritorious service. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | What think you—may we, with the warrant of womanhood and the witness of a good [[Conscience|conscience]], pursue him with any further [[Revenge|revenge]]? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | The spirit of wantonness is sure scared out of him. If the devil have him not in fee-simple, with fine and recovery, he will never, I think, in the way of waste attempt us again. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Shall we tell our husbands how we have served him? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Yes, by all means, if it be but to scrape the figures out of your husband’s brains. If they can find in their hearts the poor, unvirtuous, fat knight shall be any further afflicted, we two will still be the ministers. |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | I’ll warrant they’ll have him publicly shamed, and methinks there would be no period to the jest should he not be publicly shamed. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Come, to the forge with it, then shape it. I would not have things cool. Exeunt |
