| *(didascalia)* | Enter Caesar and his army |
| CAESAR | But being charged, we will be still by land—<br>Which, as I take’t, we shall, for his best force<br>Is forth to man his galleys. To the vales,<br>And hold our best advantage. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
| *(didascalia)* | 4.13 Alarum afar off, as at a sea fight. Enter Antony and Scarus |
| ANTONY | Yet they are not joined. Where yon pine does stand<br>I shall discover all. I’ll bring thee word<br>Straight how ’tis like to go. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| SCARUS | Swallows have built<br>In [[Cleopatra]]’s sails their nests. The augurs<br>Say they know not, they cannot tell, look grimly,<br>And dare not speak their knowledge. Antony<br>Is valiant, and dejected, and by starts<br>His fretted fortunes give him [[Hope|hope]] and [[Fear|fear]]<br>Of what he has and has not. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Antony |
| ANTONY | All is lost.<br>This foul Egyptian hath betrayed me.<br>My fleet hath yielded to the foe, and yonder<br>They cast their caps up, and carouse together<br>Like friends long lost. Triple-turned whore! ’Tis thou<br>Hast sold me to this novice, and my heart<br>Makes only wars on thee. Bid them all fly;<br>For when I am revenged upon my charm,<br>I have done all. Bid them all fly. Be gone. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Scarus |
| ANTONY | O sun, thy uprise shall I see no more.<br>Fortune and Antony part here; even here<br>Do we shake hands. All come to this? The hearts<br>That spanieled me at heels, to whom I gave<br>Their wishes, do discandy, melt their sweets<br>On blossoming Caesar; and this pine is barked<br>That overtopped them all. Betrayed I am.<br>O this false [[Soul|soul]] of [[Egypt]]! This [[Grave|grave]] charm,<br>Whose eye becked forth my wars and called them [[Home|home]],<br>Whose bosom was my crownet, my chief end,<br>Like a right gipsy hath at fast and loose<br>Beguiled me to the very heart of loss.<br>What, Eros, Eros! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Cleopatra]] |
| ANTONY | Ah, thou spell! Avaunt. |
| [[Cleopatra|CLEOPATRA]] | Why is my lord enraged against his [[Love|love]]? |
| ANTONY | Vanish, or I shall give thee thy deserving<br>And blemish Caesar’s triumph. Let him take thee<br>And hoist thee up to the shouting plebeians;<br>Follow his chariot, like the greatest spot<br>Of all thy sex; most [[Monster|monster]]-like be shown<br>For poor’st diminutives, for dolts, and let<br>Patient [[Octavia]] plough thy visage up<br>With her prepared nails. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Cleopatra]] |
| ANTONY | ’Tis [[Well|well]] thou’rt gone,<br>If it be [[Well|well]] to live. But better ’twere<br>Thou fell’st into my fury, for one [[Death|death]]<br>Might have prevented many. Eros, ho!<br>The shirt of Nessus is upon me. Teach me,<br>Alcides, thou mine ancestor, thy rage.<br>Let me lodge Lichas on the horns o’th’ [[Moon|moon]],<br>And with those hands that grasped the heaviest club<br>Subdue my worthiest [[Self|self]]. The [[Witch|witch]] shall die.<br>To the young Roman boy she hath sold me, and I fall<br>Under this plot. She dies for’t. Eros, ho! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
