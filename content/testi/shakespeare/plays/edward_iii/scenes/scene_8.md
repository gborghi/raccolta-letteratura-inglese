| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Edward and Lord Audley |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Lord Audley, whiles our son is in the chase,<br>Withdraw our powers unto this little [[Hill|hill]],<br>And here a season let us breathe ourselves. |
| AUDLEY | I will, my lord. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound retreat within |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Just-dooming heaven, whose secret [[Providence|providence]]<br>To our gross judgement is inscrutable,<br>How are we bound to praise thy wondrous works<br>That hast, this day, given way unto the right,<br>And made the wicked stumble at themselves? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Comte d’Artois |
| COMTE D’ARTOIS | Rescue, [[King]] Edward! Rescue for thy son! |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Rescue, Artois? What, is he prisoner,<br>Or fell by violence beside his [[Horse|horse]]? |
| COMTE D’ARTOIS | Neither, my lord, but narrowly beset<br>With turning Frenchmen, whom he did pursue,<br>As ’tis impossible that he should scape<br>Except your highness presently descend. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Tut, let him fight. We gave him arms today,<br>And he is labouring for a knighthood, man! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Derby |
| EARL OF DERBY | The Prince, my lord, the Prince! O, succour him!<br>He’s close encompassed with a world of odds. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Then will he win a world of [[Honour|honour]] too<br>If he by valour can redeem him thence.<br>If not, what remedy? We have more sons<br>Than one to comfort our declining age. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord Audley |
| AUDLEY | Renowned Edward, give me leave, I pray,<br>To lead my soldiers where I may relieve<br>Your grace’s son, in danger to be slain.<br>The snares of French, like emmets on a bank,<br>Muster about him whilst he, [[Lion|lion]]-like,<br>Entangled in the net of their assaults,<br>Franticly rends and bites the woven toil.<br>But all in vain. He cannot free himself. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Audley, content. I will not have a man,<br>On [[Pain|pain]] of [[Death|death]], sent forth to succour him.<br>This is the day ordained by destiny<br>To season his green [[Courage|courage]] with those grievous<br>thoughts<br>That, if he breaketh out, Nestor’s years on earth<br>Will make him savour still of this exploit. |
| EARL OF DERBY | Ah, but he shall not live to see those days! |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Why, then, his epitaph is lasting praise. |
| AUDLEY | Yet, good my lord, ’tis too much wilfulness<br>To let his [[Blood|blood]] be spilt, that may be saved. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Exclaim no more, for none of you can tell<br>Whether a borrowed aid will serve or no.<br>Perhaps he is already slain or ta’en.<br>And dare a falcon when she’s in her flight,<br>And ever after she’ll be haggard-like.<br>Let Edward be delivered by our hands<br>And, still in danger, he’ll expect the like.<br>But if himself himself redeem from thence,<br>He will have vanquished, cheerful, [[Death|death]] and [[Fear|fear]],<br>And ever after dread their force no more<br>Than if they were but babes or captive slaves. |
| AUDLEY | O cruel father! Farewell Edward, then. |
| EARL OF DERBY | Farewell, sweet Prince, the [[Hope|hope]] of chivalry. |
| COMTE D’ARTOIS | O, would my life might ransom him from [[Death|death]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets sound retreat within |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | But soft—methinks I hear<br>The dismal charge of trumpets’ loud retreat.<br>All are not slain, I [[Hope|hope]], that went with him.<br>Some will return with tidings, good or bad. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Edward Prince of Wales in triumph, bearing in his hand his shivered lance. The [[Body|body]] of the [[King]] of Bohemia is borne before, wrapped in the colours of Bohemia. The English run and embrace the Prince |
| AUDLEY | O, joyful sight! Victorious Edward lives! |
| EARL OF DERBY | Welcome, brave Prince. |
| KINC EDWARD | Welcome, Plantagenet.<br>The Prince kneels and kisses his father’s hand |
| PRINCE OF WALES | First, having done my duty as beseemed,<br>Lords, I regreet you all with hearty thanks.<br>And now behold after my [[Winter|winter]]’s toil<br>My painful voyage on the boist’rous sea<br>Of war’s devouring gulfs and steely rocks<br>I bring my freight unto the wished port,<br>My [[Summer|summer]]’s [[Hope|hope]], my travail’s sweet reward.<br>(Pointing at Bohemia’s [[Body|body]]) And here, with humble duty, I present<br>This sacrifice, this first fruit of my [[Sword|sword]],<br>Cropped and cut down even at the gate of [[Death|death]]:<br>The [[King]] of Bohême, father, whom I slew,<br>Whose thousands had entrenched me round about,<br>And lay as thick upon my battered crest<br>As on an anvil with their ponderous glaives.<br>Yet marble [[Courage|courage]] still did underprop,<br>And when my weary arms with often blows,<br>Like the continual labouring woodman’s axe<br>That is enjoined to fell a load of oaks,<br>Began to falter, straight I would recover<br>My gifts you gave me and my zealous vow,<br>And then new [[Courage|courage]] made me fresh again<br>That, in despite, I carved my passage forth,<br>And put the multitude to speedy flight.<br>Lo, thus hath Edward’s hand filled your request<br>And done, I [[Hope|hope]], the duty of a knight.<br>His [[Sword|sword]] borne forth by a soldier |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Ay, [[Well|well]] thou hast deserved a knighthood, Ned,<br>He takes the [[Sword|sword]]<br><br>And therefore, with thy [[Sword|sword]] yet reeking warm<br>With [[Blood|blood]] of those that fought to be thy bane,<br>He knights the Prince<br>Arise Prince Edward, trusty knight-at-arms.<br>This day thou hast confounded me with joy<br>And proved thyself fit heir unto a [[King|king]]. |
| PRINCE OF WALES | (rising, and then giving the [[King]] a paper)<br>Here is a note, my gracious lord, of those<br>That in this conflict of our foes were slain:<br>Eleven princes of esteem, fourscore barons,<br>A hundred and twenty knights, and thirty thousand<br>Common soldiers—and of our men, a thousand. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Our God be praised! Now, Jean of France, I [[Hope|hope]]<br>Thou know’st [[King]] Edward for no wantonness,<br>No [[Love|love]]-sick cockney, nor his soldiers jades.<br>But which way is the fearful [[King]] escaped? |
| PRINCE OF WALES | Towards Poitiers, noble father, and his sons. |
| [[King|KING]] EDWARD | Ned, thou and Audley shall pursue them still.<br>Myself and Derby will to Calais straight,<br>And there begirt that haven town with siege.<br>Now lies it on an upshot: therefore, strike,<br>And wistly follow whiles the game’s on foot. Exeunt |
