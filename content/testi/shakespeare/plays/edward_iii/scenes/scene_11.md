| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Dauphin]] and Villiers with a paper |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | I [[Wonder|wonder]], Villiers, thou shouldst importune me<br>For one that is our deadly enemy. |
| VILLIERS | Not for his sake, my gracious lord, so much<br>Am I become an earnest advocate<br>As that, thereby, my ransom will be quit. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Thy ransom, man? Why need’st thou talk of that?<br>Art thou not free? And are not all occasions<br>That happen for advantage of our foes<br>To be accepted of and stood upon? |
| VILLIERS | No, good my lord, except the same be just.<br>For profit must with [[Honour|honour]] be commixed,<br>Or else our actions are but scandalous.<br>But, letting pass these intricate objections,<br>Will’t please your highness to subscribe or no? |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Villiers, I will not nor I cannot do it.<br>Salisbury shall not have his will so much<br>To claim a passport how it pleaseth him. |
| VILLIERS | Why then, I know the extremity, my lord.<br>I must return to [[Prison|prison]], whence I came. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Return? I [[Hope|hope]] thou wilt not!<br>What bird that hath escaped the fowler’s gin<br>Will not beware how she’s ensnared again?<br>Or what is he so senseless and secure<br>That, having hardly passed a dangerous gulf,<br>Will put himself in peril there again? |
| VILLIERS | Ah, but it is mine oath, my gracious lord,<br>Which I in [[Conscience|conscience]] may not violate—<br>Or else a kingdom should not draw me hence. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Thine oath? Why, that doth bind thee to abide.<br>Hast thou not sworn obedience to thy Prince? |
| VILLIERS | In all things that uprightly he commands.<br>But either to persuade or threaten me<br>Not to perform the covenant of my word<br>Is lawless, and I need not to obey. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Why, is it lawful for a man to kill,<br>And not to break a promise with his foe? |
| VILLIERS | To kill, my lord, when war is once proclaimed,<br>So that our quarrel be for wrongs received,<br>No [[Doubt|doubt]] is lawfully permitted us.<br>But in an oath, we must be [[Well|well]] advised<br>How we do swear, and when we once have sworn,<br>Not to infringe it, though we die therefor.<br>Therefore, my lord, as willing I return<br>As if I were to fly to paradise.<br>He begins to leave |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Stay, my Villiers. Thine honourable mind<br>Deserves to be eternally admired.<br>Thy suit shall be no longer thus deferred.<br>Give me the paper. I’ll subscribe to it.<br>Villiers gives him the paper, which the [[Dauphin]] signs<br>And wheretofore I loved thee as Villiers,<br>Hereafter I’ll embrace thee as myself.<br>Stay, and be still in favour with thy lord. |
| VILLIERS | (receiving back the paper)<br>I humbly thank your grace. I must dispatch<br>And send this passport first unto the Earl,<br>And then I will attend your highness’ pleasure. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Do so, Villiers. And Charles, when he hath need,<br>Be such his soldiers, howsoever he [[Speed|speed]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Villiers |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter ean [[King of France]] |
| [[King of France|KING OF FRANCE]] | Come, Charles, and arm thee. Edward is entrapped.<br>The Prince of Wales is fall’n into our hands,<br>And we have compassed him. He cannot scape. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | But will your highness fight today? |
| [[King of France|KING OF FRANCE]] | What else, my son? He’s scarce eight thousand strong,<br>And we are threescore thousand at the least. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | I have a prophecy, my gracious lord,<br>Wherein is written what [[success]] is like<br>To happen us in this outrageous war.<br>It was delivered me at Crécy’s field<br>By one that is an aged hermit there:<br>‘When feathered fowl shall make thine army tremble,<br>And flintstones rise and break the battle ’ray,<br>Then think on him that doth not now dissemble,<br>For that shall be the hapless dreadful day,<br>Yet in the end thy foot thou shalt advance<br>As far in England as thy foe in France.’ |
| [[King of France|KING OF FRANCE]] | By this it seems we shall be fortunate.<br>For, as it is impossible that stones<br>Should ever rise and break the battle ’ray,<br>Or airy fowl make men in arms to quake,<br>So is it like we shall not be subdued.<br>Or, say this might be true: yet in the end,<br>Since he doth promise we shall drive him hence<br>And scourge their country as they have done ours,<br>By this [[Revenge|revenge]] that loss will seem the less.<br>But all are frivolous fancies, toys and [[Dreams|dreams]].<br>Once we are sure we have ensnared the son,<br>Catch we the father after how we can. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
