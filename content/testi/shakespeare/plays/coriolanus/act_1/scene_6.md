|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter certain Romans with spoils |
| FIRST ROMAN | This will I carry to Rome. |
| SECOND ROMAN | And I this. |
| THIRD ROMAN | A murrain on’t, I took this for silver. |
| *(didascalia)* | He throws it away. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum continues still afar off. Enter Martius, |
| THIRD ROMAN | bleeding, and Lartius with a trumpeter. Exeunt<br><br>Romans with spoils |
| MARTIUS | See here these movers that do prize their honours<br>At a cracked drachma! Cushions, leaden spoons,<br>Irons of a doit, doublets that hangmen would<br>Bury with those that wore them, these base slaves,<br>Ere yet the fight be done, pack up. Down with them!<br>And hark what noise the general makes. To him.<br>There is the man of my [[Soul|soul]]’s hate, [[Aufidius]],<br>Piercing our Romans. Then, valiant Titus, take<br>Convenient numbers to make good the [[City|city]],<br>Whilst I, with those that have the spirit, will haste<br>To help [[Cominius]]. |
| LARTIUS | Worthy sir, thou bleed’st.<br>Thy exercise hath been too violent<br>For a second course of fight. |
| MARTIUS | Sir, praise me not.<br>My work hath yet not warmed me. Fare you [[Well|well]].<br>The [[Blood|blood]] I drop is rather physical<br>Than dangerous to me. To [[Aufidius]] thus<br>I will appear and fight. |
| LARTIUS | Now the fair goddess fortune<br>Fall deep in [[Love|love]] with thee, and her great charms Misguide thy opposers’ swords! Bold gentleman, Prosperity be thy page. |
| MARTIUS | Thy friend no less<br>Than those she placeth highest. So farewell. |
| LARTIUS | Thou worthiest Martius! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Martius |
| LARTIUS | Go sound thy trumpet in the market-place.<br>Call thither all the officers o’th’ town,<br>Where they shall know our mind. Away. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
