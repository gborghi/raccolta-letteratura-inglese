| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. A retreat is sounded. Flourish.Enter at one door [[Cominius]] with the Romans, at another door Martius with his arm in a scarf |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | (to Martius)<br>If I should tell thee o‘er this thy day’s work<br>Thou’lt not believe thy deeds. But I’ll report it<br>Where senators shall mingle [[Tears|tears]] with smiles,<br>Where great patricians shall attend and shrug,<br>I‘th’ end admire; where ladies shall be frighted<br>And, gladly quaked, hear more; where the dull<br>tribunes,<br>That with the fusty plebeians hate thine honours,<br>Shall say against their hearts ‘We thank the gods<br>Our Rome hath such a soldier.’<br>Yet cam’st thou to a morsel of this feast,<br>Having fully dined before. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lartius, with his power, from the pursuit |
| LARTIUS | O general,<br>Here is the steed, we the caparison.<br>Hadst thou beheld— |
| MARTIUS | Pray now, no more. My mother,<br>Who has a charter to extol her [[Blood|blood]],<br>When she does praise me grieves me. I have done<br>As you have done, that’s what I can; induced<br>As you have been, that’s for my country.<br>He that has but effected his good will<br>Hath overta’en mine act. |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | You shall not be<br>The [[Grave|grave]] of your deserving. Rome must know<br>The value of her own. ’Twere a concealment<br>Worse than a theft, no less than a traducement,<br>To hide your doings and to [[Silence|silence]] that<br>Which, to the spire and top of praises vouched,<br>Would seem but modest. Therefore, I beseech you—<br>In sign of what you are, not to reward<br>What you have done—before our army hear me. |
| MARTIUS | I have some wounds upon me, and they smart<br>To hear themselves remembered. |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | Should they not,<br>[[Well]] might they fester ‘gainst ingratitude,<br>And tent themselves with [[Death|death]]. Of all the horses—<br>Whereof we have ta’en good, and good store—of all<br>The treasure in this field achieved and [[City|city]],<br>We render you the tenth, to be ta’en forth<br>Before the common distribution<br>At your only choice. |
| MARTIUS | I thank you, general,<br>But cannot make my heart consent to take<br>A bribe to pay my [[Sword|sword]]. I do refuse it,<br>And stand upon my common part with those<br>That have upheld the doing.<br>A long flourish. They all cry ‘Martius, Martius!’, casting up their caps and lances. [[Cominius]] and Lartius stand bare<br>May these same instruments which you profane<br>Never sound more. When drums and trumpets shall<br>I’th’ field prove flatterers, let courts and cities be<br>Made all of false-faced soothing. When steel grows<br>Soft as the parasite’s silk, let him be made<br>An overture for th’ wars. No more, I say.<br>For that I have not washed my nose that bled,<br>Or foiled some debile wretch, which without note<br>Here’s many else have done, you shout me forth<br>In acclamations hyperbolical,<br>As if I loved my little should be dieted<br>In praises sauced with lies. |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | Too modest are you,<br>More cruel to your good report than grateful<br>To us that give you truly. By your patience,<br>If ‘gainst yourself you be incensed we’ll put you,<br>Like one that means his proper harm, in manacles,<br>Then reason safely with you. Therefore be it known,<br>As to us, to all the world, that Caius Martius<br>Wears this war’s garland, in token of the which<br>My noble steed, known to the camp, I give him,<br>With all his trim belonging; and from this [[Time|time]],<br>For what he did before Corioles, call him,<br>With all th’applause and clamour of the host,<br>Martius Caius [[Coriolanus]]. Bear th’addition<br>Nobly ever! |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Trumpets sound, and drums |
| ALL | Martius Caius [[Coriolanus]]! |
| [[Coriolanus|CORIOLANUS]] | (to [[Cominius]]) I will go wash,<br>And when my face is fair you shall perceive<br>Whether I blush or no. Howbeit, I thank you.<br>I mean to stride your steed, and at all times<br>To undercrest your good addition<br>To th’ fairness of my power. |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | So, to our tent,<br>Where, ere we do repose us, we will write<br>To Rome of our [[success]]. You, Titus Lartius,<br>Must to Corioles back. Send us to Rome<br>The best, with whom we may articulate<br>For their own good and ours. |
| LARTIUS | I shall, my lord. |
| [[Coriolanus|CORIOLANUS]] | The gods begin to mock me. I, that now<br>Refused most princely gifts, am bound to beg<br>Of my lord general. |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | Take‘t, ’tis yours. What is’t? |
| [[Coriolanus|CORIOLANUS]] | I sometime lay here in Corioles,<br>And at a poor man’s [[house]]. He used me kindly.<br>He cried to me; I saw him prisoner;<br>But then [[Aufidius]] was within my view,<br>And wrath o’erwhelmed my pity. I request you<br>To give my poor host [[Freedom|freedom]]. |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | O, [[Well|well]] begged!<br>Were he the butcher of my son he should<br>Be free as is the [[Wind|wind]]. Deliver him, Titus. |
| LARTIUS | Martius, his name? |
| [[Coriolanus|CORIOLANUS]] | By Jupiter, forgot!<br>I am weary, yea, my [[Memory|memory]] is tired.<br>Have we no wine here? |
| [[Cominius|COMINIUS]] | Go we to our tent.<br>The [[Blood|blood]] upon your visage dries; ’tis [[Time|time]]<br>It should be looked to. Come. |
| *(didascalia)* | A flourish of cornetts. Exeunt |
