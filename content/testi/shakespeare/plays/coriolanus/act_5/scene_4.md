| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Menenius]] and [[Sicinius]] |
| [[Menenius|MENENIUS]] | See you yon coign o’th’ Capitol, yon corner-stone? |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | Why, what of that? |
| [[Menenius|MENENIUS]] | If it be possible for you to displace it with your little finger, there is some [[Hope|hope]] the ladies of Rome, especially his mother, may prevail with him. But I say there is no [[Hope|hope]] in’t, our throats are sentenced and stay upon execution. |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | Is’t possible that so short a [[Time|time]] can alter the condition of a man? |
| [[Menenius|MENENIUS]] | There is differency between a grub and a butterfly, yet your butterfly was a grub. This Martius is grown from man to dragon. He has wings, he’s more than a creeping thing. |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | He loved his mother dearly. |
| [[Menenius|MENENIUS]] | So did he me, and he no more remembers his mother now than an eight-year old [[Horse|horse]]. The tartness of his face sours ripe grapes. When he walks, he moves like an engine, and the ground shrinks before his treading. He is able to pierce a corslet with his eye, talks like a knell, and his ‘hmh!’ is a battery. He sits in his state as a thing made for Alexander. What he bids be done is finished with his bidding. He wants nothing of a god but [[Eternity|eternity]] and a heaven to throne in. |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | Yes: [[Mercy|mercy]], if you report him truly. |
| [[Menenius|MENENIUS]] | I paint him in the character. Mark what [[Mercy|mercy]] his mother shall bring from him. There is no more [[Mercy|mercy]] in him than there is milk in a male tiger. That shall our poor [[City|city]] find; and all this is ’long of you. [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] The gods be good unto us! |
| VIENENIUS | No, in such a case the gods will not be good unto us. When we banished him we respected not them, and, he returning to break our necks, they respect not us. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | (to [[Sicinius]])<br>Sir, if you’d save your life, fly to your [[house]].<br>The plebeians have got your fellow tribune<br>And hale him up and down, all swearing if<br>The Roman ladies bring not comfort [[Home|home]]<br>They’ll give him [[Death|death]] by inches. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | What’s the news? |
| SECOND MESSENGER | Good news, good news. The ladies have prevailed,<br>The Volscians are dislodged, and Martius gone.<br>A merrier day did never yet greet Rome,<br>No, not th’expulsion of the Tarquins. |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | Friend,<br>Art thou certain this is true? Is’t most certain? |
| SECOND MESSENGER | As certain as I know the sun is [[Fire|fire]].<br>Where have you lurked that you make [[Doubt|doubt]] of it?<br>Ne’er through an arch so hurried the blown tide<br>As the recomforted through th’ gates. |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets, hautboys, drums, beat all together |
| SECOND MESSENGER | Why, hark you,<br>The trumpets, sackbuts, psalteries, and fifes,<br>Tabors and cymbals and the shouting Romans<br>Make the sun [[Dance|dance]].<br>A shout within<br>Hark you! |
| [[Menenius|MENENIUS]] | This is good news.<br>I will go meet the ladies. This [[Volumnia]]<br>Is worth of consuls, senators, patricians,<br>A [[City|city]] full; of tribunes such as you,<br>A sea and land full. You have prayed [[Well|well]] today.<br>This morning for ten thousand of your throats<br>I’d not have given a doit. |
| *(didascalia)* | Music sounds still with the shouts |
| [[Menenius|MENENIUS]] | Hark how they joy! |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | (to the Messenger)<br>First, the gods bless you for your tidings. Next, |
| *(didascalia)* | Giving money Accept my thankfulness. |
| SECOND MESSENGER | Sir, we have all great cause to give great thanks. |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | They are near the [[City|city]]. |
| SECOND MESSENGER | Almost at point to enter. |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | We’ll meet them, and help the joy. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
| *(didascalia)* | 5.5 Enter at one door Lords and Citizens, at another door two Senators with the ladies [[Volumnia]], [[Virgilia]], and Valeria, passing over the stage |
| [[Sicinius|SICINIUS]] | A SENATOR<br>Behold our patroness, the life of Rome!<br>Call all your tribes together, praise the gods,<br>And make triumphant fires. Strew [[Flowers|flowers]] before them.<br>Unshout the noise that banished Martius,<br>Repeal him with the welcome of his mother.<br>Cry ‘Welcome, ladies, welcome!’ |
| ALL | Welcome, ladies, welcome! |
| *(didascalia)* | A flourish with drums and trumpets. Exeunt |
