| *(didascalia)* | Enter Petruccio, Katherine, [[Hortensio]], and servants |
| PETRUCCIO | Come on, i’ God’s name. Once more toward our father’s.<br>Good Lord, how bright and goodly shines the [[Moon|moon]]! |
| KATHERINE | The [[Moon|moon]]?—the sun. It is not moonlight now. |
| PETRUCCIO | I say it is the [[Moon|moon]] that shines so bright. |
| KATHERINE | I know it is the sun that shines so bright. 5 |
| PETRUCCIO | Now, by my mother’s son—and that’s myself—<br>It shall be [[Moon|moon]], or star, or what I list<br>Or ere I [[Journey|journey]] to your father’s [[house]].<br>Go on, and fetch our horses back again.<br>Evermore crossed and crossed, nothing but crossed. |
| [[Hortensio|HORTENSIO]] | (to Katherine)<br>Say as he says or we shall never go. |
| KATHERINE | Forward, I pray, since we have come so far,<br>And be it [[Moon|moon]] or sun or what you please,<br>And if you please to call it a rush-candle<br>Henceforth I vow it shall be so for me. |
| PETRUCCIO | I say it is the [[Moon|moon]]. |
| KATHERINE | I know it is the [[Moon|moon]]. |
| PETRUCCIO | Nay then you lie, it is the blessed sun. |
| KATHERINE | Then God be blessed, it is the blessèd sun,<br>But sun it is not when you say it is not,<br>And the [[Moon|moon]] changes even as your mind.<br>What you will have it named, even that it is,<br>And so it shall be still for Katherine. |
| [[Hortensio|HORTENSIO]] | Petruccio, go thy ways. The field is won. |
| PETRUCCIO | [[Well]], forward, forward. Thus the bowl should run,<br>And not unluckily against the bias.<br>But soft, company is coming here. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter old Vincentio |
| PETRUCCIO | (To Vincentio) Good morrow, gentle mistress, where<br>away?<br>Tell me, sweet Kate, and tell me truly too,<br>Hast thou beheld a fresher gentlewoman,<br>Such war of [[White and Red|white and red]] within her cheeks?<br>What [[Stars|stars]] do spangle heaven with such [[Beauty|beauty]]<br>As those two eyes become that heavenly face?<br>Fair lovely maid, once more good day to thee.<br>Sweet Kate, embrace her for her [[Beauty|beauty]]’s sake. |
| [[Hortensio|HORTENSIO]] | A will make the man mad to make the woman of him. |
| KATHERINE | Young budding virgin, fair, and fresh, and sweet,<br>Whither away, or where is thy abode?<br>Happy the parents of so fair a [[Child|child]],<br>Happier the man whom favourable [[Stars|stars]]<br>Allots thee for his lovely bedfellow. |
| PETRUCCIO | Why, how now, Kate, I [[Hope|hope]] thou art not mad.<br>This is a man, old, wrinkled, faded, withered,<br>And not a maiden as thou sayst he is. |
| KATHERINE | Pardon, old father, my mistaking eyes<br>That have been so bedazzled with the sun<br>That everything I look on seemeth green.<br>Now I perceive thou art a reverend father.<br>Pardon, I pray thee, for my mad mistaking. |
| PETRUCCIO | Do, good old grandsire, and withal make known<br>Which way thou travell’st. If along with us,<br>We shall be joyful of thy company. |
| VINCENTIO | Fair sir, and you, my merry mistress,<br>That with your strange encounter much amazed me,<br>My name is called Vincentio, my dwelling Pisa,<br>And bound I am to Padua, there to visit<br>A son of mine which long I have not seen. |
| PETRUCCIO | What is his name? |
| VINCENTIO | [[Lucentio]], gentle sir. |
| PETRUCCIO | Happily met, the happier for thy son.<br>And now by law as [[Well|well]] as reverend age<br>I may entitle thee my loving father.<br>The sister to my wife, this gentlewoman,<br>Thy son by this hath married. [[Wonder]] not,<br>Nor be not grieved. She is of good esteem,<br>Her dowry wealthy, and of worthy birth,<br>Beside, so qualified as may beseem<br>The spouse of any noble gentleman.<br>Let me embrace with old Vincentio,<br>And wander we to see thy honest son,<br>Who will of thy arrival be full joyous.<br>He embraces Vincentio |
| VINCENTIO | But is this true, or is it else your pleasure<br>Like pleasant travellers to break a jest<br>Upon the company you overtake? |
| [[Hortensio|HORTENSIO]] | I do assure thee, father, so it is. |
| PETRUCCIO | Come, go along, and see the [[Truth|truth]] hereof,<br>For our first merriment hath made thee jealous. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Hortensio]] |
| [[Hortensio|HORTENSIO]] | [[Well]], Petruccio, this has put me in heart.<br>Have to my widow, and if she be froward,<br>Then hast thou taught [[Hortensio]] to be untoward. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
