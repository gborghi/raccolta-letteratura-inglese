| *(didascalia)* | Drum. Enter [[Brutus]], [[Lucius]], and the army. Lucillius, Titinius, and Pindarus meet them |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Stand, ho! |
| *(didascalia)* | SOLDIER Give the word ‘ho’, and stand. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What now, Lucillius: is [[Cassius]] near? |
| LUCILLIUS | He is at hand, and Pindarus is come<br>To do you salutation from his master. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He greets me [[Well|well]]. Your master, Pindarus,<br>In his own change or by ill officers,<br>Hath given me some worthy cause to wish<br>Things done undone. But if he be at hand,<br>I shall be satisfied. |
| PINDARUS | I do not [[Doubt|doubt]]<br>But that my noble master will appear<br>Such as he is, full of regard and [[Honour|honour]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He is not doubted.—A word, Lucillius.<br>[[Brutus]] and Lucillius speak apart<br><br>How he received you let me be resolved. |
| LUCILLIUS | With courtesy and with respect enough,<br>But not with such familiar instances,<br>Nor with such free and friendly conference,<br>As he hath used of old. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Thou hast described<br>A hot friend cooling. Ever note, Lucillius:<br>When [[Love|love]] begins to sicken and [[Decay|decay]]<br>It useth an enforced ceremony.<br>There are no tricks in plain and simple [[Faith|faith]];<br>But hollow men, like horses hot at hand,<br>Make gallant show and promise of their mettle;<br>Low march within<br>But when they should endure the bloody spur,<br>They fall their crests and, like deceitful jades,<br>Sink in the trial. Comes his army on? |
| LUCILLIUS | They mean this [[Night|night]] in Sardis to be quartered.<br>The greater part, the [[Horse|horse]] in general,<br>Are come with [[Cassius]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Cassius]] and his powers |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Hark, he is arrived.<br>March gently on to meet him.<br>The armies march |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Stand, ho! |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Stand, ho! Speak the word along. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST SOLDIER Stand! |
| *(didascalia)* | second SOLDIER Stand! |
| *(didascalia)* | THIRD SOLDIER Standl |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Most noble brother, you have done me wrong. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Judge me, you gods: wrong I mine enemies?<br>And if not so, how should I wrong a brother? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Brutus]], this sober form of yours hides wrongs,<br>And when you do them— |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Cassius]], be content.<br>Speak your griefs softly. I do know you [[Well|well]].<br>Before the eyes of both our armies here,<br>Which should perceive nothing but [[Love|love]] from us,<br>Let us not wrangle. Bid them move away,<br>Then in my tent, [[Cassius]], enlarge your griefs,<br>And I will give you audience. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Pindarus,<br>Bid our commanders lead their charges off<br>A little from this ground. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Lucillius, do you the like; and let no man<br>Come to our tent till we have done our conference.<br>Let [[Lucius]] and Titinius guard our door. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt the armies [[Brutus]] and [[Cassius]] remain, with Titinius, and [[Lucius]] guarding the door |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | That you have wronged me doth appear in this:<br>You have condemned and noted [[Lucius]] Pella<br>For taking bribes here of the Sardians,<br>Wherein my letters praying on his side,<br>Because I knew the man, was slighted off. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You wronged yourself to write in such a case. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | In such a [[Time|time]] as this it is not meet<br>That every nice offence should bear his comment. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Let me tell you, [[Cassius]], you yourself<br>Are much condemned to have an itching palm,<br>To sell and mart your offices for gold<br>To undeservers. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I, an itching palm?<br>You know that you are [[Brutus]] that speaks this, 65<br>Or, by the gods, this speech were else your last. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | The name of [[Cassius]] honours this corruption,<br>And chastisement doth therefore hide his head. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Chastisement? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Remember March, the ides of March, remember.<br>Did not great Julius bleed for justice’ sake?<br>What [[Villain|villain]] touched his [[Body|body]], that did stab,<br>And not for justice? What, shall one of us,<br>That struck the foremost man of all this world<br>But for supporting robbers, shall we now<br>Contaminate our fingers with base bribes,<br>And sell the mighty space of our large honours<br>For so much trash as may be grasped thus?<br>I had rather be a dog and bay the [[Moon|moon]]<br>Than such a Roman. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Brutus]], bay not me.<br>I’ll not endure it. You forget yourself<br>To hedge me in. I am a soldier, I,<br>Older in practice, abler than yourself<br>To make conditions. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Go to, you are not, [[Cassius]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I am. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I say you are not. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Urge me no more, I shall forget myself.<br>Have mind upon your health. Tempt me no farther. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Away, slight man. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Is’t possible? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Hear me, for I will speak.<br>Must I give way and room to your rash choler?<br>Shall I be frighted when a madman stares? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | O ye gods, ye gods! Must I endure all this? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | All this? Ay, more. Fret till your proud heart break.<br>Go show your slaves how choleric you are,<br>And make your bondmen tremble. Must I budge?<br>Must I observe you? Must I stand and crouch<br>Under your testy [[Humour|humour]]? By the gods,<br>You shall digest the venom of your spleen,<br>Though it do split you. For from this day forth<br>I’ll use you for my mirth, yea for my laughter,<br>When you are waspish. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Is it come to this? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You say you are a better soldier.<br>Let it appear so, make your vaunting true,<br>And it shall please me [[Well|well]]. For mine own part,<br>I shall be glad to learn of noble men. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | You wrong me every way, you wrong me, [[Brutus]].<br>I said an elder soldier, not a better.<br>Did I say better? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | If you did, I care not. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | When Caesar lived he durst not thus have moved me. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Peace, peace; you durst not so have tempted him. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I durst not? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | What, durst not tempt him? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | For your life you durst not. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Do not presume too much upon my [[Love|love]].<br>I may do that I shall be sorry for. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You have done that you should be sorry for.<br>There is no terror, [[Cassius]], in your threats,<br>For I am armed so strong in honesty<br>That they pass by me as the idle [[Wind|wind]],<br>Which I respect not. I did send to you<br>For certain sums of gold, which you denied me;<br>For I can raise no money by vile means.<br>By heaven, I had rather coin my heart<br>And drop my [[Blood|blood]] for drachmas than to wring<br>From the hard hands of peasants their vile trash<br>By any indirection. I did send<br>To you for gold to pay my legions,<br>Which you denied me. Was that done like [[Cassius]]?<br>Should I have answered Caius [[Cassius]] so?<br>When Marcus [[Brutus]] grows so covetous<br>To lock such rascal counters from his friends,<br>Be ready, gods, with all your thunderbolts;<br>Dash him to pieces. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I denied you not. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You did. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I did not. He was but a [[Fool|fool]]<br>That brought my answer back. [[Brutus]] hath rived my<br>heart.<br>A friend should bear his friend’s infirmities,<br>But [[Brutus]] makes mine greater than they are. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I do not, till you practise them on me. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | You [[Love|love]] me not. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I do not like your faults. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | A friendly eye could never see such faults. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | A flatterer’s would not, though they do appear<br>As huge as high Olympus. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Come, Antony and young Octavius, come,<br>[[Revenge]] yourselves [[Alone|alone]] on [[Cassius]];<br>For [[Cassius]] is aweary of the world,<br>Hated by one he loves, braved by his brother,<br>Checked like a bondman; all his faults observed,<br>Set in a notebook, learned and conned by rote,<br>To cast into my teeth. O, I could weep<br>My spirit from mine eyes! There is my dagger,<br>And here my naked breast; within, a heart<br>Dearer than Pluto’s mine, richer than gold.<br>If that thou beest a Roman, take it forth.<br>I that denied thee gold will give my heart.<br>Strike as thou didst at Caesar; for I know<br>When thou didst hate him worst, thou loved‘st him<br>better<br>Than ever thou loved’st [[Cassius]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Sheathe your dagger.<br>Be angry when you will; it shall have scope.<br>Do what you will; dishonour shall be [[Humour|humour]].<br>O [[Cassius]], you are yoked with a [[Lamb|lamb]]<br>That carries anger as the flint bears [[Fire|fire]],<br>Who, much enforced, shows a hasty spark<br>And straight is cold again. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Hath [[Cassius]] lived<br>To be but mirth and laughter to his [[Brutus]]<br>When grief and [[Blood|blood]] ill-tempered vexeth him? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | When I spoke that, I was ill-tempered too. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Do you confess so much? Give me your hand. |
| BRUTES | And my heart too. |
| *(didascalia)* | They embrace |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | O [[Brutus]]! |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What’s the matter? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Have not you [[Love|love]] enough to bear with me<br>When that rash [[Humour|humour]] which my mother gave me<br>Makes me forgetful? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Yes, [[Cassius]], and from henceforth,<br>When you are over-earnest with your [[Brutus]],<br>He’ll think your mother chides, and leave you so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lucillius and a Poet |
| POET | Let me go in to see the generals.<br>There is some grudge between ’em; ’tis not meet<br>They be [[Alone|alone]]. |
| LUCILLIUS | You shall not come to them. |
| POET | Nothing but [[Death|death]] shall stay me. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | How now! What’s the matter? |
| POET | For shame, you generals, what do you mean?<br>[[Love]] and be friends, as two such men should be,<br>For I have seen more years, I’m sure, than ye. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Ha, ha! How vilely doth this cynic rhyme! |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | (to the Poet)<br>Get you hence, sirrah; saucy fellow, hence! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Bear with him, [[Brutus]], ’tis his fashion. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I’ll know his [[Humour|humour]] when he knows his [[Time|time]].<br>What should the wars do with these jigging fools?<br>(To the Poet) Companion, hence! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (to the Poet) Away, away, be gone! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Poet |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Lucillius and Titinius, bid the commanders<br>Prepare to lodge their companies tonight. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And come yourselves, and bring Messala with you<br>Immediately to us. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Lucillius and Titinius |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Lucius]], a bowl of wine. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Lucius]] |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I did not think you could have been so angry. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O [[Cassius]], I am sick of many griefs. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Of your [[philosophy]] you make no use,<br>If you give place to accidental evils. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No man bears sorrow better. [[Portia]] is dead. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Ha! [[Portia]]? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | She is dead. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | How scaped I killing when I crossed you so?<br>O insupportable and touching loss!<br>Upon what sickness? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Impatience of my absence,<br>And grief that young Octavius with [[Mark Antony]]<br>Have made themselves so strong—for with her [[Death|death]]<br>That tidings came. With this, she fell distraught,<br>And, her attendants absent, swallowed [[Fire|fire]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And died so? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Even so. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Oye immortal gods! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]], with wine and tapers |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Speak no more of her. (To [[Lucius]]) Give me a bowl of<br>wine.<br>(To [[Cassius]]) In this I bury all unkindness, [[Cassius]].<br>He drinks |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | My heart is thirsty for that noble pledge.<br>Fill, [[Lucius]], till the wine o’erswell the cup.<br>I cannot drink too much of [[Brutus]]’ [[Love|love]].<br>He drinks. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Lucius]] Enter Titinius and Messala |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Come in, Titinius; welcome, good Messala.<br>Now sit we close about this taper here,<br>And call in question our necessities. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (aside)<br>[[Portia]], art thou gone? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No more, I pray you. |
| *(didascalia)* | They sit |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Messala, I have here received letters<br>That young Octavius and [[Mark Antony]]<br>Come down upon us with a mighty power,<br>Bending their expedition toward Philippi. |
| MESSALA | Myself have letters of the selfsame tenor. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | With what addition? |
| MESSALA | That by proscription and bills of outlawry<br>Octavius, Antony, and [[Lepidus]]<br>Have put to [[Death|death]] an hundred senators. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Therein our letters do not [[Well|well]] agree.<br>Mine speak of seventy senators that died<br>By their proscriptions, Cicero being one. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Cicero one? |
| MESSALA | Ay, Cicero is dead,<br>And by that order of proscription.<br>(To [[Brutus]])<br>Had you your letters from your wife, my lord? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No, Messala. |
| MESSALA | Nor nothing in your letters writ of her? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Nothing, Messala. |
| MESSALA | That methinks is strange. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why ask you? Hear you aught of her in yours? |
| MESSALA | No, my lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Now as you are a Roman, tell me true. |
| MESSALA | Then like a Roman bear the [[Truth|truth]] I tell;<br>For certain she is dead, and by strange manner. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why, farewell, [[Portia]]. We must die, Messala.<br>With meditating that she must die once,<br>I have the patience to endure it now. |
| MESSALA | Even so great men great losses should endure. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I have as much of this in art as you,<br>But yet my [[Nature|nature]] could not bear it so. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Well]], to our work alive. What do you think<br>Of marching to Philippi presently? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I do not think it good. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Your reason? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | This it is:<br>’Tis better that the enemy seek us;<br>So shall he waste his means, weary his soldiers,<br>Doing himself offence; whilst we, lying still,<br>Are full of rest, defence, and nimbleness. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Good reasons must of force give place to better.<br>The people ’twixt Philippi and this ground<br>Do stand but in a forced affection,<br>For they have grudged us contribution.<br>The enemy marching along by them<br>By them shall make a fuller number up,<br>Come on refreshed, new added, and encouraged;<br>From which advantage shall we cut him off,<br>If at Philippi we do face him there,<br>These people at our back. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Hear me, good brother. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Under your pardon. You must note beside<br>That we have tried the utmost of our friends;<br>Our legions are brim-full, our cause is ripe.<br>The enemy increaseth every day;<br>We at the height are ready to decline.<br>There is a tide in the affairs of men<br>Which, taken at the flood, leads on to fortune;<br>Omitted, all the voyage of their life<br>Is bound in shallows and in miseries.<br>On such a full sea are we now afloat,<br>And we must take the current when it serves,<br>Or lose our ventures. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Then, with your will, go on.<br>We’ll along ourselves, and meet them at Philippi. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | The deep of [[Night|night]] is crept upon our talk,<br>And [[Nature|nature]] must obey necessity,<br>Which we will niggard with a little rest.<br>There is no more to say. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | No more. Good [[Night|night]].<br>Early tomorrow will we rise and hence. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Lucius]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | My gown. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Lucius]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Farewell, good Messala.<br>Good [[Night|night]], Titinius. Noble, noble, [[Cassius]],<br>Good [[Night|night]] and good repose. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | O my dear brother,<br>This was an ill beginning of the [[Night|night]]!<br>Never come such division ’tween our souls.<br>Let it not, [[Brutus]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]] with the gown |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Everything is [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Good [[Night|night]], my lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Good [[Night|night]], good brother. |
| TITINIUS | and MESSALA<br>Good [[Night|night]], Lord [[Brutus]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Farewell, every one. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Cassius]], Titinius, and Messala |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Give me the gown. |
| *(didascalia)* | He puts on the gown |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Where is thy instrument? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Here in the tent. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What, thou speak’t drowsily.<br>Poor knave, I blame thee not; thou art o’erwatched.<br>Call [[Claudio]] and some other of my men.<br>I’ll have them [[Sleep|sleep]] on cushions in my tent. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Varrus and [[Claudio]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Varrus and [[Claudio]] |
| VARRUS | Calls my lord? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I pray you, sirs, lie in my tent and [[Sleep|sleep]].<br>It may be I shall raise you by and by<br>On business to my brother [[Cassius]]. |
| VARRUS | So please you, we will stand and watch your pleasure. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I will not have it so. Lie down, good sirs.<br>It may be I shall otherwise bethink me.<br>Varrus and [[Claudio]] lie down to [[Sleep|sleep]]<br><br>Look, [[Lucius]], here’s the book I sought for so.<br>I put it in the pocket of my gown. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | I was sure your lordship did not give it me. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Bear with me, good boy, I am much forgetful.<br>Canst thou hold up thy heavy eyes a while,<br>And touch thy instrument a strain or two? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Ay, my lord, an’t please you. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | It does, my boy.<br>I trouble thee too much, but thou art willing. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | It is my duty, sir. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I should not urge thy duty past thy might.<br>I know young bloods look for a [[Time|time]] of rest. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | I have slept, my lord, already. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | It was [[Well|well]] done, and thou shalt [[Sleep|sleep]] again.<br>I will not hold thee long. If I do live,<br>I will be good to thee.<br>[[Lucius]] plays music and sings a [[Song|song]], and so falls asleep<br>This is a sleepy tune. O murd‘rous slumber,<br>Lay’st thou thy leaden mace upon my boy<br>That plays thee music?—Gentle knave, good [[Night|night]].<br>I will not do thee so much wrong to wake thee.<br>If thou dost nod thou break’st thy instrument;<br>I’ll take it from thee, and, good boy, good [[Night|night]].<br>He takes away [[Lucius]]’ instrument, then opens the book<br>Let me see, let me see, is not the leaf turned down<br>Where I left reading? Here it is, I think. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Ghost]] of Caesar |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | How ill this taper burns! Ha! Who comes here?<br>I think it is the weakness of mine eyes<br>That shapes this monstrous apparition.<br>It comes upon me. Art thou any thing?<br>Art thou some god, some angel, or some devil,<br>That mak’st my [[Blood|blood]] cold and my hair to stare?<br>Speak to me what thou art. |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Thy evil spirit, [[Brutus]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why com’st thou? |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | To tell thee thou shalt see me at Philippi. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Well]]; then I shall see thee again? |
| [[Ghost|GHOST]] | Ay, at Philippi. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why, I will see thee at Philippi then. Exit [[Ghost]]<br><br>Now I have taken heart, thou vanishest.<br>Ill spirit, I would hold more talk with thee.—<br>Boy, [[Lucius]], Varrus, [[Claudio]], sirs, awake!<br>[[Claudio]]! |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | The strings, my lord, are false. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He thinks he still is at his instrument.—<br>[[Lucius]], awake! |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | My lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Didst thou dream, [[Lucius]], that thou so cried’st out? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | My lord, I do not know that I did cry. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Yes, that thou didst. Didst thou see anything? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Nothing, my lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Sleep]] again, [[Lucius]].—Sirrah [[Claudio]]!<br>(To Varrus) Fellow,<br>Thou, awake! |
| VARRUS | My lord. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | My lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why did you so cry out, sirs, in your [[Sleep|sleep]]? |
| BOTH | Did we, my lord? |
