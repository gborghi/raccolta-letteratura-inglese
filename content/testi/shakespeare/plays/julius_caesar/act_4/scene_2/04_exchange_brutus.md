|---|---|
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Lucius]], a bowl of wine. Exit [[Lucius]] |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I did not think you could have been so angry. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O [[Cassius]], I am sick of many griefs. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Of your [[philosophy]] you make no use, If you give place to accidental evils. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No man bears sorrow better. [[Portia]] is dead. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Ha! [[Portia]]? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | She is dead. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | How scaped I killing when I crossed you so? O insupportable and touching loss! Upon what sickness? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Impatience of my absence, And grief that young Octavius with [[Mark Antony]] Have made themselves so strong—for with her [[Death|death]] That tidings came. With this, she fell distraught, And, her attendants absent, swallowed [[Fire|fire]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And died so? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Even so. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Oye immortal gods! Enter [[Lucius]], with wine and tapers |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Speak no more of her. (To [[Lucius]]) Give me a bowl of wine. (To [[Cassius]]) In this I bury all unkindness, [[Cassius]]. He drinks |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | My heart is thirsty for that noble pledge. Fill, [[Lucius]], till the wine o’erswell the cup. I cannot drink too much of [[Brutus]]’ [[Love|love]]. He drinks. Exit [[Lucius]] Enter Titinius and Messala |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Come in, Titinius; welcome, good Messala. Now sit we close about this taper here, And call in question our necessities. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (aside) [[Portia]], art thou gone? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No more, I pray you. They sit Messala, I have here received letters That young Octavius and [[Mark Antony]] Come down upon us with a mighty power, Bending their expedition toward Philippi. |
| MESSALA | Myself have letters of the selfsame tenor. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | With what addition? |
| MESSALA | That by proscription and bills of outlawry Octavius, Antony, and [[Lepidus]] Have put to [[Death|death]] an hundred senators. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Therein our letters do not [[Well|well]] agree. Mine speak of seventy senators that died By their proscriptions, Cicero being one. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Cicero one? |
| MESSALA | Ay, Cicero is dead, And by that order of proscription. (To [[Brutus]]) Had you your letters from your wife, my lord? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No, Messala. |
| MESSALA | Nor nothing in your letters writ of her? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Nothing, Messala. |
| MESSALA | That methinks is strange. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why ask you? Hear you aught of her in yours? |
| MESSALA | No, my lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Now as you are a Roman, tell me true. |
| MESSALA | Then like a Roman bear the [[Truth|truth]] I tell; For certain she is dead, and by strange manner. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why, farewell, [[Portia]]. We must die, Messala. With meditating that she must die once, I have the patience to endure it now. |
| MESSALA | Even so great men great losses should endure. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I have as much of this in art as you, But yet my [[Nature|nature]] could not bear it so. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Well]], to our work alive. What do you think Of marching to Philippi presently? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I do not think it good. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Your reason? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | This it is: ’Tis better that the enemy seek us; So shall he waste his means, weary his soldiers, Doing himself offence; whilst we, lying still, Are full of rest, defence, and nimbleness. |
