| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Good reasons must of force give place to better. The people ’twixt Philippi and this ground Do stand but in a forced affection, For they have grudged us contribution. The enemy marching along by them By them shall make a fuller number up, Come on refreshed, new added, and encouraged; From which advantage shall we cut him off, If at Philippi we do face him there, These people at our back. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Hear me, good brother. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Under your pardon. You must note beside That we have tried the utmost of our friends; Our legions are brim-full, our cause is ripe. The enemy increaseth every day; We at the height are ready to decline. There is a tide in the affairs of men Which, taken at the flood, leads on to fortune; Omitted, all the voyage of their life Is bound in shallows and in miseries. On such a full sea are we now afloat, And we must take the current when it serves, Or lose our ventures. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Then, with your will, go on. We’ll along ourselves, and meet them at Philippi. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | The deep of [[Night|night]] is crept upon our talk, And [[Nature|nature]] must obey necessity, Which we will niggard with a little rest. There is no more to say. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | No more. Good [[Night|night]]. Early tomorrow will we rise and hence. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Lucius]]. Enter [[Lucius]] My gown. Exit [[Lucius]] Farewell, good Messala. Good [[Night|night]], Titinius. Noble, noble, [[Cassius]], Good [[Night|night]] and good repose. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | O my dear brother, This was an ill beginning of the [[Night|night]]! Never come such division ’tween our souls. Let it not, [[Brutus]]. Enter [[Lucius]] with the gown |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Everything is [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Good [[Night|night]], my lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Good [[Night|night]], good brother. |
| TITINIUS | and MESSALA Good [[Night|night]], Lord [[Brutus]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Farewell, every one. Exeunt [[Cassius]], Titinius, and Messala Give me the gown. He puts on the gown Where is thy instrument? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Here in the tent. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What, thou speak’t drowsily. Poor knave, I blame thee not; thou art o’erwatched. Call [[Claudio]] and some other of my men. I’ll have them [[Sleep|sleep]] on cushions in my tent. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Varrus and [[Claudio]]! Enter Varrus and [[Claudio]] |
| VARRUS | Calls my lord? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I pray you, sirs, lie in my tent and [[Sleep|sleep]]. It may be I shall raise you by and by On business to my brother [[Cassius]]. |
| VARRUS | So please you, we will stand and watch your pleasure. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I will not have it so. Lie down, good sirs. It may be I shall otherwise bethink me. Varrus and [[Claudio]] lie down to [[Sleep|sleep]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Look, [[Lucius]], here’s the book I sought for so. I put it in the pocket of my gown. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | I was sure your lordship did not give it me. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Bear with me, good boy, I am much forgetful. Canst thou hold up thy heavy eyes a while, And touch thy instrument a strain or two? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Ay, my lord, an’t please you. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | It does, my boy. I trouble thee too much, but thou art willing. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | It is my duty, sir. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I should not urge thy duty past thy might. I know young bloods look for a [[Time|time]] of rest. |
