| *(didascalia)* | Drum. Enter [[Brutus]], [[Lucius]], and the army. Lucillius, Titinius, and Pindarus meet them |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Stand, ho! SOLDIER Give the word ‘ho’, and stand. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What now, Lucillius: is [[Cassius]] near? |
| LUCILLIUS | He is at hand, and Pindarus is come To do you salutation from his master. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He greets me [[Well|well]]. Your master, Pindarus, In his own change or by ill officers, Hath given me some worthy cause to wish Things done undone. But if he be at hand, I shall be satisfied. |
| PINDARUS | I do not [[Doubt|doubt]] But that my noble master will appear Such as he is, full of regard and [[Honour|honour]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He is not doubted.—A word, Lucillius. [[Brutus]] and Lucillius speak apart |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | How he received you let me be resolved. |
| LUCILLIUS | With courtesy and with respect enough, But not with such familiar instances, Nor with such free and friendly conference, As he hath used of old. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Thou hast described A hot friend cooling. Ever note, Lucillius: When [[Love|love]] begins to sicken and [[Decay|decay]] It useth an enforced ceremony. There are no tricks in plain and simple [[Faith|faith]]; But hollow men, like horses hot at hand, Make gallant show and promise of their mettle; Low march within But when they should endure the bloody spur, They fall their crests and, like deceitful jades, Sink in the trial. Comes his army on? |
| LUCILLIUS | They mean this [[Night|night]] in Sardis to be quartered. The greater part, the [[Horse|horse]] in general, Are come with [[Cassius]]. Enter [[Cassius]] and his powers |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Hark, he is arrived. March gently on to meet him. The armies march |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Stand, ho! |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Stand, ho! Speak the word along. FIRST SOLDIER Stand! second SOLDIER Stand! THIRD SOLDIER Standl |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Most noble brother, you have done me wrong. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Judge me, you gods: wrong I mine enemies? And if not so, how should I wrong a brother? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Brutus]], this sober form of yours hides wrongs, And when you do them— |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Cassius]], be content. Speak your griefs softly. I do know you [[Well|well]]. Before the eyes of both our armies here, Which should perceive nothing but [[Love|love]] from us, Let us not wrangle. Bid them move away, Then in my tent, [[Cassius]], enlarge your griefs, And I will give you audience. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Pindarus, Bid our commanders lead their charges off A little from this ground. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Lucillius, do you the like; and let no man Come to our tent till we have done our conference. Let [[Lucius]] and Titinius guard our door. Exeunt the armies [[Brutus]] and [[Cassius]] remain, with Titinius, and [[Lucius]] guarding the door |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | That you have wronged me doth appear in this: You have condemned and noted [[Lucius]] Pella For taking bribes here of the Sardians, Wherein my letters praying on his side, Because I knew the man, was slighted off. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You wronged yourself to write in such a case. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | In such a [[Time|time]] as this it is not meet That every nice offence should bear his comment. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Let me tell you, [[Cassius]], you yourself Are much condemned to have an itching palm, To sell and mart your offices for gold To undeservers. |
