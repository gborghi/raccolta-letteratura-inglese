|---|---|
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I denied you not. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You did. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I did not. He was but a [[Fool|fool]] That brought my answer back. [[Brutus]] hath rived my heart. A friend should bear his friend’s infirmities, But [[Brutus]] makes mine greater than they are. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I do not, till you practise them on me. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | You [[Love|love]] me not. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I do not like your faults. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | A friendly eye could never see such faults. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | A flatterer’s would not, though they do appear As huge as high Olympus. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Come, Antony and young Octavius, come, [[Revenge]] yourselves [[Alone|alone]] on [[Cassius]]; For [[Cassius]] is aweary of the world, Hated by one he loves, braved by his brother, Checked like a bondman; all his faults observed, Set in a notebook, learned and conned by rote, To cast into my teeth. O, I could weep My spirit from mine eyes! There is my dagger, And here my naked breast; within, a heart Dearer than Pluto’s mine, richer than gold. If that thou beest a Roman, take it forth. I that denied thee gold will give my heart. Strike as thou didst at Caesar; for I know When thou didst hate him worst, thou loved‘st him better Than ever thou loved’st [[Cassius]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Sheathe your dagger. Be angry when you will; it shall have scope. Do what you will; dishonour shall be [[Humour|humour]]. O [[Cassius]], you are yoked with a [[Lamb|lamb]] That carries anger as the flint bears [[Fire|fire]], Who, much enforced, shows a hasty spark And straight is cold again. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Hath [[Cassius]] lived To be but mirth and laughter to his [[Brutus]] When grief and [[Blood|blood]] ill-tempered vexeth him? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | When I spoke that, I was ill-tempered too. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Do you confess so much? Give me your hand. |
| BRUTES | And my heart too. They embrace |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | O [[Brutus]]! |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What’s the matter? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Have not you [[Love|love]] enough to bear with me When that rash [[Humour|humour]] which my mother gave me Makes me forgetful? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Yes, [[Cassius]], and from henceforth, When you are over-earnest with your [[Brutus]], He’ll think your mother chides, and leave you so. Enter Lucillius and a Poet |
| POET | Let me go in to see the generals. There is some grudge between ’em; ’tis not meet They be [[Alone|alone]]. |
| LUCILLIUS | You shall not come to them. |
| POET | Nothing but [[Death|death]] shall stay me. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | How now! What’s the matter? |
| POET | For shame, you generals, what do you mean? [[Love]] and be friends, as two such men should be, For I have seen more years, I’m sure, than ye. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Ha, ha! How vilely doth this cynic rhyme! |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | (to the Poet) Get you hence, sirrah; saucy fellow, hence! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Bear with him, [[Brutus]], ’tis his fashion. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I’ll know his [[Humour|humour]] when he knows his [[Time|time]]. What should the wars do with these jigging fools? (To the Poet) Companion, hence! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (to the Poet) Away, away, be gone! Exit Poet |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Lucillius and Titinius, bid the commanders Prepare to lodge their companies tonight. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And come yourselves, and bring Messala with you Immediately to us. Exeunt Lucillius and Titinius |
