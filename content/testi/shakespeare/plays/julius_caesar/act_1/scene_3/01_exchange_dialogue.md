| *(didascalia)* | Thunder and [[Lightning|lightning]]. Enter [[Casca]], at one door, with his [[Sword|sword]] drawn, and Cicero at another |
| CICERO | Good even, [[Casca]]. Brought you Caesar [[Home|home]]? Why are you breathless, and why stare you so? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Are not you moved, when all the sway of earth Shakes like a thing unfirm? O Cicero, I have seen tempests when the scolding winds Have rived the knotty oaks, and I have seen Th‘ambitious ocean swell and rage and foam To be exalted with the threat’ning clouds; But never till tonight, never till now, Did I go through a tempest dropping [[Fire|fire]]. Either there is a civil strife in heaven, Or else the world, too saucy with the gods, Incenses them to send destruction. |
| CICERO | Why, saw you anything more wonderful? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | A common slave—you know him [[Well|well]] by sight— Held up his left hand, which did flame and burn Like twenty torches joined; and yet his hand, Not sensible of [[Fire|fire]], remained unscorched. Besides—I ha’ not since put up my [[Sword|sword]]— Against the Capitol I met a [[Lion|lion]] Who glazed upon me, and went surly by Without annoying me. And there were drawn Upon a heap a hundred ghastly women, Transformed with their [[Fear|fear]], who swore they saw Men all in [[Fire|fire]] walk up and down the streets. And yesterday the bird of [[Night|night]] did sit Even at noonday upon the market-place, Hooting and shrieking. When these prodigies Do so conjointly meet, let not men say ‘These are their reasons’, ‘they are natural’, For I believe they are portentous things Unto the climate that they point upon. |
| CICERO | Indeed it is a strange-disposed [[Time|time]]; But men may construe things after their fashion, Clean from the purpose of the things themselves. Comes Caesar to the Capitol tomorrow? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | He doth, for he did bid [[Antonio]] Send word to you he would be there tomorrow. |
| CICERO | Good [[Night|night]] then, [[Casca]]. This disturbed sky Is not to walk in. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Farewell, Cicero. Exit Cicero Enter [[Cassius]], unbraced |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Who’s there? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | A Roman. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Casca]], by your voice. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Your ear is good. [[Cassius]], what [[Night|night]] is this? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | A very pleasing [[Night|night]] to honest men. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Who ever knew the heavens menace so? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Those that have known the earth so full of faults. For my part, I have walked about the streets, Submitting me unto the perilous [[Night|night]]; And thus unbracèd, [[Casca]], as you see, Have bared my bosom to the thunder-stone; And when the [[Cross|cross]] blue [[Lightning|lightning]] seemed to open The breast of heaven, I did present myself Even in the aim and very flash of it. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | But wherefore did you so much tempt the heavens? It is the part of men to [[Fear|fear]] and tremble When the most mighty gods by tokens send Such dreadful heralds to astonish us. |
