| *(didascalia)* | Thunder and [[Lightning|lightning]]. Enter [[Casca]], at one door, with his [[Sword|sword]] drawn, and Cicero at another |
| CICERO | Good even, [[Casca]]. Brought you Caesar [[Home|home]]?<br>Why are you breathless, and why stare you so? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Are not you moved, when all the sway of earth<br>Shakes like a thing unfirm? O Cicero,<br>I have seen tempests when the scolding winds<br>Have rived the knotty oaks, and I have seen<br>Th‘ambitious ocean swell and rage and foam<br>To be exalted with the threat’ning clouds;<br>But never till tonight, never till now,<br>Did I go through a tempest dropping [[Fire|fire]].<br>Either there is a civil strife in heaven,<br>Or else the world, too saucy with the gods,<br>Incenses them to send destruction. |
| CICERO | Why, saw you anything more wonderful? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | A common slave—you know him [[Well|well]] by sight—<br>Held up his left hand, which did flame and burn<br>Like twenty torches joined; and yet his hand,<br>Not sensible of [[Fire|fire]], remained unscorched.<br>Besides—I ha’ not since put up my [[Sword|sword]]—<br>Against the Capitol I met a [[Lion|lion]]<br>Who glazed upon me, and went surly by<br>Without annoying me. And there were drawn<br>Upon a heap a hundred ghastly women,<br>Transformed with their [[Fear|fear]], who swore they saw<br>Men all in [[Fire|fire]] walk up and down the streets.<br>And yesterday the bird of [[Night|night]] did sit<br>Even at noonday upon the market-place,<br>Hooting and shrieking. When these prodigies<br>Do so conjointly meet, let not men say<br>‘These are their reasons’, ‘they are natural’,<br>For I believe they are portentous things<br>Unto the climate that they point upon. |
| CICERO | Indeed it is a strange-disposed [[Time|time]];<br>But men may construe things after their fashion,<br>Clean from the purpose of the things themselves.<br>Comes Caesar to the Capitol tomorrow? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | He doth, for he did bid [[Antonio]]<br>Send word to you he would be there tomorrow. |
| CICERO | Good [[Night|night]] then, [[Casca]]. This disturbed sky<br>Is not to walk in. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Farewell, Cicero. Exit Cicero |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Cassius]], unbraced |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Who’s there? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | A Roman. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Casca]], by your voice. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Your ear is good. [[Cassius]], what [[Night|night]] is this? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | A very pleasing [[Night|night]] to honest men. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Who ever knew the heavens menace so? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Those that have known the earth so full of faults.<br>For my part, I have walked about the streets,<br>Submitting me unto the perilous [[Night|night]];<br>And thus unbracèd, [[Casca]], as you see,<br>Have bared my bosom to the thunder-stone;<br>And when the [[Cross|cross]] blue [[Lightning|lightning]] seemed to open<br>The breast of heaven, I did present myself<br>Even in the aim and very flash of it. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | But wherefore did you so much tempt the heavens?<br>It is the part of men to [[Fear|fear]] and tremble<br>When the most mighty gods by tokens send<br>Such dreadful heralds to astonish us. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | You are dull, [[Casca]], and those sparks of life<br>That should be in a Roman you do want,<br>Or else you use not. You look pale, and gaze,<br>And put on [[Fear|fear]], and cast yourself in [[Wonder|wonder]],<br>To see the strange impatience of the heavens;<br>But if you would consider the true cause<br>Why all these fires, why all these gliding [[Ghosts|ghosts]],<br>Why [[Birds|birds]] and beasts from quality and kind–<br>Why old men, fools, and children calculate—<br>Why all these things change from their ordinance,<br>Their natures, and preformed faculties,<br>To monstrous quality—why, you shall find<br>That heaven hath infused them with these spirits<br>To make them instruments of [[Fear|fear]] and warning<br>Unto some monstrous state. Now could I, [[Casca]],<br>Name to thee a man most like this dreadful [[Night|night]],<br>That thunders, lightens, opens graves, and roars<br>As doth the [[Lion|lion]] in the Capitol;<br>A man no mightier than thyself or me<br>In personal action, yet prodigious grown,<br>And fearful, as these strange eruptions are. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | ‘Tis Caesar that you mean, is it not, [[Cassius]]? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Let it be who it is; for Romans now<br>Have thews and limbs like to their ancestors.<br>But woe the while! Our fathers’ minds are dead,<br>And we are governed with our mothers’ spirits.<br>Our yoke and sufferance show us womanish. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Indeed they say the senators tomorrow<br>Mean to establish Caesar as a [[King|king]],<br>And he shall wear his [[Crown|crown]] by sea and land<br>In every place save here in [[Italy]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (drawing his dagger)<br>I know where I will wear this dagger then:<br>[[Cassius]] from bondage will deliver [[Cassius]].<br>Therein, ye gods, you make the weak most strong;<br>Therein, ye gods, you tyrants do defeat.<br>Nor stony tower, nor walls of beaten brass,<br>Nor airless dungeon, nor strong links of iron,<br>Can be retentive to the strength of spirit;<br>But life, being weary of these worldly bars,<br>Never lacks power to dismiss itself.<br>If I know this, know all the world besides,<br>That part of tyranny that I do bear<br>I can shake off at pleasure. |
| *(didascalia)* | Thunder still |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | So can I.<br>So every bondman in his own hand bears<br>The power to cancel his captivity. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And why should Caesar be a [[Tyrant|tyrant]] then?<br>Poor man, I know he would not be a wolf<br>But that he sees the Romans are but sheep.<br>He were no [[Lion|lion]], were not Romans hinds.<br>Those that with haste will make a mighty [[Fire|fire]]<br>Begin it with weak straws. What trash is Rome,<br>What rubbish, and what offal, when it serves<br>For the base matter to illuminate<br>So vile a thing as Caesar! But, O grief,<br>Where hast thou led me? I perhaps speak this<br>Before a willing bondman; then I know<br>My answer must be made. But I am armed,<br>And dangers are to me indifferent. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | You speak to [[Casca]], and to such a man<br>That is no fleering tell-tale. Hold. My hand.<br>Be factious for redress of all these griefs,<br>And I will set this foot of mine as far<br>As who goes farthest.<br>They join hands |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | There’s a bargain made.<br>Now know you, [[Casca]], I have moved already<br>Some certain of the noblest-minded Romans<br>To undergo with me an enterprise<br>Of honourable-dangerous consequence.<br>And I do know by this they stay for me<br>In [[Pompey]]’s Porch; for now, this fearful [[Night|night]],<br>There is no stir or walking in the streets,<br>And the complexion of the element<br>In favour’s like the work we have in hand,<br>Most bloody, fiery, and most terrible. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Cinna |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Stand close a while, for here comes one in haste. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | ‘Tis Cinna; I do know him by his gait.<br>He is a friend.—Cinna, where haste you so? |
| CINNA | To find out you. Who’s that? Metellus Cimber? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | No, it is [[Casca]], one incorporate<br>To our attempts. Am I not stayed for, Cinna? |
| CINNA | I am glad on’t. What a fearful [[Night|night]] is this!<br>There’s two or three of us have seen strange sights. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Am I not stayed for? Tell me. |
| CINNA | Yes, you are.<br>O [[Cassius]], if you could<br>But win the noble [[Brutus]] to our party— |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Be you content. Good Cinna, take this paper,<br>He gives Cinna letters<br><br>And look you lay it in the Praetor’s Chair,<br>Where [[Brutus]] may but find it; and throw this<br>In at his [[Window|window]]. Set this up with wax<br>Upon old [[Brutus]]’ statue. All this done,<br>Repair to [[Pompey]]’s Porch where you shall find us.<br>Is Decius [[Brutus]] and Trebonius there? |
| CINNA | All but Metellus Cimber, and he’s gone<br>To seek you at your [[house]]. [[Well]], I will hie,<br>And so bestow these papers as you bade me. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | That done, repair to [[Pompey]]’s Theatre. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Cinna |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Come, [[Casca]], you and I will yet ere day<br>See [[Brutus]] at his [[house]]. Three parts of him<br>Is ours already, and the man entire<br>Upon the next encounter yields him ours. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | O, he sits high in all the people’s hearts,<br>And that which would appear offence in us<br>His countenance, like richest alchemy,<br>Will change to virtue and to worthiness. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Him and his worth, and our great need of him,<br>You have right [[Well|well]] conceited. Let us go,<br>For it is after midnight, and ere day<br>We will awake him and be sure of him. Exeunt |
