| OCTAVIUS | So I [[Hope|hope]]. I was not born to die on [[Brutus]]’ [[Sword|sword]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O, if thou wert the noblest of thy strain, Young man, thou couldst not die more honourable. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | A peevish schoolboy, worthless of such [[Honour|honour]], Joined with a masquer and a reveller! |
| ANTONY | Old [[Cassius]] still. |
| OCTAVIUS | Come, Antony, away. Defiance, traitors, hurl we in your teeth. If you dare fight today, come to the field. If not, when you have stomachs. Exeunt Octavius, Antony, and their army |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Why, now blow [[Wind|wind]], swell billow, and swim bark. The [[Storm|storm]] is up, and all is on the hazard. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Ho, Lucillius! Hark, a word with you. |
| LUCILLIUS | My lord. He stands forth, and speaks with [[Brutus]] |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Messala. |
| MESSALA | (standing forth) What says my general? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Messala, This is my birthday; as this very day Was [[Cassius]] born. Give me thy hand, Messala. Be thou my witness that, against my will, As [[Pompey]] was, am I compelled to set Upon one battle all our liberties. You know that I held Epicurus strong, And his opinion. Now I change my mind, And partly credit things that do presage. Coming from Sardis, on our former ensigns Two mighty eagles fell, and there they perched, Gorging and feeding from our soldiers’ hands, Who to Philippi here consorted us. This morning are they fled away and gone, And in their steads do ravens, crows, and kites Fly o’er our heads and downward look on us, As we were sickly prey. Their shadows seem A canopy most fatal, under which Our army lies ready to give the [[Ghost|ghost]]. |
| MESSALA | Believe not so. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I but believe it partly, For I am fresh of spirit, and resolved To meet all perils very constantly. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Even so, Lucillius. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (joining [[Brutus]]) Now, most noble [[Brutus]], The gods today stand friendly, that we may, [[lovers|Lovers]] in peace, lead on our days to age. But since the affairs of men rest still incertain, Let’s reason with the worst that may befall. If we do lose this battle, then is this The very last [[Time|time]] we shall speak together. What are you then determined to do? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Even by the rule of that [[philosophy]] By which I did blame Cato for the [[Death|death]] Which he did give himself—I know not how, But I do find it cowardly and vile For [[Fear|fear]] of what might fall so to prevent The [[Time|time]] of life—arming myself with patience To stay the [[Providence|providence]] of some high powers That govern us below. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Then if we lose this battle, You are contented to be led in triumph Thorough the streets of Rome? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No, [[Cassius]], no. Think not, thou noble Roman, That ever [[Brutus]] will go bound to Rome. He bears too great a mind. But this same day Must end that work the ides of March begun; And whether we shall meet again I know not. Therefore our everlasting farewell take. For ever and for ever farewell, [[Cassius]]. If we do meet again, why, we shall smile. If not, why then, this parting was [[Well|well]] made. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | For ever and for ever farewell, [[Brutus]]. If we do meet again, we’ll smile indeed. If not, ’tis true this parting was [[Well|well]] made. |
