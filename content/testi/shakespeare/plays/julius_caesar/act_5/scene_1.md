| *(didascalia)* | Enter Octavius, Antony, and their army |
| OCTAVIUS | Now, Antony, our hopes are answered.<br>You said the enemy would not come down,<br>But keep the hills and upper regions.<br>It proves not so; their battles are at hand.<br>They mean to warn us at Philippi here,<br>Answering before we do demand of them. |
| ANTONY | Tut, I am in their bosoms, and I know<br>Wherefore they do it. They could be content<br>To visit other places; and come down<br>With fearful bravery, thinking by this face<br>To fasten in our thoughts that they have [[Courage|courage]];<br>But ’tis not so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | Prepare you, generals.<br>The enemy comes on in gallant show.<br>Their bloody sign of battle is hung out,<br>And something to be done immediately. |
| ANTONY | Octavius, lead your battle softly on<br>Upon the left hand of the even field. |
| OCTAVIUS | Upon the right hand, I; keep thou the left. |
| ANTONY | Why do you [[Cross|cross]] me in this exigent? |
| OCTAVIUS | I do not [[Cross|cross]] you, but I will do so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Drum. Antony and Octavius march with their army. Drum within. Enter, marching, [[Brutus]], [[Cassius]], and their army, amongst them Titinius, Lucillius, and Messala. Octavius’ and Antony’s army makes a stand |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | They stand, and would have parley. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Stand fast, Titinius. We must out and talk.<br>[[Brutus]]’ and [[Cassius]]’ army makes a stand |
| OCTAVIUS | [[Mark Antony]], shall we give sign of battle? |
| ANTONY | No, Caesar, we will answer on their charge.<br>Make forth, the generals would have some words. |
| OCTAVIUS | (to his army)<br>Stir not until the signal.<br>Antony and Octavius meet [[Brutus]] and [[Cassius]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Words before blows: is it so, countrymen? |
| OCTAVIUS | Not that we [[Love|love]] words better, as you do. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Good words are better than bad strokes, Octavius. |
| ANTONY | In your bad strokes, [[Brutus]], you give good words.<br>Witness the hole you made in Caesar’s heart,<br>Crying ‘Long live, hail Caesar’. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Antony,<br>The posture of your blows are yet unknown;<br>But for your words, they rob the Hybla [[Bees|bees]],<br>And leave them honeyless. |
| ANTONY | Not stingless too. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O yes, and soundless too,<br>For you have stolen their buzzing, Antony,<br>And very wisely threat before you sting. |
| ANTONY | Villains, you did not so when your vile daggers<br>Hacked one another in the sides of Caesar.<br>You showed your teeth like apes, and fawned like<br>hounds,<br>And bowed like bondmen, kissing Caesar’s feet,<br>Whilst damnèd [[Casca]], like a cur, behind,<br>Struck Caesar on the neck. O you flatterers! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Flatterers? Now, [[Brutus]], thank yourself.<br>This tongue had not offended so today<br>If [[Cassius]] might have ruled. |
| OCTAVIUS | Come, come, the cause. If arguing make us sweat,<br>The proof of it will turn to redder drops.<br>He draws<br><br>Look, I draw a [[Sword|sword]] against conspirators.<br>When think you that the [[Sword|sword]] goes up again?<br>Never till Caesar’s three and thirty wounds<br>Be [[Well|well]] avenged, or till another Caesar<br>Have added slaughter to the swords of traitors. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Caesar, thou canst not die by traitors’ hands,<br>Unless thou bring’st them with thee. |
| OCTAVIUS | So I [[Hope|hope]].<br>I was not born to die on [[Brutus]]’ [[Sword|sword]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O, if thou wert the noblest of thy strain,<br>Young man, thou couldst not die more honourable. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | A peevish schoolboy, worthless of such [[Honour|honour]],<br>Joined with a masquer and a reveller! |
| ANTONY | Old [[Cassius]] still. |
| OCTAVIUS | Come, Antony, away.<br>Defiance, traitors, hurl we in your teeth.<br>If you dare fight today, come to the field.<br>If not, when you have stomachs. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Octavius, Antony, and their army |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Why, now blow [[Wind|wind]], swell billow, and swim bark.<br>The [[Storm|storm]] is up, and all is on the hazard. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Ho, Lucillius! Hark, a word with you. |
| LUCILLIUS | My lord.<br>He stands forth, and speaks with [[Brutus]] |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Messala. |
| MESSALA | (standing forth) What says my general? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Messala,<br>This is my birthday; as this very day<br>Was [[Cassius]] born. Give me thy hand, Messala.<br>Be thou my witness that, against my will,<br>As [[Pompey]] was, am I compelled to set<br>Upon one battle all our liberties.<br>You know that I held Epicurus strong,<br>And his opinion. Now I change my mind,<br>And partly credit things that do presage.<br>Coming from Sardis, on our former ensigns<br>Two mighty eagles fell, and there they perched,<br>Gorging and feeding from our soldiers’ hands,<br>Who to Philippi here consorted us.<br>This morning are they fled away and gone,<br>And in their steads do ravens, crows, and kites<br>Fly o’er our heads and downward look on us,<br>As we were sickly prey. Their shadows seem<br>A canopy most fatal, under which<br>Our army lies ready to give the [[Ghost|ghost]]. |
| MESSALA | Believe not so. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I but believe it partly,<br>For I am fresh of spirit, and resolved<br>To meet all perils very constantly. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Even so, Lucillius. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (joining [[Brutus]]) Now, most noble [[Brutus]],<br>The gods today stand friendly, that we may,<br>[[lovers|Lovers]] in peace, lead on our days to age.<br>But since the affairs of men rest still incertain,<br>Let’s reason with the worst that may befall.<br>If we do lose this battle, then is this<br>The very last [[Time|time]] we shall speak together.<br>What are you then determined to do? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Even by the rule of that [[philosophy]]<br>By which I did blame Cato for the [[Death|death]]<br>Which he did give himself—I know not how,<br>But I do find it cowardly and vile<br>For [[Fear|fear]] of what might fall so to prevent<br>The [[Time|time]] of life—arming myself with patience<br>To stay the [[Providence|providence]] of some high powers<br>That govern us below. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Then if we lose this battle,<br>You are contented to be led in triumph<br>Thorough the streets of Rome? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No, [[Cassius]], no.<br>Think not, thou noble Roman,<br>That ever [[Brutus]] will go bound to Rome.<br>He bears too great a mind. But this same day<br>Must end that work the ides of March begun;<br>And whether we shall meet again I know not.<br>Therefore our everlasting farewell take.<br>For ever and for ever farewell, [[Cassius]].<br>If we do meet again, why, we shall smile.<br>If not, why then, this parting was [[Well|well]] made. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | For ever and for ever farewell, [[Brutus]].<br>If we do meet again, we’ll smile indeed.<br>If not, ’tis true this parting was [[Well|well]] made. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why then, lead on. O that a man might know<br>The end of this day’s business ere it come!<br>But it sufliceth that the day will end,<br>And then the end is known.—Come, ho, away! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
