| *(didascalia)* | Enter at one door Artemidorus, the Soothsayer, |
| *(didascalia)* | and citizens. Flourish. Enter at another door Caesar, [[Brutus]], [[Cassius]], [[Casca]], Decius, Metellus, Trebonius, Cinna, Ligarius, Antony, [[Lepidus]], Publius, Popillius, and other senators |
| CAESAR | (to the Soothsayer) The ides of March are come. |
| SOOTHSAYER | Ay, Caesar, but not gone. |
| ARTEMIDORUS | Hail, Caesar! Read this schedule. |
| DECIUS | (to Caesar)<br>Trebonius doth [[Desire|desire]] you to o’er-read<br>At your best leisure this his humble suit. |
| ARTEMIDORUS | O Caesar, read mine first, for mine’s a suit<br>That touches Caesar nearer. Read it, great Caesar. |
| CAESAR | What touches us ourself shall be last served. |
| ARTEMIDORUS | Delay not, Caesar, read it instantly. |
| CAESAR | What, is the fellow mad? |
| PUBLIUS | (to Artemidorus)<br>Sirrah, give place. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (to Artemidorus)<br>What, urge you your petitions in the street?<br>Come to the Capitol. |
| *(didascalia)* | They walk about the stage |
| POPILLIUS | (aside to [[Cassius]])<br>I wish your enterprise today may thrive. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | What enterprise, Popillius? |
| POPILLIUS | Fare you [[Well|well]].<br>He [[Leaves|leaves]] [[Cassius]], and makes to Caesar |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What said Popillius Laena? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | He wished today our enterprise might thrive.<br>I [[Fear|fear]] our purpose is discovered. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Look how he makes to Caesar. Mark him. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Casca]], be sudden, for we [[Fear|fear]] prevention.—<br>[[Brutus]], what shall be done? If this be known,<br>[[Cassius]] or Caesar never shall turn back,<br>For I will slay myself. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Cassius]], be constant.<br>Popillius Laena speaks not of our purposes,<br>For look, he smiles, and Caesar doth not change. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Trebonius knows his [[Time|time]], for look you, [[Brutus]],<br>He draws [[Mark Antony]] out of the way. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Trebonius and Antony |
| DECIUS | Where is Metellus Cimber? Let him go<br>And presently prefer his suit to Caesar. |
| *(didascalia)* | Caesar sits |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He is addressed. Press near, and second him. |
| CINNA | [[Casca]], you are the first that rears your hand. |
| *(didascalia)* | The conspirators and the other senators take their places |
| CAESAR | Are we all ready? What is now amiss<br>That Caesar and his Senate must redress? |
| METELLUS | (coming forward and kneeling)<br>Most high, most mighty, and most puissant Caesar,<br>Metellus Cimber throws before thy seat<br>An humble heart. |
| CAESAR | I must prevent thee, Cimber.<br>These couchings and these lowly courtesies<br>Might [[Fire|fire]] the [[Blood|blood]] of ordinary men,<br>And turn preordinance and first decree<br>Into the law of children. Be not fond<br>To think that Caesar bears such [[Rebel|rebel]] [[Blood|blood]]<br>That will be thawed from the true quality<br>With that which melteth fools: I mean sweet words,<br>Low-crooked curtsies, and base spaniel fawning.<br>Thy brother by decree is banished.<br>If thou dost bend and pray and fawn for him,<br>I spurn thee like a cur out of my way.<br>Know Caesar doth not wrong but with just cause,<br>Nor without cause will he be satisfied. |
| METELLUS | Is there no voice more worthy than my own<br>To sound more sweetly in great Caesar’s ear<br>For the repealing of my banished brother? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | (coming forward and kneeling)<br>I kiss thy hand, but not in flattery, Caesar,<br>Desiring thee that Publius Cimber may<br>Have an immediate [[Freedom|freedom]] of repeal. |
| CAESAR | What, [[Brutus]]? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (coming forward and kneeling)<br>Pardon, Caesar; Caesar, pardon.<br>As low as to thy foot doth [[Cassius]] fall<br>To beg enfranchisement for Publius Cimber. |
| CAESAR | I could be [[Well|well]] moved if I were as you.<br>If I could pray to move, prayers would move me.<br>But I am constant as the Northern Star,<br>Of whose true fixed and resting quality<br>There is no fellow in the firmament.<br>The skies are painted with unnumbered sparks;<br>They are all [[Fire|fire]], and every one doth shine;<br>But there’s but one in all doth hold his place.<br>So in the world: ‘tis furnished [[Well|well]] with men,<br>And men are flesh and [[Blood|blood]], and apprehensive;<br>Yet in the number I do know but one<br>That unassailable holds on his rank,<br>Unshaked of motion; and that I am he<br>Let me a little show it even in this—<br>That I was constant Cimber should be banished,<br>And constant do remain to keep him so. |
| CINNA | (coming forward and kneeling) O Caesar! |
| CAESAR | Hence! Wilt thou lift up Olympus? |
| *(didascalia)* | DECIUS (coming forward With Ligarius and kneeling) Great Caesar! |
| CAESAR | Doth not [[Brutus]] bootless kneel? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Casca|CASCA]] (coming forward and kneeling) |
| CAESAR | Speak hands for me. |
| *(didascalia)* | They stab Caesar, [[Casca]] first, [[Brutus]] last |
| CAESAR | Et tu, Bruté?—Then fall Caesar. He dies |
| CINNA | Liberty! [[Freedom]]! Tyranny is dead!<br>Run hence, proclaim, cry it about the streets. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Some to the common pulpits, and cry out<br>‘Liberty, [[Freedom|freedom]], and enfranchisement!’ |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | People and senators, be not affrighted. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt in a tumult [[Lepidus]], Popillius, other senators, Artemidorus, Soothsayer, and citizen |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Fly not! Stand still! [[Ambition]]’s debt is paid. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Go to the pulpit, [[Brutus]]. |
| DECIUS | And [[Cassius]] too. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Where’s Publius? |
| CINNA | Here, quite confounded with this mutiny. |
| METELLUS | Stand fast together, lest some friend of Caesar’s<br>Should chance—- |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Talk not of standing.—Pubtius, good cheer!<br>There is no harm intended to your person,<br>Nor to no Roman else—so tell them, Publius. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And leave us, Publius, lest that the people,<br>Rushing on us, should do your age some mischief. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Do so; and let no man abide this deed<br>But we the doers. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Publius Enter Trebonius |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Where is Antony? |
| TREBONIUS | Fled to his [[house]], amazed.<br>Men, wives, and children stare, cry out, and run,<br>As it were doomsday. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Fates, we will know your pleasures.<br>That we shall die, we know; ‘tis but the [[Time|time]]<br>And drawing days out that men stand upon. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Why, he that cuts off twenty years of life<br>Cuts off so many years of fearing [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Grant that, and then is [[Death|death]] a benefit.<br>So are we Caesar’s friends, that have abridged<br>His [[Time|time]] of fearing [[Death|death]]. Stoop, Romans, stoop,<br>And let us bathe our hands in Caesar’s [[Blood|blood]]<br>Up to the elbows, and besmear our swords;<br>Then walk we forth even to the market-place,<br>And, waving our red weapons o‘er our heads,<br>Let’s all cry ‘peace, [[Freedom|freedom]], and liberty!’ |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Stoop, then, and wash.<br>They smear their hands with Caesar’s [[Blood|blood]] How many ages hence<br>Shall this our lofty scene be acted over,<br>In states [[unborn]] and accents yet unknown! |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | How many times shall Caesar bleed in sport,<br>That now on [[Pompey]]’s basis lies along,<br>No worthier than the [[Dust|dust]]! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | So oft as that shall be,<br>So often shall the knot of us be called<br>The men that gave their country liberty. |
| DECIUS | What, shall we forth? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Ay, every man away.<br>[[Brutus]] shall lead, and we will grace his heels<br><br>With the most boldest and best hearts of Rome. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Antony’s Servant |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Soft; who comes here? A friend of Antony’s. |
| SERVANT | (kneeling and falling prostrate)<br>Thus, [[Brutus]], did my master bid me kneel.<br>Thus did [[Mark Antony]] bid me fall down,<br>And, being prostrate, thus he bade me say.<br>‘[[Brutus]] is noble, wise, valiant, and honest.<br>Caesar was mighty, bold, royal, and loving.<br>Say I [[Love|love]] [[Brutus]], and I [[Honour|honour]] him.<br>Say I feared Caesar, honoured him, and loved him.<br>If [[Brutus]] will vouchsafe that Antony<br>May safely come to him and be resolved<br>How Caesar hath deserved to lie in [[Death|death]],<br>[[Mark Antony]] shall not [[Love|love]] Caesar dead<br>So [[Well|well]] as [[Brutus]] living, but will follow<br>The fortunes and affairs of noble [[Brutus]]<br>Thorough the hazards of this untrod state<br>With all true [[Faith|faith]].’ So says my master Antony. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Thy master is a wise and valiant Roman.<br>I never thought him worse.<br>Tell him, so please him come unto this place,<br>He shall be satisfied, and, by my [[Honour|honour]],<br>Depart untouched. |
| *(didascalia)* | SERVANT rising I’ll fetch him presently. Exit |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I know that we shall have him [[Well|well]] to friend. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I wish we may. But yet have I a mind<br>That fears him much; and my misgiving still<br>Falls shrewdly to the purpose. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Antony |
| BRUTES | But here comes Antony.—Welcome, [[Mark Antony]]. |
| ANTONY | O mighty Caesarl Dost thou lie so low?<br>Are all thy conquests, glories, triumphs, spoils,<br>Shrunk to this little measure? Fare thee [[Well|well]].—<br>I know not, gentlemen, what you intend—<br>Who else must be let [[Blood|blood]], who else is rank.<br>If I myself, there is no hour so fit<br>As Caesar’s [[Death|death]]’s hour, nor no instrument<br>Of half that worth as those your swords, made rich<br>With the most noble [[Blood|blood]] of all this world.<br>I do beseech ye, if you bear me hard,<br>Now, whilst your purpled hands do reek and smoke,<br>Fulfil your pleasure. Live a thousand years,<br>I shall not find myself so apt to die.<br>No place will please me so, no mean of [[Death|death]],<br>As here by Caesar, and by you cut off,<br>The choice and master spirits of this age. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O Antony, beg not your [[Death|death]] of us!<br>Though now we must appear bloody and cruel,<br>As by our hands and this our present act<br>You see we do, yet see you but our hands,<br>And this the bleeding business they have done.<br>Our hearts you see not; they are pitiful;<br>And pity to the general wrong of Rome—<br>As [[Fire|fire]] drives out [[Fire|fire]], so pity pity–<br>Hath done this deed on Caesar. For your part,<br>To you our swords have leaden points, [[Mark Antony]].<br>Our arms, unstrung of malice, and our hearts<br>Of brothers’ temper, do receive you in<br><br>With all kind [[Love|love]], good thoughts, and reverence. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Your voice shall be as strong as any man’s<br>In the disposing of new dignities. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Only be patient till we have appeased<br>The multitude, beside themselves with [[Fear|fear]],<br>And then we will deliver you the cause<br>Why I, that did [[Love|love]] Caesar when I struck him,<br>Have thus proceeded. |
| ANTONY | I [[Doubt|doubt]] not of your wisdom.<br>Let each man render me his bloody hand.<br>He shakes hands with the conspirators<br><br>First, Marcus [[Brutus]], will I shake with you.—<br>Next, Caius [[Cassius]], do I take your hand.—<br>Now, Decius [[Brutus]], yours;—now yours, Metellus;—<br>Yours, Cinna;—and my valiant [[Casca]], yours;—<br>Though last, not least in [[Love|love]], yours, good Trebonius.<br>Gentlemen all—alas, what shall I say?<br>My credit now stands on such slippery ground<br>That one of two bad ways you must conceit me:<br>Either a coward or a flatterer.<br>That I did [[Love|love]] thee, Caesar, O, ’tis true.<br>If then thy spirit look upon us now,<br>Shall it not grieve thee dearer than thy [[Death|death]]<br>To see thy Antony making his peace,<br>Shaking the bloody fingers of thy foes—<br>Most noble!—in the presence of thy corpse?<br>Had I as many eyes as thou hast wounds,<br>Weeping as fast as they stream forth thy [[Blood|blood]],<br>It would become me better than to close<br>In terms of [[Friendship|friendship]] with thine enemies.<br>Pardon me, Julius. Here wast thou bayed, brave hart;<br>Here didst thou fall, and here thy hunters stand<br>Signed in thy spoil and crimsoned in thy lethe.<br>O world, thou wast the forest to this hart;<br>And this indeed, O world, the heart of thee.<br>How like a deer strucken by many princes<br>Dost thou here lie! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Mark Antony]]. |
| ANTONY | Pardon me, Caius [[Cassius]].<br>The enemies of Caesar shall say this;<br>Then in a friend it is cold modesty. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I blame you not for praising Caesar so;<br>But what compact mean you to have with us?<br>Will you be pricked in number of our friends,<br>Or shall we on, and not depend on you? |
| ANTONY | Therefore I took your hands, but was indeed<br>Swayed from the point by looking down on Caesar.<br>Friends am I with you all, and [[Love|love]] you all<br>Upon this [[Hope|hope]]: that you shall give me reasons<br>Why and wherein Caesar was dangerous. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Or else were this a savage spectacle.<br>Our reasons are so full of good regard,<br>That were you, Antony, the son of Caesar,<br>You should be satisfied. |
| ANTONY | That’s all I seek;<br>And am, moreover, suitor that I may<br>Produce his [[Body|body]] to the market-place,<br>And in the pulpit, as becomes a friend,<br>Speak in the order of his funeral. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You shall, [[Mark Antony]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | [[Brutus]], a word with you.<br>(Aside to [[Brutus]]) You know not what you do. Do not<br>consent<br>That Antony speak in his funeral.<br>Know you how much the people may be moved<br>By that which he will utter? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | (aside to [[Cassius]]) By your pardon,<br>I will myself into the pulpit first,<br>And show the reason of our Caesar’s [[Death|death]].<br>What Antony shall speak I will protest<br>He speaks by leave and by permission;<br>And that we are contented Caesar shall<br>Have all true rites and lawful ceremonies,<br>It shall advantage more than do us wrong. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (aside to [[Brutus]])<br>I know not what may fall. I like it not. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Mark Antony]], here, take you Caesar’s [[Body|body]].<br>You shall not in your funeral speech blame us;<br>But speak all good you can devise of Caesar,<br>And say you do’t by our permission;<br>Else shall you not have any hand at all<br>About his funeral. And you shall speak<br>In the same pulpit whereto I am going,<br>After my speech is ended. |
| ANTONY | Be it so;<br>I do [[Desire|desire]] no more. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Prepare the [[Body|body]] then, and follow us. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Antony |
| ANTONY | O pardon me, thou bleeding piece of earth,<br>That I am meek and gentle with these butchers.<br>Thou art the ruins of the noblest man<br>That ever lived in the tide of times.<br>Woe to the hand that shed this costly [[Blood|blood]]!<br>Over thy wounds now do I prophesy—<br>Which like dumb mouths do ope their ruby lips<br>To beg the voice and utterance of my tongue—<br>A curse shall light upon the limbs of men;<br>Domestic fury and fierce civil strife<br>Shall cumber all the parts of [[Italy]];<br>[[Blood]] and destruction shall be so in use,<br>And dreadful objects so familiar,<br>That mothers shall but smile when they behold<br>Their infants quartered with the hands of war,<br>All pity choked with custom of fell deeds;<br>And Caesar’s spirit, ranging for [[Revenge|revenge]],<br>With Ate by his side come hot from hell,<br>Shall in these confines with a monarch’s voice<br>Cry ‘havoc!’ and let slip the [[dogs]] of war,<br>That this foul deed shall smell above the earth<br>With carrion men, groaning for burial. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Octavius’ Servant |
| ANTONY | You serve [[Octavius Caesar]], do you not? |
| SERVANT | I do, [[Mark Antony]]. |
| ANTONY | Caesar did write for him to come to Rome. |
| SERVANT | He did receive his letters, and is coming,<br>And bid me say to you by word of mouth—<br>(Seeing the [[Body|body]]) O Caesar! |
| ANTONY | Thy heart is big. Get thee apart and weep.<br>Passion, I see, is catching, for mine eyes,<br>Seeing those beads of sorrow stand in thine,<br>Began to [[Water|water]]. Is thy master coming? |
| SERVANT | He lies tonight within seven leagues of Rome. |
| ANTONY | Post back with [[Speed|speed]] and tell him what hath<br>chanced.<br>Here is a mourning Rome, a dangerous Rome,<br>No Rome of safety for Octavius yet.<br>Hie hence and tell him so.—Yet stay awhile.<br>Thou shalt not back till I have borne this corpse<br>Into the market-place. There shall I try<br>In my oration how the people take<br>The cruel issue of these bloody men;<br>According to the which thou shalt discourse<br>To young Octavius of the state of things.<br>Lend me your hand. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt with Caesar’s [[Body|body]] |
