| ANTONY | If you have [[Tears|tears]], prepare to shed them now. You all do know this mantle. I remember The first [[Time|time]] ever Caesar put it on. ’Twas on a [[Summer|summer]]’s evening in his tent, That day he overcame the Nervii. Look, in this place ran [[Cassius]]’ dagger through. See what a rent the envious [[Casca]] made. Through this the [[Well|well]]-beloved [[Brutus]] stabbed; And as he plucked his cursèd steel away, Mark how the [[Blood|blood]] of Caesar followed it, As rushing out of doors to be resolved If [[Brutus]] so unkindly knocked or no— For [[Brutus]], as you know, was Caesar’s angel. Judge, O you gods, how dearly Caesar loved him! This was the most unkindest cut of all. For when the noble Caesar saw him stab, Ingratitude, more strong than traitors’ arms, Quite vanquished him. Then burst his mighty heart, And in his mantle muffling up his face, Even at the base of [[Pompey]]’s statue, Which all the while ran [[Blood|blood]], great Caesar fell. O, what a fall was there, my countrymen! Then I, and you, and all of us fell down, Whilst bloody treason flourished over us. O now you weep, and I perceive you feel The dint of pity. These are gracious drops. Kind souls, what, weep you when you but behold Our Caesar’s vesture wounded? Look you here. Here is himself, marred, as you see, with traitors. He uncovers Caesar’s [[Body|body]] |
