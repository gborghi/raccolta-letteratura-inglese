| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No, not an oath. If not the face of men, The sufferance of our souls, the [[Time|time]]’s abuse— If these be motives weak, break off betimes, And every man hence to his idle bed. So let high-sighted tyranny range on Till each man drop by lottery. But if these, As I am sure they do, bear [[Fire|fire]] enough To kindle cowards and to steel with valour The melting spirits of women, then, countrymen, What need we any spur but our own cause To prick us to redress? What other bond Than secret Romans, that have spoke the word And will not palter? And what other oath Than honesty to honesty engaged That this shall be or we will fall for it? Swear priests and cowards and men cautelous, Old feeble carrions, and such [[Suffering|suffering]] souls That welcome wrongs; unto bad causes swear Such creatures as men [[Doubt|doubt]]; but do not stain The even virtue of our enterprise, Nor th’insuppressive mettle of our spirits, To think that or our cause or our performance Did need an oath, when every drop of [[Blood|blood]] That every Roman bears, and nobly bears, Is guilty of a several bastardy If he do break the smallest particle Of any promise that hath passed from him. |
