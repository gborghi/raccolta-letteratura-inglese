<span class="atom-split" data-atom="l0145_mr_samuel_bowlers_and_mrs_bowles_sunday_night" data-title="To Mr. Samuel Bowlers and Mrs. Bowles, Sunday Night" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


DEAR MARY,—Could you leave “Charlie” long enough? Have you [[Time|time]] for me? I sent Mr. Bowles a little note, last Saturday morning, asking him to do an errand for me.

I forgot he was going to Washington, or I shouldn't have troubled him, so late. Now, Mary, I [[Fear|fear]] he did not get it, and you tried to do the errand for me—and it troubled you. Did it? Will you tell me? Just say with your pencil “It didn't tire me, Emily,” and then I shall be sure, for with all your care, I would not have taxed you for the world.

You never refused me, Mary, you cherished me many times, but I thought it must seem so selfish to ask the favor of Mr. Bowles just as he went from [[Home|home]], only I forgot that. Tell me to-[[Night|night]] just a word, Mary, with your own hand, so I shall know I harassed none—and I will be so glad.

Austin told us of Charlie—I send a [[Rose|rose]] for his small hands.

Put it in, when he goes to [[Sleep|sleep]], and then he will dream of Emily, and when you bring him to Amherst we shall be “old friends.” Don't [[Love|love]] him so [[Well|well]], you know, as to forget us. We shall wish he wasn't there, if you do, I'm afraid, sha'n't we?

I'll remember you, if you like me to, while Mr. Bowles is gone, and that will stop the lonely, some, but I cannot agree to stop when he gets [[Home|home]] from Washington.

Good-[[Night|night]], Mary. You won't forget my little note, tomorrow, in the mail. It will be the first one you ever wrote me in your life, and yet, was I the little friend a long [[Time|time]]? Was I, Mary?
