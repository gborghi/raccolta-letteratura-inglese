<span class="atom-split" data-atom="j_g_holland_and_mrs_holland_1857" data-title="To Dr. J. G. Holland, and Mrs. Holland, 1857?" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/death,motif/garden,motif/spring,motif/river,motif/animals,concept/friendship,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/immortality_of_soul,concept/mortality,form/letter,histref/the_bible,setting/a_garden,character/adam,character/david,character/goliath,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


DEAR SISTER,—After you went, a low [[motifs/wind|wind]] warbled through the house like a spacious bird, making it high but lonely. When you had gone the [[motifs/love|love]] came. I supposed it would. The supper of the heart is when the guest has gone.

Shame is so intrinsic in a strong affection we must all experience Adam's reticence. I suppose the street that the [[archetypes/lover|lover]] travels is thenceforth divine, incapable of turnpike aims.

That you be with me annuls [[concepts/fear|fear]] and I await Commencement with merry [[concepts/resignation|resignation]]. Smaller than David you clothe me with extreme Goliath.

Friday I tasted life. It was a vast morsel. A circus passed the house—still I feel the red in my mind though the drums are out.

[[motifs/the-book|The book]] you mention, I have not met. Thank you for tenderness.

The lawn is full of south and the odors tangle, and I hear to-day for the first the [[motifs/river|river]] in the [[motifs/tree|tree]].

You mentioned [[motifs/spring|spring]]'s delaying—I blamed her for the opposite. I would eat evanescence slowly.

Vinnie is deeply afflicted in the [[motifs/death|death]] of her dappled cat, though I convince her it is immortal which assists her some. Mother resumes lettuce, involving my transgression—suggestive of yourself, however, which endears disgrace.

“House” is being “cleaned.” I prefer pestilence. That is more classic and less fell.

Yours was my first arbutus. It was a rosy boast.

I will send you the first [[characters/witch|witch]] hazel.

A woman died last week, young and in [[concepts/hope|hope]] but a little while—at the end of our [[motifs/garden|garden]]. I thought since of the power of [[motifs/death|Death]], not upon affection, but its mortal signal. It is to us the Nile.

You refer to the unpermitted delight to be with those we [[motifs/love|love]]. I suppose that to be the license not granted of [[characters/god|God]].

Count not that far that can be had, Though sunset lie between— Nor that adjacent, that beside, Is further than the [[motifs/sun|sun]].

[[motifs/love|Love]] for your embodiment of it.




<span class="atom-split" data-atom="j_g_holland_and_mrs_holland_autumn_1859" data-title="To Dr. J. G. Holland, and Mrs. Holland, Autumn, 1859" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/autumn,motif/frost,motif/flowers,motif/birds,motif/death,concept/friendship,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/mortality,concept/endurance,form/letter,setting/new_england,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


DEAR HOLLANDS,—Belong to me! We have no fires yet, and the evenings grow cold. To-morrow, stoves are set. How many barefoot shiver I trust their Father knows who saw not fit to give them shoes.

Vinnie is sick to-[[motifs/night|night]], which gives the world a russet tinge, usually so red. It is only a headache, but when the head aches next to you, it becomes important. When she is [[motifs/well|well]], [[motifs/time|time]] leaps. When she is ill, he lags, or stops entirely.

Sisters are brittle things. [[characters/god|God]] was penurious with me, which makes me shrewd with Him.

One is a dainty sum! One bird, one cage, one flight; one song in those far woods, as yet suspected by [[concepts/faith|faith]] only!

This is September, and you were coming in September. Come! Our parting is too long. There has been [[motifs/frost|frost]] enough. We must have [[motifs/summer|summer]] now, and “whole legions” of daisies.

The gentian is a greedy flower, and overtakes us all. Indeed, this world is short, and I wish, until I tremble, to touch the ones I [[motifs/love|love]] before the hills are red—are gray—are white—are “born again”! If we knew how deep the crocus lay, we never should let her go. Still, crocuses stud many mounds whose gardeners till in anguish some tiny, vanished bulb.

We saw you that Saturday afternoon, but heedlessly forgot to ask where you were going, so did not know, and could not write. Vinnie saw Minnie flying by, one afternoon at Palmer. She supposed you were all there on your way from the [[motifs/sea|sea]], and untied her fancy! To say that her fancy wheedled her is superfluous.

We talk of you together, then diverge on life, then hide in you again, as a safe fold. Don't leave us long, dear friends! You know we're children still, and children [[concepts/fear|fear]] the dark.

Are you [[motifs/well|well]] at [[motifs/home|home]]? Do you work now? Has it altered much since I was there? Are the children women, and the women thinking it will soon be afternoon? We will help each other bear our unique burdens.

Is Minnie with you now? Take her our [[motifs/love|love]], if she is. Do her eyes grieve her now? Tell her she may have half ours.

Mother's favorite sister is sick, and mother will have to bid her good-[[motifs/night|night]]. It brings mists to us all;—the aunt whom Vinnie visits, with whom she spent, I [[concepts/fear|fear]], her last inland [[motifs/christmas|Christmas]]. Does [[characters/god|God]] take care of those at [[motifs/sea|sea]]? My aunt is such a timid woman!

Will you write to us? I bring you all their loves—many.

They tire me.




<span class="atom-split" data-atom="j_g_holland_and_mrs_holland_1864" data-title="To Dr. J. G. Holland, and Mrs. Holland, 1864?" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="archetype/death_personified,motif/death,motif/autumn,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/mortality,concept/friendship,concept/truth,form/letter,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


DEAR SISTER,—Father called to say that our steelyard was fraudulent, exceeding by an ounce the rates of honest men. He had been selling oats. I cannot stop smiling, though it is hours since, that even our steelyard will not tell the [[concepts/truth|truth]].

Besides wiping the dishes for Margaret, I wash them now, while she becomes Mrs. Lawler, vicarious papa to four previous babes. Must she not be an adequate bride?

I winced at her loss, because I was in the habit of her, and even a new rolling-pin has an embarrassing element, but to all except anguish, the mind soon adjusts.

It is also November. The noons are more laconic and the sundowns sterner, and Gibraltar lights make the village foreign. November always seemed to me the Norway of the year.——is still with the sister who put her [[archetypes/child|child]] in an ice nest last Monday forenoon. The redoubtable [[characters/god|God]]! I notice where [[motifs/death|Death]] has been introduced, he frequently calls, making it desirable to forestall his advances.

It is hard to be told by the papers that a friend is failing, not even know where the [[motifs/water|water]] lies. Incidentally, only, that he comes to land. Is there no voice for these? Where is [[motifs/love|Love]] to-day?

Tell the dear Doctor we mention him with a foreign accent, party already to transactions spacious and untold. Nor have we omitted to breathe shorter for our little sister. Sharper than dying is the [[motifs/death|death]] for the dying's sake.

News of these would comfort, when convenient or possible.




<span class="atom-split" data-atom="j_g_holland_and_mrs_holland" data-title="To Dr. J. G. Holland, and Mrs. Holland" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/death,motif/home,concept/friendship,concept/mortality,concept/equality_in_death,concept/grief_and_loss,form/letter,histref/the_bible,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


DEAR SISTER,—It was incredibly sweet that Austin had seen you, and had stood in the dear house which had lost its friend. To see one who had seen you was a strange assurance. It helped dispel the [[concepts/fear|fear]] that you departed too, for notwithstanding the loved notes and the lovely gift, there lurked a dread that you had gone or would seek to go. “Where the treasure is,” there is the prospective.

Austin spoke very warmly and strongly of you, and we all felt firmer, and drew a vocal portrait of Kate at Vinnie's request, so vivid that we saw her. . . .

Not all die early, dying young, Maturity of fate Is consummated equally In ages or a [[motifs/night|night]]. A hoary boy I've known to drop Whole-statured, by the side Of junior of fourscore—'t was act, Not period, that died.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="_misses_april_1860" data-title="To the Misses, April, 1860" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="topos/ubi_sunt,motif/death,motif/birds,motif/flowers,motif/summer,motif/sleep,concept/death,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/immortality_divine,concept/faith_and_doubt,form/letter,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


VINNIE,—I can't believe it, when your letters come, saying what Aunt L——said “just before she died.” Blessed Aunt L——now; all the world goes out, and I see nothing but her room, and angels bearing her into those great countries in the blue sky of which we don't know anything.

Then I sob and cry till I can hardly see my way 'round the house again; and then sit still and [[concepts/wonder|wonder]] if she sees us now, if she sees me, who said that she “loved Emily.” Oh! Vinnie, it is dark and strange to think of [[motifs/summer|summer]] afterward! How she loved the [[motifs/summer|summer]]! The [[motifs/birds|birds]] keep singing just the same. Oh! The thoughtless [[motifs/birds|birds]]!

Poor little L——! Poor F——! You must comfort them!

If you were with me, Vinnie, we could talk about her together.

And I thought she would live! I wanted her to live so, I thought she could not die! To think how still she lay while I was making the little loaf, and fastening her [[motifs/flowers|flowers]]! Did you get my [[forms/letter|letter]] in [[motifs/time|time]] to tell her how happy I would be to do what she requested? Mr. Brady is coming to-morrow to bring arbutus for her. Dear little aunt! Will she look down?

You must tell me all you can think about her. Did she carry my little bouquet? So many broken-hearted people have got to hear the [[motifs/birds|birds]] sing, and see all the little [[motifs/flowers|flowers]] grow, just the same as if the [[motifs/sun|sun]] hadn't stopped shining forever! . . . How I wish I could comfort you! How I wish you could comfort me, who weep at what I did not see and never can believe. I will try and share you a little longer, but it is so long, Vinnie.

We didn't think, that morning when I wept that you left me, and you, for other things, that we should weep more bitterly before we saw each other.

[[motifs/well|Well]], she is safer now than “we know or even think.” Tired little aunt, sleeping ne'er so peaceful! Tuneful little aunt, singing, as we trust, hymns than which the robins have no sweeter ones.

Good-[[motifs/night|night]], broken hearts, L——, and F——, and Uncle L——. Vinnie, remember




<span class="atom-split" data-atom="_misses_december_1861" data-title="To the Misses, December, 1861" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/frost,motif/snow,motif/winter,motif/birds,motif/garden,motif/clothing,concept/friendship,concept/melancholy,form/letter,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


DEAR PEACOCK,—I received your feather with profound emotion. It has already surmounted a work, and crossed the Delaware. Doubtless you are moulting à la canary bird —[[concepts/hope|hope]] you will not suffer from the reduction of plumage these December days. The latitude is quite stiff for a few nights, and gentlemen and ladies who go barefoot in our large cities must find the climate uncomfortable. A land of frosts and zeros is not precisely the land for me; [[concepts/hope|hope]] you find it congenial. I believe it is several hundred years since I met you and F——, yet I am pleased to say, you do not become dim; I think you rather brighten as the hours fly. I should [[motifs/love|love]] to see you dearly, girls; perhaps I may, before south winds, but I feel rather confused to-day, and the future looks “higglety-pigglety.”

You seem to take a smiling view of my finery. If you knew how solemn it was to me, you might be induced to curtail your jests. My sphere is doubtless calicoes, nevertheless I thought it meet to sport a little wool. The mirth it has occasioned will deter me from further exhibitions! Won't you tell “the public” that at present I wear a brown dress with a cape if possible browner, and carry a parasol of the same! We have at present one cat, and twenty-four hens, who do nothing so vulgar as lay an egg, which checks the ice-cream tendency.

I miss the grasshoppers much, but suppose it is all for the best. I should become too much attached to a trotting world.

My [[motifs/garden|garden]] is all covered up by [[motifs/snow|snow]]; picked gilliflower Tuesday, now gilliflowers are asleep. The hills take off their purple frocks, and dress in long white nightgowns.

There is something fine and something sad in the year's toilet. . . .

We often talk of you and your father these new [[motifs/winter|winter]] days. Write, dear, when you feel like it.




<span class="atom-split" data-atom="_misses_after_the_death_of_the_m" data-title="To the Misses, After the death of the Misses—–'s father, Jan. 1864" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/death,motif/birds,motif/home,motif/frost,motif/winter,concept/death,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/friendship,concept/immortality_divine,concept/charity,form/letter,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


What shall I tell these darlings except that my father and mother are half their father and mother, and my [[motifs/home|home]] half theirs, whenever, and for as long as, they will. And sometimes a dearer thought than that creeps into my mind, but it is not for to-[[motifs/night|night]]. Wasn't dear papa so tired always after mamma went, and wasn't it almost sweet to think of the two together these new [[motifs/winter|winter]] nights? The grief is our side, darlings, and the glad is theirs. Vinnie and I sit down to-[[motifs/night|night]], while mother tells what makes us cry, though we know it is [[motifs/well|well]] and easy with uncle and papa, and only our part hurts. Mother tells how gently he looked on all who looked at him—how he held his bouquet sweet, as he were a guest in a friend's parlor and must still do honor. The meek, mild gentleman who thought no harm, but peace toward all.

Vinnie intended to go, but the day was cold, and she wanted to keep Uncle L——as she talked with him, always, instead of this new way. She thought too, for the crowd, she could not see you, children, and she would be another one to give others care. Mother said Mr. V——, yes, dears, even Mr. V——, at whom we sometimes smile, talked about “Lorin” and “Laviny” and his [[concepts/friendship|friendship]] towards them, to your father's guests. We won't smile at him any more now, will we? Perhaps he'll live to tell some gentleness of us, who made merry of him.

But never mind that now. When you have strength, tell us how it is, and what we may do for you, of comfort, or of service. Be sure you crowd all others out, precious little cousins. Good-[[motifs/night|night]]. Let Emily sing for you because she cannot pray:—

It is not dying hurts us so,— 'Tis living hurts us more; But dying is a different way, A kind, behind the [[motifs/door|door]],— The southern custom of the bird That soon as frosts are due Adopts a better latitude. We are the [[motifs/birds|birds]] that stay, The shiverers round farmers' doors, For whose reluctant crumb We stipulate, till pitying snows Persuade our feathers [[motifs/home|home]].




<span class="atom-split" data-atom="_misses_early_summer_1872" data-title="To the Misses, Early Summer, 1872" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/flowers,motif/nature,motif/summer,concept/friendship,concept/grief_and_loss,concept/wonder,form/letter,character/marian_erle,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


DEAR CHILDREN,—We received the news of your loving kindness through Uncle J——last evening, and Vinnie is negotiating with neighbor Gray, who goes to a wedding in Boston next week, for the procuring of the nest. Vinnie's views of expressage do not abate with [[motifs/time|time]]. The crocuses are with us and several other colored friends. Cousin H——broke her hip, and is in a polite bed, surrounded by mint juleps. I think she will hate to leave it as badly as Marian Erle did. Vinnie says there is a [[motifs/tree|tree]] in Mr. Sweetser's woods that shivers. I am afraid it is cold. I am going to make it a little coat. I must make several, because it is tall as the barn, and put them on as the circus men stand on each other's shoulders. . . . There is to be a “show” next week, and little Maggie's bed is to be moved to the [[motifs/door|door]] so she can see the tents. Folding her own like the Arabs gives her no apprehension. While I write, dear children, the colors Eliza loved quiver on the pastures, and day goes gay to the northwest, [[archetypes/innocent|innocent]] as she.

DEAR FRIEND,—I find you with dusk, for day is tired, and lays her antediluvian cheek to the [[motifs/hill|hill]] like a [[archetypes/child|child]].

[[concepts/nature|Nature]] confides now.

I [[concepts/hope|hope]] you are joyful frequently, these beloved days, and the health of your friend bolder.

I remember her with my blossoms and wish they were hers

Whose pink career may have a close Portentous as our own, who knows? To imitate these neighbors fleet, In awe and innocence, were meet.

[[motifs/summer|Summer]] is so kind I had hoped you might come. Since my father's dying, everything sacred enlarged so it was dim to own. When a few years old, I was taken to a funeral which I now know was of peculiar distress, and the clergyman asked, “Is the arm of the Lord shortened, that it cannot save?”

He italicised the “cannot.” I mistook the accent for a [[concepts/doubt|doubt]] of [[concepts/immortality|Immortality]], and not daring to ask, it besets me still, though we know that the mind of the heart must live if its clerical part do not. <span class="atom-split" data-atom="f_d_clark_late_autumn_1882" data-title="To Mr. F. D. DEAR FRIEND,—It pains us very much that you have been more ill. We [[concepts/hope|hope]] you may not be [[concepts/suffering|suffering]] now. Thank you for speaking so earnestly when our mother died. We have spoken daily of writing you, but have felt unable. The great attempt to save her life had it been successful would have been fatigueless, but failing, strength forsook us.

No verse in the [[historical-references/bible|Bible]] has frightened me so much from a [[archetypes/child|child]] as “from him that hath not, shall be taken even that he hath.” Was it because its dark menace deepened our own [[motifs/door|door]]? You speak as if you still missed your mother. I wish we might speak with you. As we bore her dear form through the wilderness, light seemed to have stopped.

Her dying feels to me like many kinds of cold—at times electric, at times benumbing,—then a trackless waste [[motifs/love|love]] has never trod. . . .

The [[forms/letter|letter]] from the skies, which accompanied yours, was indeed a boon. A [[forms/letter|letter]] always seemed to me like [[concepts/immortality|Immortality]], for is it not the mind alone, without corporeal friend?

I [[concepts/hope|hope]] you may tell us that you are better.

Thank you for much kindness. The friend anguish reveals is the slowest forgot.

<span class="atom-split" data-atom="c_h_clark" data-title="To Mr. C. H. Clark" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro" data-tags="motif/death,concept/death,concept/immortality,concept/friendship,form/letter,cluster/death,cluster/silence,cluster/bells"></span>


This [[forms/letter|letter]], in her bold, clear, detached hand (to an accustomed eye slightly less firm than usual), is the last one, so far as known, which Emily Dickinson wrote. Afterward, she continued to be delicate, though hardly alarmingly so; but just before the fifteenth of May, 1886, she sent two words to her cousins, freighted with startling import,—

LITLLE COUSINS,—Called back.
