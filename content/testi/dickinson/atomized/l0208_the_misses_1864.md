So many ask for the children that I must make a separate [[Letter|letter]] to tell them what they say, and leave my kisses till next [[Time|time]].

Eliza wrote last week, faint note in pencil—dressed in blankets, and propped up, having been so sick—and yet too weak to talk much, even with her slate. She said this of you, I give it in her own word, “Make them know I [[Love|love]] them,” and added, should have written immediately herself, except for weakness.

Mr. Dwight asks for you in the phrase “Of your sweet cousins.” He does not yet know papa is asleep—only very weary.

The milliner at the head of the street wipes her eye for F——and L——, and a tear rumples her ribbons. Mr. and Mrs. Sweetser care—Mrs. Sweetser most tenderly.

. . . Even Dick's wife, simple dame, with a kitchen full, and the [[Grave|grave]] besides, of little ragged ones, wants to know “more about” you, and follows mother to the [[Door|door]], who has called with bundle.

Dick says, in his wise way, he “shall always be interested in them young ladies.” One little young lady of his own, you know, is in Paradise. That makes him tenderer-minded.

Be sure you don't [[Doubt|doubt]] about the sparrow.

Poor——and——, in their genteel, antique way, express their [[Sympathy|sympathy]], mixing admiring anecdotes of your father and mother's youth, when they, [[God]] help them, were not so sere. Besides these others, children, shall we tell them who else cherishes, every day the same, the bright one and the black one too? Could it be Emily?

Would it interest the children to know that crocuses come up, in the [[Garden|garden]] off the dining-room, and a fuchsia, that pussy partook, mistaking it for strawberries? And that we have primroses, like the little pattern sent in last [[Winter|winter]]'s note, and heliotrope by the aprons full—the mountain colored one—and a jasmine bud, you know the little odor like Lubin—and gilliflowers, magenta, and few mignonette, and sweet alyssum bountiful, and carnation bud?

Will it please them to know that the ice-house is filled, to make their tumblers cool next [[Summer|summer]], and once in a while a cream?

And that father has built a new [[Road|road]] round the pile of trees between our house and Mr. S——'s, where they can take the soldier's shirt to make, or a sweet [[Poem|poem]], and no man find them but the fly, and he such a little man?

[[Love]], dears, from us all, and won't you tell us how you are?

We seem to hear so little.
