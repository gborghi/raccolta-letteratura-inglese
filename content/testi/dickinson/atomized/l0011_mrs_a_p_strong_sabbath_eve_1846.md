MY DEAR A.,—When I last wrote you I was in Boston, where I spent a delightful visit of four weeks. I returned [[Home|home]] about the middle of September in very good health and spirits, for which it seems to me I cannot be sufficiently grateful to the Giver of all mercies. I expected to go into the Academy upon my return [[Home|home]], but as I stayed longer than I expected to, and as the school had already commenced, I made up my mind to remain at [[Home|home]] during the fall term and pursue my studies the [[Winter|winter]] term, which commences a week after Thanksgiving. I kept my good resolution for once in my life, and have been sewing, practising upon the piano, and assisting mother in household affairs. I am anticipating the commencement of the next term with a great deal of pleasure, for I have been an [[Exile|exile]] from school two terms on account of my health, and you know what it is to “[[Love|love]] school.” Miss Adams is with us now, and will remain through the [[Winter|winter]], and we have an excellent Principal in the person of Mr. Leonard Humphrey, who was the last valedictorian. We now have a fine school. I thank you a thousand times for your long and affectionate [[Letter|letter]]. . . . I found a quantity of sewing waiting with open arms to embrace me, or rather for me to embrace it, and I could hardly give myself up to “[[Nature]]'s sweet restorer,” for the [[Ghosts|ghosts]] of out-of-order garments crying for vengeance upon my defenceless head. However, I am happy to inform you, my dear friend, that I have nearly finished my sewing for [[Winter|winter]], and will answer all the letters which you shall deem worthy to send so naughty a girl as myself, at short notice. . . .

Write soon. Your affectionate
