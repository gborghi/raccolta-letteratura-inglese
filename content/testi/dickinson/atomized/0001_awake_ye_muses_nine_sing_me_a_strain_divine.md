Awake ye muses nine, sing me a strain divine,  
Unwind the solemn twine, and tie my [[Valentine]]!  

Oh the Earth was made for [[lovers]], for [[Damsel|damsel]], and hopeless swain,  
For sighing, and gentle whispering, and [[unity]] made of twain.  
All things do go a courting, in earth, or [[Sea|sea]], or air,  
[[God]] hath made nothing single but thee in His world so fair!  
The bride, and then the bridegroom, the two, and then the one,  
Adam, and Eve, his consort, the [[Moon|moon]], and then the [[Sun|sun]];  
The life doth prove the precept, who obey shall happy be,  
Who will not serve the sovereign, be hanged on fatal [[Tree|tree]].  
The high do seek the lowly, the great do seek the small,  
None cannot find who seeketh, on this terrestrial ball;  
The bee doth [[Court|court]] the flower, the flower his suit receives,  
And they make merry wedding, whose guests are hundred [[Leaves|leaves]];  
The [[Wind|wind]] doth woo the branches, the branches they are won,  
And the father fond demandeth the maiden for his son.  
The [[Storm|storm]] doth walk the seashore humming a mournful tune,  
The wave with eye so pensive, looketh to see the [[Moon|moon]],  
Their spirits meet together, they make their solemn vows,  
No more he singeth mournful, her sadness she doth lose.  
The worm doth woo the mortal, [[Death|death]] claims a living bride,  
[[Night]] unto day is married, morn unto eventide;  
Earth is a merry [[Damsel|damsel]], and heaven a knight so true,  
And Earth is quite coquettish, and beseemeth in vain to sue.  
Now to the application, to the reading of the roll,  
To bringing thee to justice, and marshalling thy [[Soul|soul]]:  
Thou art a human solo, a being cold, and lone,  
Wilt have no kind companion, thou reap'st what thou hast sown.  
Hast never silent hours, and minutes all too long,  
And a deal of sad reflection, and wailing instead of [[Song|song]]?

There's Sarah, and Eliza, and Emeline so fair,  
And Harriet, and Susan, and she with curling hair!  
Thine eyes are sadly blinded, but yet thou mayest see  
Six true, and comely maidens sitting upon the [[Tree|tree]];  
Approach that [[Tree|tree]] with caution, then up it boldly climb,  
And seize the one thou lovest, nor care for space, or [[Time|time]]!  
Then bear her to the greenwood, and build for her a bower,  
And give her what she asketh, [[Jewel|jewel]], or bird, or flower —  
And bring the fife, and trumpet, and beat upon the drum —  
And bid the world Goodmorrow, and go to glory [[Home|home]]!
