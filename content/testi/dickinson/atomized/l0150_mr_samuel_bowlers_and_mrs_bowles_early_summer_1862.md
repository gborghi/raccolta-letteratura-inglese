DEAR MARY,—When the best is gone, I know that other things are not of consequence. The heart wants what it wants, or else it does not care.

You [[Wonder|wonder]] why I write so. Because I cannot help. I like to have you know some care—so when your life gets faint for its other life, you can lean on us. We won't break, Mary. We look very small, but the reed can carry weight.

Not to see what we [[Love|love]] is very terrible, and talking doesn't ease it, and nothing does but just itself. The eyes and hair we chose are all there are—to us. Isn't it so, Mary?

I often [[Wonder|wonder]] how the [[Love|love]] of [[Christ]] is done when that below holds so.

I [[Hope|hope]] the little “Robert” coos away the [[Pain|pain]]. Perhaps your [[Flowers|flowers]] help, some. . . .

The frogs sing sweet to-day—they have such pretty, lazy times—how nice to be a frog! . . .

Mother sends her [[Love|love]] to you—she has a sprained foot, and can go but little in the house, and not abroad at all.

Don't dishearten, Mary, we'll keep thinking of you. Kisses for all.
