DEAR AUSTIN,—A week ago we were all here—to-day we are not all here—yet the bee hums just as merrily, and all the busy things work on as if the same. They do not miss you, [[Child|child]], but there is a humming-bee whose song is not so merry, and there are busy ones who pause to drop a tear. Let us thank [[God]], to-day, Austin, that we can [[Love|love]] our friends, our brothers and our sisters, and weep when they are gone, and smile at their return. It is indeed a [[Joy|joy]] which we are blest to know.

To-day is very beautiful—just as bright, just as blue, just as green and as white and as crimson as the cherry-trees full in bloom, and the half-opening peach-blossoms, and the grass just waving, and sky and [[Hill|hill]] and cloud can make it, if they try. How I wish you were here, Austin; you thought last Saturday beautiful, yet to this golden day 'twas but one single gem to whole handfuls of jewels. You will ride to-day, I [[Hope|hope]], or take a long walk somewhere, and recollect us all, Vinnie and me and father and mother and [[Home|home]]. Yes, Austin, every one of us, for we all think of you, and bring you to recollection many times each day—not bring you to recollection, for we never put you away, but keep recollecting on. . . .

You must think of us to-[[Night|night]] while Mr. Dwight takes tea here, and we will think of you far away down in Cambridge.

Don't mind the can, Austin, if it is rather dry, don't mind the daily [[Road|road]] though it is rather dusty, but remember the brooks and the hills, and remember while you're but one, we are but four at [[Home|home]]!
