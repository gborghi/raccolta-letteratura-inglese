<span class="atom-split" data-atom="l0370_the_rev_j_l_jenkins_and_mrs_jenkins_june_1878" data-title="To the Rev. J. L. Jenkins and Mrs. Jenkins, June, 1878" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


You deserved a tiding before, dear.

Your little punctualities are generous and precious. . . .

There is a circus here, and farmers' Commencement, and boys and girls from Tripoli, and Governors and swords parade the [[Summer|summer]] streets. They lean upon the fence that guards the quiet church ground, and jar the grass, now warm and soft as a tropic nest.

Many people call, and wish for you with [[Tears|tears]], and Vinnie beats her wings like a maddened bird, whose [[Home|home]] has been invaded.

So much has been sorrow, that to fall asleep in [[Tennyson]]'s verse, seems almost a pillow. “To where beyond these voices there is Peace.”

I [[Hope|hope]] you are each safe. It is homeless without you, and we think of others possessing you with the throe of [[Othello]].

Mother gives her [[Love|love]]—Maggie pleads her own. Austin smiles when you mention him. . . .

Daisies and ferns are with us, and he whose meadow they magnify, is always linked with you.
