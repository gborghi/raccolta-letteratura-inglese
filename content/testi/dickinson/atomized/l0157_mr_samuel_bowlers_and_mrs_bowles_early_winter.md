<span class="atom-split" data-atom="l0157_mr_samuel_bowlers_and_mrs_bowles_early_winter" data-title="To Mr. Samuel Bowlers and Mrs. Bowles, Early Winter," data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


DEAR FRIEND,—Had we the art like you, to endow so many, by just recovering our health, 'twould give us tender [[Pride|pride]], nor could we keep the news, but carry it to you, who seem to us to own it most.

So few that live have life, it seems of quick importance not one of those escape by [[Death|death]]. And since you gave us [[Fear|fear]], congratulate us for ourselves—you give us safer peace.

How extraordinary that life's large population contain so few of power to us—and those a vivid species who leave no mode, like Tyrian dye.

Remembering these minorities, permit our gratitude for you. We ask that you be cautious, for many sakes, excelling ours. To recapitulate the [[Stars|stars]] were useless as supreme. Yourself is yours, dear friend, but ceded, is it not, to here and there a minor life? Do not defraud these, for gold may be bought, and purple may be bought, but the sale of the spirit never did occur.

Do not yet work. No public so exorbitant of any as its friend, and we can wait your health. Besides, there is an idleness more tonic than toil.

The loss of sickness—was it loss? Or that ethereal gain You earned by measuring the [[Grave|grave]], Then measuring the [[Sun|sun]].

Be sure, dear friend, for want you have estates of lives.
