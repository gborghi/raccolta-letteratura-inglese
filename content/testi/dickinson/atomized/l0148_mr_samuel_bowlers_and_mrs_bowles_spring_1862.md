<span class="atom-split" data-atom="l0148_mr_samuel_bowlers_and_mrs_bowles_spring_1862" data-title="To Mr. Samuel Bowlers and Mrs. Bowles, Spring, 1862" data-chapter="" data-kind="intro"></span>


DEAR FRIEND,—The hearts in Amherst ache to-[[Night|night]] —you could not know how hard. They thought they could not wait, last [[Night|night]], until the engine sang a pleasant tune that [[Time|time]], because that you were coming. The [[Flowers|flowers]] waited, in the vase, and [[Love|love]] got peevish, watching. A railroad person rang, to bring an evening paper—Vinnie tipped pussy over, in haste to let you in, and I, for [[Joy|joy]] and dignity, held tight in my chair. My [[Hope|hope]] put out a petal.

You would come, to-day,—but . . . we don't believe it, now; “Mr. Bowles not coming!” Wouldn't you, to-morrow, and this but be a bad dream, gone by next morning?

Please do not take our [[Spring|spring]] away, since you blot [[Summer|summer]] out! We cannot count our [[Tears|tears]] for this, because they drop so fast. . . .

Dear friend, we meant to make you brave, but moaned before we thought. . . . If you'll be sure and get [[Well|well]], we'll try to bear it. If we could only care the less, it would be so much easier. Your [[Letter|letter]] troubled my throat. It gave that little scalding we could not know the reason for till we grew far up.

I must do my good-[[Night|night]] in crayon I meant to in red.

[[Love]] for Mary.
