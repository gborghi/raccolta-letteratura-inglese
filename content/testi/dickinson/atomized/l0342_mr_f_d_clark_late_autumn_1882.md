DEAR FRIEND,—It pains us very much that you have been more ill. We [[Hope|hope]] you may not be [[Suffering|suffering]] now. Thank you for speaking so earnestly when our mother died. We have spoken daily of writing you, but have felt unable. The great attempt to save her life had it been successful would have been fatigueless, but failing, strength forsook us.

No verse in the [[Bible]] has frightened me so much from a [[Child|child]] as “from him that hath not, shall be taken even that he hath.” Was it because its dark menace deepened our own [[Door|door]]? You speak as if you still missed your mother. I wish we might speak with you. As we bore her dear form through the wilderness, light seemed to have stopped.

Her dying feels to me like many kinds of cold—at times electric, at times benumbing,—then a trackless waste [[Love|love]] has never trod. . . .

The [[Letter|letter]] from the skies, which accompanied yours, was indeed a boon. A [[Letter|letter]] always seemed to me like [[Immortality]], for is it not the mind alone, without corporeal friend?

I [[Hope|hope]] you may tell us that you are better.

Thank you for much kindness. The friend anguish reveals is the slowest forgot.
