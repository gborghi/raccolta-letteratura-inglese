| *(didascalia)* | This unfinished play follows [[characters/myrrhina\|Myrrhina]], an Alexandrian noblewoman, who travels to the [[settings/mountains\|mountains]] to tempt [[characters/honorius\|Honorius]], a Christian hermit, away from goodness with her [[concepts/beauty\|beauty]] and wealth. After they talk, he decides to return to sin in Alexandria, while she discovers religion and chooses to remain in the [[motifs/desert\|desert]].<br>Wilde had begun work on the play in 1894, between writing Salomé and The Importance of Being Earnest, but he was unable to complete it before his trial and [[concepts/imprisonment\|imprisonment]]. He considered revisiting the play in 1897 after his release from [[motifs/prison\|prison]], but he then lacked motivation for literary work, although during his [[concepts/imprisonment\|imprisonment]], it was much on his mind and he had described it in a letter to a friend as one among his “beautiful coloured, musical things”. Before his [[concepts/imprisonment\|imprisonment]], the fragments had been entrusted to Mrs. Leverson, who in 1897 went to [[settings/paris\|Paris]] on purpose to restore the manuscript to the author. However, Wilde accidently left the papers in a taxi cab and now only a portion of a first draft survives.<br><br>Wilde, c. 190<br><br>LA SAINTE COURTISANE<br>OR, THE WOMAN COVERED WITH JEWELS<br>The scene represents a corner of a valley in the Thebaid. On the right hand of the stage is a cavern. In front of the cavern stands a great crucifix.<br>On the left<br>[sand dunes].<br>The sky is blue like the inside of a cup of lapis lazuli. The hills are of red sand. Here and there on the hills there are clumps of thorns. |
