[[tolstoy|TOLSTOY]]

IF any one wishes to form the fullest estimate of the real
character and influence of the great man whose name is prefixed
to these remarks, he will not find it in his novels, splendid as
they are, or in his ethical views, clearly and finely as they
are conceived and expanded.  He will find it best expressed
in the news that has recently come from Canada, that a sect
of Russian Christian anarchists has turned all its [[Animals|animals]] loose,
on the ground that it is immoral to possess them or control them.
About such an incident as this there is a quality altogether independent
of the rightness or wrongness, the [[Sanity|sanity]] or insanity, of the view.
It is first and foremost a reminder that the world is still young.
There are still theories of life as insanely reasonable as those which
were disputed under the clear blue skies of Athens.  There are still
examples of a [[Faith|faith]] as fierce and practical as that of the Mahometans,
who swept across Africa and Europe, shouting a single word.
To the languid contemporary politician and philosopher it seems doubtless
like something out of [[A Dream|a dream]], that in this iron-bound, homogeneous,
and clockwork age, a company of European men in boots and waistcoats
should begin to insist on taking the [[Horse|horse]] out of the shafts
of the omnibus, and lift the pig out of his pig-sty, and the dog
out of his kennel, because of a moral scruple or theory.
It is like a page from some fairy farce to imagine the Doukhabor
solemnly escorting a hen to the door of the yard and bidding
it a benevolent farewell as it sets out on its travels.
All this, as I say, seems mere muddle-headed absurdity to the typical
leader of human society in this decade, to a man like Mr. Balfour,
or Mr. Wyndham.  But there is nevertheless a further thing to be said,
and that is that, if Mr. Balfour could be converted to a religion
which taught him that he was morally bound to walk into the [[house|House]]
of Commons on his hands, and he did walk on his hands, if Mr. Wyndham
could accept a creed which taught that he ought to dye his hair blue,
and he did dye his hair blue, they would both of them be,
almost beyond description, better and happier men than they are.
For there is only one happiness possible or conceivable under the sun,
and that is enthusiasm--that strange and splendid word that has passed
through so many vicissitudes, which meant, in the eighteenth century
the condition of a lunatic, and in [[Ancient Greece|ancient Greece]] the presence
of a god.

This great act of heroic consistency which has taken place
in Canada is the best example of the work of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]].  It is true
(as I believe) that the Doukhabors have an origin quite
independent of the great Russian moralist, but there can
surely be little [[Doubt|doubt]] that their emergence into importance
and the growth and mental distinction of their sect, is due to his
admirable summary and justification of their scheme of ethics.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], besides being a magnificent novelist, is one of the very
few men alive who have a real, solid, and serious view of life.
He is a [[Catholic Church|Catholic church]], of which he is the only member,
the somewhat arrogant Pope and the somewhat submissive layman.
He is one of the two or three men in Europe, who have
an attitude towards things so entirely their own, that we
could supply their inevitable view on anything--a silk hat,
a [[Home]] Rule Bill, an Indian poem, or a pound of tobacco.
There are three men in existence who have such an attitude:
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], Mr. Bernard [[Shaw]], and my friend Mr. Hilaire Belloc.  They are
all diametrically opposed to each other, but they all have this
essential resemblance, that, given their basis of thought,
their soil of conviction, their opinions on every earthly
subject grow there naturally, like [[Flowers|flowers]] in a field.
There are certain views of certain things that they must take;
they do not form opinions, the opinions form themselves.
Take, for instance, in the case of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], the mere list
of miscellaneous objects which I wrote down at random above,
a silk hat, a [[Home]] Rule Bill, an Indian poem, and a pound of tobacco.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] would say:  "I believe in the utmost possible simplification
of life; therefore, this silk hat is a black abortion."
He would say:  "I believe in the utmost possible simplification
of life; therefore, this [[Home]] Rule Bill is a mere peddling compromise;
it is no good to break up a centralised empire into nations,
you must break the nation up into individuals."  He would say:
"I believe in the utmost possible simplification
of life; therefore, I am interested in this Indian poem,
for Eastern ethics, under all their apparent gorgeousness,
are far simpler and more Tolstoyan than Western."  He would say:
"I believe in the utmost possible simplification of life;
therefore, this pound of tobacco is a thing of evil; take it away."
Everything in the world, from the [[Bible]] to a bootjack,
can be, and is, reduced by [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] to this great fundamental
Tolstoyan principle, the simplification of life.
When we deal with a [[Body|body]] of opinion like this we are dealing
with an incident in the [[history]] of Europe infinitely more
important than the appearance of Napoleon Buonaparte.

This emergence of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], with his awful and simple ethics,
is important in more ways than one.  Among other things it is a very
interesting commentary on an attitude which has been taken up for
the matter of half a century by all the avowed opponents of religion.
The secularist and the sceptic have denounced [[Christianity]]
first and foremost, because of its encouragement of [[Fanaticism|fanaticism]];
because religious excitement led men to burn their neighbours
and to [[Dance|dance]] naked down the street.  How queer it all sounds now.
Religion can be swept out of the matter altogether, and still
there are philosophical and ethical theories which can produce
[[Fanaticism|fanaticism]] enough to fill the world.  [[Fanaticism]] has nothing at all
to do with religion.  There are [[Grave|grave]] scientific theories which,
if carried out logically, would result in the same fires
in the market-place and the same [[Nakedness|nakedness]] in the street.
There are modern esthetes who would expose themselves
like the Adamites if they could do it in elegant attitudes.
There are modern scientific moralists who would burn their
opponents alive, and would be quite contented if they were burnt
by some new chemical process.  And if any one doubts this
proposition--that [[Fanaticism|fanaticism]] has nothing to do with religion,
but has only to do with human [[Nature|nature]]--let him take this case of
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] and the Doukhabors.  A sect of men start with no theology
at all, but with the simple doctrine that we ought to [[Love|love]] our
neighbour and use no force against him, and they end in thinking
it wicked to carry a leather handbag, or to ride in a cart.
A great modern writer who erases theology altogether,
denies the validity of the Scriptures and the Churches alike,
forms a purely ethical theory that [[Love|love]] should be the instrument
of [[Reform|reform]], and ends by maintaining that we have no right
to strike a man if he is torturing a [[Child|child]] before our eyes.
He goes on, he develops a theory of the mind and the emotions,
which might be held by the most rigid atheist, and he ends
by maintaining that the sexual relation out of which all humanity
has come, is not only not moral, but is positively not natural.
This is [[Fanaticism|fanaticism]] as it has been and as it will always be.
Destroy the last copy of he [[Bible]], and persecution and insane orgies
will be founded on Mr. Herbert Spencer's "Synthetic [[philosophy|Philosophy]]."  Some of
the broadest thinkers of the Middle Ages believed in faggots,
and some of the broadest thinkers in the nineteenth century
believe in dynamite.

The [[Truth|truth]] is that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], with his immense genius, with his
colossal [[Faith|faith]], with his vast fearlessness and vast knowledge
of life, is deficient in one faculty and one faculty [[Alone|alone]].
He is not a mystic:  and therefore he has a tendency to go mad.
Men talk of the extravagances and frenzies that have been
produced by mysticism:  they are a mere drop in the bucket.
In the main, and from the beginning of [[Time|time]], mysticisrn has
kept men sane.  The thing that has driven them mad was [[logic]].
It is significant that, with all that has been said about
the excitability of poets, only one English poet ever went mad,
and he went mad from a logical system of theology.
He was Cowper, and his poetry retarded his insanity for many years.
So poetry, in which [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] is deficient, has always been
a tonic and sanative thing.  The only thing that has kept
the race of men from the mad extremes of the convent and
the pirate-galley, the [[Night|night]]-club and the lethal chamber,
has been mysticism--the belief that [[logic]] is misleading,
and that things are not what they seem.

G. K. CHESTERTON.

LEO [[tolstoy|TOLSTOY]] AS WRITER

HALF the ignorance or misunderstanding of this greatest living
figure in literature comes of the attempt to judge him as we judge
the specialised Western novelist--an utterly futile method of approach.
He is a Russian, in the first place.  Had he come to [[Paris]]
with Turguenieff, he might have been similarly re-nationalised,
might possibly have developed into a writer pure and simple;
the world might so have gained a few great romances--it would have lost
infinitely in other directions.  Turguenieff wished it so.  "My friend,"
he wrote to [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] from his deathbed, "return to literature!
Reflect that that gift comes to you whence everything comes to us.
Ah! how happy I should be if I could think that my prayers could influence
you.... My friend, great writer of our Russian land, hear my entreaty!"
For once, the second greatest of modern Russians took a narrow view
of character and destiny.  Genius must work itself out on its own lines.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] remained a Russian from tip to toe--that is one of his
supreme values for us; and he remained an indivisible personality.
The [[artist]] and the moralist are inseparable in his works.
"We are not to take 'Anna Karenina' as a work of art,"
said Matthew Arnold; "we are to take it as a piece of life."
The distinction is not very satisfactorily stated, but the meaning
is clear.  So, too, W. D. Howells, in his introduction to an American
edition of the "Sebastopol Sketches": "I do not know how it is with
others to whom these books of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s have come, but for my part
I cannot think of them as literature in the artistic sense at all.
Some people complain to me when I praise them that they are too long,
too diffuse, too confused, that the characters' names are hard
to pronounce, and that the life they portray is very sad and not amusing.
In the presence of these criticisms I can only say that I find them
nothing of the kind, but that each [[history]] of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s is as clear,
as orderly, as brief, as something I have lived through myself.
I cannot think of any service which imaginative literature has
done the race so great as that which [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] has done in his
conception of Karenina at that crucial moment when the cruelly
outraged man sees that he cannot be good with dignity.  This [[Leaves|leaves]]
all tricks of fancy, all effects of art, immeasurably behind."
So much being said, however, we may be allowed to emphasise
in this qualities and achievements of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] as [[artist]],
rather than the expositions of Christian Anarchism and the social
philippics under which those achievements have been somewhat hidden
in recent years.

Morbid introspectiveness and the spirit of revolt inevitably
colour what is best in nineteenth-century [[Russia]].  Born at
Yasnaya Polyana ("Clear Field"), Tula, in 1828, and early orphaned,
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s youth synchronised with the period of reaction
that brought the Empire to the humiliating disasters of the
Crimean War.  No [[Hope|hope]] was left in the thin layer of society
lying between the two mill-stones of the [[Court]] and the serfs;
none in the little sphere of art where Byronic romanticism
was ready to expire.  The boy saw from the first the rottenness
of the patriarchal aristocracy in which his lot seemed
to be cast.  Precocious, abnormally sensitive and observant,
impatient of discipline and formal learning, awkward and bashful,
always brooding, not a little conceited, he was a sceptic at fifteen,
and left the University of Kazan in disgust at the stupid
conventions of the [[Time|time]] and place, without taking his degree.
"[[Childhood]], Boyhood, and Youth"--which appeared in three
sections between 1852 and 1857--tells the story of this period,
though the figure of Irtenieff is probably a projection rather
than a portrait of himself, to whom he is always less fair,
not to say merciful, than to others.  This book is a most
uncompromising exercise in [[Self|self]]-analysis. It of great length,
there is no plot, and few outer events are recorded.

The realism is generally morbid, but is varied by some passages of great
descriptive power, such as the account of the [[Storm|storm]], and occasionally
with tender pathos, as in the story of the soldier's [[Death|death]], as [[Well|well]]
as by grimly vivid pages, such as the narrative of the mother's [[Death|death]].
In this earliest work will be found the seeds both of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s artistic
genius and of his ethical gospel.

After five years of mildly benevolent efforts among his serfs
at Yasnaya Polyana (the disappointments of which he related a few
years later in "A Landlord's Morning," intended to have been
part of a full [[Novel|novel]] called "A Russian Proprietor"), his elder
brother Nicholas persuaded him to join the army, and in 1851
he was drafted to the Caucasus as an artillery officer.
On this favourite stage of Russian [[Romance|romance]], where for the first
[[Time|time]] he saw the towering [[Mountains|mountains]] and the tropical sun, and met
the rugged adventurous highlanders, [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] felt his [[Imagination|imagination]]
stirred as Byron among the isles of Greece, and his early revulsion
against [[City|city]] life confirmed as Wordsworth amid the Lakes,
as Thoreau at Walden, by a direct call from [[Nature]] to his own heart.
The largest result of this experience was "The Cossacks"
(1852). Turguenieff described this fine prose [[Epic|epic]] of the contact of
civilised and savage man as "the best [[Novel|novel]] written in our language."
"The Raid" (or "The Invaders," as Mr. Dole's [[Translation|translation]] is
entitled), same year, "The Wood-Cutting Expedition" (1855), "Meeting
an Old Acquaintance" (1856), and "A Prisoner in the Caucasus"
(1862) are also drawn from recollections of this sojourn,
and show the same descriptive and romantic power.  Upon the outbreak
of the Crimean War the Count was called to Sebastopol, where he had
command of a battery, and took part in the defence of the citadel.
The immediate product of these dark months of bloodshed was the
thrilling series of impressions reprinted from one of the leading
Russian reviews as "Sebastopol Sketches" (1856). From that day
onward [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] knew and hateful [[Truth|truth]] about war and the thoughtless
pseudo-patriotism which hurries nations into fratricidal slaughter.
From that there was expunged from his mind all the cheap romanticism
which depends upon the glorification of the savage [[Nature|nature]].
These wonderful pictures of the routine of the [[Battlefield|battlefield]] established
his position in [[Russia]] as a writer, and later on created in Western
countries an impression like that of the canvases of Verestchagin.

For a brief [[Time|time]] [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] became a figure in the old and new
capitals of [[Russia]] by right of talent as [[Well|well]] as birth.
His very chequered [[Friendship|friendship]] with Turguenieff, one of the oddest
chapters in literary [[history]], can only be mentioned here.
In 1857 he travelled in [[Germany]], France, and [[Italy]].  It was
of these years that he declared in "My Confession" that he could
not think of them without horror, disgust, and [[Pain|pain]] of heart.
The catalogue of crime which he charged against himself in his
salvationist crisis of twenty years later must not be taken literally;
but that there was some ground for it we may guess from the scenic
and incidental realism of the "Recollections of a Billiard Marker"
(1856), and of many a later page.  Several other powerful
short novels date from about this [[Time|time]], including "Albert"
and "Lucerne," both of which remind us of the Count's
susceptibility to music; "Polikushka," a tale of peasant life;
and "Family Happiness," the story of a [[Marriage|marriage]] that failed,
a most clear, consistent, forceful, and in parts beautiful
piece of work, anticipating in essentials "The Kreutzer Sonata"
that was to scandalise the world thirty years afterward.

After all, it was family happiness that saved Leo [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]].  For the
third [[Time|time]] the hand of [[Death|death]] had snatched away one of the nearest
to him--his brother Nicholas.  Two years later, in 1862, he married
Miss Behrs, daughter of the army surgeon in Tula--the most fortunate
thing that has happened to him in his whole life, I should think.
Family responsibilities, those [[Novel|novel]] and daring experiments in peasant
[[Education|education]] which are recorded in several volumes of the highest interest,
the supervision of the estate, magisterial work, and last,
but not least, the prolonged labours upon "War and Peace" and
"Anna Karenina" fill up the next fifteen years.  "War and Peace"
(1864-9) is a huge panorama of the Napoleonic campaign of 1812,
with preceding and succeeding episodes in Russian society.
These four volumes display in their superlative degree [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s
indifference to plot and his absorption in individual character;
they are rather a series of scenes threaded upon the fortunes of several
families than a set [[Novel|novel]]; but they contain passages of penetrating
psychology and vivid description, as [[Well|well]] as a certain amount of
anarchist theorising.  Of this work, by which its author became known
in the West, Flaubert (how the name carries us backward!) wrote:
"It is of the first order.  What a painter and what a psychologist!
The two first volumes are [[Sublime|sublime]], but the third drags frightfully.
There are some quite Shakespearean things in it."
The [[artist]]'s hand was now strengthening for his highest attainment.
In 1876 appeared "Anna Karenina," his greatest, and as he intended
at the [[Time|time]] (but Art is not so easily jilted), his last [[Novel|novel]].
The fine qualities of this book, which, though long, is dramatically
unified and vitally coherent, have been so fully recognised that I
need not attempt to describe them.  Mr. George Meredith has
described Anna as "the most perfectly depicted female character
in all fiction," which, from the author of "[[Diana]]," is praise indeed.
Parallel with the main subject of the illicit [[Love|love]] of Anna and
Vronsky there is a minor subject in the fortunes of Levin and Kitty,
wherein the reader will discover many of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s own experiences.
Matthew Arnold complained that the book contained too many
characters and a burdensome multiplicity of actions, but praised
its author's extraordinarily fine perception and no less
extraordinary truthfulness, and frankly revelled in Anna's
"large, fresh, rich, generous, delightful [[Nature|nature]]."  "When I had
ended my work 'Anna Karenina,'" said [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] in "My Confession"
(1879-82), "my [[Despair|despair]] reached such a height that I could
do nothing but think of the horrible condition in which I found
myself.... I saw only one thing--[[Death]]. Everything else was a lie."
Of that spiritual crisis nothing need be said here except that it
only intensified, and did not really, as it seemed to do,
vitally change, principles and instincts which had possessed
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] from the beginning.  His subsequent ethical and religious
development may be traced in a long series of books and pamphlets,
of which the most important are "The Gospels Translated, Compared,
and Harmonised" (1880-2), "What I Believe" ["My Religion"],
produced abroad in 1884, "What is to be Done?" (1884-5), "Life"
(1887), "Work" (1888), "The Kingdom of God is Within You"
(1893), "Non-Action" (1894), "Patriotism and [[Christianity]]"
(1896)--crusade, in the foreign and the clandestine presses at least,
against all Imperial authority and social maladjustments.
Mr. Tchertkoff, Mr. Aylmer Maudej the "Brotherhood Publishing Co.,"
and the "Free Age Press" deserve praise for their efforts to popularise
these and other works of the Count in thoroughly good translations.
In "What is Art?" (1898), not content with the bare utilitarian argument
that it is merely a means of social [[Union|union]], he launched a jehad against
all modern ideas of Art which rely upon a conception of [[Beauty|beauty]] and all
ideas of [[Beauty|beauty]] into which pleasure enters as a leading constituent.
A short but luminous [[Essay|essay]] on "Guy de Maupassant and the Art
of Fiction" is a scathing attack upon militarism in general and
the Franco-Russian Alliance in particular--"The Christian Teaching"
(1898), and "The [[Slavery]] of our Times" (1900). Various letters
on the successive famines and on the religious persecutions in [[Russia]]
deserve separate mention; they remind us that since the failure
of the revolutionary movement miscalled "Nihilism" [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] has
gradually risen to the position of the one man who can continue
with impunity a public more satisfactory contribution to the subject.

It is more to our purpose to note that in this volcanic and fecund
if fundamentally simple personality the [[artist]] has dogged
the steps of the evangelist to the last.  "Master and Man"
(1895) is one of the most exquisite short stories ever written.
"The [[Death]] of Ivan Ilyitch" (1884) and "[[Resurrection]]"
(1899) are in some ways the most powerful of all his works.
The much condemned "Dominion of Darkness" (1886) and "Kreutzer Sonata"
(1889) will be more fairly judged when the average Englishman has
learned the supreme merit of that uncompromising truthfulness
which gives nobility to every line the grand Russian ever wrote.
To submit a work like "[[Resurrection]]" to the summary treatment
which the ordinary [[Novel|novel]] receives and merits is absurd.
It is a large picture of the fall and rise of man done by the swift
and restless hand of a master who stands in a category apart, with an eye
that sees externals and essentials with like accuracy and rapidity.
Because the dramatic quality of these living pictures lies,
not in their organisation into a conventionally limited plot,
but first in the challenging idea upon which they are founded,
then the inexorable development of individual characters, and ever
and anon in the grip of particular episodes, the little critics scoff.
The idea, the characters, the episodes are all too real and
precious British [[Self|self]]-complacency. The grandmotherly Athenoeum
permits some person to describe this Promethean figure as "a
precious vase that has been broken," and can now only be pieced
together to make "the ornament of a museum,"--which reminds me
that I heard a lecturer before a [[Well|well]]-known literary society
in [[London]] describe him lately as a "scavenger," and that a [[City|city]]
bookseller assured me the other day that there was something
almost amounting to a boycott against his fiction in the shops.
The publisher who is preparing a complete edition of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]--
enormous work!--knows better, knows that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] is one of the
world-spirits whose advance out of the obscurity of a benighted
land into the largest contemporary circulation is but a foretaste
of an influence that will soon be co-extensive with the commonwealth
of thinking men and women.

His service to literature is precisely the same as his service to morals.
Like Bunyan and Burns, Dickens and Whitman, he throws down in a
world of decadent conventions the gauge of the democratic ideal.
As he calls the politician and the social reformer back to the land
and the common people, so he calls the [[artist]] back to the elemental
forces ever at work beneath the surface-show of [[Nature|nature]] and humanity.
With an extraordinary penetration into the hidden recesses of character,
he joins a terrible truthfulness, and that absolute simplicity
of manner which we generally associate with genius.  He is a realist,
not merely of the outer, but more especially of the [[Inner Life|inner life]].  There is
no staginess, no sentimentality, in his work.  He has no heroes in our
Western sense, none, even, of those sensational types of personality
which glorify the name of his Northern contemporary, Ibsen.  His style
is always natural, direct, irresistible as a physical process.
He has rarely strayed beyond the channel of his own experience,
and the reader who prefers breadth to depth of knowledge must
seek elsewhere.  He has little [[Humour|humour]], but a grimly satiric note has
sometimes crept into his writing, as Archdeacon Farrar will remember.
Of artifice designed for vulgar entertainment he knows nothing;
in the world of true art, which is the wine-press of the [[Soul|soul]] of man,
he stands, a princely figure.  Theories, prescriptions, and discussions
are forgotten, and we think only with [[Love|love]] and reverence of this
modern patriarch, so lonely amid the daily enlarging congregation
of the hearts he has awakened to a sense of the mystery, the terror,
the joy, the splendour of human destinies.

G. H. PERRIS.

[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s Place in European Literature

The justness of the word _great_ applied to a nation's writers is perhaps
best tested by simply taking each writer in turn from out his Age,
and seeing how far our conception of his Age remains unaffected.  We may
take away hundreds of clever writers, scores of distinguished creators,
and the Age remains before our eyes, solidly unaffected by their absence;
but touch one or two central figures, and lo! the whole framework
of the Age gives in your hands, and you realise that the World's
insight into, and understanding of that Age's life has been supplied
us by the special interpretation offered by two or three great minds.
In fact, every Age seems dwarfed, chaotic, full of confused tendencies and
general contradiction till the few great men have arisen, and symbolised
in themselves what their nation's growth or strife signifies.
How many dumb ages are there in which no great writer has appeared,
ages to whose [[Inner Life|inner life]] in consequence we have no key!

[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s significance as the great writer of modern [[Russia]] can
scarcely be augmented in Russian eyes by his exceeding significance
to Europe as symbolising the spiritual unrest of the modern world.
Yet so inevitably must the main stream of each age's tendency and the main
movement of the world's thought be discovered for us by the great writers,
whenever they appear, that [[Russia]] can no more keep [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s
significance to herself than could [[Germany]] keep Goethe's to herself.
True it is that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], as great novelist, has been absorbed
in mirroring the peculiar world of half-feudal, modern [[Russia]],
a world strange to Western Europe, but the spirit of analysis with
which the creator of Anna Karenina and War and Peace has confronted
the modern world is more truly representative of our Age's outlook
than is the spirit of any other of his great contemporaries.
Between the days of Wilhelm Meister and of [[Resurrection]] what an
extraordinary volume of the rushing tide of modern life has swept by!
A century of that "liberation of modern Europe from the old routine" has
passed since Goethe stood forth for "the awakening of the modern spirit."
A century of emancipation, of Science, of unbelief, of incessant shock,
change, and Progress all over the face of Europe, and even as Goethe
a hundred years ago typified the triumph of the new intelligence
of Europe over the shackles of its old institutions, routine, and dogma
(as Matthew Arnold affirms), so [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] today stands for the triumph
of the European [[Soul|soul]] against civilisation's routine and dogma.
The peculiar modernness of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s attitude, however, as we
shall presently show, is that he is inspired largely by the modern
scientific spirit in his searching analysis of modern life.
Apparently at war with Science and Progress, his extraordinary fascination
for the mind of Europe lies in the fact that he of all great contemporary
writers has come nearest to demonstrating, to realising what the life
of the modern man is.  He of all the analysts of the civilised man's
thoughts, emotions, and actions has least idealized, least beautified,
and least distorted the complex daily life of the European world.
With a marked moral bias, driven onward in his search for [[Truth|truth]]
by his passionate religious temperament, [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], in his pictures
of life, has constructed a truer whole, a human world less bounded
by the [[artist]]'s individual limitations, more mysteriously living in its
vast flux and flow than is the world of any writer of the century.
War and Peace and Anna Karenina, those great worlds where the
physical environment, mental outlook, emotional [[Aspiration|aspiration]], and moral
code of the whole community of [[Russia]] are reproduced by his art,
as some mighty cunning phantasmagoria of changing life, are superior
in the sense of containing a whole nation's life, to the world of
Goethe, Byron, Scott, Victor Hugo, Balzac, Dickens, Thackeray, Maupassant,
or any latter day creator we can name.

And not only so, but [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s analysis of life throws more light
on the main currents of thought in our Age, raises deeper problems,
and explores more untouched territories of the mind than does any
corresponding analysis by his European contemporaries.

It is by [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s passionate seeking of the life of the [[Soul|soul]]
that the great Russian writer towers above the men of our day,
and it is because his hunger for spiritual [[Truth|truth]] has led him to probe
contemporary life, to examine all modern formulas and appearances,
to penetrate into the secret thought and emotion of men
of all grades in our complex society, that his work is charged
with the essence of nearly all that modernity thinks and feels,
believes and suffers, hopes and fears as it evolves in more
and more complex forms of our terribly complex civilisation.
The [[Soul|soul]] of humanity is, however, always the appeal of men
from the life that environs, moulds, and burdens them,
to instincts that go beyond and transcend their present life.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] is the appeal of the modern world, the cry of the modern
[[Conscience|conscience]] against the blinded fate of its own progress.
To the eye of science everything is possible in human life,
the sacrifice of the [[Innocent|innocent]] for the sake of the progress
of the guilty, the crushing and deforming of the weak
so that the strong may triumph over them, the evolution
of new serf classes at the dictates of a ruling class.
All this the nineteenth century has seen accomplished,
and not seen [[Alone|alone]] in [[Russia]].  It is [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s distinction
to have combined in his life-work more than any other great
[[artist]] two main conflicting points of view.  He has fused
by his art the science that defines the way Humanity is forced
forward blindly and irresponsibly from century to century
by the mere pressure of events, he has fused with this science
of our modern world the [[Soul|soul]]'s protest against the earthly fate
of man which leads the generations into taking the ceaseless
roads of evil which every age unwinds.

Let us cite [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s treatment of War as an instance of how this
great [[artist]] symbolises the Age for us and so marks the advance
in [[Self|self]]-consciousness of the modern mind, and as a nearer
approximation to a realisation of what life is.  We have only got
to compare [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s "Sebastopol" (1856) with any other document
on war by other European writers to perceive that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] [[Alone|alone]]
among artists has realised war, his fellows have idealised it.
To quote a passage from a former article let us say that:
"Sebastopol" gives us war under all aspects--war as a squalid,
honourable, daily affair of mud and glory, of [[Vanity|vanity]], disease,
hard work, stupidity, patriotism, and inhuman agony.  [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] gets
the complex effects of "Sebastopol" by keenly analysing the effect
of the sights and sounds, dangers and pleasures, of war on the brains
of a variety of typical men, and by placing a special valuation
of his own on these men's actions, thoughts, and emotions, on their
[[Courage|courage]], altruism, and show of indifference in the face of [[Death|death]].
He lifts up, in fact, the veil of appearances conventionally drawn
by society over the actualities of the glorious trade of killing men,
and he does this chiefly by analysing keenly the insensitiveness
and indifference of the average mind, which says of the worst
of war's realities, "I felt so and so, and did so and so:
but as to what those other thousands may have felt in their agony,
that I did not enter into at all."  "Sebastopol," therefore,
though an exceedingly short and exceedingly simple narrative,
is a psychological document on modern war of extraordinary value,
for it simply relegates to the lumber-room, as unlife-like and
hopelessly limited, all those theatrical glorifications of war which
men of letters, romantic poets, and [[Grave|grave]] historians alike have been
busily piling up on humanity's shelves from generation to generation.
And more:  we feel that in "Sebastopol" we have at last the skeptical
modern spirit, absorbed in actual life, demonstrating what war is,
and expressing at length the confused sensations of countless men,
who have heretofore, recognising this man [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] as the most advanced
product of our civilisation, and likening him to a great surgeon,
who, not deceived by the world's presentation of its own life,
penetrates into the essential joy and [[Suffering|suffering]], health and disease
of multitudes of men; a surgeon who, face to face with the strangest
of [[Nature]]'s laws in the constitution of human society, puzzled by
all the illusions, fatuities, and conventions of the human mind,
resolutely sets himself to lay bare the roots of all its passions,
appetites, and incentives in the struggle for life, so that at least
human reason may advance farther along the path of [[Self-Knowledge|self-knowledge]]
in advancing towards a general sociological study of man.

[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s place in nineteenth-century literature is, therefore,
in our view, no less fixed and certain than is Voltaire's place
in the eighteenth century.  Both of these writers focus for us in a
marvelously complete manner the respective methods of analysing life
by which the rationalism of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries,
and the science and [[Humanitarianism|humanitarianism]] of the nineteenth century have
moulded for us the modern world.  All the movements, all the problems,
all the speculation, all the agitations of the world of today in
contrast with the immense materialistic civilisation that science has
hastily built up for us in three or four generations, all the spirit
of modern life is condensed in the pages of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s writings,
because, as we have said, he typifies the [[Soul|soul]] of the modern
man gazing, now undaunted, and now in alarm, at the formidable array
of the newly-tabulated cause and effect of humanity's progress,
at the appalling cheapness and waste of human life in [[Nature]]'s hands.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] thus stands for the modern [[Soul|soul]]'s alarm in contact with science.
And just as science's work after its first destruction of the past ages'
formalism, superstition, and dogma is directed more and more to
the examination and amelioration of human life, so [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s work has
been throughout inspired by a passionate [[Love|love]] of humanity, and by his
ceaseless struggle against conventional religion, dogmatic science,
and society's mechanical influence on the minds of its members.
To make man more conscious of his acts, to show society its real
motives and what it is feeling, and not cry out in admiration at
what it pretends to feel--this has been the great novelist's aim
in his delineation of [[Russia]]'s life.  Ever seeking the one [[Truth|truth]]--
to arrive at men's thoughts and sensations under the daily pressure
of life--never flinching from his exploration of the dark world
of man's animalism and incessant [[Self-Deception|self-deception]], [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s realism
in art is symbolical of our absorption in the world of fact,
in the modern study of natural law, a study of ultimately without loss
of spirituality, nay, resulting in immense gain to the spiritual life.
The realism of the great Russian's novels is, therefore, more in line
with the modern tendency and outlook than is the general tendency
of other schools of Continental literature.  And [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] must
be finally looked on, not merely as the [[Conscience|conscience]] of the Russian
world revolting against the too heavy burden which the Russian
people have now to bear in Holy [[Russia]]'s onward march towards
the building-up of her great Asiatic Empire, but also as the [[Soul|soul]]
of the modern world seeking to replace in its [[Love|love]] of humanity the life
of those old religions which science is destroying day by day.
In this sense [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] will stand in European literature as the [[Conscience|conscience]]
of the modern world.

# [[tolstoy]] (part 1)

Leo [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]

Chesterton, G. K.; Perris, G. H.; Garnett, Edward

Hodder and Stoughton 1903

http://etext.lib.virginia.edu/modeng/modeng0.browse.html

[[tolstoy|TOLSTOY]]

IF any one wishes to form the fullest estimate of the real
character and influence of the great man whose name is prefixed
to these remarks, he will not find it in his novels, splendid as
they are, or in his ethical views, clearly and finely as they
are conceived and expanded.  He will find it best expressed
in the news that has recently come from Canada, that a sect
of Russian Christian anarchists has turned all its [[Animals|animals]] loose,
on the ground that it is immoral to possess them or control them.
About such an incident as this there is a quality altogether independent
of the rightness or wrongness, the [[Sanity|sanity]] or insanity, of the view.
It is first and foremost a reminder that the world is still young.
There are still theories of life as insanely reasonable as those which
were disputed under the clear blue skies of Athens.  There are still
examples of a [[Faith|faith]] as fierce and practical as that of the Mahometans,
who swept across Africa and Europe, shouting a single word.
To the languid contemporary politician and philosopher it seems doubtless
like something out of [[A Dream|a dream]], that in this iron-bound, homogeneous,
and clockwork age, a company of European men in boots and waistcoats
should begin to insist on taking the [[Horse|horse]] out of the shafts
of the omnibus, and lift the pig out of his pig-sty, and the dog
out of his kennel, because of a moral scruple or theory.
It is like a page from some fairy farce to imagine the Doukhabor
solemnly escorting a hen to the door of the yard and bidding
it a benevolent farewell as it sets out on its travels.
All this, as I say, seems mere muddle-headed absurdity to the typical
leader of human society in this decade, to a man like Mr. Balfour,
or Mr. Wyndham.  But there is nevertheless a further thing to be said,
and that is that, if Mr. Balfour could be converted to a religion
which taught him that he was morally bound to walk into the [[house|House]]
of Commons on his hands, and he did walk on his hands, if Mr. Wyndham
could accept a creed which taught that he ought to dye his hair blue,
and he did dye his hair blue, they would both of them be,
almost beyond description, better and happier men than they are.
For there is only one happiness possible or conceivable under the sun,
and that is enthusiasm--that strange and splendid word that has passed
through so many vicissitudes, which meant, in the eighteenth century
the condition of a lunatic, and in [[Ancient Greece|ancient Greece]] the presence
of a god.

This great act of heroic consistency which has taken place
in Canada is the best example of the work of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]].  It is true
(as I believe) that the Doukhabors have an origin quite
independent of the great Russian moralist, but there can
surely be little [[Doubt|doubt]] that their emergence into importance
and the growth and mental distinction of their sect, is due to his
admirable summary and justification of their scheme of ethics.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], besides being a magnificent novelist, is one of the very
few men alive who have a real, solid, and serious view of life.
He is a [[Catholic Church|Catholic church]], of which he is the only member,
the somewhat arrogant Pope and the somewhat submissive layman.
He is one of the two or three men in Europe, who have
an attitude towards things so entirely their own, that we
could supply their inevitable view on anything--a silk hat,
a [[Home]] Rule Bill, an Indian poem, or a pound of tobacco.
There are three men in existence who have such an attitude:
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], Mr. Bernard [[Shaw]], and my friend Mr. Hilaire Belloc.  They are
all diametrically opposed to each other, but they all have this
essential resemblance, that, given their basis of thought,
their soil of conviction, their opinions on every earthly
subject grow there naturally, like [[Flowers|flowers]] in a field.
There are certain views of certain things that they must take;
they do not form opinions, the opinions form themselves.
Take, for instance, in the case of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], the mere list
of miscellaneous objects which I wrote down at random above,
a silk hat, a [[Home]] Rule Bill, an Indian poem, and a pound of tobacco.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] would say:  "I believe in the utmost possible simplification
of life; therefore, this silk hat is a black abortion."
He would say:  "I believe in the utmost possible simplification
of life; therefore, this [[Home]] Rule Bill is a mere peddling compromise;
it is no good to break up a centralised empire into nations,
you must break the nation up into individuals."  He would say:
"I believe in the utmost possible simplification
of life; therefore, I am interested in this Indian poem,
for Eastern ethics, under all their apparent gorgeousness,
are far simpler and more Tolstoyan than Western."  He would say:
"I believe in the utmost possible simplification of life;
therefore, this pound of tobacco is a thing of evil; take it away."
Everything in the world, from the [[Bible]] to a bootjack,
can be, and is, reduced by [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] to this great fundamental
Tolstoyan principle, the simplification of life.
When we deal with a [[Body|body]] of opinion like this we are dealing
with an incident in the [[history]] of Europe infinitely more
important than the appearance of Napoleon Buonaparte.

This emergence of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], with his awful and simple ethics,
is important in more ways than one.  Among other things it is a very
interesting commentary on an attitude which has been taken up for
the matter of half a century by all the avowed opponents of religion.
The secularist and the sceptic have denounced [[Christianity]]
first and foremost, because of its encouragement of [[Fanaticism|fanaticism]];
because religious excitement led men to burn their neighbours
and to [[Dance|dance]] naked down the street.  How queer it all sounds now.
Religion can be swept out of the matter altogether, and still
there are philosophical and ethical theories which can produce
[[Fanaticism|fanaticism]] enough to fill the world.  [[Fanaticism]] has nothing at all
to do with religion.  There are [[Grave|grave]] scientific theories which,
if carried out logically, would result in the same fires
in the market-place and the same [[Nakedness|nakedness]] in the street.
There are modern esthetes who would expose themselves
like the Adamites if they could do it in elegant attitudes.
There are modern scientific moralists who would burn their
opponents alive, and would be quite contented if they were burnt
by some new chemical process.  And if any one doubts this
proposition--that [[Fanaticism|fanaticism]] has nothing to do with religion,
but has only to do with human [[Nature|nature]]--let him take this case of
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] and the Doukhabors.  A sect of men start with no theology
at all, but with the simple doctrine that we ought to [[Love|love]] our
neighbour and use no force against him, and they end in thinking
it wicked to carry a leather handbag, or to ride in a cart.
A great modern writer who erases theology altogether,
denies the validity of the Scriptures and the Churches alike,
forms a purely ethical theory that [[Love|love]] should be the instrument
of [[Reform|reform]], and ends by maintaining that we have no right
to strike a man if he is torturing a [[Child|child]] before our eyes.
He goes on, he develops a theory of the mind and the emotions,
which might be held by the most rigid atheist, and he ends
by maintaining that the sexual relation out of which all humanity
has come, is not only not moral, but is positively not natural.
This is [[Fanaticism|fanaticism]] as it has been and as it will always be.
Destroy the last copy of he [[Bible]], and persecution and insane orgies
will be founded on Mr. Herbert Spencer's "Synthetic [[philosophy|Philosophy]]."  Some of
the broadest thinkers of the Middle Ages believed in faggots,
and some of the broadest thinkers in the nineteenth century
believe in dynamite.

[[tolstoy|TOLSTOJ]]

SE qualcuno desidera formarsi la stima più piena del carattere e dell'influenza reali del grande uomo il cui nome è premesso a queste osservazioni, non la troverà nei suoi romanzi, per quanto splendidi essi siano, né nelle sue vedute etiche, per quanto chiaramente e finemente concepite ed esposte. La troverà espressa nel modo migliore nella notizia giunta di recente dal Canada, secondo cui una setta di anarchici cristiani russi ha liberato tutti i suoi [[Animals|animali]], sulla base del fatto che è immorale possederli o controllarli. In un episodio come questo c'è una qualità del tutto indipendente dalla giustezza o dall'erroneità, dalla [[Sanity|sanità mentale]] o dalla follia, di quella veduta. Esso è anzitutto e soprattutto un promemoria che il mondo è ancora giovane. Vi sono ancora teorie della vita altrettanto folli e insieme ragionevoli di quelle che si dibattevano sotto i limpidi cieli azzurri di Atene. Vi sono ancora esempi di una [[Faith|fede]] fiera e concreta come quella dei maomettani, che dilagarono per l'Africa e l'Europa gridando una sola parola. Al languido politico e filosofo contemporaneo pare senza dubbio qualcosa uscito da [[A Dream|un sogno]] che, in questa età ferrigna, omogenea e meccanica, una compagnia di uomini europei in stivali e panciotto si metta a insistere per togliere il [[Horse|cavallo]] dalle stanghe dell'omnibus, e sollevare il maiale dal suo porcile e il cane dal suo canile, per uno scrupolo o una teoria morale. È come una pagina di qualche farsa fatata immaginare il Doukhabor scortare solennemente una gallina fino al cancello del cortile e darle un benevolo commiato mentre essa parte per i suoi viaggi. Tutto ciò, come dico, pare pura assurdità confusa al tipico capo della società umana in questo decennio, a un uomo come il signor Balfour o il signor Wyndham. Eppure c'è un'ulteriore cosa da dire, ed è questa: che se il signor Balfour potesse essere convertito a una religione che gli insegnasse che era moralmente tenuto a entrare nella [[house|Camera]] dei Comuni camminando sulle mani, ed egli davvero camminasse sulle mani; se il signor Wyndham potesse accogliere un credo che insegnasse che egli dovrebbe tingersi i capelli di azzurro, ed egli davvero si tingesse i capelli di azzurro, entrambi sarebbero, quasi al di là di ogni descrizione, uomini migliori e più felici di quanto siano. Poiché non vi è che una sola felicità possibile o concepibile sotto il sole, ed è l'entusiasmo—quella strana e splendida parola passata attraverso tante vicissitudini, che nel diciottesimo secolo significava la condizione di un pazzo, e nell'[[Ancient Greece|antica Grecia]] la presenza di un dio.

Questo grande atto di eroica coerenza avvenuto in Canada è il migliore esempio dell'opera di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]]. È vero (come credo) che i Doukhabor hanno un'origine del tutto indipendente dal grande moralista russo, ma non può esservi che poco [[Doubt|dubbio]] che la loro emersione all'importanza e la crescita e distinzione intellettuale della loro setta siano dovute alla sua mirabile sintesi e giustificazione del loro schema etico. [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], oltre a essere un magnifico romanziere, è uno dei pochissimi uomini viventi che abbiano una veduta della vita reale, solida e seria. Egli è una [[Catholic Church|chiesa cattolica]] di cui è l'unico membro, il papa alquanto arrogante e il laico alquanto sottomesso. È uno dei due o tre uomini in Europa che hanno verso le cose un atteggiamento così interamente proprio che potremmo fornire la loro inevitabile veduta su qualsiasi cosa—un cilindro di seta, un disegno di legge sull'[[Home]] Rule, un poema indiano, o una libbra di tabacco. Vi sono tre uomini al mondo che possiedono un tale atteggiamento: [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], il signor Bernard [[Shaw]] e il mio amico signor Hilaire Belloc. Sono tutti diametralmente opposti l'uno all'altro, ma hanno tutti questa somiglianza essenziale: che, data la loro base di pensiero, il loro terreno di convinzione, le loro opinioni su ogni argomento terreno vi crescono naturalmente, come [[Flowers|fiori]] in un campo. Vi sono certe vedute su certe cose che essi devono per forza avere; non formano opinioni, le opinioni si formano da sé. Prendete, per esempio, nel caso di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], la semplice lista di oggetti disparati che ho annotato a caso qui sopra: un cilindro di seta, un disegno di legge sull'[[Home]] Rule, un poema indiano e una libbra di tabacco. [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] direbbe: "Io credo nella massima semplificazione possibile della vita; perciò questo cilindro di seta è un nero aborto." Direbbe: "Io credo nella massima semplificazione possibile della vita; perciò questo disegno di legge sull'[[Home]] Rule è un misero compromesso da mercantucolo; non serve a nulla frantumare un impero centralizzato in nazioni, bisogna frantumare la nazione in individui." Direbbe: "Io credo nella massima semplificazione possibile della vita; perciò mi interessa questo poema indiano, poiché l'etica orientale, sotto tutta la sua apparente sfarzosità, è assai più semplice e più tolstoiana di quella occidentale." Direbbe: "Io credo nella massima semplificazione possibile della vita; perciò questa libbra di tabacco è cosa malvagia; portatela via." Ogni cosa al mondo, dalla [[Bible|Bibbia]] a un cavastivali, può essere, ed è, ridotta da [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] a questo grande principio fondamentale tolstoiano, la semplificazione della vita. Quando ci occupiamo di un [[Body|corpo]] di opinioni come questo, ci occupiamo di un episodio nella [[history|storia]] d'Europa infinitamente più importante della comparsa di Napoleone Buonaparte.

Questa emersione di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], con la sua etica tremenda e semplice, è importante per più di una ragione. Tra le altre cose è un commento assai interessante a un atteggiamento assunto da mezzo secolo da tutti i dichiarati avversari della religione. Il laicista e lo scettico hanno denunciato il [[Christianity|Cristianesimo]] prima di tutto e soprattutto a causa del suo incoraggiamento al [[Fanaticism|fanatismo]]; perché l'eccitazione religiosa portava gli uomini a bruciare i loro vicini e a [[Dance|danzare]] nudi per la strada. Come suona tutto ciò strano ora. La religione può essere spazzata via del tutto dalla questione, e restano ancora teorie filosofiche ed etiche che possono produrre [[Fanaticism|fanatismo]] a sufficienza per riempire il mondo. Il [[Fanaticism|fanatismo]] non ha nulla a che vedere con la religione. Vi sono [[Grave|gravi]] teorie scientifiche che, se portate alle loro conseguenze logiche, produrrebbero gli stessi roghi nella piazza del mercato e la stessa [[Nakedness|nudità]] per la strada. Vi sono esteti moderni che si esporrebbero come gli Adamiti, se potessero farlo in eleganti pose. Vi sono moralisti scientifici moderni che brucerebbero vivi i loro avversari, e sarebbero del tutto soddisfatti se venissero bruciati mediante qualche nuovo procedimento chimico. E se qualcuno dubita di questa proposizione—che il [[Fanaticism|fanatismo]] non ha nulla a che vedere con la religione, ma solo con la [[Nature|natura]] umana—prenda in esame questo caso di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] e dei Doukhabor. Una setta di uomini parte senza teologia alcuna, ma con la semplice dottrina che dobbiamo [[Love|amare]] il nostro prossimo e non usare alcuna forza contro di lui, e finisce col ritenere cosa malvagia portare una borsa di cuoio, o viaggiare in carretto. Un grande scrittore moderno che cancella del tutto la teologia, nega la validità delle Scritture e delle Chiese allo stesso modo, formula una teoria puramente etica secondo cui l'[[Love|amore]] dovrebbe essere lo strumento della [[Reform|riforma]], e finisce col sostenere che non abbiamo il diritto di colpire un uomo se sta torturando un [[Child|bambino]] sotto i nostri occhi. Prosegue, sviluppa una teoria della mente e delle emozioni che potrebbe essere sostenuta dal più rigido ateo, e finisce col sostenere che la relazione sessuale da cui è venuta tutta l'umanità non è soltanto non morale, ma è positivamente non naturale. Questo è il [[Fanaticism|fanatismo]] come è stato e come sarà sempre. Distruggete l'ultima copia della [[Bible|Bibbia]], e persecuzione e orge insensate verranno fondate sulla "[[philosophy|Filosofia]] Sintetica" del signor Herbert Spencer. Alcuni dei pensatori più larghi del Medioevo credevano nelle fascine, e alcuni dei pensatori più larghi del diciannovesimo secolo credono nella dinamite.

# [[tolstoy]] (part 2)

The [[Truth|truth]] is that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], with his immense genius, with his
colossal [[Faith|faith]], with his vast fearlessness and vast knowledge
of life, is deficient in one faculty and one faculty [[Alone|alone]].
He is not a mystic:  and therefore he has a tendency to go mad.
Men talk of the extravagances and frenzies that have been
produced by mysticism:  they are a mere drop in the bucket.
In the main, and from the beginning of [[Time|time]], mysticisrn has
kept men sane.  The thing that has driven them mad was [[logic]].
It is significant that, with all that has been said about
the excitability of poets, only one English poet ever went mad,
and he went mad from a logical system of theology.
He was Cowper, and his poetry retarded his insanity for many years.
So poetry, in which [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] is deficient, has always been
a tonic and sanative thing.  The only thing that has kept
the race of men from the mad extremes of the convent and
the pirate-galley, the [[Night|night]]-club and the lethal chamber,
has been mysticism--the belief that [[logic]] is misleading,
and that things are not what they seem.

G. K. CHESTERTON.

LEO [[tolstoy|TOLSTOY]] AS WRITER

HALF the ignorance or misunderstanding of this greatest living
figure in literature comes of the attempt to judge him as we judge
the specialised Western novelist--an utterly futile method of approach.
He is a Russian, in the first place.  Had he come to [[Paris]]
with Turguenieff, he might have been similarly re-nationalised,
might possibly have developed into a writer pure and simple;
the world might so have gained a few great romances--it would have lost
infinitely in other directions.  Turguenieff wished it so.  "My friend,"
he wrote to [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] from his deathbed, "return to literature!
Reflect that that gift comes to you whence everything comes to us.
Ah! how happy I should be if I could think that my prayers could influence
you.... My friend, great writer of our Russian land, hear my entreaty!"
For once, the second greatest of modern Russians took a narrow view
of character and destiny.  Genius must work itself out on its own lines.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] remained a Russian from tip to toe--that is one of his
supreme values for us; and he remained an indivisible personality.
The [[artist]] and the moralist are inseparable in his works.
"We are not to take 'Anna Karenina' as a work of art,"
said Matthew Arnold; "we are to take it as a piece of life."
The distinction is not very satisfactorily stated, but the meaning
is clear.  So, too, W. D. Howells, in his introduction to an American
edition of the "Sebastopol Sketches": "I do not know how it is with
others to whom these books of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s have come, but for my part
I cannot think of them as literature in the artistic sense at all.
Some people complain to me when I praise them that they are too long,
too diffuse, too confused, that the characters' names are hard
to pronounce, and that the life they portray is very sad and not amusing.
In the presence of these criticisms I can only say that I find them
nothing of the kind, but that each [[history]] of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s is as clear,
as orderly, as brief, as something I have lived through myself.
I cannot think of any service which imaginative literature has
done the race so great as that which [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] has done in his
conception of Karenina at that crucial moment when the cruelly
outraged man sees that he cannot be good with dignity.  This [[Leaves|leaves]]
all tricks of fancy, all effects of art, immeasurably behind."
So much being said, however, we may be allowed to emphasise
in this qualities and achievements of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] as [[artist]],
rather than the expositions of Christian Anarchism and the social
philippics under which those achievements have been somewhat hidden
in recent years.

Morbid introspectiveness and the spirit of revolt inevitably
colour what is best in nineteenth-century [[Russia]].  Born at
Yasnaya Polyana ("Clear Field"), Tula, in 1828, and early orphaned,
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s youth synchronised with the period of reaction
that brought the Empire to the humiliating disasters of the
Crimean War.  No [[Hope|hope]] was left in the thin layer of society
lying between the two mill-stones of the [[Court]] and the serfs;
none in the little sphere of art where Byronic romanticism
was ready to expire.  The boy saw from the first the rottenness
of the patriarchal aristocracy in which his lot seemed
to be cast.  Precocious, abnormally sensitive and observant,
impatient of discipline and formal learning, awkward and bashful,
always brooding, not a little conceited, he was a sceptic at fifteen,
and left the University of Kazan in disgust at the stupid
conventions of the [[Time|time]] and place, without taking his degree.
"[[Childhood]], Boyhood, and Youth"--which appeared in three
sections between 1852 and 1857--tells the story of this period,
though the figure of Irtenieff is probably a projection rather
than a portrait of himself, to whom he is always less fair,
not to say merciful, than to others.  This book is a most
uncompromising exercise in [[Self|self]]-analysis. It of great length,
there is no plot, and few outer events are recorded.

The realism is generally morbid, but is varied by some passages of great
descriptive power, such as the account of the [[Storm|storm]], and occasionally
with tender pathos, as in the story of the soldier's [[Death|death]], as [[Well|well]]
as by grimly vivid pages, such as the narrative of the mother's [[Death|death]].
In this earliest work will be found the seeds both of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s artistic
genius and of his ethical gospel.

After five years of mildly benevolent efforts among his serfs
at Yasnaya Polyana (the disappointments of which he related a few
years later in "A Landlord's Morning," intended to have been
part of a full [[Novel|novel]] called "A Russian Proprietor"), his elder
brother Nicholas persuaded him to join the army, and in 1851
he was drafted to the Caucasus as an artillery officer.
On this favourite stage of Russian [[Romance|romance]], where for the first
[[Time|time]] he saw the towering [[Mountains|mountains]] and the tropical sun, and met
the rugged adventurous highlanders, [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] felt his [[Imagination|imagination]]
stirred as Byron among the isles of Greece, and his early revulsion
against [[City|city]] life confirmed as Wordsworth amid the Lakes,
as Thoreau at Walden, by a direct call from [[Nature]] to his own heart.
The largest result of this experience was "The Cossacks"
(1852). Turguenieff described this fine prose [[Epic|epic]] of the contact of
civilised and savage man as "the best [[Novel|novel]] written in our language."
"The Raid" (or "The Invaders," as Mr. Dole's [[Translation|translation]] is
entitled), same year, "The Wood-Cutting Expedition" (1855), "Meeting
an Old Acquaintance" (1856), and "A Prisoner in the Caucasus"
(1862) are also drawn from recollections of this sojourn,
and show the same descriptive and romantic power.  Upon the outbreak
of the Crimean War the Count was called to Sebastopol, where he had
command of a battery, and took part in the defence of the citadel.
The immediate product of these dark months of bloodshed was the
thrilling series of impressions reprinted from one of the leading
Russian reviews as "Sebastopol Sketches" (1856). From that day
onward [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] knew and hateful [[Truth|truth]] about war and the thoughtless
pseudo-patriotism which hurries nations into fratricidal slaughter.
From that there was expunged from his mind all the cheap romanticism
which depends upon the glorification of the savage [[Nature|nature]].
These wonderful pictures of the routine of the [[Battlefield|battlefield]] established
his position in [[Russia]] as a writer, and later on created in Western
countries an impression like that of the canvases of Verestchagin.


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

La [[Truth|verità]] è che [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], con il suo immenso genio, con la sua colossale [[Faith|fede]], con la sua vasta intrepidezza e la sua vasta conoscenza della vita, è carente in una facoltà e in una facoltà [[Alone|soltanto]]. Non è un mistico: e perciò ha una tendenza a impazzire. Gli uomini parlano delle stravaganze e delle frenesie prodotte dal misticismo: sono una goccia nel mare. Nel complesso, e fin dall'inizio del [[Time|tempo]], il misticismo ha mantenuto sani gli uomini. La cosa che li ha spinti alla follia è stata la [[logic|logica]]. È significativo che, con tutto ciò che è stato detto sull'eccitabilità dei poeti, un solo poeta inglese sia mai impazzito, ed egli impazzì per un sistema logico di teologia. Era Cowper, e la sua poesia ne ritardò per molti anni la follia. Così la poesia, in cui [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] è carente, è sempre stata una cosa tonica e risanatrice. L'unica cosa che ha tenuto la razza degli uomini lontana dagli estremi folli del convento e della galea del pirata, del [[Night|night]]-club e della camera letale, è stata il misticismo—la convinzione che la [[logic|logica]] sia fuorviante, e che le cose non siano ciò che sembrano.

G. K. CHESTERTON.

LEO [[tolstoy|TOLSTOJ]] COME SCRITTORE

METÀ dell'ignoranza o dell'incomprensione di questa figura, la più grande figura vivente della letteratura, deriva dal tentativo di giudicarlo come giudichiamo lo specializzato romanziere occidentale—un metodo di approccio del tutto futile. Egli è un russo, in primo luogo. Se fosse venuto a [[Paris|Parigi]] con Turgenev, forse sarebbe stato similmente ri-nazionalizzato, forse si sarebbe potuto sviluppare in uno scrittore puro e semplice; il mondo avrebbe così potuto guadagnare qualche grande romanzo—ma avrebbe perduto infinitamente in altre direzioni. Turgenev lo desiderava. "Amico mio," scrisse a [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] dal letto di morte, "torna alla letteratura! Rifletti che quel dono ti viene di là donde ci viene ogni cosa. Ah! come sarei felice se potessi pensare che le mie preghiere possano influenzarti.... Amico mio, grande scrittore della nostra terra russa, ascolta la mia supplica!" Per una volta, il secondo più grande dei russi moderni prese una veduta angusta del carattere e del destino. Il genio deve svilupparsi secondo le proprie linee. [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] rimase russo dalla testa ai piedi—questo è uno dei suoi supremi valori per noi; e rimase una personalità indivisibile. L'[[artist|artista]] e il moralista sono inseparabili nelle sue opere. "Non dobbiamo prendere 'Anna Karenina' come un'opera d'arte," disse Matthew Arnold; "dobbiamo prenderla come un pezzo di vita." La distinzione non è formulata in modo molto soddisfacente, ma il significato è chiaro. Così, del pari, W. D. Howells, nella sua introduzione a un'edizione americana dei "Racconti di Sebastopoli": "Non so come sia per gli altri a cui sono giunti questi libri di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], ma per parte mia non riesco a pensarli affatto come letteratura in senso artistico. Alcuni si lamentano con me, quando li lodo, che sono troppo lunghi, troppo diffusi, troppo confusi, che i nomi dei personaggi sono difficili da pronunciare, e che la vita che ritraggono è molto triste e poco divertente. Di fronte a queste critiche posso solo dire che li trovo tutt'altro, e che ogni [[history|storia]] di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] è chiara, ordinata, breve come qualcosa che io stesso abbia vissuto. Non riesco a pensare ad alcun servizio reso alla razza dalla letteratura d'immaginazione così grande come quello che [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] ha reso nella sua concezione della Karenina in quel momento cruciale in cui l'uomo crudelmente oltraggiato vede di non poter essere buono con dignità. Ciò lascia immensamente indietro tutti gli artifici della fantasia, tutti gli effetti dell'arte." Detto ciò, tuttavia, ci sia consentito porre l'accento qui sulle qualità e sulle conquiste di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] come [[artist|artista]], piuttosto che sulle esposizioni dell'Anarchismo Cristiano e sulle filippiche sociali sotto cui quelle conquiste sono state in qualche modo nascoste negli anni recenti.

L'introspettività morbosa e lo spirito di rivolta colorano inevitabilmente ciò che di meglio vi è nella [[Russia]] del diciannovesimo secolo. Nato a Yasnaya Polyana ("Campo Chiaro"), Tula, nel 1828, e presto orfano, la giovinezza di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] si sincronizzò con il periodo di reazione che condusse l'Impero agli umilianti disastri della Guerra di Crimea. Nessuna [[Hope|speranza]] restava nel sottile strato di società posto fra le due macine della [[Court|Corte]] e dei servi della gleba; nessuna nella piccola sfera dell'arte, dove il romanticismo byroniano era pronto a spegnersi. Il fanciullo vide fin da principio il marciume dell'aristocrazia patriarcale in cui la sua sorte pareva essere gettata. Precoce, anormalmente sensibile e osservatore, insofferente della disciplina e dell'apprendimento formale, goffo e timido, sempre rimuginante, non poco presuntuoso, era uno scettico a quindici anni, e lasciò l'Università di Kazan disgustato dalle stupide convenzioni del [[Time|tempo]] e del luogo, senza conseguire la laurea. "[[Childhood|Infanzia]], Adolescenza e Giovinezza"—apparso in tre parti fra il 1852 e il 1857—narra la storia di questo periodo, benché la figura di Irtenieff sia probabilmente una proiezione più che un ritratto di se stesso, verso il quale è sempre meno giusto, per non dire misericordioso, che verso gli altri. Questo libro è un esercizio quanto mai intransigente di analisi del [[Self|sé]]. È di grande lunghezza, non vi è alcun intreccio, e pochi eventi esteriori sono registrati.

Il realismo è generalmente morboso, ma è variato da alcuni passi di grande potenza descrittiva, come il racconto della [[Storm|tempesta]], e occasionalmente da tenero pathos, come nella storia della [[Death|morte]] del soldato, [[Well|nonché]] da pagine cupamente vivide, come la narrazione della [[Death|morte]] della madre. In quest'opera primissima si troveranno i semi tanto del genio artistico di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] quanto del suo vangelo etico.

Dopo cinque anni di sforzi mitemente benevoli tra i suoi servi della gleba a Yasnaya Polyana (le cui delusioni egli narrò pochi anni dopo in "La mattinata di un possidente", pensata per far parte di un intero [[Novel|romanzo]] intitolato "Un proprietario russo"), il fratello maggiore Nicholas lo persuase ad arruolarsi nell'esercito, e nel 1851 fu inviato nel Caucaso come ufficiale d'artiglieria. Su questo prediletto palcoscenico del [[Romance|romanzo]] russo, dove per la prima [[Time|volta]] vide le [[Mountains|montagne]] torreggianti e il sole tropicale, e incontrò i rudi e avventurosi montanari, [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] sentì la propria [[Imagination|immaginazione]] scossa come Byron tra le isole di Grecia, e la sua precoce ripulsa per la vita di [[City|città]] confermata come Wordsworth fra i Laghi, come Thoreau a Walden, da un richiamo diretto della [[Nature|Natura]] al suo cuore. Il maggior frutto di questa esperienza fu "I Cosacchi" (1852). Turgenev descrisse questa bella [[Epic|epopea]] in prosa del contatto fra l'uomo civilizzato e l'uomo selvaggio come "il miglior [[Novel|romanzo]] scritto nella nostra lingua." "L'incursione" (o "Gli invasori", come è intitolata la [[Translation|traduzione]] del signor Dole), dello stesso anno, "Il taglio del bosco" (1855), "L'incontro con un vecchio conoscente" (1856) e "Un prigioniero nel Caucaso" (1862) sono anch'essi tratti dai ricordi di questo soggiorno, e mostrano la medesima potenza descrittiva e romantica. Allo scoppio della Guerra di Crimea il Conte fu chiamato a Sebastopoli, dove ebbe il comando di una batteria e prese parte alla difesa della cittadella. Il prodotto immediato di quei bui mesi di spargimento di sangue fu l'emozionante serie di impressioni ristampata da una delle principali riviste russe come "Racconti di Sebastopoli" (1856). Da quel giorno in poi [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] conobbe l'odiosa [[Truth|verità]] sulla guerra e sullo sconsiderato pseudo-patriottismo che precipita le nazioni nella strage fratricida. Da allora fu espunto dalla sua mente tutto il romanticismo a buon mercato che dipende dalla glorificazione della [[Nature|natura]] selvaggia. Questi meravigliosi quadri della routine del [[Battlefield|campo di battaglia]] consolidarono la sua posizione in [[Russia]] come scrittore, e più tardi crearono nei paesi occidentali un'impressione pari a quella delle tele di Verestchagin.

# [[tolstoy]] (part 3)

For a brief [[Time|time]] [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] became a figure in the old and new
capitals of [[Russia]] by right of talent as [[Well|well]] as birth.
His very chequered [[Friendship|friendship]] with Turguenieff, one of the oddest
chapters in literary [[history]], can only be mentioned here.
In 1857 he travelled in [[Germany]], France, and [[Italy]].  It was
of these years that he declared in "My Confession" that he could
not think of them without horror, disgust, and [[Pain|pain]] of heart.
The catalogue of crime which he charged against himself in his
salvationist crisis of twenty years later must not be taken literally;
but that there was some ground for it we may guess from the scenic
and incidental realism of the "Recollections of a Billiard Marker"
(1856), and of many a later page.  Several other powerful
short novels date from about this [[Time|time]], including "Albert"
and "Lucerne," both of which remind us of the Count's
susceptibility to music; "Polikushka," a tale of peasant life;
and "Family Happiness," the story of a [[Marriage|marriage]] that failed,
a most clear, consistent, forceful, and in parts beautiful
piece of work, anticipating in essentials "The Kreutzer Sonata"
that was to scandalise the world thirty years afterward.

After all, it was family happiness that saved Leo [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]].  For the
third [[Time|time]] the hand of [[Death|death]] had snatched away one of the nearest
to him--his brother Nicholas.  Two years later, in 1862, he married
Miss Behrs, daughter of the army surgeon in Tula--the most fortunate
thing that has happened to him in his whole life, I should think.
Family responsibilities, those [[Novel|novel]] and daring experiments in peasant
[[Education|education]] which are recorded in several volumes of the highest interest,
the supervision of the estate, magisterial work, and last,
but not least, the prolonged labours upon "War and Peace" and
"Anna Karenina" fill up the next fifteen years.  "War and Peace"
(1864-9) is a huge panorama of the Napoleonic campaign of 1812,
with preceding and succeeding episodes in Russian society.
These four volumes display in their superlative degree [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s
indifference to plot and his absorption in individual character;
they are rather a series of scenes threaded upon the fortunes of several
families than a set [[Novel|novel]]; but they contain passages of penetrating
psychology and vivid description, as [[Well|well]] as a certain amount of
anarchist theorising.  Of this work, by which its author became known
in the West, Flaubert (how the name carries us backward!) wrote:
"It is of the first order.  What a painter and what a psychologist!
The two first volumes are [[Sublime|sublime]], but the third drags frightfully.
There are some quite Shakespearean things in it."
The [[artist]]'s hand was now strengthening for his highest attainment.
In 1876 appeared "Anna Karenina," his greatest, and as he intended
at the [[Time|time]] (but Art is not so easily jilted), his last [[Novel|novel]].
The fine qualities of this book, which, though long, is dramatically
unified and vitally coherent, have been so fully recognised that I
need not attempt to describe them.  Mr. George Meredith has
described Anna as "the most perfectly depicted female character
in all fiction," which, from the author of "[[Diana]]," is praise indeed.
Parallel with the main subject of the illicit [[Love|love]] of Anna and
Vronsky there is a minor subject in the fortunes of Levin and Kitty,
wherein the reader will discover many of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s own experiences.
Matthew Arnold complained that the book contained too many
characters and a burdensome multiplicity of actions, but praised
its author's extraordinarily fine perception and no less
extraordinary truthfulness, and frankly revelled in Anna's
"large, fresh, rich, generous, delightful [[Nature|nature]]."  "When I had
ended my work 'Anna Karenina,'" said [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] in "My Confession"
(1879-82), "my [[Despair|despair]] reached such a height that I could
do nothing but think of the horrible condition in which I found
myself.... I saw only one thing--[[Death]]. Everything else was a lie."
Of that spiritual crisis nothing need be said here except that it
only intensified, and did not really, as it seemed to do,
vitally change, principles and instincts which had possessed
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] from the beginning.  His subsequent ethical and religious
development may be traced in a long series of books and pamphlets,
of which the most important are "The Gospels Translated, Compared,
and Harmonised" (1880-2), "What I Believe" ["My Religion"],
produced abroad in 1884, "What is to be Done?" (1884-5), "Life"
(1887), "Work" (1888), "The Kingdom of God is Within You"
(1893), "Non-Action" (1894), "Patriotism and [[Christianity]]"
(1896)--crusade, in the foreign and the clandestine presses at least,
against all Imperial authority and social maladjustments.
Mr. Tchertkoff, Mr. Aylmer Maudej the "Brotherhood Publishing Co.,"
and the "Free Age Press" deserve praise for their efforts to popularise
these and other works of the Count in thoroughly good translations.
In "What is Art?" (1898), not content with the bare utilitarian argument
that it is merely a means of social [[Union|union]], he launched a jehad against
all modern ideas of Art which rely upon a conception of [[Beauty|beauty]] and all
ideas of [[Beauty|beauty]] into which pleasure enters as a leading constituent.
A short but luminous [[Essay|essay]] on "Guy de Maupassant and the Art
of Fiction" is a scathing attack upon militarism in general and
the Franco-Russian Alliance in particular--"The Christian Teaching"
(1898), and "The [[Slavery]] of our Times" (1900). Various letters
on the successive famines and on the religious persecutions in [[Russia]]
deserve separate mention; they remind us that since the failure
of the revolutionary movement miscalled "Nihilism" [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] has
gradually risen to the position of the one man who can continue
with impunity a public more satisfactory contribution to the subject.


<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>

Per un breve [[Time|tempo]] [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] divenne una figura nelle capitali vecchia e nuova della [[Russia]] per diritto di talento [[Well|oltre]] che di nascita. La sua assai variegata [[Friendship|amicizia]] con Turgenev, uno dei capitoli più singolari della [[history|storia]] letteraria, può qui soltanto essere accennata. Nel 1857 viaggiò in [[Germany|Germania]], Francia e [[Italy|Italia]]. Fu di questi anni che egli dichiarò in "La mia confessione" di non poter pensare a essi senza orrore, disgusto e [[Pain|pena]] del cuore. Il catalogo di colpe di cui si accusò nella sua crisi salvazionista di vent'anni più tardi non va preso alla lettera; ma che vi fosse qualche fondamento possiamo indovinarlo dal realismo scenico e incidentale dei "Ricordi di un marcatore di biliardo" (1856), e di molte pagine successive. Diverse altre potenti novelle risalgono all'incirca a questo [[Time|tempo]], tra cui "Albert" e "Lucerna", entrambe le quali ci ricordano la sensibilità del Conte per la musica; "Polikushka", un racconto di vita contadina; e "Felicità familiare", la storia di un [[Marriage|matrimonio]] fallito, un pezzo di lavoro quanto mai chiaro, coerente, vigoroso e in alcune parti bello, che anticipa nell'essenziale "La sonata a Kreutzer" che avrebbe scandalizzato il mondo trent'anni dopo.

Dopotutto, fu la felicità familiare che salvò Leo [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]]. Per la terza [[Time|volta]] la mano della [[Death|morte]] gli aveva strappato uno dei più prossimi a lui—il fratello Nicholas. Due anni dopo, nel 1862, sposò la signorina Behrs, figlia del chirurgo dell'esercito a Tula—la cosa più fortunata che gli sia capitata in tutta la sua vita, direi. Le responsabilità familiari, quei nuovi e arditi esperimenti di [[Education|educazione]] contadina che sono registrati in vari volumi del più alto interesse, la sovrintendenza della tenuta, il lavoro di magistrato, e ultimo ma non meno importante, le prolungate fatiche su "Guerra e pace" e "Anna Karenina" riempiono i quindici anni successivi. "Guerra e pace" (1864-9) è un enorme panorama della campagna napoleonica del 1812, con episodi precedenti e seguenti nella società russa. Questi quattro volumi mostrano al loro grado superlativo l'indifferenza di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] per l'intreccio e il suo assorbimento nel carattere individuale; sono piuttosto una serie di scene infilate sulle sorti di più famiglie che un [[Novel|romanzo]] compiuto; ma contengono passi di psicologia penetrante e vivida descrizione, [[Well|oltre]] che una certa dose di teorizzazione anarchica. Di quest'opera, per la quale il suo autore divenne noto in Occidente, Flaubert (come il nome ci trasporta indietro!) scrisse: "È di primissimo ordine. Che pittore e che psicologo! I due primi volumi sono [[Sublime|sublimi]], ma il terzo si trascina spaventosamente. Vi sono in esso alcune cose del tutto shakespeariane." La mano dell'[[artist|artista]] si andava ora rafforzando per la sua più alta conquista. Nel 1876 apparve "Anna Karenina", il suo più grande romanzo e, come egli intendeva al [[Time|tempo]] (ma l'Arte non si lascia piantare in asso tanto facilmente), il suo ultimo [[Novel|romanzo]]. Le fini qualità di questo libro, che, benché lungo, è drammaticamente unificato e vitalmente coerente, sono state riconosciute così pienamente che non ho bisogno di tentare di descriverle. Il signor George Meredith ha descritto Anna come "il personaggio femminile più perfettamente ritratto in tutta la narrativa", il che, dall'autore di "[[Diana]]", è davvero un elogio. Parallelamente al soggetto principale dell'[[Love|amore]] illecito di Anna e Vronskij vi è un soggetto minore nelle sorti di Levin e Kitty, in cui il lettore scoprirà molte delle esperienze di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] stesso. Matthew Arnold lamentò che il libro contenesse troppi personaggi e una gravosa molteplicità di azioni, ma lodò la percezione straordinariamente fine dell'autore e la sua non meno straordinaria veridicità, e si abbandonò francamente al piacere della "ampia, fresca, ricca, generosa, deliziosa [[Nature|natura]]" di Anna. "Quando ebbi finito la mia opera 'Anna Karenina'," disse [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] in "La mia confessione" (1879-82), "la mia [[Despair|disperazione]] raggiunse un'altezza tale che non potevo far altro che pensare all'orribile condizione in cui mi trovavo.... Vedevo una sola cosa—la [[Death|Morte]]. Tutto il resto era menzogna." Di quella crisi spirituale nulla occorre dire qui, se non che essa non fece che intensificare, e non mutò veramente, per quanto sembrasse farlo, i principi e gli istinti che avevano posseduto [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] fin dal principio. Il suo successivo sviluppo etico e religioso può essere seguito in una lunga serie di libri e opuscoli, dei quali i più importanti sono "I Vangeli tradotti, confrontati e armonizzati" (1880-2), "In che cosa credo" ["La mia religione"], prodotto all'estero nel 1884, "Che fare?" (1884-5), "La vita" (1887), "Il lavoro" (1888), "Il regno di Dio è dentro di voi" (1893), "La non-azione" (1894), "Patriottismo e [[Christianity|Cristianesimo]]" (1896)—una crociata, almeno nelle stampe estere e clandestine, contro ogni autorità imperiale e contro le disfunzioni sociali. Il signor Tchertkoff, il signor Aylmer Maude, la "Brotherhood Publishing Co." e la "Free Age Press" meritano lode per i loro sforzi di divulgare queste e altre opere del Conte in traduzioni davvero buone. In "Che cos'è l'arte?" (1898), non contento del nudo argomento utilitario secondo cui essa è meramente un mezzo di [[Union|unione]] sociale, lanciò una jihad contro tutte le idee moderne dell'Arte che si fondano su una concezione della [[Beauty|Bellezza]] e contro tutte le idee della [[Beauty|Bellezza]] in cui il piacere entra come elemento dominante. Un breve ma luminoso [[Essay|saggio]] su "Guy de Maupassant e l'arte della narrativa" è un feroce attacco al militarismo in generale e all'Alleanza franco-russa in particolare—"L'insegnamento cristiano" (1898), e "La [[Slavery|Schiavitù]] dei nostri tempi" (1900). Varie lettere sulle successive carestie e sulle persecuzioni religiose in [[Russia]] meritano menzione separata; esse ci ricordano che, dal fallimento del movimento rivoluzionario malamente chiamato "Nichilismo", [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] si è gradualmente elevato alla posizione dell'unico uomo che possa continuare impunemente a offrire un contributo pubblico più soddisfacente all'argomento.

# [[tolstoy]] (part 4)

It is more to our purpose to note that in this volcanic and fecund
if fundamentally simple personality the [[artist]] has dogged
the steps of the evangelist to the last.  "Master and Man"
(1895) is one of the most exquisite short stories ever written.
"The [[Death]] of Ivan Ilyitch" (1884) and "[[Resurrection]]"
(1899) are in some ways the most powerful of all his works.
The much condemned "Dominion of Darkness" (1886) and "Kreutzer Sonata"
(1889) will be more fairly judged when the average Englishman has
learned the supreme merit of that uncompromising truthfulness
which gives nobility to every line the grand Russian ever wrote.
To submit a work like "[[Resurrection]]" to the summary treatment
which the ordinary [[Novel|novel]] receives and merits is absurd.
It is a large picture of the fall and rise of man done by the swift
and restless hand of a master who stands in a category apart, with an eye
that sees externals and essentials with like accuracy and rapidity.
Because the dramatic quality of these living pictures lies,
not in their organisation into a conventionally limited plot,
but first in the challenging idea upon which they are founded,
then the inexorable development of individual characters, and ever
and anon in the grip of particular episodes, the little critics scoff.
The idea, the characters, the episodes are all too real and
precious British [[Self|self]]-complacency. The grandmotherly Athenoeum
permits some person to describe this Promethean figure as "a
precious vase that has been broken," and can now only be pieced
together to make "the ornament of a museum,"--which reminds me
that I heard a lecturer before a [[Well|well]]-known literary society
in [[London]] describe him lately as a "scavenger," and that a [[City|city]]
bookseller assured me the other day that there was something
almost amounting to a boycott against his fiction in the shops.
The publisher who is preparing a complete edition of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]--
enormous work!--knows better, knows that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] is one of the
world-spirits whose advance out of the obscurity of a benighted
land into the largest contemporary circulation is but a foretaste
of an influence that will soon be co-extensive with the commonwealth
of thinking men and women.

His service to literature is precisely the same as his service to morals.
Like Bunyan and Burns, Dickens and Whitman, he throws down in a
world of decadent conventions the gauge of the democratic ideal.
As he calls the politician and the social reformer back to the land
and the common people, so he calls the [[artist]] back to the elemental
forces ever at work beneath the surface-show of [[Nature|nature]] and humanity.
With an extraordinary penetration into the hidden recesses of character,
he joins a terrible truthfulness, and that absolute simplicity
of manner which we generally associate with genius.  He is a realist,
not merely of the outer, but more especially of the [[Inner Life|inner life]].  There is
no staginess, no sentimentality, in his work.  He has no heroes in our
Western sense, none, even, of those sensational types of personality
which glorify the name of his Northern contemporary, Ibsen.  His style
is always natural, direct, irresistible as a physical process.
He has rarely strayed beyond the channel of his own experience,
and the reader who prefers breadth to depth of knowledge must
seek elsewhere.  He has little [[Humour|humour]], but a grimly satiric note has
sometimes crept into his writing, as Archdeacon Farrar will remember.
Of artifice designed for vulgar entertainment he knows nothing;
in the world of true art, which is the wine-press of the [[Soul|soul]] of man,
he stands, a princely figure.  Theories, prescriptions, and discussions
are forgotten, and we think only with [[Love|love]] and reverence of this
modern patriarch, so lonely amid the daily enlarging congregation
of the hearts he has awakened to a sense of the mystery, the terror,
the joy, the splendour of human destinies.

G. H. PERRIS.

[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s Place in European Literature

The justness of the word _great_ applied to a nation's writers is perhaps
best tested by simply taking each writer in turn from out his Age,
and seeing how far our conception of his Age remains unaffected.  We may
take away hundreds of clever writers, scores of distinguished creators,
and the Age remains before our eyes, solidly unaffected by their absence;
but touch one or two central figures, and lo! the whole framework
of the Age gives in your hands, and you realise that the World's
insight into, and understanding of that Age's life has been supplied
us by the special interpretation offered by two or three great minds.
In fact, every Age seems dwarfed, chaotic, full of confused tendencies and
general contradiction till the few great men have arisen, and symbolised
in themselves what their nation's growth or strife signifies.
How many dumb ages are there in which no great writer has appeared,
ages to whose [[Inner Life|inner life]] in consequence we have no key!

[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s significance as the great writer of modern [[Russia]] can
scarcely be augmented in Russian eyes by his exceeding significance
to Europe as symbolising the spiritual unrest of the modern world.
Yet so inevitably must the main stream of each age's tendency and the main
movement of the world's thought be discovered for us by the great writers,
whenever they appear, that [[Russia]] can no more keep [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s
significance to herself than could [[Germany]] keep Goethe's to herself.
True it is that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], as great novelist, has been absorbed
in mirroring the peculiar world of half-feudal, modern [[Russia]],
a world strange to Western Europe, but the spirit of analysis with
which the creator of Anna Karenina and War and Peace has confronted
the modern world is more truly representative of our Age's outlook
than is the spirit of any other of his great contemporaries.
Between the days of Wilhelm Meister and of [[Resurrection]] what an
extraordinary volume of the rushing tide of modern life has swept by!
A century of that "liberation of modern Europe from the old routine" has
passed since Goethe stood forth for "the awakening of the modern spirit."
A century of emancipation, of Science, of unbelief, of incessant shock,
change, and Progress all over the face of Europe, and even as Goethe
a hundred years ago typified the triumph of the new intelligence
of Europe over the shackles of its old institutions, routine, and dogma
(as Matthew Arnold affirms), so [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] today stands for the triumph
of the European [[Soul|soul]] against civilisation's routine and dogma.
The peculiar modernness of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s attitude, however, as we
shall presently show, is that he is inspired largely by the modern
scientific spirit in his searching analysis of modern life.
Apparently at war with Science and Progress, his extraordinary fascination
for the mind of Europe lies in the fact that he of all great contemporary
writers has come nearest to demonstrating, to realising what the life
of the modern man is.  He of all the analysts of the civilised man's
thoughts, emotions, and actions has least idealized, least beautified,
and least distorted the complex daily life of the European world.
With a marked moral bias, driven onward in his search for [[Truth|truth]]
by his passionate religious temperament, [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]], in his pictures
of life, has constructed a truer whole, a human world less bounded
by the [[artist]]'s individual limitations, more mysteriously living in its
vast flux and flow than is the world of any writer of the century.
War and Peace and Anna Karenina, those great worlds where the
physical environment, mental outlook, emotional [[Aspiration|aspiration]], and moral
code of the whole community of [[Russia]] are reproduced by his art,
as some mighty cunning phantasmagoria of changing life, are superior
in the sense of containing a whole nation's life, to the world of
Goethe, Byron, Scott, Victor Hugo, Balzac, Dickens, Thackeray, Maupassant,
or any latter day creator we can name.

È più consono al nostro proposito osservare che in questa personalità vulcanica e feconda, se pur fondamentalmente semplice, l'[[artist|artista]] ha seguito le orme dell'evangelista fino all'ultimo. "Padrone e servo" (1895) è una delle novelle più squisite mai scritte. "La [[Death|Morte]] di Ivan Ilic" (1884) e "[[Resurrection|Resurrezione]]" (1899) sono per certi versi le più potenti di tutte le sue opere. Il tanto condannato "La potenza delle tenebre" (1886) e "La sonata a Kreutzer" (1889) saranno giudicati più equamente quando l'inglese medio avrà appreso il supremo merito di quell'intransigente veridicità che dà nobiltà a ogni riga che il grande russo abbia mai scritto. Sottoporre un'opera come "[[Resurrection|Resurrezione]]" al trattamento sommario che il comune [[Novel|romanzo]] riceve e merita è assurdo. È un grande quadro della caduta e della risalita dell'uomo, tracciato dalla mano rapida e inquieta di un maestro che sta in una categoria a sé, con un occhio che vede l'esteriore e l'essenziale con pari accuratezza e rapidità. Poiché la qualità drammatica di questi quadri viventi risiede non nella loro organizzazione in un intreccio convenzionalmente circoscritto, ma anzitutto nell'idea provocatoria su cui essi si fondano, poi nell'inesorabile sviluppo dei caratteri individuali, e di quando in quando nella presa di episodi particolari, i piccoli critici sghignazzano. L'idea, i personaggi, gli episodi sono tutti troppo reali e preziosi per la compiacenza di [[Self|sé]] britannica. Il nonnesco Athenoeum permette a qualcuno di descrivere questa figura prometeica come "un vaso prezioso andato in frantumi", che ora può soltanto essere ricomposto per farne "l'ornamento di un museo"—il che mi ricorda che udii un conferenziere, davanti a una [[Well|nota]] società letteraria di [[London|Londra]], descriverlo di recente come uno "spazzino", e che un libraio di [[City|città]] mi assicurò l'altro giorno che nelle librerie esisteva qualcosa che quasi equivaleva a un boicottaggio della sua narrativa. L'editore che sta preparando un'edizione completa di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]]—opera immane!—la sa più lunga, sa che [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] è uno di quegli spiriti universali il cui avanzare dall'oscurità di una terra ottenebrata fino alla più ampia diffusione contemporanea è soltanto un anticipo di un'influenza che presto sarà coestensiva alla comunità degli uomini e delle donne pensanti.

Il suo servizio alla letteratura è precisamente lo stesso del suo servizio alla morale. Come Bunyan e Burns, Dickens e Whitman, egli getta, in un mondo di convenzioni decadenti, il guanto di sfida dell'ideale democratico. Come richiama il politico e il riformatore sociale alla terra e al popolo comune, così richiama l'[[artist|artista]] alle forze elementari sempre all'opera sotto lo spettacolo di superficie della [[Nature|natura]] e dell'umanità. A una straordinaria penetrazione nei recessi nascosti del carattere egli unisce una terribile veridicità, e quella assoluta semplicità di maniera che generalmente associamo al genio. È un realista, non soltanto della vita esteriore, ma più specialmente della [[Inner Life|vita interiore]]. Non vi è teatralità, non vi è sentimentalismo nella sua opera. Non ha eroi nel nostro senso occidentale, nessuno, neppure, di quei tipi sensazionali di personalità che glorificano il nome del suo contemporaneo del Nord, Ibsen. Il suo stile è sempre naturale, diretto, irresistibile come un processo fisico. Egli ha raramente errato oltre l'alveo della propria esperienza, e il lettore che preferisce l'ampiezza alla profondità della conoscenza deve cercare altrove. Ha poco [[Humour|umorismo]], ma una nota cupamente satirica si è talvolta insinuata nel suo scrivere, come l'arcidiacono Farrar ricorderà. Dell'artificio ideato per il divertimento volgare egli non sa nulla; nel mondo dell'arte vera, che è il torchio dell'[[Soul|anima]] dell'uomo, egli sta, figura principesca. Teorie, prescrizioni e discussioni sono dimenticate, e noi pensiamo soltanto con [[Love|amore]] e reverenza a questo patriarca moderno, così solo in mezzo alla congregazione ogni giorno più ampia dei cuori che egli ha risvegliati al senso del mistero, del terrore, della gioia, dello splendore dei destini umani.

G. H. PERRIS.

Il posto di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] nella letteratura europea

La giustezza della parola _grande_ applicata agli scrittori di una nazione si prova forse meglio prendendo semplicemente a turno ciascuno scrittore fuori dalla sua Epoca, e vedendo fino a che punto la nostra concezione della sua Epoca resti inalterata. Possiamo togliere via centinaia di scrittori abili, decine di illustri creatori, e l'Epoca rimane davanti ai nostri occhi, saldamente inalterata dalla loro assenza; ma toccate una o due figure centrali, ed ecco! l'intera armatura dell'Epoca cede tra le vostre mani, e vi rendete conto che la penetrazione e la comprensione che il Mondo ha della vita di quell'Epoca ci sono state fornite dall'interpretazione speciale offerta da due o tre grandi menti. In effetti, ogni Epoca sembra rimpicciolita, caotica, piena di tendenze confuse e di generale contraddizione, finché i pochi grandi uomini non sono sorti, e non hanno simboleggiato in sé ciò che la crescita o la lotta della loro nazione significa. Quante epoche mute vi sono in cui nessun grande scrittore è comparso, epoche alla cui [[Inner Life|vita interiore]] non abbiamo di conseguenza alcuna chiave!

Il significato di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] come grande scrittore della [[Russia]] moderna difficilmente può essere accresciuto agli occhi russi dal suo eccezionale significato per l'Europa in quanto simbolo dell'inquietudine spirituale del mondo moderno. Eppure, tanto inevitabilmente la corrente principale della tendenza di ogni epoca e il movimento principale del pensiero del mondo devono esserci rivelati dai grandi scrittori, ogni volta che compaiono, che la [[Russia]] non può tenere per sé il significato di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] più di quanto la [[Germany|Germania]] potesse tenere per sé quello di Goethe. È vero che [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], come grande romanziere, è stato assorbito nel rispecchiare il peculiare mondo della [[Russia]] moderna, semifeudale, un mondo estraneo all'Europa occidentale, ma lo spirito di analisi con cui il creatore di Anna Karenina e di Guerra e pace ha affrontato il mondo moderno è più veramente rappresentativo della prospettiva della nostra Epoca di quanto non lo sia lo spirito di qualsiasi altro dei suoi grandi contemporanei. Fra i giorni di Wilhelm Meister e quelli di [[Resurrection|Resurrezione]] che straordinaria massa dell'onda impetuosa della vita moderna è passata! Un secolo di quella "liberazione dell'Europa moderna dalla vecchia routine" è trascorso da quando Goethe si levò a difesa del "risveglio dello spirito moderno". Un secolo di emancipazione, di Scienza, di incredulità, di incessante scossa, mutamento e Progresso su tutta la faccia dell'Europa, e proprio come Goethe cent'anni fa impersonò il trionfo della nuova intelligenza dell'Europa sui ceppi delle sue vecchie istituzioni, routine e dogmi (come afferma Matthew Arnold), così [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] oggi incarna il trionfo dell'[[Soul|anima]] europea contro la routine e il dogma della civiltà. La peculiare modernità dell'atteggiamento di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], tuttavia, come mostreremo tra breve, sta nel fatto che egli è ispirato in gran parte dal moderno spirito scientifico nella sua penetrante analisi della vita moderna. Apparentemente in guerra con la Scienza e il Progresso, la sua straordinaria attrazione per la mente d'Europa risiede nel fatto che egli, fra tutti i grandi scrittori contemporanei, si è avvicinato più di ogni altro a dimostrare, a rendere reale che cosa sia la vita dell'uomo moderno. Egli, fra tutti gli analisti dei pensieri, delle emozioni e delle azioni dell'uomo civilizzato, ha meno idealizzato, meno abbellito e meno distorto la complessa vita quotidiana del mondo europeo. Con una marcata inclinazione morale, spinto avanti nella sua ricerca della [[Truth|Verità]] dal suo appassionato temperamento religioso, [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], nei suoi quadri della vita, ha costruito un tutto più veritiero, un mondo umano meno circoscritto dai limiti individuali dell'[[artist|artista]], più misteriosamente vivo nel suo vasto flusso e riflusso di quanto non lo sia il mondo di qualsiasi scrittore del secolo. Guerra e pace e Anna Karenina, quei grandi mondi in cui l'ambiente fisico, la prospettiva mentale, l'[[Aspiration|aspirazione]] emotiva e il codice morale dell'intera comunità della [[Russia]] sono riprodotti dalla sua arte, come una potente e ingegnosa fantasmagoria di vita mutevole, sono superiori, nel senso di contenere la vita di un'intera nazione, al mondo di Goethe, Byron, Scott, Victor Hugo, Balzac, Dickens, Thackeray, Maupassant, o di qualsiasi altro creatore recente che possiamo nominare.

# [[tolstoy]] (part 5)

And not only so, but [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s analysis of life throws more light
on the main currents of thought in our Age, raises deeper problems,
and explores more untouched territories of the mind than does any
corresponding analysis by his European contemporaries.

It is by [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s passionate seeking of the life of the [[Soul|soul]]
that the great Russian writer towers above the men of our day,
and it is because his hunger for spiritual [[Truth|truth]] has led him to probe
contemporary life, to examine all modern formulas and appearances,
to penetrate into the secret thought and emotion of men
of all grades in our complex society, that his work is charged
with the essence of nearly all that modernity thinks and feels,
believes and suffers, hopes and fears as it evolves in more
and more complex forms of our terribly complex civilisation.
The [[Soul|soul]] of humanity is, however, always the appeal of men
from the life that environs, moulds, and burdens them,
to instincts that go beyond and transcend their present life.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] is the appeal of the modern world, the cry of the modern
[[Conscience|conscience]] against the blinded fate of its own progress.
To the eye of science everything is possible in human life,
the sacrifice of the [[Innocent|innocent]] for the sake of the progress
of the guilty, the crushing and deforming of the weak
so that the strong may triumph over them, the evolution
of new serf classes at the dictates of a ruling class.
All this the nineteenth century has seen accomplished,
and not seen [[Alone|alone]] in [[Russia]].  It is [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s distinction
to have combined in his life-work more than any other great
[[artist]] two main conflicting points of view.  He has fused
by his art the science that defines the way Humanity is forced
forward blindly and irresponsibly from century to century
by the mere pressure of events, he has fused with this science
of our modern world the [[Soul|soul]]'s protest against the earthly fate
of man which leads the generations into taking the ceaseless
roads of evil which every age unwinds.

Let us cite [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s treatment of War as an instance of how this
great [[artist]] symbolises the Age for us and so marks the advance
in [[Self|self]]-consciousness of the modern mind, and as a nearer
approximation to a realisation of what life is.  We have only got
to compare [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s "Sebastopol" (1856) with any other document
on war by other European writers to perceive that [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] [[Alone|alone]]
among artists has realised war, his fellows have idealised it.
To quote a passage from a former article let us say that:
"Sebastopol" gives us war under all aspects--war as a squalid,
honourable, daily affair of mud and glory, of [[Vanity|vanity]], disease,
hard work, stupidity, patriotism, and inhuman agony.  [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] gets
the complex effects of "Sebastopol" by keenly analysing the effect
of the sights and sounds, dangers and pleasures, of war on the brains
of a variety of typical men, and by placing a special valuation
of his own on these men's actions, thoughts, and emotions, on their
[[Courage|courage]], altruism, and show of indifference in the face of [[Death|death]].
He lifts up, in fact, the veil of appearances conventionally drawn
by society over the actualities of the glorious trade of killing men,
and he does this chiefly by analysing keenly the insensitiveness
and indifference of the average mind, which says of the worst
of war's realities, "I felt so and so, and did so and so:
but as to what those other thousands may have felt in their agony,
that I did not enter into at all."  "Sebastopol," therefore,
though an exceedingly short and exceedingly simple narrative,
is a psychological document on modern war of extraordinary value,
for it simply relegates to the lumber-room, as unlife-like and
hopelessly limited, all those theatrical glorifications of war which
men of letters, romantic poets, and [[Grave|grave]] historians alike have been
busily piling up on humanity's shelves from generation to generation.
And more:  we feel that in "Sebastopol" we have at last the skeptical
modern spirit, absorbed in actual life, demonstrating what war is,
and expressing at length the confused sensations of countless men,
who have heretofore, recognising this man [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] as the most advanced
product of our civilisation, and likening him to a great surgeon,
who, not deceived by the world's presentation of its own life,
penetrates into the essential joy and [[Suffering|suffering]], health and disease
of multitudes of men; a surgeon who, face to face with the strangest
of [[Nature]]'s laws in the constitution of human society, puzzled by
all the illusions, fatuities, and conventions of the human mind,
resolutely sets himself to lay bare the roots of all its passions,
appetites, and incentives in the struggle for life, so that at least
human reason may advance farther along the path of [[Self-Knowledge|self-knowledge]]
in advancing towards a general sociological study of man.

[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s place in nineteenth-century literature is, therefore,
in our view, no less fixed and certain than is Voltaire's place
in the eighteenth century.  Both of these writers focus for us in a
marvelously complete manner the respective methods of analysing life
by which the rationalism of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries,
and the science and [[Humanitarianism|humanitarianism]] of the nineteenth century have
moulded for us the modern world.  All the movements, all the problems,
all the speculation, all the agitations of the world of today in
contrast with the immense materialistic civilisation that science has
hastily built up for us in three or four generations, all the spirit
of modern life is condensed in the pages of [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s writings,
because, as we have said, he typifies the [[Soul|soul]] of the modern
man gazing, now undaunted, and now in alarm, at the formidable array
of the newly-tabulated cause and effect of humanity's progress,
at the appalling cheapness and waste of human life in [[Nature]]'s hands.
[[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] thus stands for the modern [[Soul|soul]]'s alarm in contact with science.
And just as science's work after its first destruction of the past ages'
formalism, superstition, and dogma is directed more and more to
the examination and amelioration of human life, so [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s work has
been throughout inspired by a passionate [[Love|love]] of humanity, and by his
ceaseless struggle against conventional religion, dogmatic science,
and society's mechanical influence on the minds of its members.
To make man more conscious of his acts, to show society its real
motives and what it is feeling, and not cry out in admiration at
what it pretends to feel--this has been the great novelist's aim
in his delineation of [[Russia]]'s life.  Ever seeking the one [[Truth|truth]]--
to arrive at men's thoughts and sensations under the daily pressure
of life--never flinching from his exploration of the dark world
of man's animalism and incessant [[Self-Deception|self-deception]], [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]]'s realism
in art is symbolical of our absorption in the world of fact,
in the modern study of natural law, a study of ultimately without loss
of spirituality, nay, resulting in immense gain to the spiritual life.
The realism of the great Russian's novels is, therefore, more in line
with the modern tendency and outlook than is the general tendency
of other schools of Continental literature.  And [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] must
be finally looked on, not merely as the [[Conscience|conscience]] of the Russian
world revolting against the too heavy burden which the Russian
people have now to bear in Holy [[Russia]]'s onward march towards
the building-up of her great Asiatic Empire, but also as the [[Soul|soul]]
of the modern world seeking to replace in its [[Love|love]] of humanity the life
of those old religions which science is destroying day by day.
In this sense [[tolstoy|Tolstoy]] will stand in European literature as the [[Conscience|conscience]]
of the modern world.

E non solo, ma l'analisi della vita di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] getta più luce sulle correnti principali del pensiero nella nostra Epoca, solleva problemi più profondi, ed esplora territori della mente più inviolati di qualsiasi analisi corrispondente compiuta dai suoi contemporanei europei.

È per l'appassionata ricerca di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] della vita dell'[[Soul|anima]] che il grande scrittore russo torreggia sopra gli uomini del nostro tempo, ed è perché la sua fame di [[Truth|Verità]] spirituale lo ha condotto a sondare la vita contemporanea, a esaminare tutte le formule e apparenze moderne, a penetrare nel pensiero e nell'emozione segreti degli uomini di ogni grado nella nostra complessa società, che la sua opera è carica dell'essenza di quasi tutto ciò che la modernità pensa e sente, crede e soffre, spera e teme mentre si evolve in forme sempre più complesse della nostra terribilmente complessa civiltà. L'[[Soul|anima]] dell'umanità è, tuttavia, sempre l'appello degli uomini dalla vita che li circonda, li plasma e li opprime, agli istinti che vanno al di là e trascendono la loro vita presente. [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] è l'appello del mondo moderno, il grido della [[Conscience|coscienza]] moderna contro il cieco destino del proprio progresso. All'occhio della scienza tutto è possibile nella vita umana: il sacrificio dell'[[Innocent|innocente]] in nome del progresso del colpevole, lo schiacciamento e la deformazione del debole affinché il forte trionfi su di lui, l'evoluzione di nuove classi servili per dettato di una classe dominante. Tutto ciò il diciannovesimo secolo lo ha visto compiersi, e non lo ha visto [[Alone|soltanto]] in [[Russia]]. È merito di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] l'aver combinato nella sua opera di una vita, più di ogni altro grande [[artist|artista]], due principali punti di vista in conflitto. Egli ha fuso con la sua arte la scienza che definisce il modo in cui l'Umanità è spinta avanti ciecamente e irresponsabilmente di secolo in secolo dalla mera pressione degli eventi; ha fuso con questa scienza del nostro mondo moderno la protesta dell'[[Soul|anima]] contro il destino terreno dell'uomo, che conduce le generazioni a imboccare le incessanti strade del male che ogni epoca srotola.

Citiamo il trattamento della Guerra da parte di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] come esempio di come questo grande [[artist|artista]] simboleggia per noi l'Epoca e così segna l'avanzamento nella consapevolezza di [[Self|sé]] della mente moderna, e come un'approssimazione più prossima alla comprensione di ciò che la vita è. Non abbiamo che da confrontare "Sebastopoli" (1856) di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] con qualsiasi altro documento sulla guerra di altri scrittori europei per percepire che [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] [[Alone|solo]] fra gli artisti ha reso reale la guerra, mentre i suoi simili l'hanno idealizzata. Per citare un passo da un precedente articolo, diciamo che: "Sebastopoli" ci dà la guerra sotto tutti gli aspetti—la guerra come squallida, onorevole, quotidiana faccenda di fango e di gloria, di [[Vanity|vanità]], malattia, duro lavoro, stupidità, patriottismo e agonia disumana. [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] ottiene i complessi effetti di "Sebastopoli" analizzando acutamente l'effetto delle immagini e dei suoni, dei pericoli e dei piaceri della guerra sui cervelli di una varietà di uomini tipici, e ponendo una speciale valutazione tutta sua sulle azioni, i pensieri e le emozioni di questi uomini, sul loro [[Courage|coraggio]], altruismo e ostentazione d'indifferenza di fronte alla [[Death|morte]]. Egli solleva, di fatto, il velo delle apparenze convenzionalmente steso dalla società sulle realtà del glorioso mestiere di uccidere gli uomini, e fa questo soprattutto analizzando acutamente l'insensibilità e l'indifferenza della mente media, la quale dice della peggiore delle realtà della guerra: "Io provai questo e quello, e feci questo e quello: ma quanto a ciò che quelle altre migliaia possono aver provato nella loro agonia, in ciò non entrai affatto." "Sebastopoli", dunque, benché racconto oltremodo breve e oltremodo semplice, è un documento psicologico sulla guerra moderna di straordinario valore, poiché relega semplicemente in soffitta, come non conformi alla vita e senza speranza limitati, tutte quelle teatrali glorificazioni della guerra che letterati, poeti romantici e [[Grave|gravi]] storici allo stesso modo sono andati assiduamente accumulando sugli scaffali dell'umanità di generazione in generazione. E c'è di più: sentiamo che in "Sebastopoli" abbiamo finalmente lo spirito moderno scettico, assorto nella vita reale, che dimostra che cos'è la guerra ed esprime per esteso le confuse sensazioni di innumerevoli uomini, i quali finora, riconoscendo quest'uomo, [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], come il prodotto più avanzato della nostra civiltà, e paragonandolo a un grande chirurgo che, non ingannato dalla presentazione che il mondo dà della propria vita, penetra nella gioia e nel [[Suffering|patire]] essenziali, nella salute e nella malattia di moltitudini di uomini; un chirurgo che, faccia a faccia con le più strane delle leggi della [[Nature|Natura]] nella costituzione della società umana, sconcertato da tutte le illusioni, le fatuità e le convenzioni della mente umana, si accinge risolutamente a mettere a nudo le radici di tutte le sue passioni, appetiti e incentivi nella lotta per la vita, affinché almeno la ragione umana possa avanzare più oltre lungo il cammino della [[Self-Knowledge|conoscenza di sé]] nel procedere verso uno studio sociologico generale dell'uomo.

Il posto di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] nella letteratura del diciannovesimo secolo è, dunque, a nostro avviso, non meno fisso e certo di quanto sia il posto di Voltaire nel diciottesimo secolo. Entrambi questi scrittori mettono a fuoco per noi in maniera meravigliosamente completa i rispettivi metodi di analizzare la vita mediante i quali il razionalismo del diciassettesimo e del diciottesimo secolo, e la scienza e l'[[Humanitarianism|umanitarismo]] del diciannovesimo secolo hanno plasmato per noi il mondo moderno. Tutti i movimenti, tutti i problemi, tutta la speculazione, tutte le agitazioni del mondo d'oggi in contrasto con l'immensa civiltà materialistica che la scienza ha frettolosamente edificato per noi in tre o quattro generazioni, tutto lo spirito della vita moderna è condensato nelle pagine degli scritti di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]], perché, come abbiamo detto, egli impersona l'[[Soul|anima]] dell'uomo moderno che contempla, ora impavido e ora allarmato, la formidabile schiera delle cause ed effetti del progresso dell'umanità appena catalogati, l'atroce vilezza e lo spreco della vita umana nelle mani della [[Nature|Natura]]. [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] rappresenta così l'allarme dell'[[Soul|anima]] moderna al contatto con la scienza. E proprio come l'opera della scienza, dopo la sua prima distruzione del formalismo, della superstizione e del dogma delle età passate, è rivolta sempre più all'esame e al miglioramento della vita umana, così l'opera di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] è stata per tutto il tempo ispirata da un appassionato [[Love|amore]] dell'umanità, e dalla sua incessante lotta contro la religione convenzionale, la scienza dogmatica e l'influenza meccanica della società sulle menti dei suoi membri. Rendere l'uomo più consapevole dei propri atti, mostrare alla società i suoi veri moventi e ciò che essa sente, e non gridare d'ammirazione per ciò che finge di sentire—questo è stato lo scopo del grande romanziere nella sua rappresentazione della vita della [[Russia]]. Cercando sempre l'unica [[Truth|Verità]]—giungere ai pensieri e alle sensazioni degli uomini sotto la quotidiana pressione della vita—senza mai indietreggiare dalla sua esplorazione del buio mondo dell'animalità dell'uomo e del suo incessante [[Self-Deception|inganno di sé]], il realismo di [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] nell'arte è simbolico del nostro assorbimento nel mondo del fatto, nello studio moderno della legge naturale, uno studio in ultima analisi senza perdita di spiritualità, anzi, con immenso guadagno per la vita spirituale. Il realismo dei romanzi del grande russo è, perciò, più in linea con la tendenza e la prospettiva moderne di quanto non lo sia la tendenza generale delle altre scuole della letteratura continentale. E [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] dev'essere infine considerato non soltanto come la [[Conscience|coscienza]] del mondo russo che si ribella al troppo pesante fardello che il popolo russo deve ora portare nella marcia in avanti della Santa [[Russia]] verso l'edificazione del suo grande Impero asiatico, ma anche come l'[[Soul|anima]] del mondo moderno che cerca di sostituire, nel suo [[Love|amore]] dell'umanità, la vita di quelle antiche religioni che la scienza va distruggendo giorno dopo giorno. In questo senso [[tolstoy|Tolstoj]] resterà nella letteratura europea come la [[Conscience|coscienza]] del mondo moderno.
