"Perhaps you think I am as mad as he; and I have told you that I
am at once like him and unlike him.  I am like him because I
also can go on the wild journeys of such wild minds, and have
a sympathy with his [[Love|love]] of liberty.  I am unlike him because,
thank God, I can generally find my way [[Home|home]] again.  The lunatic is
he who loses his way and cannot return.  Now, almost before my eyes,
this man had made the great stride from liberty to lunacy.
The man who opened the bird-cage loved [[Freedom|freedom]]; possibly too much;
certainly very much.  But the man who broke the bowl merely
because he thought it a [[Prison|prison]] for the fish, when it was their only
possible [[house]] of life... that man was already outside the world
of reason, raging with a [[Desire|desire]] to be outside everything.
In a most literal and living sense, he was out of his wits.
And there was another thing revealed to me by the grey [[Ghosts|ghosts]] of
the fishes.  The rise of the insanity had been very rapid and steep.
To send the bird into danger was only a disputable kindness,
to fling the fish to [[Death|death]] was a [[Dance|dance]] of raving destruction.
What would he do next?

"I have spoken of a round [[Prison|prison]].  After all, to any mind that can
move parallel to a mood like this, there really is a round [[Prison|prison]].
The sky itself, studded with [[Stars|stars]], the serene arch of what
we call infinity..."

As he spoke he staggered, clutched at the air, and fell all his
great length on the grass.  At the same moment Mallow was hurled
against a tree, and the girl collapsed against him, clinging to him
in a way which even in that blinding whirl, was an answer to many
of his questionings.  It was only when they had picked themselves
up and pulled themselves together that they were fully conscious
that the valley was still resounding with echoes of one hideous
and rending uproar; or that the darkness had just shut down upon
a blaze that blinded them like red [[Lightning|lightning]].  For the instant
that it lasted it was a standing glory, like a great sunrise.
There [[Rose|rose]] to the surface of Mallow's [[Memory|memory]] only one word for it;
the word radiant.

Mallow found himself reflecting in a dull fashion that it was lighter
than it might have been at that hour, because of a friendly flame
that was licking itself in a lively fashion a few yards away.
Then he saw that it was the smouldering ruin of the blue
wooden gate-post at which he had gazed that morning,
flung all the way through the air like a flaming thunderbolt.
They had come just far enough from the [[house]] to be out of danger.
Then he looked again at the blue-painted wood curling with golden flames,
and for the first [[Time|time]] began to tremble.

The next moment he caught sight of the faces of his other friends,
Walton and Hutton, pale in the flame as they hurried up the [[Road|road]]
from the remoter inn to which they had retired for the evening.

"What was it?"  Walton was calling out.

"An explosion," said Hutton rather hazily.

"An expansion," replied Mallow, and mastered himself with the effort
of a grim smile.

By this [[Time|time]], more people were running out from remote cottages,
and Gabriel Gale turned his face to something like a small crowd.

"It was only the [[Prison|prison]] gun," he said, "the signal that a
prisoner has escaped."
