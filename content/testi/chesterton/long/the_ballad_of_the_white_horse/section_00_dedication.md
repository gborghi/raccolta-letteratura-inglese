DEDICATION  
F great limbs gone to chaos,  
       A great face turned to [[Night|night]]—  
     Why bend above a shapeless shroud  
     Seeking in such archaic cloud  
       Sight of strong lords and light? [5]  
     Where seven sunken Englands  
       Lie buried one by one,  
     Why should one idle spade, I [[Wonder|wonder]],  
     Shake up the [[Dust|dust]] of thanes like thunder  
       To smoke and choke the sun? [10]  
     In cloud of clay so cast to heaven  
     What shape shall man discern?  
     These lords may light the mystery  
     Of mastery or victory,  
     And these ride high in [[history]], [15]  
       But these shall not return.  
     Gored on the Norman gonfalon  
       The Golden Dragon died;  
     We shall not wake with ballad strings  
     The good [[Time|time]] of the smaller things, [20]  
     We shall not see the holy kings  
       Ride down by Severn side.  
     Stiff, strange, and quaintly coloured  
       As the broidery of Bayeux  
     The England of that [[Dawn|dawn]] remains, [25]  
     And this of Alfred and the Danes  
     Seems like the tales a whole tribe feigns  
       Too English to be true.  
     Of a good [[King|king]] on an island  
       That ruled once on a [[Time|time]]; [30]  
     And as he walked by an apple tree  
     There came green devils out of the sea  
     With sea-plants trailing heavily  
       And tracks of opal slime.  
     Yet Alfred is no fairy tale; [35]  
       His days as our days ran,  
     He also looked forth for an hour  
     On peopled plains and skies that lower,  
     From those few windows in the tower  
       That is the head of a man. [40]  
     But who shall look from Alfred’s hood  
       Or breathe his breath alive?  
     His century like a small dark cloud  
     Drifts far; it is an eyeless crowd,  
     Where the tortured trumpets scream aloud [45]  
       And the dense arrows drive.  
     Lady, by one light only  
       We look from Alfred’s eyes,  
     We know he saw athwart the wreck  
     The sign that hangs about your neck, [50]  
     Where One more than Melchizedek  
       Is dead and never dies.  
     Therefore I bring these rhymes to you,  
       Who brought the [[Cross|cross]] to me,  
     Since on you flaming without flaw [55]  
     I saw the sign that Guthrum saw  
     When he let break his ships of awe,  
       And laid peace on the sea.  
     Do you remember when we went  
       Under a dragon [[Moon|moon]], [60]  
     And ‘mid volcanic tints of [[Night|night]]  
     Walked where they fought the unknown fight  
     And saw black trees on the battle-height,  
       Black thorn on Ethandune?  
     And I thought, “I will go with you, [65]  
     As man with God has gone,  
     And wander with a wandering star,  
     The wandering heart of things that are,  
     The fiery [[Cross|cross]] of [[Love|love]] and war  
       That like yourself, goes on.” [70]  
     O go you onward; where you are  
       Shall [[Honour|honour]] and laughter be,  
     Past purple forest and pearled foam,  
     God’s winged pavilion free to roam,  
     Your face, that is a wandering [[Home|home]], [75]  
       A flying [[Home|home]] for me.  
     Ride through the silent earthquake lands,  
       Wide as a waste is wide,  
     Across these days like deserts, when  
     [[Pride]] and a little scratching pen [80]  
     Have dried and split the hearts of men,  
       Heart of the heroes, ride.  
     Up through an empty [[house]] of [[Stars|stars]],  
       Being what heart you are,  
     Up the inhuman steeps of space [85]  
     As on a staircase go in grace,  
     Carrying the firelight on your face  
       Beyond the loneliest star.  
     Take these; in [[Memory|memory]] of the hour [90]  
       We strayed a space from [[Home|home]]  
     And saw the smoke-hued hamlets, quaint  
     With Westland [[King|king]] and Westland saint,  
     And watched the western glory faint  
       Along the [[Road|road]] to Frome.  
G. K. C.
