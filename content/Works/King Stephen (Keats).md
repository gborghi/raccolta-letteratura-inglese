*by Keats*  ·  **Cluster:** [[Crown · Pride · Fortune's Wheel]]

> [!abstract] 
> An unfinished verse history play set during the medieval English civil war between King Stephen and Empress Maud, depicting battle, defiant valour, the question of a usurper's legitimacy, and a king's heroic refusal to yield.

King Stephen (1092–1154)

### SCENE I.

| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Field of Battle. - |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter King Stephen, Knights, and Soldiers. - |
| STEPHEN | If shame can on a soldier’s vein-swoll’n front<br>Spread deeper crimson than the battle’s toil,<br>Blush in your casing helmets! for see, see!<br>Yonder my chivalry, my pride of war,<br>Wrench’d with an iron hand from firm array,<br>Are routed loose about the plashy meads,<br>Of honour forfeit. O that my known voice<br>Could reach your dastard ears, and fright you more!<br>Fly, cowards, fly! Glocester is at your backs!<br>Throw your slack bridles o’er the flurried manes,<br>Ply well the rowel with faint trembling heels,<br>Scampering to death at last! |
| FIRST KNIGHT | The enemy<br>Bears his flaunt standard close upon their rear. |
| SECOND KNIGHT | Sure of a bloody prey, seeing the fens<br>Will swamp them girth-deep. |
| STEPHEN | Over head and ears,<br>No matter! ’Tis a gallant enemy;<br>How like a comet he goes streaming on.<br>But we must plague him in the flank,- hey, friends.<br>We are well breathed,- follow! - |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Earl Baldwin and Soldiers, as defeated. - |
| STEPHEN | De Redvers!<br>What is the monstrous bugbear that can fright<br>Baldwin? |
| BALDWIN | No scare-crow, but the fortunate star<br>Of boisterous Chester, whose fell truncheon now<br>Points level to the goal of victory.<br>This way he comes, and if you would maintain<br>Your person unaffronted by vile odds,<br>Take horse, my Lord. |
| STEPHEN | And which way spur for life?<br>Now I thank Heaven I am in the toils,<br>That soldiers may bear witness how my arm<br>Can burst the meshes. Not the eagle more<br>Loves to beat up against a tyrannous blast,<br>Than I to meet the torrent of my foes.<br>This is a brag,- be’t so,- but if I fall,<br>Carve it upon my ‘scutcheon’d sepulchre.<br>On, fellow soldiers! Earl of Redvers, back!<br>Not twenty Earls of Chester shall brow-beat<br>The diadem.<br>[Exeunt. Alarum. |

### SCENE II.

| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Another part of the Field. - |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets sounding a Victory. Enter Glocester, |
| *(didascalia)* | Knights, and Forces. - |
| GLOCESTER | Now may we lift our bruised vizors up,<br>And take the flattering freshness of the air,<br>While the wide din of battle dies away<br>Into times past, yet to be echoed sure<br>In the silent pages of our chroniclers. |
| FIRST KNIGHT | Will Stephen’s death be mark’d there, my good<br>Lord,<br>Or that we gave him lodging in yon towers? |
| GLOCESTER | Fain would I know the great usurper’s fate. - |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter two Captains severally. - |
| FIRST CAPTAIN | My Lord! |
| SECOND CAPTAIN | Most noble Earl! |
| FIRST CAPTAIN | The King- |
| SECOND CAPTAIN | The Empress greets- |
| GLOCESTER | What of the King? |
| FIRST CAPTAIN | He sole and lone maintains<br>A hopeless bustle mid our swarming arms,<br>And with a nimble savageness attacks,<br>Escapes, makes fiercer onset, then anew<br>Eludes death, giving death to most that dare<br>Trespass within the circuit of his sword!<br>He must by this have fallen. Baldwin is taken;<br>And for the Duke of Bretagne, like a stag<br>He flies, for the Welsh beagles to hunt down.<br>God save the Empress! |
| GLOCESTER | Now our dreaded Queen:<br>What message from her Highness? |
| SECOND CAPTAIN | Royal Maud<br>From the throng’d towers of Lincoln hath look’d down,<br>Like Pallas from the walls of Ilion,<br>And seen her enemies havock’d at her feet.<br>She greets most noble Glocester from her heart,<br>Intreating him, his captains, and brave knights,<br>To grace a banquet. The high city gates<br>Are envious which shall see your triumph pass;<br>The streets are full of music. - |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Second Knight. - |
| GLOCESTER | Whence come you? |
| SECOND KNIGHT | From Stephen, my good Prince,- Stephen!<br>Stephen! |
| GLOCESTER | Why do you make such echoing of his name? |
| SECOND KNIGHT | Because I think, my lord, he is no man,<br>But a fierce demon, ‘nointed safe from wounds,<br>And misbaptized with a Christian name. |
| GLOCESTER | A mighty soldier!- Does he still hold out? |
| SECOND KNIGHT | He shames our victory. His valour still<br>Keeps elbow-room amid our eager swords,<br>And holds our bladed falchions all aloof-<br>His gleaming battle-axe being slaughter-sick,<br>Smote on the morion of a Flemish knight,<br>Broke short in his hand; upon the which he flung<br>The heft away with such a vengeful force,<br>It paunch’d the Earl of Chester’s horse, who then<br>Spleen-hearted came in full career at him. |
| GLOCESTER | Did no one take him at a vantage then? |
| SECOND KNIGHT | Three then with tiger leap upon him flew,<br>Whom, with his sword swift-drawn and nimbly held,<br>He stung away again, and stood to breathe, |
| Smiling | Anon upon him rush’d once more<br>A throng of foes, and in this renew’d strife,<br>My sword met his and snapp’d off at the hilts. |
| GLOCESTER | Come, lead me to this Mars- and let us move<br>In silence, not insulting his sad doom<br>With clamorous trumpets. To the Empress bear<br>My salutation as befits the time.<br>[Exeunt Glocester and Forces. |

### SCENE III.

| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | The Field of Battle. Enter Stephen unarmed. - |
| STEPHEN | Another sword! And what if I could seize<br>One from Bellona’s gleaming armoury,<br>Or choose the fairest of her sheaved spears!<br>Where are my enemies? Here, close at hand,<br>Here come the testy brood. O for a sword!<br>I’m faint- a biting sword! A noble sword!<br>A hedge-stake- or a ponderous stone to hurl<br>With brawny vengeance, like the labourer Cain.<br>Come on! Farewell my kingdom, and all hail<br>Thou superb, plumb’d, and helmeted renown,<br>All hail- I would not truck this brilliant day<br>To rule in Pylos with a Nestor’s beard-<br>Come on! - |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter De Kaims and Knights, &c. - |
| DE KAIMS | Is’t madness, or a hunger after death,<br>That makes thee thus unarm’d throw taunts at us?<br>Yield, Stephen, or my sword’s point dip in<br>The gloomy current of a traitor’s heart. |
| STEPHEN | Do it, De Kaims, I will not budge an inch. |
| DE KAIMS | Yes, of thy madness thou shalt take the meed. |
| STEPHEN | Darest thou? |
| DE KAIMS | How dare, against a man disarm’d? |
| STEPHEN | What weapons has the lion but himself?<br>Come not near me, De Kaims, for by the price<br>Of all the glory I have won this day,<br>Being a king, I will not yield alive<br>To any but the second man of the realm,<br>Robert of Glocester. |
| DE KAIMS | Thou shalt vail to me. |
| STEPHEN | Shall I, when I have sworn against it, sir?<br>Thou think’st it brave to take a breathing king,<br>That, on a court-day bow’d to haughty Maud<br>The awed presence-chamber may be bold<br>To whisper, there’s the man who took alive<br>Stephen- me- prisoner. Certes, De Kaims,<br>The ambition is a noble one. |
| DE KAIMS | ’Tis true,<br>And, Stephen, I must compass it. |
| STEPHEN | No, no,<br>Do not tempt me to throttle you on the gorge,<br>Or with my gauntlet crush your hollow breast,<br>Just when your knighthood is grown ripe and full<br>For lordship.<br>A SOLDIER. Is an honest yeoman’s spear<br>Of no use at a need? Take that. |
| STEPHEN | Ah, dastard! |
| DE KAIMS | What, you are vulnerable! my prisoner! |
| STEPHEN | No not yet. I disclaim it, and demand<br>Death as a sovereign right unto a king<br>Who ‘sdains to yield to any but his peer,<br>If not in title, yet in noble deeds,<br>The Earl of Glocester. Stab to the hilts, De Kaims,<br>For I will never by mean hands be led<br>From this so famous field. Do ye hear! Be quick! - |
| *(didascalia)* | Trumpets. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Chester and Knights. |
