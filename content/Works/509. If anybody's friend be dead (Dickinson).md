# 509. If anybody's friend be dead

*by Dickinson*  ·  **Cluster:** [[Grief and Loss · Elegy · Clothing and Costume]]

> [!abstract] 
> Grief that fixes on remembered living details of the dead, who have become a grand, estranged thing in the sepulchre.

**Motifs:** [[Death]], [[Memory]], [[Grave]], [[Clothing and Costume]]

## Testo integrale / Full text

If anybody's friend be dead 
It's sharpest of the theme 
The thinking how they walked alive — 
At such and such a time — 
 
Their costume, of a Sunday, 
Some manner of the Hair — 
A prank nobody knew but them 
Lost, in the Sepulchre — 
 
How warm, they were, on such a day, 
You almost feel the date — 
So short way off it seems — 
And now — they're Centuries from that — 
 
How pleased they were, at what you said — 
You try to touch the smile 
And dip your fingers in the frost — 
When was it — Can you tell — 
 
You asked the Company to tea — 
Acquaintance — just a few — 
And chatted close with this Grand Thing 
That don't remember you — 
 
Past Bows, and Invitations — 
Past Interview, and Vow — 
Past what Ourself can estimate — 
That — makes the Quick of Woe!
