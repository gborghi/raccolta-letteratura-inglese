| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]] in [[Prison|prison]], in shackles, above |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | How do you, noble cousin? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | How do you, sir? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Why, strong enough to laugh at misery And bear the chance of war. Yet we are prisoners, I [[Fear|fear]], for ever, cousin. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I believe it, And to that destiny have patiently Laid up my hour to come. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | O, cousin [[Arcite]], Where is Thebes now? Where is our noble country? Where are our friends and kindreds? Never more Must we behold those comforts, never see The hardy youths strive for the games of [[Honour|honour]], Hung with the painted favours of their ladies, Like tall ships under sail; then start amongst ’em And, as an east [[Wind|wind]], leave ’em all behind us, Like lazy clouds, whilst [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]], Even in the wagging of a wanton leg, Outstripped the people’s praises, won the garlands Ere they have [[Time|time]] to wish ‘em ours. O never Shall we two exercise, like twins of [[Honour|honour]], Our arms again and feel our fiery horses Like proud seas under us. Our good swords, now— Better the red-eyed god of war ne’er wore— Ravished our sides, like age must run to rust And deck the temples of those gods that hate us. These hands shall never draw ’em out like [[Lightning|lightning]] To blast whole armies more. |
