| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]] in [[Prison|prison]], in shackles, above |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | How do you, noble cousin? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | How do you, sir? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Why, strong enough to laugh at misery<br>And bear the chance of war. Yet we are prisoners,<br>I [[Fear|fear]], for ever, cousin. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I believe it, And to that destiny have patiently<br>Laid up my hour to come. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | O, cousin [[Arcite]], Where is Thebes now? Where is our noble country?<br>Where are our friends and kindreds? Never more<br>Must we behold those comforts, never see<br>The hardy youths strive for the games of [[Honour|honour]],<br>Hung with the painted favours of their ladies,<br>Like tall ships under sail; then start amongst ’em<br>And, as an east [[Wind|wind]], leave ’em all behind us,<br>Like lazy clouds, whilst [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]],<br>Even in the wagging of a wanton leg,<br>Outstripped the people’s praises, won the garlands<br>Ere they have [[Time|time]] to wish ‘em ours. O never<br>Shall we two exercise, like twins of [[Honour|honour]],<br>Our arms again and feel our fiery horses<br>Like proud seas under us. Our good swords, now—<br>Better the red-eyed god of war ne’er wore—<br>Ravished our sides, like age must run to rust<br>And deck the temples of those gods that hate us.<br>These hands shall never draw ’em out like [[Lightning|lightning]]<br>To blast whole armies more. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | No, [[Palamon]], Those hopes are prisoners with us. Here we are,<br>And here the graces of our youths must wither,<br>Like a too-timely [[spring]]. Here age must find us<br>And, which is heaviest, [[Palamon]], unmarried—<br>The sweet embraces of a loving wife<br>Loaden with kisses, armed with thousand Cupids,<br>Shall never clasp our necks; no issue know us;<br>No figures of ourselves shall we e’er see<br>To glad our age, and, like young eagles, teach ’em<br>Boldly to gaze against bright arms and say,<br>‘Remember what your fathers were, and conquer.’<br>The fair-eyed maids shall weep our banishments,<br>And in their [[songs]] curse ever-blinded fortune,<br>Till she for shame see what a wrong she has done<br>To youth and [[Nature|nature]]. This is all our world.<br>We shall know nothing here but one another,<br>Hear nothing but the [[Clock|clock]] that tells our woes.<br>The vine shall grow, but we shall never see it;<br>[[Summer]] shall come, and with her all delights,<br>But dead-cold [[Winter|winter]] must inhabit here still. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | ’Tis too true, [[Arcite]]. To our Theban hounds<br>That shook the aged forest with their echoes,<br>No more now must we holler; no more shake<br>Our pointed javelins whilst the angry swine<br>Flies like a Parthian quiver from our rages,<br>Struck with our [[Well|well]]-steeled darts. All valiant uses—<br>The food and nourishment of noble minds—<br>In us two here shall perish; we shall die—<br>Which is the curse of [[Honour|honour]]—lastly,<br>Children of grief and ignorance. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yet, cousin, Even from the [[Bottom|bottom]] of these miseries,<br>From all that fortune can inflict upon us,<br>I see two comforts rising—two mere blessings,<br>If the gods please, to hold here a brave patience<br>And the enjoying of our griefs together.<br>Whilst [[Palamon]] is with me, let me perish<br>If I think this our [[Prison|prison]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Certainly ’Tis a main goodness, cousin, that our fortunes<br>Were twined together. ’Tis most true, two souls<br>Put in two noble bodies, let ’em suffer<br>The gall of hazard, so they grow together,<br>Will never sink; they must not, say they could.<br>A willing man dies sleeping and all’s done. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Shall we make worthy uses of this place<br>That all men hate so much? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | How, gentle cousin? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Let’s think this [[Prison|prison]] holy sanctuary,<br>To keep us from corruption of worse men.<br>We are young, and yet [[Desire|desire]] the ways of [[Honour|honour]]<br>That liberty and common conversation,<br>The [[Poison|poison]] of pure spirits, might, like women,<br>Woo us to wander from. What worthy blessing<br>Can be, but our imaginations<br>May make it ours? And here being thus together,<br>We are an endless mine to one another:<br>We are one another’s wife, ever begetting<br>New births of [[Love|love]]; we are father, friends,<br>acquaintance;<br>We are in one another, families—<br>I am your heir, and you are mine; this place<br>Is our inheritance: no hard oppressor<br>Dare take this from us. Here, with a little patience,<br>We shall live long and loving. No surfeits seek us—<br>The hand of war hurts none here, nor the seas<br>[[Swallow]] their youth. Were we at liberty<br>A wife might part us lawfully, or business;<br>Quarrels consume us; envy of ill men<br>Crave our acquaintance. I might sicken, cousin,<br>Where you should never know it, and so perish<br>Without your noble hand to close mine eyes,<br>Or prayers to the gods. A thousand chances,<br>Were we from hence, would sever us. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | You have made me—I thank you, cousin [[Arcite]]—almost wanton<br>With my captivity. What a misery<br>It is to live abroad, and everywhere!<br>’Tis like a beast, methinks. I find the [[Court|court]] here;<br>I am sure, a more content; and all those pleasures<br>That woo the wills of men to [[Vanity|vanity]]<br>I see through now, and am sufficient<br>To tell the world ’tis but a gaudy [[Shadow|shadow]],<br>That old [[Time]], as he passes by, takes with him.<br>What had we been, old in the [[Court|court]] of Creon,<br>Where sin is justice, lust and ignorance<br>The virtues of the great ones? Cousin [[Arcite]],<br>Had not the loving gods found this place for us,<br>We had died as they do, ill old men, unwept,<br>And had their epitaphs, the people’s curses.<br>Shall I say more? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I would hear you still. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Ye shall. Is there record of any two that loved<br>Better than we do, [[Arcite]]? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Sure there cannot. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I do not think it possible our [[Friendship|friendship]]<br>Should ever leave us. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Till our deaths it cannot, |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Emilia]] and her Woman below. [[Palamon]] sees [[Emilia]] and is silent |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | And after [[Death|death]] our spirits shall be led<br>To those that [[Love|love]] eternally. Speak on, sir. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to her Woman)<br>This [[Garden|garden]] has a world of pleasure in’t.<br>What flower is this? |
| WOMAN | ’Tis called narcissus, madam. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | That was a fair boy, certain, but a [[Fool|fool]]<br>To [[Love|love]] himself. Were there not maids enough? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | (to [[Palamon]])<br>Pray forward. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to her Woman) Or were they all hard-hearted? |
| WOMAN | They could not be to one so fair. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Thou wouldst not. |
| WOMAN | I think I should not, madam. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | That’s a good wench—But take heed to your kindness, though. |
| WOMAN | Why, madam? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Men are mad things. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | (to [[Palamon]]) Will ye go forward, cousin? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to her Woman)<br>Canst not thou work such [[Flowers|flowers]] in silk, wench? |
| WOMAN | Yes. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | I’ll have a gown full of ’em, and of these.<br>This is a pretty colour—will’t not do<br>Rarely upon a skirt, wench? |
| WOMAN | Dainty, madam. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | (to [[Palamon]])<br>Cousin, cousin, how do you, sir? Why, [[Palamon]]! |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Never till now was I in [[Prison|prison]], [[Arcite]]. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Why, what’s the matter, man? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Behold and [[Wonder|wonder]]!<br>[[Arcite]] sees [[Emilia]]<br>By heaven, she is a goddess! |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Ha! |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Do reverence.<br>She is a goddess, [[Arcite]]. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to her Woman)<br>Of all [[Flowers|flowers]]<br>Methinks a [[Rose|rose]] is best. |
| WOMAN | Why, gentle madam? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | It is the very emblem of a maid—<br>For when the west [[Wind|wind]] courts her gently,<br>How modestly she blows, and paints the sun<br>With her chaste blushes! When the north comes near<br>her,<br>Rude and impatient, then, like chastity,<br>She locks her beauties in her bud again,<br>And [[Leaves|leaves]] him to base briers. |
| WOMAN | Yet, good madam, Sometimes her modesty will blow so far<br>She falls for’t—a maid,<br>If she have any [[Honour|honour]], would be loath<br>To take example by her. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Thou art wanton. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | (to [[Palamon]])<br>She is wondrous fair. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | She is all the [[Beauty|beauty]] extant. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | (to her Woman)<br>The sun grows high—let’s walk in. Keep these [[Flowers|flowers]].<br>We’ll see how close art can come near their colours.<br>I am wondrous merry-hearted—I could laugh now. |
| WOMAN | I could lie down, I am sure. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | And take one with you? |
| WOMAN | That’s as we bargain, madam. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | [[Well]], agree then. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Emilia]] and her Woman |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | What think you of this [[Beauty|beauty]]? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | ’Tis a rare one. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Is’t but a rare one? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yes, a matchless [[Beauty|beauty]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Might not a man [[Well|well]] lose himself and [[Love|love]] her? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I cannot tell what you have done; I have,<br>Beshrew mine eyes for’t. Now I feel my shackles. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | You [[Love|love]] her then? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Who would not? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | And [[Desire|desire]] her? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Before my liberty. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I saw her first. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | That’s nothing. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | But it shall be. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I saw her too. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes, but you must not [[Love|love]] her. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I will not, as you do, to worship her<br>As she is heavenly and a blessèd goddess!<br>I [[Love|love]] her as a woman, to enjoy her—<br>So both may [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | You shall not [[Love|love]] at all. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Not [[Love|love]] at all—who shall deny me? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I that first saw her, I that took possession<br>First with mine eye of all those beauties<br>In her revealed to mankind. If thou lov’st her,<br>Or entertain’st a [[Hope|hope]] to blast my wishes,<br>Thou art a traitor, [[Arcite]], and a fellow<br>False as thy title to her. [[Friendship]], [[Blood|blood]],<br>And all the ties between us I disclaim,<br>If thou once think upon her. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yes, I [[Love|love]] her—And if the lives of all my name lay on it,<br>I must do so. I [[Love|love]] her with my [[Soul|soul]]—<br>If that will lose ye, farewell, [[Palamon]]!<br>I say again,<br>I [[Love|love]] her, and in loving her maintain<br>I am as worthy and as free a [[Lover|lover]],<br>And have as just a title to her [[Beauty|beauty]],<br>As any [[Palamon]], or any living<br>That is a man’s son. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Have I called thee friend? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yes, and have found me so. Why are you moved<br>thus?<br>Let me deal coldly with you. Am not I<br>Part of your [[Blood|blood]], part of your [[Soul|soul]]? You have<br>told me<br>That I was [[Palamon]] and you were [[Arcite]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Am not I liable to those affections,<br>Those joys, griefs, angers, fears, my friend shall<br>suffer? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Ye may be. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Why then would you deal so cunningly, So strangely, so unlike a noble kinsman,<br>To [[Love|love]] [[Alone|alone]]? Speak truly. Do you think me<br>Unworthy of her sight? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | No, but unjust If thou pursue that sight. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Because another First sees the enemy, shall I stand still,<br>And let mine [[Honour|honour]] down, and never charge? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes, if he be but one. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | But say that one<br>Had rather combat me? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Let that one say so, And use thy [[Freedom|freedom]]; else, if thou pursuest her,<br>Be as that cursed man that hates his country,<br>A branded [[Villain|villain]]. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | You are mad. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I must be. Till thou art worthy, [[Arcite]], it concerns me;<br>And in this [[Madness|madness]] if I hazard thee<br>And take thy life, I deal but truly. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Fie, sir. You play the [[Child|child]] extremely. I will [[Love|love]] her,<br>I must, I ought to do so, and I dare—<br>And all this justly. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | O, that now, that now Thy false [[Self|self]] and thy friend had but this fortune—<br>To be one hour at liberty and grasp<br>Our good swords in our hands! I would quickly teach<br>thee<br>What ’twere to filch affection from another.<br>Thou art baser in it than a cutpurse.<br>Put but thy head out of this [[Window|window]] more<br>And, as I have a [[Soul|soul]], I’ll nail thy life to’t. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Thou dar’st not, [[Fool|fool]]; thou canst not; thou art feeble.<br>Put my head out? I’ll throw my [[Body|body]] out<br>And leap the [[Garden|garden]] when I see her next, |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Jailer above |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | And pitch between her arms to anger thee. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | No more—the keeper’s coming. I shall live<br>To knock thy brains out with my shackles. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Do. |
| JAILER | By your leave, gentlemen. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Now, honest keeper? |
| JAILER | Lord [[Arcite]], you must presently to th’ Duke.<br>The cause I know not yet. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I am ready, keeper. |
| JAILER | Prince [[Palamon]], I must a while bereave you<br>Of your fair cousin’s company. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Arcite]] and the Jailer |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | And me, too, Even when you please, of life. Why is he sent for?<br>It may be he shall marry her—he’s goodly,<br>And like enough the Duke hath taken notice<br>Both of his [[Blood|blood]] and [[Body|body]]. But his falsehood!<br>Why should a friend be treacherous? If that<br>Get him a wife so noble and so fair,<br>Let honest men ne’er [[Love|love]] again. Once more<br>I would but see this fair one. Blessèd [[Garden|garden]],<br>And fruit and [[Flowers|flowers]] more blessed, that still blossom<br>As her bright eyes shine on ye! Would I were,<br>For all the fortune of my life hereafter,<br>Yon little tree, yon blooming apricot—<br>How I would spread and fling my wanton arms<br>In at her [[Window|window]]! I would bring her fruit<br>Fit for the gods to feed on; youth and pleasure<br>Still as she tasted should be doubled on her;<br>And if she be not heavenly, I would make her<br>So near the gods in [[Nature|nature]] they should [[Fear|fear]] her— |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Jailer bove |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | And then I am sure she would [[Love|love]] me. How now,<br>keeper,<br>Where’s [[Arcite]]? |
| JAILER | Banished—Prince Pirithous Obtained his liberty; but never more,<br>Upon his oath and life, must he set foot<br>Upon this kingdom. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Palamon|PALAMON]] aside |
| JAILER | He’s a blessed man. He shall see Thebes again, and call to arms<br>The bold young men that, when he bids ’em charge,<br>Fall on like [[Fire|fire]]. [[Arcite]] shall have a fortune,<br>If he dare make himself a worthy [[Lover|lover]],<br>Yet in the field to strike a battle for her;<br>And if he lose her then, he’s a cold coward.<br>How bravely may he bear himself to win her<br>If he be noble [[Arcite]]; thousand ways!<br>Were I at liberty I would do things<br>Of such a virtuous [[Greatness|greatness]] that this lady,<br>This blushing virgin, should take manhood to her<br>And seek to ravish me. |
| JAILER | My lord, for you<br>I have this charge to— |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | To discharge my life. |
| JAILER | No, but from this place to remove your lordship—<br>The windows are too open. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Devils take ’em That are so envious to me—prithee kill me. |
| JAILER | And hang for’t afterward? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | By this good light, Had I as word I would kill thee. |
| JAILER | Why, my lord? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Thou bring’st such pelting scurvy news continually,<br>Thou art not worthy life. I will not go. |
| JAILER | Indeed you must, my lord. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | May I see the [[Garden|garden]]? |
| JAILER | No. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Then I am resolved—I will not go. |
| JAILER | I must constrain you, then; and for you are dangerous,<br>I’ll clap more irons on you. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Do, good keeper. I’ll shake ’em so ye shall not [[Sleep|sleep]]:<br>I’ll make ye a new morris. Must I go? |
| JAILER | There is no remedy. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Farewell, kind [[Window|window]]. May rude [[Wind|wind]] never hurt thee. O, my lady,<br>If ever thou hast felt what sorrow was,<br>Dream how I suffer. Come, now bury me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Palamon]] and the Jailer |
