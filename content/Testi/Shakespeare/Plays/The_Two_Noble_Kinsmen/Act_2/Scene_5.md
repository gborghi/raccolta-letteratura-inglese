| *(didascalia)* | Short flourish of cornetts and shouts within. Enter Theseus, Hippolyta, Pirithous, Emilia, Arcite disguised, with a garland, and attendants |
| THESEUS | You have done worthily. I have not seen<br>Since Hercules a man of tougher sinews.<br>Whate’er you are, you run the best and wrestle<br>That these times can allow. |
| ARCITE | I am proud to please you. |
| THESEUS | What country bred you? |
| ARCITE | This—but far off, prince. |
| THESEUS | Are you a gentleman? |
| ARCITE | My father said so, And to those gentle uses gave me life. |
| THESEUS | Are you his heir? |
| ARCITE | His youngest, sir. |
| THESEUS | Your father Sure is a happy sire, then. What proves you? |
| ARCITE | A little of all noble qualities.<br>I could have kept a hawk and well have hollered<br>To a deep cry of dogs; I dare not praise<br>My feat in horsemanship, yet they that knew me<br>Would say it was my best piece; last and greatest,<br>I would be thought a soldier. |
| THESEUS | You are perfect. |
| PIRITHOIIS | Upon my soul, a proper man. |
| EMILIA | He is so. |
| PIRITHOUS | (to Hippolyta)<br>How do you like him, lady? |
| HIPPOLYTA | I admire him. I have not seen so young a man so noble—<br>If he say true—of his sort. |
| EMILIA | Believe His mother was a wondrous handsome woman—<br>His face methinks goes that way. |
| HIPPOLYTA | But his body And fiery mind illustrate a brave father. |
| PIRITHOUS | Mark how his virtue, like a hidden sun,<br>Breaks through his baser garments. |
| HIPPOLYTA | He’s well got, sure. |
| THESEUS | (to Arcite)<br>What made you seek this place, sir? |
| ARCITE | Noble Theseus, To purchase name and do my ablest service<br>To such a well-found wonder as thy worth,<br>For only in thy court of all the world<br>Dwells fair-eyed honour. |
| PIRITHOUS | All his words are worthy. |
| THESEUS | (to Arcite)<br>Sir, we are much indebted to your travel,<br>Nor shall you lose your wish.—Pirithous,<br>Dispose of this fair gentleman. |
| PIRITHOUS | Thanks, Theseus. (To Arcite) Whate’er you are, you’re mine, and I shall<br>give you<br>To a most noble service, to this lady,<br>This bright young virgin; pray observe her goodness.<br>You have honoured her fair birthday with your<br>virtues,<br>And as your due you’re hers. Kiss her fair hand, sir. |
| ARCITE | Sir, you’re a noble giver. (To Emilia) Dearest beauty,<br>Thus let me seal my vowed faith.<br>He kisses her hand<br>When your servant,<br>Your most unworthy creature, but offends you,<br>Command him die, he shall. |
| EMILIA | That were too cruel. If you deserve well, sir, I shall soon see’t.<br>You’re mine, and somewhat better than your rank I’ll<br>use you. |
| PIRITHOUS | (to Arcite)<br>I’ll see you furnished, and, because you say<br>You are a horseman, I must needs entreat you<br>This afternoon to ride—but ’tis a rough one. |
| ARCITE | I like him better, prince—I shall not then<br>Freeze in my saddle. |
| THESEUS | (to Hippolyta) |
| *(didascalia)* | Sweet, you must be ready—And you, Emilia, to Pirithous] and you, friend—and |
| THESEUS | all,<br>Tomorrow by the sun, to do observance<br>To flow’ry May in Dian’s wood. (To Arcite) Wait well,<br>sir,<br>Upon your mistress.—Emity, I hope<br>He shall not go afoot. |
| EMILIA | That were a shame, sir, While I have horses. (To Arcite) Take your choice, and<br>what<br>You want, at any time, let me but know it.<br>If you serve faithfully, I dare assure you,<br>You’ll find a loving mistress. |
| ARCITE | If I do not, Let me find that my father ever hated—<br>Disgrace and blows. |
| THESEUS | Go, lead the way—you have won it. It shall be so: you shall receive all dues<br>Fit for the honour you have won. ’Twere wrong else.<br>(To Emilia) Sister, beshrew my heart, you have a<br>servant<br>That, if I were a woman, would be master.<br>But you are wise. |
| EMILIA | I hope too wise for that, sir. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exeunt |
