| *(didascalia)* | Enter Gerald (a schoolmaster), five Countrymen, one of whom is dressed as a Babion, five Wenches, and Timothy, a taborer. All are attired as morris dancers |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Fie, fie,<br>What tediosity and disinsanity<br>Is here among yet Have my rudiments<br>Been laboured so long with ye, milked unto ye,<br>And, by a figure, even the very plum-broth<br>And marrow of my understanding laid upon ye?<br>And do you still cry ‘where?’ and ‘how?’ and<br>‘wherefore?’<br>You most coarse frieze capacities, ye jean judgements,<br>Have I said, ‘thus let be’, and ‘there let be’,<br>And ‘then let be’, and no man understand me?<br>Proh deum, medius fidius-ye are all dunces.<br>Forwhy, here stand I. Here the Duke comes. There are<br>you,<br>Close in the thicket. The Duke appears. I meet him,<br>And unto him I utter learnèd things<br>And many figures. He hears, and nods, and hums,<br>And then cries, ‘Rare!’, and I go forward. At length<br>I fling my cap up—mark there—then do you,<br>As once did Meleager and the boar,<br>Break comely out before him, like true [[lovers]],<br>Cast yourselves in a [[Body|body]] decently,<br>And sweetly, by a figure, trace and turn, boys. |
| FIRST COUNTRYMAN | And sweetly we will do it, master Gerald. |
| SECOND COUNTRYMAN | Draw up the company. Where’s the taborer? |
| THIRD COUNTRYMAN | Why, Timothy! |
| TABORER | Here, my mad boys, have at ye! |
| SCHOOLMASTER | But I say, where’s these women? |
| FOURTH COUNTRYMAN | Here’s Friz and [[Madeline]]. |
| SECOND COUNTRYMAN | And little Luce with the white legs, and bouncing Barbara. |
| FIRST COUNTRYMAN | And freckled Nell, that never failed her master. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Where be your ribbons, maids? Swim with your bodies<br>And carry it sweetly and deliverly,<br>And now and then a favour and a frisk. |
| NELL | Let us [[Alone|alone]], sir. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Where’s the rest o’th’ music? |
| THIRD COUNTRYMAN | Dispersed as you commanded. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Couple, then,<br>And see what’s wanting. Where’s the babion?<br>(To the Babion) My friend, carry your tail without<br>offence<br>Or scandal to the ladies; and be sure<br>You tumble with audacity and manhood,<br>And when you bark, do it with judgement. |
| BABION | Yes, sir. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Quousque tandem? Here is a woman wanting! |
| FOURTH COUNTRYMAN | We may go whistle—all the fat’s i’th’ [[Fire|fire]]. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | We have,<br>As learned authors utter, washed a tile;<br>We have been fatuus, and laboured vainly. |
| SECOND COUNTRYMAN | This is that scornful piece, that scurvy hilding<br>That gave her promise faithfully she would be here—<br>Cicely, the seamstress’ daughter.<br>The next gloves that I give her shall be dogskin.<br>Nay, an she fail me once—you can tell, Areas,<br>She swore by wine and bread she would not break. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | An eel and woman,<br>A learned poet says, unless by th’ tail<br>And with thy teeth thou hold, will either fail—<br>In manners this was false position. |
| FIRST COUNTRYMAN | A [[Fire|fire]]-ill take her! Does she flinch now? |
| THIRD COUNTRYMAN | What<br>Shall we determine, sir? |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Nothing;<br>Our business is become a nullity,<br>Yea, and a woeful and a piteous nullity. |
| FOURTH COUNTRYMAN | Now, when the credit of our town lay on it,<br>Now to be frampold, now to piss o’th’ nettle!<br>Go thy ways—I’ll remember thee, I’ll fit thee! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Jailer’s Daughter |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | (sings)<br>The George Alow came from the south,<br>From the coast of Barbary-a;<br>And there he met with brave gallants of war,<br>By one, by two, by three-a.<br>‘[[Well]] hailed, [[Well|well]] hailed, you jolly gallants,<br>And whither now are you bound-a?<br>O let me have your company<br>Till I come to the sound-a.’<br>There was three fools fell out about an owlet—<br>The one he said it was an owl,<br>The other he said nay,<br>The third he said it was a hawk,<br>And her [[Bells|bells]] were cut away. |
| THIRD COUNTRYMAN | There’s a dainty [[Madwoman|madwoman]], master,<br>Comes i’th’ nick, as mad as a March hare.<br>If we can get her [[Dance|dance]], we are made again.<br>I warrant her, she’ll do the rarest gambols. |
| FIRST COUNTRYMAN | A [[Madwoman|madwoman]]? We are made, boys. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | (to the Jailer’s Daughter)<br>And are you mad, good woman? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | I would be sorry else.<br>Give me your hand. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Why? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | I can tell your fortune. |
| *(didascalia)* | She examines his hand |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | You are a [[Fool|fool]]. Tell ten—I have posed him. Buzz!<br>Friend, you must eat no white bread—if you do,<br>Your teeth will bleed extremely. Shall we [[Dance|dance]], ho?<br>I know you—you’re a tinker. Sirrah tinker,<br>Stop no more holes but what you should. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Dii boni—<br>A tinker, [[Damsel|damsel]]? |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | Or a conjurer—<br>Raise me a devil now and let him play<br>Qui passa o’th’ [[Bells|bells]] and bones. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Go, take her,<br>And fluently persuade her to a peace.<br>Et opus exegi, quod nec Iovis ira, nec ignis—<br><br>Strike up, and lead her in. |
| SECOND COUNTRYMAN | Come, lass, let’s trip it. |
| JAILER’S DAUGHTER | I’ll lead. |
| THIRD COUNTRYMAN | Do, do. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Persuasively and cunningly— |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Wind]] horns within |
| SCHOOLMASTER | away, boys,<br>I hear the horns. Give me some meditation,<br>And mark your cue. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Gerald the Schoolmaster Pallas inspire me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Theseus]], Pirithous, [[Hippolyta]], [[Emilia]], [[Arcite]], and train |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | This way the stag took. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Stay and edify. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What have we here? |
| PIRITHOUS | Some country sport, upon my life, sir. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | (to the Schoolmaster)<br>[[Well]], sir, go forward—we will edify.<br>Ladies, sit down—we’ll stay it. |
| *(didascalia)* | They sit: [[Theseus|theseus]] in a chair, the others on stools |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Thou doughty Duke, all hail! All hail, sweet ladies. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | This is a cold beginning. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | If you but favour, our country pastime made is.<br>We are a few of those collected here,<br>That ruder tongues distinguish ‘villager’;<br>And to say verity, and not to [[Fable|fable]],<br>We are a merry rout, or else a rabble,<br>Or company, or, by a figure, [[Chorus|chorus]],<br>That fore thy dignity will [[Dance|dance]] a morris.<br>And I, that am the rectifier of all,<br>By title pedagogus, that let fall<br>The birch upon the breeches of the small ones,<br>And humble with a ferula the tall ones,<br>Do here present this [[Machine|machine]], or this frame;<br>And dainty Duke, whose doughty dismal fame<br>From Dis to Daedalus, from post to pillar,<br>Is blown abroad, help me, thy poor [[Well|well]]-willer,<br>And with thy twinkling eyes, look right and straight<br>Upon this mighty ‘Moor’—of mickle weight—<br>‘Ice’ now comes in, which, being glued together,<br>Makes ‘morris’, and the cause that we came hither.<br>The [[Body|body]] of our sport, of no small study,<br>I first appear, though rude, and raw, and muddy,<br>To speak, before thy noble grace, this tenor<br>At whose great feet I offer up my penner.<br>The next, the Lord of May and Lady bright;<br>The Chambermaid and Servingman, by [[Night|night]]<br>That seek out silent hanging; then mine Host<br>And his fat Spouse, that welcomes, to their cost,<br>The galled traveller, and with a beck’ning<br>Informs the tapster to inflame the reck‘ning;<br>Then the beest-eating Clown; and next, the [[Fool]];<br>The babion with long tail and eke long tool,<br>Cum multis aliis that make a [[Dance|dance]]—<br>Say ‘ay’, and all shall presently advance. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Ay, ay, by any means, dear dominie. |
| PIRITHOUS | Produce. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | (knocks for the [[Dance|dance]])<br>Intrate filii, come forth and foot it. |
| *(didascalia)* | He flings up his cap. Music. |
| *(didascalia)* | The Schoolmaster ushers in |
| SCHOOLMASTER | May Lord,<br>Servingman,<br>A Country Clown,<br>or Shepherd,<br>An Host,<br>A He-babion,<br>May Lady.<br><br>Chambermaid.<br>Country Wench.<br><br>Hostess.<br>She-babion.<br><br>A He-[[Fool|fool]], The Jailer’s Daughter as<br><br>She-[[Fool|fool]].<br>All these persons apparelled to the life, the men<br>issuing out of one door and the wenches from the |
| *(didascalia)* | other. They [[Dance|dance]] a morris |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Ladies, if we have been merry,<br>And have pleased ye with a derry,<br>And a derry, and a down,<br>Say the schoolmaster’s no clown.<br>Duke, if we have pleased thee too,<br>And have done as good boys should do,<br>Give us but a tree or twain<br>For a maypole, and again,<br>Ere another year run out,<br>We’ll make thee laugh, and all this rout. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Take twenty, dominie. (To [[Hippolyta]]) How does my sweetheart? |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | Never so pleased, sir. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | ’Twas an excellent [[Dance|dance]],<br>And for a preface, I never heard a better. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Schoolmaster, I thank you. One see ’em all rewarded. |
| PIRITHOUS | And here’s something to paint your pole withal.<br>He gives them money |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Now to our sports again. |
| SCHOOLMASTER | May the stag thou hunt’st stand long,<br>And thy [[dogs]] be swift and strong;<br>May they kill him without lets,<br>And the ladies eat his dowsets. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Theseus]] and train. [[Wind]] horns within |
| SCHOOLMASTER | Come, we are all made. Dii deaeque omnes,<br>Ye have danced rarely, wenches. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
