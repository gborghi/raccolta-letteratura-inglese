| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Palamon]] from the bush |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | About this hour my cousin gave his [[Faith|faith]]<br>To visit me again, and with him bring<br>Two swords and two good armours; if he fail,<br>He’s neither man nor soldier. When he left me,<br>I did not think a week could have restored<br>My lost strength to me, I was grown so low<br>And crest-fall’n with my wants. I thank thee, [[Arcite]],<br>Thou art yet a fair foe, and I feel myself,<br>With this refreshing, able once again<br>To out-dure danger. To delay it longer<br>Would make the world think, when it comes to<br>hearing,<br>That I lay fatting, like a swine, to fight,<br>And not a soldier. Therefore this blest morning<br>Shall be the last; and that [[Sword|sword]] he refuses,<br>If it but hold, I kill him with; ’tis justice.<br>So, [[Love|love]] and fortune for me! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Arcite]] with two armours and two swords |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | O, good morrow. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Good morrow, noble kinsman. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I have put you<br>To too much pains, sir. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | That too much, fair cousin,<br>Is but a debt to [[Honour|honour]], and my duty. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Would you were so in all, sir—I could wish ye<br>As kind a kinsman, as you force me find<br>A beneficial foe, that my embraces<br>Might thank ye, not my blows. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I shall think either,<br>[[Well]] done, a noble recompense. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Then I shall quit you. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Defy me in these fair terms, and you show<br>More than a mistress to me—no more anger,<br>As you [[Love|love]] anything that’s honourable.<br>We were not bred to talk, man. When we are armed<br>And both upon our guards, then let our fury,<br>Like meeting of two tides, fly strongly from us;<br>And then to whom the birthright of this [[Beauty|beauty]]<br>Truly pertains—without upbraidings, scorns,<br>Despisings of our persons, and such poutings<br>Fitter for girls and schoolboys—will be seen,<br>And quickly, yours or mine. Will’t please you arm,<br>sir?<br>Or, if you feel yourself not fitting yet,<br>And furnished with your old strength, I’ll stay,<br>cousin,<br>And every day discourse you into health,<br>As I am spared. Your person I am friends with,<br>And I could wish I had not said I loved her,<br>Though I had died; but loving such a lady,<br>And justifying my [[Love|love]], I must not fly from’t. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | [[Arcite]], thou art so brave an enemy<br>That no man but thy cousin’s fit to kill thee.<br>I am [[Well|well]] and lusty—choose your arms. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Choose you, sir. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Wilt thou exceed in all, or dost thou do it<br>To make me spare thee? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | If you think so, cousin,<br>You are deceived, for as I am a soldier,<br>I will not spare you. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | That’s [[Well|well]] said. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | You’ll find it. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Then as I am an honest man, and [[Love|love]]<br>With all the justice of affection,<br>I’ll pay thee soundly.<br>He chooses one armour<br>This I’ll take. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | (indicating the remaining armour)<br>That’s mine, then.<br>I’ll arm you first. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Do.<br>[[Arcite]] arms [[Palamon]]<br>Pray thee tell me, cousin,<br>Where gott’st thou this good armour? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | ‘Tis the Duke’s,<br>And to say true, I stole it. Do I pinch you? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | No. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Is’t not too heavy? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I have worn a lighter—<br>But I shall make it serve. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I’ll buckle’t close. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | By any means. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | You care not for a grand guard? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | No, no, we’ll use no horses. I perceive<br>You would fain be at that fight. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I am indifferent. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | [[Faith]], so am I. Good cousin, thrust the buckle<br>Through far enough. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I warrant you. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | My casque now. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Will you fight bare-armed? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | We shall be the nimbler. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | But use your gauntlets, though—those are o’th’ least.<br>Prithee take mine, good cousin. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Thank you, [[Arcite]].<br>How do I look? Am I fall’n much away? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | [[Faith]], very little—[[Love|love]] has used you kindly. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I’ll warrant thee, I’ll strike [[Home|home]]. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Do, and spare not—<br>I’ll give you cause, sweet cousin. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Now to you, sir.<br>[[Palamon]] arms [[Arcite]]<br>Methinks this armour’s very like that, [[Arcite]],<br>Thou wor’st that day the three kings fell, but lighter. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | That was a very good one, and that day,<br>I [[Well|well]] remember, you outdid me, cousin.<br>I never saw such valour. When you charged<br>Upon the left wing of the enemy,<br>I spurred hard to come up, and under me<br>I had a right good [[Horse|horse]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | You had indeed—<br>A bright bay, I remember. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Yes. But all<br>Was vainly laboured in me—you outwent me,<br>Nor could my wishes reach you. Yet a little<br>I did by [[Imitation|imitation]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | More by virtue—<br>You are modest, cousin. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | When I saw you charge first,<br>Methought I heard a dreadful clap of thunder<br>Break from the troop. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | But still before that flew<br>The [[Lightning|lightning]] of your valour. Stay a little,<br>Is not this piece too strait? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | No, no, ’tis [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I would have nothing hurt thee but my [[Sword|sword]]—<br>A bruise would be dishonour. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Now I am perfect. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Stand off, then. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Take my [[Sword|sword]]; I hold it better. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I thank ye. No, keep it—your life lies on it.<br>Here’s one—if it but hold, I ask no more<br>For all my hopes. My cause and [[Honour|honour]] guard me. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | And me, my [[Love|love]].<br>They bow several ways, then advance and stand<br>Is there aught else to say? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | This only, and no more. Thou art mine aunt’s son,<br>And that [[Blood|blood]] we [[Desire|desire]] to shed is mutual:<br>In me, thine, and in thee, mine. My [[Sword|sword]]<br>Is in my hand, and if thou kill’st me,<br>The gods and I forgive thee. If there be<br>A place prepared for those that [[Sleep|sleep]] in [[Honour|honour]],<br>I wish his weary [[Soul|soul]] that falls may win it.<br>Fight bravely, cousin. Give me thy noble hand. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Here, [[Palamon]]. This hand shall never more<br>Come near thee with such [[Friendship|friendship]]. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I commend thee. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | If I fall, curse me, and say I was a coward—<br>For none but such dare die in these just trials.<br>Once more farewell, my cousin. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Farewell, [[Arcite]].<br>Fight. Horns within; they stand |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Lo, cousin, lo, our folly has undone us. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Why? |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | This is the Duke a-hunting, as I told you.<br>If we be found, we are wretched. O, retire,<br>For [[Honour|honour]]’s sake, and safely, presently,<br>Into your bush again. Sir, we shall find<br>Too many hours to die. In, gentle cousin—<br>If you be seen, you perish instantly<br>For breaking [[Prison|prison]], and I, if you reveal me,<br>For my contempt. Then all the world will scorn us,<br>And say we had a noble difference,<br>But base disposers of it. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | No, no, cousin,<br>I will no more be hidden, nor put off<br>This great adventure to a second trial.<br>I know your cunning and I know your cause—<br>He that faints now, shame take him! Put thyself<br>Upon thy present guard— |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | You are not mad? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Or I will make th’advantage of this hour<br>Mine own, and what to come shall threaten me<br>I [[Fear|fear]] less than my fortune. Know, weak cousin,<br>I [[Love|love]] [[Emilia]], and in that I’ll bury<br>Thee and all crosses else. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | Then come what can come,<br>Thou shalt know, [[Palamon]], I dare as [[Well|well]]<br>Die as discourse or [[Sleep|sleep]]. Only this fears me,<br>The law will have the [[Honour|honour]] of our ends.<br>Have at thy life! |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Look to thine own [[Well|well]], [[Arcite]]!<br>They fight again. |
| *(didascalia)* | Horns. Enter [[Theseus]], [[Hippolyta]], [[Emilia]], Pirithous, and train. [[Theseus]] separates [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]] |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What ignorant and mad malicious traitors<br>Are you, that ’gainst the tenor of my laws<br>Are making battle, thus like knights appointed,<br>Without my leave and officers of arms?<br>By Castor, both shall die. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Hold thy word, [[Theseus]].<br>We are certainly both traitors, both despisers<br>Of thee and of thy goodness. I am [[Palamon]],<br>That cannot [[Love|love]] thee, he that broke thy [[Prison|prison]]—<br>Think [[Well|well]] what that deserves. And this is [[Arcite]];<br>A bolder traitor never trod thy ground,<br>A falser ne’er seemed friend. This is the man<br>Was begged and banished; this is he contemns thee,<br>And what thou dar’st do; and in this disguise,<br>Against thine own edict, follows thy sister,<br>That fortunate bright star, the fair [[Emilia]],<br>Whose servant—if there be a right in seeing<br>And first bequeathing of the [[Soul|soul]] to—justly<br>I am; and, which is more, dares think her his.<br>This treachery, like a most trusty [[Lover|lover]],<br>I called him now to answer. If thou be’st<br>As thou art spoken, great and virtuous,<br>The true decider of all injuries,<br>Say, ’Fight again’, and thou shalt see me, [[Theseus]],<br>Do such a justice thou thyself wilt envy.<br>Then take my life—I’ll woo thee to’t. |
| PIRITHOUS | O heaven,<br>What more than man is this! |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | I have sworn. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | We seek not<br>Thy breath of [[Mercy|mercy]], [[Theseus]]. ‘Tis to me<br>A thing as soon to die as thee to say it,<br>And no more moved. Where this man calls me traitor<br>Let me say thus much—if in [[Love|love]] be treason,<br>In service of so excellent a [[Beauty|beauty]],<br>As I [[Love|love]] most, and in that [[Faith|faith]] will perish,<br>As I have brought my life here to confirm it,<br>As I have served her truest, worthiest,<br>As I dare kill this cousin that denies it,<br>So let me be most traitor and ye please me.<br>For scorning thy edict, Duke, ask that lady<br>Why she is fair, and why her eyes command me<br>Stay here to [[Love|love]] her, and if she say, ‘Traitor’,<br>I am a [[Villain|villain]] fit to lie unburied. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Thou shalt have pity of us both, O [[Theseus]],<br>If unto neither thou show [[Mercy|mercy]]. Stop,<br>As thou art just, thy noble ear against us;<br>As thou art valiant, for thy cousin’s [[Soul|soul]],<br>Whose twelve strong labours [[Crown|crown]] his [[Memory|memory]],<br>Let’s die together, at one instant, Duke.<br>Only a little let him fall before me,<br>That I may tell my [[Soul|soul]] he shall not have her. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | I grant your wish; for to say true, your cousin<br>Has ten times more offended, for I gave him<br>More [[Mercy|mercy]] than you found, sir, your offences<br>Being no more than his. None here speak for ’em,<br>For ere the sun set both shall [[Sleep|sleep]] for ever. |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | (to [[Emilia]])<br>Alas, the pity! Now or never, sister,<br>Speak, not to be denied. That face of yours<br>Will bear the curses else of after ages<br>For these lost cousins. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | In my face, dear sister,<br>I find no anger to ’em, nor no ruin.<br>The misadventure of their own eyes kill ’em.<br>Yet that I will be woman and have pity, |
| *(didascalia)* | She kneels |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | My knees shall grow to th’ ground, but I’ll get [[Mercy|mercy]].<br>Help me, dear sister—in a deed so virtuous<br>The powers of all women will be with us.<br>[[Hippolyta]] kneels<br>Most royal brother— |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | Sir, by our tie of [[Marriage|marriage]]— |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | By your own spotless [[Honour|honour]]— |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | By that [[Faith|faith]],<br>That fair hand, and that honest heart you gave me— |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | By that you would have pity in another,<br>By your own virtues infinite— |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | By valour,<br>By all the chaste nights I have ever pleased you— |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | These are strange conjurings. |
| PIRITHOUS | Nay, then, I’ll in too. |
| *(didascalia)* | He kneels |
| PIRITHOUS | By all our [[Friendship|friendship]], sir, by all our dangers,<br>By all you [[Love|love]] most: wars, and this sweet lady— |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | By that you would have trembled to deny<br>A blushing maid— |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | By your own eyes, by strength—<br>In which you swore I went beyond all women,<br>Almost all men—and yet I yielded, [[Theseus]]— |
| PIRITHOUS | To [[Crown|crown]] all this, by your most noble [[Soul|soul]],<br>Which cannot want due [[Mercy|mercy]], I beg first— |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | Next hear my prayers— |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Last let me entreat, sir— |
| PIRITHOUS | For [[Mercy|mercy]]. |
| [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] | [[Mercy]]. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | [[Mercy]] on these princes. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Ye make my [[Faith|faith]] reel. Say I felt<br>Compassion to ’em both, how would you place it? |
| *(didascalia)* | They rise |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Upon their lives—but with their banishments. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | You are a right woman, sister: you have pity,<br>But want the understanding where to use it.<br>If you [[Desire|desire]] their lives, invent a way<br>Safer than banishment. Can these two live,<br>And have the agony of [[Love|love]] about ‘em,<br>And not kill one another? Every day<br>They’d fight about you, hourly bring your [[Honour|honour]]<br>In public question with their swords. Be wise, then,<br>And here forget ’em. It concerns your credit<br>And my oath equally. I have said—they die.<br>Better they fall by th’ law than one another.<br>Bow not my [[Honour|honour]]. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | O my noble brother,<br>That oath was rashly made, and in your anger.<br>Your reason will not hold it. If such vows<br>Stand for express will, all the world must perish.<br>Beside, I have another oath ’gainst yours,<br>Of more authority, I am sure more [[Love|love]]—<br>Not made in passion, neither, but good heed. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What is it, sister? |
| PIRITHOUS | (to [[Emilia]]) Urge it [[Home|home]], brave lady. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | That you would ne’er deny me anything<br>Fit for my modest suit and your free granting.<br>I tie you to your word now; if ye fail in’t,<br>Think how you maim your [[Honour|honour]]—<br>For now I am set a-begging, sir. I am deaf<br>To all but your compassion—how their lives<br>Might breed the ruin of my name, opinion.<br>Shall anything that loves me perish for me?<br>That were a cruel wisdom: do men prune<br>The straight young boughs that blush with thousand<br>blossoms<br>Because they may be rotten? O, Duke [[Theseus]],<br>The goodly mothers that have groaned for these,<br>And all the [[Longing|longing]] maids that ever loved,<br>If your vow stand, shall curse me and my [[Beauty|beauty]],<br>And in their funeral [[songs]] for these two cousins<br>Despise my cruelty and cry woe worth me,<br>Till I am nothing but the scorn of women.<br>For heaven’s sake, save their lives and banish ’em. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | On what conditions? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Swear ’em never more To make me their contention, or to know me,<br>To tread upon thy dukedom; and to be,<br>Wherever they shall travel, ever strangers<br>To one another. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | I’ll be cut a-pieces<br>Before I take this oath—forget I [[Love|love]] her?<br>O all ye gods, despise me, then. Thy banishment<br>I not mislike, so we may fairly carry<br>Our swords and cause along—else, never trifle,<br>But take our lives, Duke. I must [[Love|love]], and will;<br>And for that [[Love|love]] must and dare kill this cousin<br>On any piece the earth has. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Will you, [[Arcite]],<br>Take these conditions? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | He’s a [[Villain|villain]] then. |
| PIRITHOUS | These are men! |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | No, never, Duke. ’Tis worse to me than begging,<br>To take my life so basely. Though I think<br>I never shall enjoy her, yet I’ll preserve<br>The [[Honour|honour]] of affection and die for her,<br>Make [[Death|death]] a devil. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | What may be done? For now I feel compassion. |
| PIRITHOUS | Let it not fall again, sir. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Say, [[Emilia]], If one of them were dead—as one must—are you<br>Content to take the other to your husband?<br>They cannot both enjoy you. They are princes<br>As goodly as your own eyes, and as noble<br>As ever fame yet spoke of. Look upon ’em,<br>And if you can [[Love|love]], end this difference.<br>I give consent. (To [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]]) Are you<br>content too, princes? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | and [[Arcite|ARCITE]]<br>With all our souls. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | He that she refuses<br>Must die, then. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | and [[Arcite|ARCITE]]<br>Any [[Death|death]] thou canst invent, Duke. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | If I fall from that mouth, I fall with favour,<br>And [[lovers]] yet [[unborn]] shall bless my ashes. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | If she refuse me, yet my [[Grave|grave]] will wed me,<br>And soldiers sing my epitaph. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | (to [[Emilia]])<br>Make choice, then. |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | I cannot, sir. They are both too excellent.<br>For me, a hair shall never fall of these men. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Hippolyta|HIPPOLYTA]] to [[Theseus]] |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | What will become of ’em? |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Thus I ordain it, And by mine [[Honour|honour]] once again it stands,<br>Or both shall die. (To [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]]) You shall<br>both to your country,<br>And each within this month, accompanied<br>With three fair knights, appear again in this place,<br>In which I’ll plant a pyramid; and whether,<br>Before us that are here, can force his cousin,<br>By fair and knightly strength, to touch the pillar,<br>He shall enjoy her; the other lose his head,<br>And all his friends; nor shall he grudge to fall,<br>Nor think he dies with interest in this lady.<br>Will this content ye? |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | Yes. Here, cousin [[Arcite]],<br>I am friends again till that hour. |
| [[Arcite|ARCITE]] | I embrace ye. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | (to [[Emilia]])<br>Are you content, sister? |
| [[Emilia|EMILIA]] | Yes, I must, sir,<br>Else both miscarry. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | (to [[Palamon]] and [[Arcite]])<br>Come, shake hands again, then,<br>And take heed, as you are gentlemen, this quarrel<br>[[Sleep]] till the hour prefixed, and hold your course. |
| [[Palamon|PALAMON]] | We dare not fail thee, [[Theseus]]. |
| [[Theseus|THESEUS]] | Come, I’ll give ye<br>Now usage like to princes and to friends.<br>When ye return, who wins I’ll settle here,<br>Who loses, yet I’ll weep upon his bier. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt. ln the act-[[Time|time]] the bush is removed |
