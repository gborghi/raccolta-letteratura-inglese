| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Henry in his bed, attended by the [[Earl of Warwick]], Thomas Duke of [[Clarence]], Humphrey [[Duke of Gloucester]], and others |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Now, lords, if God doth give successful end<br>To this [[debate]] that bleedeth at our doors,<br>We will our youth lead on to higher fields,<br>And draw no swords but what are sanctified.<br>Our navy is addressed, our power collected,<br>Our substitutes in absence [[Well|well]] invested,<br>And everything lies level to our wish;<br>Only we want a little personal strength,<br>And pause us till these rebels now afoot<br>Come underneath the yoke of [[government]]. 10 |
| WARWICK | Both which we [[Doubt|doubt]] not but your majesty<br>Shall soon enjoy. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Humphrey, my son of [[Gloucester]],<br>Where is the Prince your brother? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I think he’s gone to hunt, my lord, at Windsor. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | And how accompanied? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I do not know, my lord. 15 |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Is not his brother Thomas of [[Clarence]] with him? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | No, my good lord, he is in presence here. |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | What would my lord and father? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Nothing but [[Well|well]] to thee, Thomas of [[Clarence]].<br>How chance thou art not with the Prince thy brother?<br>He loves thee, and thou dost neglect him, Thomas.<br>Thou hast a better place in his affection<br>Than all thy brothers. Cherish it, my boy,<br>And noble offices thou mayst effect<br>Of mediation, after I am dead, 25<br>Between his [[Greatness|greatness]] and thy other brethren.<br>Therefore omit him not, blunt not his [[Love|love]],<br>Nor lose the good advantage of his grace<br>By seeming cold or careless of his will;<br>For he is gracious, if he be observed; 30<br>He hath a tear for pity, and a hand<br>Open as day for melting [[Charity|charity]].<br>Yet notwithstanding, being incensed, he is flint,<br>As humorous as [[Winter|winter]], and as sudden<br>As flaws congealed in the [[Spring|spring]] of day.<br>His temper therefore must be [[Well|well]] observed.<br>Chide him for faults, and do it reverently,<br>When you perceive his [[Blood|blood]] inclined to mirth;<br>But being moody, give him line and scope<br>Till that his passions, like a whale on ground,<br>Confound themselves with working. Learn this,<br>Thomas,<br>And thou shalt prove a shelter to thy friends,<br>A hoop of gold to bind thy brothers in,<br>That the united vessel of their [[Blood|blood]],<br>Mingled with venom of suggestion—<br>As force perforce the age will pour it in-<br>Shall never leak, though it do work as strong<br>As aconitum or rash gunpowder. |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | I shall observe him with all care and [[Love|love]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why art thou not at Windsor with him, Thomas? 50 |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | He is not there today; he dines in [[London]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | And how accompanied? Canst thou tell that? |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | With Poins and other his continual followers. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Most subject is the fattest soil to weeds,<br>And he, the noble image of my youth,<br>Is overspread with them; therefore my grief<br>Stretches itself beyond the hour of [[Death|death]].<br>The [[Blood|blood]] weeps from my heart when I do shape<br>In forms imaginary th’unguided days<br>And rotten times that you shall look upon 60<br>When I am sleeping with my ancestors;<br>For when his headstrong riot hath no curb,<br>When rage and hot [[Blood|blood]] are his counsellors,<br>When means and lavish manners meet together,<br>O, with what wings shall his affections fly<br>Towards fronting peril and opposed [[Decay|decay]]? |
| WARWICK | My gracious lord, you look beyond him quite.<br>The Prince but studies his companions,<br>Like a strange tongue, wherein, to gain the language,<br>’Tis needful that the most immodest word<br>Be looked upon and learnt, which once attained,<br>Your highness knows, comes to no further use<br>But to be known and hated; so, like gross terms,<br>The Prince will in the perfectness of [[Time|time]]<br>Cast off his followers, and their [[Memory|memory]]<br>Shall as a pattern or a measure live<br>By which his grace must mete the lives of other,<br>Turning past evils to advantages. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | ’Tis seldom when the bee doth leave her comb<br>In the dead carrion. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Westmorland |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Who’s here? Westmorland? 80 |
| WESTMORLAND | Health to my sovereign, and new happiness<br>Added to that that I am to deliver I<br>[[Prince John]] your son doth kiss your grace’s hand.<br>[[Mowbray]], the Bishop Scrope, [[Hastings]], and all<br>Are brought to the correction of your law.<br>There is not now a [[Rebel|rebel]]’s [[Sword|sword]] unsheathed,<br>But peace puts forth her olive everywhere.<br>The manner how this action hath been borne<br>Here at more leisure may your highness read,<br>With every course in his particular.<br>He gives the [[King]] papers |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O Westmorland, thou art a [[Summer|summer]] bird<br>Which ever in the haunch of [[Winter|winter]] sings<br>The lifting up of day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Harcourt |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Look, here’s more news. |
| HARCOURT | From enemies heaven keep your majesty;<br>And when they stand against you, may they fall<br>As those that I am come to tell you of!<br>The Earl Northumberland and the Lord Bardolph,<br>With a great power of English and of Scots,<br>Are by the sheriff of Yorkshire overthrown.<br>The manner and true order of the fight<br>This packet, please it you, contains at large.<br>He gives the [[King]] papers |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | And wherefore should these good news make me sick?<br>Will fortune never come with both hands full,<br>But write her fair words still in foulest letters?<br>She either gives a stomach and no food—<br>Such are the poor in health—or else a feast,<br>And takes away the stomach—such are the rich,<br>That have abundance and enjoy it not.<br>I should rejoice now at this happy news,<br>And now my sight fails, and my brain is giddy. 110<br>O me! Come near me now; I am much ill.<br>He swoons |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Comfort, your majesty! |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | O my royal father! |
| WESTMORLAND | My sovereign lord, cheer up yourself, look up. |
| WARWICK | Be patient, princes; you do know these fits<br>Are with his highness very ordinary. 115<br>Stand from him, give him air; he’ll straight be [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | No, no, he cannot long hold out these pangs.<br>Th’incessant care and labour of his mind<br>Hath wrought the mure that should confine it in<br>So thin that life looks through and will break out. 120 |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | The people [[Fear|fear]] me, for they do observe<br>Unfathered heirs and loathly births of [[Nature|nature]].<br>The [[Seasons|seasons]] change their manners, as the year<br>Had found some months asleep and leaped them over. |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | The [[River|river]] hath thrice flowed, no ebb between, 125<br>And the old folk, [[Time|time]]’s doting chronicles,<br>Say it did so a little [[Time|time]] before<br>That our great grandsire Edward sicked and died. |
| WARWICK | Speak lower, princes, for the [[King]] recovers. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | This apoplexy will certain be his end. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | I pray you take me up and bear me hence<br>Into some other chamber; softly, pray. |
| *(didascalia)* | The [[King]] is carried over the stage in his bed |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Let there be no noise made, my gentle friends,<br>Unless some dull and favourable hand<br>Will whisper music to my weary spirit. |
| WARWICK | Call for the music in the other room. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one or more. Still music within |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Set me the [[Crown|crown]] upon my pillow here. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Clarence]] takes the [[Crown|crown]] from the [[King]]’s head, and sets it on his pillow |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | His eye is hollow, and he changes much. |
| *(didascalia)* | A noise within |
| WARWICK | Less noise, less noise! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Prince Harry |
| PRINCE HARRY | Who saw the Duke of [[Clarence]]? |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | I am here, brother, full of heaviness. |
| PRINCE HARRY | How now, rain within doors, and none abroad?<br>How doth the [[King]]? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Exceeding ill. |
| PRINCE HARRY | Heard he the good news yet? Tell it him. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | He altered much upon the hearing it. |
| PRINCE HARRY | If he be sick with joy, he’ll recover without physic. WARWICK<br>Not so much noise, my lords! Sweet prince, speak low.<br>The [[King]] your father is disposed to [[Sleep|sleep]]. |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | Let us withdraw into the other room. |
| WARWICK | Will’t please your grace to go along with us? |
| PRINCE HARRY | No, I will sit and watch here by the [[King]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but the [[King]] and Prince Harry |
| PRINCE HARRY | Why doth the [[Crown|crown]] lie there upon his pillow,<br>Being so troublesome a bedfellow?<br>O polished perturbation, golden care,<br>That keep‘st the ports of slumber open wide<br>To many a watchful [[Night|night]]!—[[Sleep]] with it now;<br>Yet not so sound, and half so deeply sweet,<br>As he whose brow with homely biggen bound<br>Snores out the watch of [[Night|night]]. O majesty,<br>When thou dost pinch thy bearer, thou dost sit<br>Like a rich armour worn in heat of day,<br>That scald’st with safety.—By his gates of breath<br>There lies a downy feather which stirs not.<br>Did he suspire, that light and weightless down<br>Perforce must move.—My gracious lord, my father!—<br>This [[Sleep|sleep]] is sound indeed. This is a [[Sleep|sleep]]<br>That from this golden rigol hath divorced<br>So many English kings.—Thy due from me<br>Is [[Tears|tears]] and heavy sorrows of the [[Blood|blood]],<br>Which [[Nature|nature]], [[Love|love]], and filial tenderness<br>Shall, O dear father, pay thee plenteously.<br>My due from thee is this imperial [[Crown|crown]],<br>Which, as immediate from thy place and [[Blood|blood]],<br>Derives itself to me.<br>He puts the [[Crown|crown]] on his head<br>Lo where it sits,<br>Which God shall guard; and put the world’s whole<br>strength<br>Into one giant arm, it shall not force<br>This lineal [[Honour|honour]] from me. This from thee<br>Will I to mine leave, as ’tis left to me. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Music ceases. The [[King]] awakes |

| WARWICK | What would your majesty? How fares your grace? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why did you leave me here [[Alone|alone]], my lords? |
| [[Clarence|CLARENCE]] | We left the Prince my brother here, my liege,<br>Who undertook to sit and watch by you. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | The Prince of Wales? Where is he? Let me see him. |
| WARWICK | This door is open; he is gone this way. |

| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Where is the [[Crown|crown]]? Who took it from my pillow? |
| WARWICK | When we withdrew, my liege, we left it here. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | The Prince hath ta’en it hence. Go seek him out.<br>Is he so hasty that he doth suppose<br>My [[Sleep|sleep]] my [[Death|death]]?<br>Find him, my lord of Warwick; chide him hither. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Warwick |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | This part of his conjoins with my disease,<br>And helps to end me. See, sons, what things you are,<br>How quickly [[Nature|nature]] falls into revolt<br>When gold becomes her object!<br>For this the foolish over-careful fathers<br>Have broke their [[Sleep|sleep]] with thoughts, their brains with<br>care,<br>Their bones with industry; for this they have<br>Engrossed and piled up the cankered heaps<br>Of strange-achieved gold; for this they have<br>Been thoughtful to invest their sons with arts<br>And martial exercises; when, like the bee<br>Culling from every flower the virtuous sweets,<br>Our thighs packed with wax, our mouths with honey,<br>We bring it to the hive; and, like the [[Bees|bees]], 206<br>Are murdered for our pains. This bitter taste<br>Yields his engrossments to the ending father. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Earl of Warwick]] |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Now where is he that will not stay so long<br>Till his friend sickness have determined me? |
| WARWICK | My lord, I found the Prince in the next room,<br>Washing with kindly [[Tears|tears]] his gentle cheeks<br>With such a deep demeanour, in great sorrow,<br>That tyranny, which never quaffed but [[Blood|blood]],<br>Would, by beholding him, have washed his knife 215<br>With gentle eye-drops. He is coming hither. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | But wherefore did he take away the [[Crown|crown]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Prince Harry with the [[Crown|crown]] |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Lo where he comes.—Come hither to me, Harry.<br>(To the others) Depart the chamber; leave us here<br>[[Alone|alone]]. Exeunt all but the [[King]] and Prince Harry |
| PRINCE HARRY | I never thought to hear you speak again. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Thy wish was father, Harry, to that thought.<br>I stay too long by thee, I weary thee.<br>Dost thou so hunger for mine empty chair<br>That thou wilt needs invest thee with my honours<br>Before thy hour be ripe? O foolish youth,<br>Thou seek‘st the [[Greatness|greatness]] that will overwhelm thee!<br>Stay but a little, for my cloud of dignity<br>Is held from falling with so weak a [[Wind|wind]]<br>That it will quickly drop. My day is dim.<br>Thou hast stol’n that which after some few hours<br>Were thine without offence, and at my [[Death|death]]<br>Thou hast sealed up my expectation.<br>Thy life did manifest thou loved’st me not,<br>And thou wilt have me die assured of it.<br>Thou hid’st a thousand daggers in thy thoughts,<br>Whom thou hast whetted on thy stony heart<br>To stab at half an hour of my life.<br>What, canst thou not forbear me half an hour?<br>Then get thee gone and dig my [[Grave|grave]] thyself,<br>And bid the merry [[Bells|bells]] ring to thine ear<br>That thou art crowned, not that I am dead.<br>Let all the [[Tears|tears]] that should bedew my hearse<br>Be drops of balm to sanctify thy head.<br>Only compound me with forgotten [[Dust|dust]].<br>Give that which gave thee life unto the worms.<br>Pluck down my officers, break my decrees;<br>For now a [[Time|time]] is come to mock at form—<br>Harry the Fifth is crowned. Up, [[Vanity|vanity]]!<br>Down, royal state! All you sage counsellors, hence!<br>And to the English [[Court|court]] assemble now<br>From every region, apes of idleness!<br>Now, neighbour confines, purge you of your scum I<br>Have you a ruffian that will swear, drink, [[Dance|dance]],<br>Revel the [[Night|night]], rob, murder, and commit<br>The oldest sins the newest kind of ways?<br>Be happy; he will trouble you no more.<br>England shall [[Double|double]] gild his treble [[Guilt|guilt]],<br>England shall give him office, [[Honour|honour]], might;<br>For the fifth Harry from curbed licence plucks<br>The muzzle of restraint, and the wild dog 260<br>Shall flesh his tooth on every [[Innocent|innocent]].<br>O my poor kingdom, sick with civil blows!<br>When that my care could not withhold thy riots,<br>What wilt thou do when riot is thy care?<br>O, thou wilt be a wilderness again,<br>Peopled with wolves, thy old inhabitants. |
| PRINCE HARRY | O pardon me, my liege! But for my [[Tears|tears]],<br>The moist impediments unto my speech,<br>I had forestalled this dear and deep rebuke<br>Ere you with grief had spoke and I had heard<br>The course of it so far. There is your [[Crown|crown]]; |
| *(didascalia)* | He returns the [[Crown|crown]] and kneels |
| PRINCE HARRY | And He that wears the [[Crown|crown]] immortally<br>Long guard it yours! If I affect it more<br>Than as your [[Honour|honour]] and as your renown,<br>Let me no more from this obedience rise,<br>Which my most true and inward duteous spirit<br>Teacheth this prostrate and exterior bending.<br>God witness with me, when I here came in<br>And found no course of breath within your majesty,<br>How cold it struck my heart. If I do feign,<br>O, let me in my present wildness die,<br>And never live to show th‘incredulous world<br>The noble change that I have purposed.<br>Coming to look on you, thinking you dead,<br>And dead almost, my liege, to think you were,<br>I spake unto this [[Crown|crown]] as having sense,<br>And thus upbraided it: ‘The care on thee depending<br>Hath fed upon the [[Body|body]] of my father;<br>Therefore thou best of gold art worst of gold.<br>Other, less fine in carat, is more precious,<br>Preserving life in medicine potable;<br>But thou, most fine, most honoured, most renowned,<br>Hast eat thy bearer up.’ Thus, my royal liege,<br>Accusing it, I put it on my head,<br>To try with it, as with an enemy<br>That had before my face murdered my father,<br>The quarrel of a true inheritor.<br>But if it did infect my [[Blood|blood]] with joy<br>Or swell my thoughts to any strain of [[Pride|pride]],<br>If any [[Rebel|rebel]] or vain spirit of mine<br>Did with the least affection of a welcome<br>Give entertainment to the might of it,<br>Let God for ever keep it from my head,<br>And make me as the poorest vassal is,<br>That doth with awe and terror kneel to it. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O my son,<br>God put it in thy mind to take it hence,<br>That thou mightst win the more thy father’s [[Love|love]],<br>Pleading so wisely in excuse of it!<br>Come hither, Harry; sit thou by my bed,<br>And hear, I think, the very latest counsel<br>That ever I shall breathe. |
| *(didascalia)* | Prince Harry rises from kneeling and sits by the bed |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | God knows, my son,<br>By what bypaths and indirect crook’d ways<br>I met this [[Crown|crown]]; and I myself know [[Well|well]]<br>How troublesome it sat upon my head.<br>To thee it shall descend with better quiet,<br>Better opinion, better confirmation;<br>For all the soil of the achievement goes<br>With me into the earth. It seemed in me<br>But as an [[Honour|honour]] snatched with boist‘rous hand;<br>And I had many living to upbraid<br>My gain of it by their assistances,<br>Which daily grew to quarrel and to bloodshed,<br>Wounding supposed peace. All these bold fears<br>Thou seest with peril I have answerèd;<br>For all my reign hath been but as a scene<br>Acting that argument. And now my [[Death|death]]<br>Changes the mood, for what in me was purchased<br>Falls upon thee in a more fairer sort,<br>So thou the garland wear’st successively.<br>Yet though thou stand‘st more sure than I could do,<br>Thou art not firm enough, since griefs are green,<br>And all thy friends—which thou must make thy<br>friends—<br>Have but their stings and teeth newly ta’en out,<br>By whose fell working I was first advanced,<br>And by whose power I [[Well|well]] might lodge a [[Fear|fear]]<br>To be again displaced; which to avoid<br>I cut them off, and had a purpose now<br>To lead out many to the Holy Land,<br>Lest rest and lying still might make them look<br>Too near unto my state. Therefore, my Harry,<br>Be it thy course to busy giddy minds<br>With foreign quarrels, that action hence borne out<br>May waste the [[Memory|memory]] of the former days.<br>More would I, but my lungs are wasted so<br>That strength of speech is utterly denied me.<br>How I came by the [[Crown|crown]], O God forgive,<br>And grant it may with thee in true peace live! |
| PRINCE HARRY | My gracious liege,<br>You won it, wore it, kept it, gave it me;<br>Then plain and right must my possession be,<br>Which I with more than with a common [[Pain|pain]]<br>’Gainst all the world will rightfully maintain. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Prince John]] of Lancaster followed by the [[Earl of Warwick]] and others |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Look, look, here comes my John of Lancaster. |
| [[Prince John|PRINCE JOHN]] | Health, peace, and happiness to my royal father! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Thou bring’st me happiness and peace, son John;<br>But health, alack, with youthful wings is flown<br>From this bare withered trunk. Upon thy sight<br>My worldly business makes a period.<br>Where is my lord of Warwick? |
| PRINCE HARRY | My lord of Warwick! |
| *(didascalia)* | Warwick comes forward to the [[King]] |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Doth any name particular belong<br>Unto the lodging where I first did swoon? |
| WARWICK | ’Tis called Jerusalem, my noble lord. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Laud be to God! Even there my life must end.<br>It hath been prophesied to me many years<br>I should not die but in Jerusalem,<br>Which vainly I supposed the Holy Land;<br>But bear me to that chamber; there I’ll lie;<br>In that Jerusalem shall Harry die. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt, bearing the [[King]] in his bed |
