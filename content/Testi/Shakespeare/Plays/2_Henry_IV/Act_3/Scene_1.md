| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Henry in his nightgown, with a page |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (giving letters)<br>Go call the Earls of Surrey and of Warwick.<br>But ere they come, bid them o’er-read these letters<br>And [[Well|well]] consider of them. Make good [[Speed|speed]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit page |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | How many thousand of my poorest subjects<br>Are at this hour asleep? O [[Sleep|sleep]], O gentle [[Sleep|sleep]],<br>[[Nature]]’s soft [[Nurse|nurse]], how have I frighted thee,<br>That thou no more wilt weigh my eyelids down<br>And steep my senses in forgetfulness?<br>Why rather, [[Sleep|sleep]], liest thou in smoky cribs,<br>Upon uneasy pallets stretching thee,<br>And hushed with buzzing [[Night|night]]-flies to thy slumber,<br>Than in the perfumed chambers of the great,<br>Under the canopies of costly state,<br>And lulled with sound of sweetest melody?<br>O thou dull god, why li‘st thou with the vile 15<br>In loathsome beds, and leav’st the kingly couch<br>A watch-case, or a common ’larum-bell?<br>Wilt thou upon the high and giddy mast<br>Seal up the [[Ship|ship]]-boy’s eyes, and rock his brains<br>In cradle of the rude imperious surge,<br>And in the visitation of the winds,<br>Who take the ruffian billows by the top,<br>Curling their monstrous heads, and hanging them<br>With deafing clamour in the slippery clouds,<br>That, with the hurly, [[Death|death]] itself awakes?<br>Canst thou, O partial [[Sleep|sleep]], give thy repose<br>To the wet sea-boy in an hour so rude,<br>And in the calmest and most stillest [[Night|night]],<br>With all appliances and means to boot,<br>Deny it to a [[King|king]]? Then happy low, lie down.<br>Uneasy lies the head that wears a [[Crown|crown]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earls of Warwick and Surrey |
| WARWICK | Many good morrows to your majesty! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Is it good morrow, lords? |
| WARWICK | ’Tis one o’[[Clock|clock]], and past. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why then, good morrow to you all, my lords.<br>Have you read o’er the letter that I sent you? |
| WARWICK | We have, my liege. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Then you perceive the [[Body|body]] of our kingdom,<br>How foul it is, what rank diseases grow,<br>And with what danger near the heart of it. |
| WARWICK | It is but as a [[Body|body]] yet distempered,<br>Which to his former strength may be restored<br>With good advice and little medicine.<br>My lord Northumberland will soon be cooled. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O God, that one might read the book of fate,<br>And see the revolution of the times<br>Make [[Mountains|mountains]] level, and the continent,<br>Weary of solid firmness, melt itself<br>Into the sea; and other times to see<br>The beachy girdle of the ocean<br>Too wide for Neptune’s hips; how chance’s mocks<br>And changes fill the cup of alteration<br>With divers liquors!‘Tis not ten years gone<br>Since [[Richard]] and Northumberland, great friends,<br>Did feast together; and in two year after<br>Were they at wars. It is but eight years since<br>This Percy was the man nearest my [[Soul|soul]],<br>Who like a brother toiled in my affairs,<br>And laid his [[Love|love]] and life under my foot,<br>Yea, for my sake, even to the eyes of [[Richard]]<br>Gave him defiance. But which of you was by—<br>(To Warwick) You, cousin Neville, as I may<br>remember—<br>When [[Richard]], with his eye brimful of [[Tears|tears]],<br>Then checked and rated by Northumberland,<br>Did speak these words, now proved a prophecy?—<br>‘Northumberland, thou ladder by the which<br>My cousin Bolingbroke ascends my throne’—<br>Though then, God knows, I had no such intent,<br>But that necessity so bowed the state<br>That I and [[Greatness|greatness]] were compelled to kiss—<br>‘The [[Time|time]] shall come’—thus did he follow it—<br>‘The [[Time|time]] will come that foul sin, gathering head,<br>Shall break into corruption’; so went on,<br>Foretelling this same [[Time|time]]’s condition,<br>And the division of our amity. |
| WARWICK | There is a [[history]] in all men’s lives<br>Figuring the natures of the times deceased;<br>The which observed, a man may prophesy,<br>With a near aim, of the main chance of things<br>As yet not come to life, who in their seeds<br>And weak beginnings lie intreasurèd. 80<br>Such things become the hatch and brood of [[Time|time]];<br>And by the necessary form of this<br>[[King]] [[Richard]] might create a perfect guess<br>That great Northumberland, then false to him,<br>Would of that seed grow to a greater falseness,<br>Which should not find a ground to root upon<br>Unless on you. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Are these things then necessities?<br>Then let us meet them like necessities;<br>And that same word even now cries out on us.<br>They say the Bishop and Northumberland<br>Are fifty thousand strong. |
| WARWICK | It cannot be, my lord.<br>Rumour doth [[Double|double]], like the voice and echo,<br>The numbers of the feared. Please it your grace<br>To go to bed? Upon my [[Soul|soul]], my lord,<br>The powers that you already have sent forth<br>Shall bring this prize in very easily.<br>To comfort you the more, I have received<br>A certain instance that Glyndwr is dead.<br>Your majesty hath been this fortnight ill,<br>And these unseasoned hours perforce must add<br>Unto your sickness. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | I will take your counsel.<br>And were these inward wars once out of hand,<br>We would, dear lords, unto the Holy Land. Exeunt |
