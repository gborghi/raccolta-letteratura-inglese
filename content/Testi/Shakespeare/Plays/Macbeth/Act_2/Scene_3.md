| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Porter. Knocking within |
| PORTER | Here’s a knocking indeed! If a man were porter<br>of hell-gate he should have old turning the key.<br>Knock within<br><br>Knock, knock, knock. Who’s there, i‘th’ name of<br>Beelzebub? Here’s a farmer that hanged himself on<br>th’expectation of plenty. Come in time! Have napkins<br>enough about you; here you’ll sweat for’t.<br>Knock within<br><br>Knock, knock. Who’s there, in th‘other devil’s name?<br>Faith, here’s an equivocator that could swear in both<br>the scales against either scale, who committed treason<br>enough for God’s sake, yet could not equivocate to<br>heaven. O, come in, equivocator.<br>Knock within<br><br>Knock, knock, knock. Who’s there? ’Faith, here’s an<br>English tailor come hither for stealing out of a French<br>hose. Come in, tailor. Here you may roast your goose.<br>Knock within<br><br>Knock, knock. Never at quiet. What are you?—But this<br>place is too cold for hell. I’ll devil-porter it no further.<br>I had thought to have let in some of all professions<br>that go the primrose way to th’everlasting bonfire.<br>Knock within<br><br>Anon, anon!<br>He opens the gate<br><br>I pray you remember the porter. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Macduff and Lennox |
| MACDUFF | Was it so late, friend, ere you went to bed<br>That you do lie so late? |
| PORTER | Faith, sir, we were carousing till the second cock, and drink, sir, is a great provoker of three things. |
| MACDUFF | What three things does drink especially provoke? |
| PORTER | Marry, sir, nose-painting, sleep, and urine. Lechery, sir, it provokes and unprovokes: it provokes the desire but it takes away the performance. Therefore much drink may be said to be an equivocator with lechery: it makes him and it mars him; it sets him on and it takes him off; it persuades him and disheartens him, makes him stand to and not stand to; in conclusion, equivocates him in a sleep, and, giving him the lie, leaves him. |
| MACDUFF | I believe drink gave thee the lie last night. |
| PORTER | That it did, sir, i’the very throat on me; but I requited him for his lie, and, I think, being too strong for him, though he took up my legs sometime, yet I made a shift to cast him. |
| MACDUFF | Is thy master stirring? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Macbeth |
| MACDUFF | Our knocking has awaked him: here he comes. [Exit Porter] |
| LENNOX | (to Macbeth)<br>Good morrow, noble sir. |
| MACBETH | Good morrow, both. |
| MACDUFF | Is the King stirring, worthy thane? |
| MACBETH | Not yet. |
| MACDUFF | He did command me to call timely on him.<br>I have almost slipped the hour. |
| MACBETH | I’ll bring you to him. |
| MACDUFF | I know this is a joyful trouble to you,<br>But yet ’tis one. |
| MACBETH | The labour we delight in physics pain.<br>This is the door. |
| MACDUFF | I’ll make so bold to call,<br>For ’tis my limited service. Exit Macduff |
| LENNOX | Goes the King hence today? |
| MACBETH | He does; he did appoint so. |
| LENNOX | The night has been unruly. Where we lay<br>Our chimneys were blown down, and, as they say,<br>Lamentings heard i’th’ air, strange screams of death,<br>And prophesying with accents terrible<br>Of dire combustion and confused events<br>New-hatched to th’ woeful time. The obscure bird<br>Clamoured the livelong night. Some say the earth<br>Was feverous and did shake. |
| MACBETH | ’Twas a rough night. |
| LENNOX | My young remembrance cannot parallel<br>A fellow to it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Macduff |
| MACDUFF | O horror, horror, horror!<br>Tongue nor heart cannot conceive nor name thee. |
| MACBETH | and LENNOX What’s the matter? |
| MACDUFF | Confusion now hath made his masterpiece.<br>Most sacrilegious murder hath broke ope<br>The Lord’s anointed temple and stole thence<br>The life o’th’ building. |
| MACBETH | What is’t you say—the life? |
| LENNOX | Mean you his majesty? |
| MACDUFF | Approach the chamber and destroy your sight<br>With a new Gorgon. Do not bid me speak.<br>See, and then speak yourselves. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Macbeth and Lennox |
| MACDUFF | Awake, awake!<br>Ring the alarum bell. Murder and treason!<br>Banquo and Donalbain, Malcolm, awake!<br>Shake off this downy sleep, death’s counterfeit,<br>And look on death itself. Up, up, and see<br>The great doom’s image. Malcolm, Banquo,<br>As from your graves rise up, and walk like sprites<br>To countenance this horror.<br>Bell rings. Enter Lady Macbeth |
| LADY MACBETH | What’s the business,<br>That such a hideous trumpet calls to parley<br>The sleepers of the house? Speak, speak. |
| MACDUFF | O gentle lady,<br>’Tis not for you to hear what I can speak.<br>The repetition in a woman’s ear<br>Would murder as it fell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Banquo |
| MACDUFF | O Banquo, Banquo,<br>Our royal master’s murdered! |
| LADY MACBETH | Woe,<br>alas—<br>What, in our house? |
| BANQUO | Too cruel anywhere.<br>Dear Duff, I prithee contradict thyself,<br>And say it is not so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Macbeth, Lennox, [and Ross] |
| MACBETH | Had I but died an hour before this chance<br>I had lived a blessed time, for from this instant<br>There’s nothing serious in mortality.<br>All is but toys. Renown and grace is dead.<br>The wine of life is drawn, and the mere lees<br>Is left this vault to brag of. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Malcolm and Donalbain |
| DONALBAIN | What is amiss? |
| MACBETH | You are, and do not know’t.<br>The spring, the head, the fountain of your blood<br>Is stopped, the very source of it is stopped. |
| MACDUFF | Your royal father’s murdered. |
| MALCOLM | O, by whom? |
| LENNOX | Those of his chamber, as it seemed, had done’t.<br>Their hands and faces were all badged with blood,<br>So were their daggers, which, unwiped, we found<br>Upon their pillows. They stared and were distracted.<br>No man’s life was to be trusted with them. |
| MACBETH | O, yet I do repent me of my fury<br>That I did kill them. |
| MACDUFF | Wherefore did you so? |
| MACBETH | Who can be wise, amazed, temp‘rate and furious,<br>Loyal and neutral in a moment? No man.<br>Th’expedition of my violent love<br>Outran the pauser, reason. Here lay Duncan,<br>His silver skin laced with his golden blood,<br>And his gashed stabs looked like a breach in nature<br>For ruin’s wasteful entrance; there the murderers,<br>Steeped in the colours of their trade, their daggers<br>Unmannerly breeched with gore. Who could refrain,<br>That had a heart to love, and in that heart<br>Courage to make ’s love known? |
| LADY MACBETH | Help me hence, ho! |
| MACDUFF | Look to the lady. |
| MALCOLM | (aside to Donalbain)<br>Why do we hold our tongues,<br>That most may claim this argument for ours? |
| DONALBAIN | (aside to Malcolm)<br>What should be spoken here, where our fate,<br>Hid in an auger-hole, may rush and seize us?<br>Let’s away. Our tears are not yet brewed. |
| MALCOLM | (aside to Donalbain)<br>Nor our strong sorrow<br>Upon the foot of motion. |
| BANQUO | Look to the lady; Exit Lady Macbeth, attended<br>And when we have our naked frailties hid,<br>That suffer in exposure, let us meet<br>And question this most bloody piece of work,<br>To know it further. Fears and scruples shake us.<br>In the great hand of God I stand, and thence<br>Against the undivulged pretence I fight<br>Of treasonous malice. |
| MACDUFF | And SO do I. |
| SO | all. |
| MACBETH | Let’s briefly put on manly readiness,<br>And meet i’th’ hall together. |
| ALL | Well contented. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Malcolm and Donalbain |
| MALCOLM | What will you do? Let’s not consort with them.<br>To show an unfelt sorrow is an office<br>Which the false man does easy. I’ll to England. |
| DONALBAIN | To Ireland, I. Our separated fortune<br>Shall keep us both the safer. Where we are<br>There’s daggers in men’s smiles. The nea’er in blood,<br>The nearer bloody. |
| MALCOLM | This murderous shaft that’s shot<br>Hath not yet lighted, and our safest way<br>Is to avoid the aim. Therefore to horse,<br>And let us not be dainty of leave-taking,<br>But shift away. There’s warrant in that theft<br>Which steals itself when there’s no mercy left. Exeunt |
