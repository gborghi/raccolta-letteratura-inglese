| *(didascalia)* | Enter Varro’s two Servants, meeting others, all Servants of [[Timon]]’s creditors, to wait for his coming out. Then enter Servants of [[Lucius]], Titus, and Hortensius |
| *(didascalia)* | VARRO’S FIRST SERVANT |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Well]] met; good morrow, Titus and Hortensius. |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | The like to you, kind Varro. |
| HORTENSIUS’ SERVANT | [[Lucius]], what, do we meet together? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Ay, and I think one business does command us all,<br>For mine is money. |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | So is theirs and ours. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servant of  Philotus |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | And Sir Philotus too! |
| PHILOTUS’ SERVANT | Good day at once. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Welcome, good brother. What do you think the hour? |
| PHILOTUS’ SERVANT | Labouring for nine. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | So much? |
| PHILOTUS’ SERVANT | Is not my lord seen yet? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Not yet. |
| PHILOTUS’ SERVANT | I [[Wonder|wonder]] on’t; he was wont to shine at seven. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Ay, but the days are waxed shorter with him.<br>You must consider that a prodigal course<br>Is like the sun‘s,<br>But not, like his, recoverable. I [[Fear|fear]]<br>’Tis deepest [[Winter|winter]] in Lord [[Timon]]’s purse; that is,<br>One may reach deep enough, and yet find little. |
| PHILOTUS’ SERVANT | I am of your [[Fear|fear]] for that. |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | I’ll show you how t’observe a strange event.<br>Your lord sends now for money? |
| HORTENSIUS’ SERVANT | Most true, he does. |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | And he wears jewels now of [[Timon]]’s gift,<br>For which I wait for money. |
| HORTENSIUS’ SERVANT | It is against my heart. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Mark how strange it shows.<br>[[Timon]] in this should pay more than he owes,<br>And e‘en as if your lord should wear rich jewels<br>And send for money for ’em. |
| HORTENSIUS’ SERVANT | I’m weary of this charge, the gods can witness.<br>I know my lord hath spent of [[Timon]]’s wealth,<br>And now ingratitude makes it worse than stealth. |
| VARRO’S FIRST SERVANT | Yes; mine’s three thousand crowns. What’s yours? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Five thousand, mine. |
| VARRO’S FIRST SERVANT | ’is much deep, and it should seem by th’ sum<br>Your master’s confidence was above mine,<br>Else surely his had equalled. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Flaminius |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | One of Lord [[Timon]]’s men. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Flaminius! Sir, a word. Pray, is my lord 36<br>Ready to come forth? |
| FLAMINIUS | No, indeed he is not. |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | We attend his lordship.<br>Pray signify so much. |
| FLAMINIUS | I need not tell<br>Him that; he knows you are too diligent. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Flavius]], muffled in a cloak |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Ha, is not that his steward muffled so?<br>He goes away in a cloud. Call him, call him. |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | (to [[Flavius]]) Do you hear, sir? |
| VARRO’S SECOND SERVANT | (to [[Flavius]]) By your leave, sir. |
| [[Flavius|FLAVIUS]] | What do ye ask of me, my friend? |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | We wait for certain money here, sir. |
| [[Flavius|FLAVIUS]] | Ay,<br>If money were as certain as your waiting,<br>‘Twere sure enough.<br>Why then preferred you not your sums and bills<br>When your false masters ate of my lord’s meat?<br>Then they could smile and fawn upon his debts,<br>And take down th’int’est into their glutt’nous maws.<br>You do yourselves but wrong to stir me up.<br>Let me pass quietly.<br>Believe’, my lord and I have made an end.<br>I have no more to reckon, he to spend. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Ay, but this answer will not serve. |
| [[Flavius|FLAVIUS]] | If ‘twill not serve ’tis not so base as you,<br>For you serve knaves. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| VARRO’S FIRST SERVANT | How? What does his cashiered worship mutter? |
| VARRO’S SECOND SERVANT | No matter what; he’s poor, and that’s [[Revenge|revenge]] enough. Who can speak broader than he that has no [[house]] to put his head in? Such may rail against great buildings. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Servilius |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | O, here’s Servilius. Now we shall know some answer. |
| SERVILIUS | If I might beseech you, gentlemen, to repair some other hour, I should derive much from’t; for, take’t of my [[Soul|soul]], my lord leans wondrously to discontent. His comfortable temper has forsook him. He’s much out of health, and keeps his chamber. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Many do keep their chambers are not sick,<br>And if it be so far beyond his health<br>Methinks he should the sooner pay his debts<br>And make a clear way to the gods. |
| SERVILIUS | Good gods! |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | We cannot take this for an answer, sir. |
| FLAMINIUS | (Within)<br>Servilius, helpl My lord, my lord! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Timon]] in a rage |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | What, are my doors opposed against my passage?<br>Have I been ever free, and must my [[house]]<br>Be my retentive enemy, my jail?<br>The place which I have feasted, does it now,<br>Like all mankind, show me an iron heart? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Put in now, Titus. |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | My lord, here is my bill. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Here’s mine. |
| *(didascalia)* | HORTENSUS’ SERVANT And mine, my lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | VARRO’S FIRST and SECOND SERVANTS And ours, my lord. |
| PHILOTUS’ SERVANT | All our bills. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Knock me down with ’em, cleave me to the girdle. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Alas, my lord. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Cut my heart in sums. 90 |
| TITUS’ SERVANT | Mine fifty talents. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Tell out my [[Blood|blood]]. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]]’ SERVANT | Five thousand crowns, my lord. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Five thousand drops pays that. What yours? And yours? |
| VARRO’S FIRST SERVANT | My lord— |
| VARRO’S SECOND SERVANT | My lord— |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Tear me, take me, and the gods fall upon you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| HORTENSIUS’ SERVANT | [[Faith]], I perceive our masters may throw their caps at their money. These debts may [[Well|well]] be called desperate ones, for a madman owes ’em. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
