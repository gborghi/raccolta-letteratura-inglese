| *(didascalia)* | Enter Poet and Painter |
| PAINTER | As I took note of the place, it cannot be far where he abides. |
| POET | What’s to be thought of him? Does the rumour hold for true that he’s so full of gold? |
| PAINTER | Certain. [[Alcibiades]] reports it. Phrynia and Timandra had gold of him. He likewise enriched poor straggling soldiers with great quantity. ’Tis said he gave unto his steward a mighty sum. |
| POET | Then this breaking of his has been but a try for his friends? |
| PAINTER | Nothing else. You shall see him a palm in Athens again, and flourish with the highest. Therefore ’tis not amiss we tender our loves to him in this supposed distress of his. It will show honestly in us, and is very likely to load our purposes with what they travail for, if it be a just and true report that goes of his having. |
| POET | What have you now to present unto him? |
| PAINTER | Nothing at this [[Time|time]], but my visitation; only I will promise him an excellent piece. |
| POET | I must serve him so too, tell him of an intent that’s coming toward him. |
| PAINTER | Good as the best. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Timon]] from his [[Cave|cave]], unobserved |
| PAINTER | Promising is the very air o’th’ [[Time|time]]; it opens the eyes of expectation. Performance is ever the duller for his act, and but in the plainer and simpler kind of people the deed of saying is quite out of use. To promise is most courtly and fashionable. Performance is a kind of will or testament which argues a great sickness in his judgement that makes it. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | (aside) Excellent workman, thou canst not paint a man so bad as is thyself. |
| POET | (to Painter) I am thinking what I shall say I have provided for him. It must be a personating of himself, a [[Satire|satire]] against the softness of prosperity, with a discovery of the infinite flatteries that follow youth and opulency. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | (aside) Must thou needs stand for a [[Villain|villain]] in thine own work? Wilt thou whip thine own faults in other men? Do so; I have gold for thee. |
| POET | (to Painter) Nay, let’s seek him. Then do we sin against our own estate When we may profit meet and come too late. |
| PAINTER | True.<br>When the day serves, before black-cornered [[Night|night]],<br>Find what thou want’st by free and offered fight.<br>Come. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | (aside)<br>I’ll meet you at the turn. What a god’s gold,<br>That he is worshipped in a baser temple<br>Than where swine feed!<br>‘Tis thou that rigg’st the barque and plough’st the foam,<br>Settlest admired reverence in a slave.<br>To thee be worship, and thy saints for aye<br>Be crowned with plagues, that thee [[Alone|alone]] obey.<br>Fit I meet them.<br>He comes forward to them |
| POET | Hail, worthy [[Timon]]! |
| PAINTER | Our late noble master! |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Have I once lived to see two honest men? |
| POET | Sir, having often of your open bounty tasted,<br>Hearing you were retired, your friends fall’n off,<br>Whose thankless natures, O abhorred spirits,<br>Not all the whips of heaven are large enough—<br>What, to you,<br>Whose star-like nobleness gave life and infiuence<br>To their whole being! I am rapt, and cannot cover<br>The monstrous bulk of this ingratitude<br>With any size of words. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Let it go naked; men may see’t the better.<br>You that are honest, by being what you are<br>Make them best seen and known. |
| PAINTER | He and myself<br>Have travelled in the great show’r of your gifts,<br>And sweetly felt it. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Ay, you are honest men. |
| PAINTER | We are hither come to offer you our service. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Most honest men. Why, how shall I requite you?<br>Can you eat roots and drink cold [[Water|water]]? No. |
| POET | and PAINTER<br>What we can do we’ll do to do you service. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | You’re honest men. You’ve heard that I have gold,<br>I am sure you have. Speak [[Truth|truth]]; you’re honest men. |
| PAINTER | So it is said, my noble lord, but therefor<br>Came not my friend nor I. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Good honest men. (To Painter) Thou draw‘st a<br>counterfeit<br>Best in all Athens; thou’rt indeed the best;<br>Thou counterfeit’st most lively. |
| PAINTER | So so, my lord. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | E‘en so, sir, as I say. (To Poet) And for thy fiction,<br>Why, thy verse swells with stuff so fine and smooth<br>That thou art even natural in thine art.<br>But for all this, my honest-natured friends,<br>I must needs say you have a little fault.<br>Marry, ’tis not monstrous in you, neither wish I<br>You take much pains to mend. |
| POET | and PAINTER<br>Beseech your [[Honour|honour]]<br>To make it known to us. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | You’ll take it ill. |
| POET | and PAINTER Most thankfully, my lord. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Will you indeed? |
| POET | and PAINTER [[Doubt]] it not, worthy lord. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | There’s never a one of you but trusts a knave<br>That mightily deceives you. |
| POET | and PAINTER Do we, my lord? |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Ay, and you hear him cog, see him dissemble,<br>Know his gross patchery, [[Love|love]] him, feed him,<br>Keep in your bosom; yet remain assured<br>That he’s a made-up [[Villain|villain]]. |
| PAINTER | I know none such, my lord. |
| POET | Nor I. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | Look you, I [[Love|love]] you [[Well|well]]. I’ll give you gold,<br>Rid me these villains from your companies.<br>Hang them or stab them, drown them in a draught,<br>Confound them by some course, and come to me,<br>I’ll give you gold enough. |
| POET | and PAINTER<br>Name them, my lord, let’s know them. |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | You that way and you this—but two in company-<br>Each man apart, all single and [[Alone|alone]],<br>Yet an arch-[[Villain|villain]] keeps him company. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Painter If where thou art two villains shall not be, |
| *(didascalia)* | Come not near him. To Poet If thou wouldst not |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | reside<br>But where one [[Villain|villain]] is, then him abandon. |
| *(didascalia)* | Hence; pack! Striking him There’s gold. You came |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | for gold, ye slaves. |
| *(didascalia)* | Striking Painter You have work for me; there’s |
| [[Timon|TIMON]] | payment. Hence! |
| *(didascalia)* | Striking Poet You are an alchemist; make gold of that. |
| *(didascalia)* | Out, rascal [[dogs]]! Exeunt Poet and Painter one way, |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Timon]] into his [[Cave|cave]] |
