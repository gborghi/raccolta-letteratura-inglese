| *(didascalia)* | Enter Flavius, with two or three Servants |
| FIRST SERVANT | Hear you, master steward, where’s our master?<br>Are we undone, cast off, nothing remaining? |
| FLAVIUS | Alack, my fellows, what should I say to you?<br>Let me be recorded: by the righteous gods,<br>I am as poor as you. |
| FIRST SERVANT | Such a house broke,<br>So noble a master fall’n? All gone, and not<br>One friend to take his fortune by the arm<br>And go along with him? |
| SECOND SERVANT | As we do turn our backs<br>From our companion thrown into his grave,<br>So his familiars to his buried fortunes<br>Slink all away, leave their false vows with him<br>Like empty purses picked; and his poor self,<br>A dedicated beggar to the air,<br>With his disease of all-shunned poverty,<br>Walks like contempt alone. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter other Servants |
| SECOND SERVANT | More of our fellows. |
| FLAVIUS | All broken implements of a ruined house. |
| THIRD SERVANT | Yet do our hearts wear Timon’s livery.<br>That see I by our faces. We are fellows still,<br>Serving alike in sorrow. Leaked is our barque,<br>And we, poor mates, stand on the dying deck<br>Hearing the surges’ threat. We must all part<br>Into this sea of air. |
| FLAVIUS | Good fellows all,<br>The latest of my wealth I’ll share amongst you.<br>Wherever we shall meet, for Timon’s sake<br>Let’s yet be fellows. Let’s shake our heads and say,<br>As ‘twere a knell unto our master’s fortunes,<br>’We have seen better days.’<br>He gives them money<br>Let each take some.<br>Nay, put out all your hands. Not one word more.<br>Thus part we rich in sorrow, parting poor.<br>They embrace, and the Servants part several ways<br>O, the fierce wretchedness that glory brings us!<br>Who would not wish to be from wealth exempt,<br>Since riches point to misery and contempt?<br>Who would be so mocked with glory, or to live<br>But in a dream of friendship,<br>To have his pomp and all what state compounds<br>But only painted like his varnished friends?<br>Poor honest lord, brought low by his own heart,<br>Undone by goodness! Strange, unusual blood<br>When man’s worst sin is he does too much good!<br>Who then dares to be half so kind again?<br>For bounty, that makes gods, does still mar men.<br>My dearest lord, blessed to be most accursed,<br>Rich only to be wretched, thy great fortunes<br>Are made thy chief afflictions. Alas, kind lord!<br>He’s flung in rage from this ingrateful seat<br>Of monstrous friends;<br>Nor has he with him to supply his life,<br>Or that which can command it.<br>I’ll follow and enquire him out.<br>I’ll ever serve his mind with my best will.<br>Whilst I have gold I’ll be his steward still. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
