| *(didascalia)* | Enter Edward Prince of Wales with his prisoners: jean [[King of France]] and the [[Dauphin]], and all with ensigns spread. Retreat sounded |
| PRINCE OF WALES | (to the [[King]] and then the [[Dauphin]])<br>Now, Jean in France, and lately Jean of France,<br>Thy bloody ensigns are my captive colours—<br>And you, high-vaunting Charles of Normandy,<br>That once today sent me a [[Horse|horse]] to fly,<br>Are now the subjects of my clemency. 5<br>Fie, lords, is it not a shame that English boys,<br>Whose early days are yet not worth a beard,<br>Should in the bosom of your kingdom, thus,<br>One against twenty, beat you up together? |
| [[King of France|KING OF FRANCE]] | Thy fortune, not thy force, hath conquered us. |
| PRINCE OF WALES | An argument that heaven aids the right. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Comte d’Artois with Prince Philippe |
| PRINCE OF WALES | See, see—Artois doth bring with him along<br>The late good counsel-giver to my [[Soul|soul]].<br>Welcome, Artois, and welcome Philippe too!<br>Who now, of you or I, have need to pray?<br>Now is the proverb verified in you—<br>‘Too bright a morning breeds a louring day’. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound trumpets. Enter Lord Audley supported by the two Squires |
| PRINCE OF WALES | But say, what grim discouragement comes here?<br>Alas, what thousand armèd men of France<br>Have writ that note of [[Death|death]] in Audley’s face?<br>(To Audley) Speak thou, that woo‘st [[Death|death]] with thy<br>careless smile,<br>And look’st so merrily upon thy [[Grave|grave]]<br>As if thou wert enamoured on thine end.<br>What hungry [[Sword|sword]] hath so bereaved thy face<br>And lopped a true friend from my loving [[Soul|soul]]? |
| AUDLEY | O, Prince, thy sweet bemoaning speech to me<br>Is as a mournful knell to one dead sick. |
| PRINCE OF WALES | (embracing him)<br>Dear Audley, if my tongue ring out thy end<br>My arms shall be thy [[Grave|grave]]. What may I do<br>To win thy life or to [[Revenge|revenge]] thy [[Death|death]]?<br>If thou wilt drink the [[Blood|blood]] of captive kings,<br>Or that it were restorative, command<br>A health of [[King|king]]’s [[Blood|blood]], and I’ll drink to thee.<br>If [[Honour|honour]] may dispense for thee with [[Death|death]],<br>The never-dying [[Honour|honour]] of this day<br>Share wholly, Audley, to thyself, and live. |
| AUDLEY | Victorious Prince—that thou art so, behold<br>A Caesar’s fame in kings’ captivity—<br>If I could hold dim [[Death|death]] but at a bay<br>Till I did see my liege, thy royal father,<br>My [[Soul|soul]] should yield this [[Castle|castle]] of my flesh,<br>This mangled tribute, with all willingness,<br>To darkness, consummation, [[Dust|dust]] and worms. |
| PRINCE OF WALES | Cheerly, bold man. Thy [[Soul|soul]] is all too proud<br>To yield her [[City|city]] for one little breach. |
| *(didascalia)* |  |
| PRINCE OF WALES | Should be divorced from her earthly spouse<br>By the soft temper of a Frenchman’s [[Sword|sword]].<br>Lo, to repair thy life I give to thee<br>Three thousand marks a year in English land. |
| AUDLEY | I take thy gift to pay the debts I owe.<br>These two poor squires redeemed me from the French<br>With lusty and dear hazard of their lives.<br>What thou hast given me, I give to them,<br>And as thou lov’st me, Prince, lay thy consent<br>To this bequeath in my last testament. |
| PRINCE OF WALES | Renowned Audley, live, and have from me<br>This gift twice doubled to these squires and thee.<br>But live or die, what thou hast given away<br>To these and theirs shall lasting [[Freedom|freedom]] stay.<br>(To the Squires) Come, gentlemen, I’ll see my friend bestowed |
| *(didascalia)* | Within an easy litter. Then we’ll march |
| PRINCE OF WALES | Proudly toward Calais with triumphant pace,<br>Unto my royal father, and there bring<br>The tribute of my wars: fair France his [[King|king]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
