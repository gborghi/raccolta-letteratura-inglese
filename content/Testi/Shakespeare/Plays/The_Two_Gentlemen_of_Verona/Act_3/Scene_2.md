| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Duke and [[Thurio]] |
| DUKE | Sir [[Thurio]], [[Fear|fear]] not but that she will [[Love|love]] you<br>Now [[Valentine]] is banished from her sight. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | Since his [[Exile|exile]] she hath despised me most,<br>Forsworn my company, and railed at me,<br>That I am desperate of obtaining her. |
| DUKE | This weak impress of [[Love|love]] is as a figure<br>Trenched in ice, which with an hour’s heat<br>Dissolves to [[Water|water]] and doth lose his form.<br>A little [[Time|time]] will melt her frozen thoughts,<br>And worthless [[Valentine]] shall be forgot. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Proteus]] |
| DUKE | How now, Sir [[Proteus]], is your countryman,<br>According to our proclamation, gone? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Gone, my good lord. |
| DUKE | My daughter takes his going grievously? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | A little [[Time|time]], my lord, will kill that grief. |
| DUKE | So I believe, but [[Thurio]] thinks not so.<br>[[Proteus]], the good conceit I hold of thee—<br>For thou hast shown some sign of good [[Desert|desert]]—<br>Makes me the better to confer with thee. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Longer than I prove loyal to your grace<br>Let me not live to look upon your grace. |
| DUKE | Thou know’st how willingly I would effect<br>The match between Sir [[Thurio]] and my daughter? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | I do, my lord. |
| DUKE | And also, I think, thou art not ignorant<br>How she opposes her against my will? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | She did, my lord, when [[Valentine]] was here. |
| DUKE | Ay, and perversely she persevers so.<br>What might we do to make the girl forget<br>The [[Love|love]] of [[Valentine]], and [[Love|love]] Sir [[Thurio]]? |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | The best way is to slander [[Valentine]]<br>With falsehood, cowardice, and poor descent,<br>Three things that women highly hold in hate. |
| DUKE | Ay, but she’ll think that it is spoke in hate. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Ay, if his enemy deliver it.<br>Therefore it must with circumstance be spoken<br>By one whom she esteemeth as his friend. |
| DUKE | Then you must undertake to slander him. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | And that, my lord, I shall be loath to do.<br>’Tis an ill office for a gentleman,<br>Especially against his very friend. |
| DUKE | Where your good word cannot advantage him<br>Your slander never can endamage him.<br>Therefore the office is indifferent,<br>Being entreated to it by your friend. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | You have prevailed, my lord. If I can do it<br>By aught that I can speak in his dispraise<br>She shall not long continue [[Love|love]] to him.<br>But say this weed her [[Love|love]] from [[Valentine]],<br>It follows not that she will [[Love|love]] Sir [[Thurio]]. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | Therefore, as you unwind her [[Love|love]] from him,<br>Lest it should ravel and be good to none<br>You must provide to [[Bottom|bottom]] it on me;<br>Which must be done by praising me as much<br>As you in worth dispraise Sir [[Valentine]]. |
| DUKE | And [[Proteus]], we dare trust you in this kind<br>Because we know, on [[Valentine]]’s report,<br>You are already [[Love|love]]’s firm votary,<br>And cannot soon revolt, and change your mind.<br>Upon this warrant shall you have access<br>Where you with [[Silvia]] may confer at large.<br>For she is lumpish, heavy, [[Melancholy|melancholy]],<br>And for your friend’s sake will be glad of you;<br>Where you may temper her, by your [[Persuasion|persuasion]],<br>To hate young [[Valentine]] and [[Love|love]] my friend. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | As much as I can do, I will effect.<br>But you, Sir [[Thurio]], are not sharp enough.<br>You must lay lime to tangle her desires<br>By wailful sonnets, whose composed rhymes<br>Should be full-fraught with serviceable vows. |
| DUKE | Ay, much is the force of heaven-bred poesy. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | Say that upon the altar of her [[Beauty|beauty]]<br>You sacrifice your [[Tears|tears]], your sighs, your heart.<br>Write till your ink be dry, and with your [[Tears|tears]]<br>Moist it again; and frame some feeling line<br>That may discover such integrity;<br>For Orpheus’ lute was strung with poets’ sinews,<br>Whose golden touch could soften steel and stones,<br>Make tigers tame, and huge leviathans<br>Forsake unsounded deeps to [[Dance|dance]] on sands.<br>After your dire-lamenting elegies,<br>Visit by [[Night|night]] your lady’s chamber-[[Window|window]]<br>With some sweet consort. To their instruments<br>Tune a deploring dump. The [[Night|night]]’s dead [[Silence|silence]]<br>Will [[Well|well]] become such sweet-complaining grievance.<br>This, or else nothing, will inherit her. |
| DUKE | This discipline shows thou hast been in [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Thurio|THURIO]] | And thy advice this [[Night|night]] I’ll put in practice.<br>Therefore, sweet [[Proteus]], my direction-giver,<br>Let us into the [[City|city]] presently<br>To sort some gentlemen [[Well|well]] skilled in music.<br>I have a [[Sonnet|sonnet]] that will serve the turn<br>To give the onset to thy good advice. |
| DUKE | About it, gentlemen. |
| [[Proteus|PROTEUS]] | We’ll wait upon your grace till after supper,<br>And afterward determine our proceedings. |
| DUKE | Even now about it. I will pardon you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Thurio]] and [[Proteus]] at one door, and the Duke at another |
