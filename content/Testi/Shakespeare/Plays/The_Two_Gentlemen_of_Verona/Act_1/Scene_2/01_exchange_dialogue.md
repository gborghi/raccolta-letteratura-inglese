| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Julia]] and Lucetta |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | But say, Lucetta, now we are [[Alone|alone]]— Wouldst thou then counsel me to fall in [[Love|love]]? |
| LUCETTA | Ay, madam, so you stumble not unheedfully. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Of all the fair resort of gentlemen That every day with parle encounter me, In thy opinion which is worthiest [[Love|love]]? |
| LUCETTA | Please you repeat their names, I’ll show my mind According to my shallow simple skill. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | What think’st thou of the fair Sir Eglamour? |
| LUCETTA | As of a knight [[Well|well]] spoken, neat, and fine, But were I you, he never should be mine. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | What think’st thou of the rich Mercatio? |
| LUCETTA | [[Well]] of his wealth, but of himself, so-so. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | What think’st thou of the gentle [[Proteus]]? |
| LUCETTA | Lord, lord, to see what folly reigns in us! |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | How now? What means this passion at his name? |
| LUCETTA | Pardon, dear madam, ‘tis a passing shame That I, unworthy [[Body|body]] as I am, Should censure thus on lovely gentlemen. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Why not on [[Proteus]], as of all the rest? |
| LUCETTA | Then thus: of many good, I think him best. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Your reason? |
| LUCETTA | I have no other but a woman’s reason: I think him so because I think him so. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | And wouldst thou have me cast my [[Love|love]] on him? |
| LUCETTA | Ay, if you thought your [[Love|love]] not cast away. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Why, he of all the rest hath never moved me. |
| LUCETTA | Yet he of all the rest I think best loves ye. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | His little speaking shows his [[Love|love]] but small. |
| LUCETTA | [[Fire]] that’s closest kept burns most of all. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | They do not [[Love|love]] that do not show their [[Love|love]]. |
| LUCETTA | O, they [[Love|love]] least that let men know their [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | I would I knew his mind. |
| LUCETTA | (giving [[Proteus]]’ letter) Peruse this paper, madam. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | ‘To [[Julia]]’—say, from whom? |
| LUCETTA | That the contents will show. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Say, say—who gave it thee? |
| LUCETTA | Sir [[Valentine]]’s page; and sent, I think, from [[Proteus]]. He would have given it you, but I being in the way Did in your name receive it. Pardon the fault, I pray. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Now, by my modesty, a goodly broker. Dare you presume to harbour wanton lines? To whisper, and conspire against my youth? Now trust me, ‘tis an office of great worth, And you an officer fit for the place. There. Take the paper. She gives Lucetta the letter See it be returned, Or else return no more into my sight. |
| LUCETTA | To plead for [[Love|love]] deserves more fee than hate. |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | Will ye be gone? |
| LUCETTA | That you may ruminate. Exit |
| [[Julia|JULIA]] | And yet I would I had o‘erlooked the letter. It were a shame to call her back again And pray her to a fault for which I chid her. What [[Fool|fool]] is she, that knows I am a maid And would not force the letter to my view, Since maids in modesty say ‘No’ to that Which they would have the profferer construe ‘Ay’. Fie, fie, how wayward is this foolish [[Love|love]] That like a testy babe will scratch the [[Nurse|nurse]] And presently, all humbled, kiss the rod. How churlishly I chid Lucetta hence When willingly I would have had her here. How angerly I taught my brow to frown When inward joy enforced my heart to smile. My penance is to call Lucetta back And ask remission for my folly past. What ho! Lucetta! Enter Lucetta |
