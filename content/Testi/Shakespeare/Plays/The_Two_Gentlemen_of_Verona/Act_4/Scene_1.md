| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Outlaws |
| FIRST OUTLAW | Fellows, stand fast. I see a passenger. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | If there be ten, shrink not, but down with ‘em. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Valentine and Speed |
| THIRD OUTLAW | Stand, sir, and throw us that you have about ye.<br>If not, we’ll make you sit, and rifle you. |
| SPEED | (to Valentine)<br>Sir, we are undone. These are the villains<br>That all the travellers do fear so much. |
| VALENTINE | (to the Outlaws) My friends. |
| FIRST OUTLAW | That’s not so, sir. We are your enemies. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | Peace. We’ll hear him. |
| THIRD OUTLAW | Ay, by my beard will we. For he is a proper man. |
| VALENTINE | Then know that I have little wealth to lose.<br>A man I am, crossed with adversity.<br>My riches are these poor habiliments,<br>Of which if you should here disfurnish me<br>You take the sum and substance that I have. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | Whither travel you? |
| VALENTINE | To Verona. |
| FIRST OUTLAW | Whence came you? |
| VALENTINE | From Milan. 20 |
| THIRD OUTLAW | Have you long sojourned there? |
| VALENTINE | Some sixteen months, and longer might have stayed<br>If crooked fortune had not thwarted me. |
| FIRST OUTLAW | What, were you banished thence? |
| VALENTINE | I was. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | For what offence? |
| VALENTINE | For that which now torments me to rehearse.<br>I killed a man, whose death I much repent,<br>But yet I slew him manfully, in fight,<br>Without false vantage or base treachery. |
| FIRST OUTLAW | Why, ne’er repent it, if it were done so.<br>But were you banished for so small a fault? |
| VALENTINE | I was, and held me glad of such a doom. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | Have you the tongues? |
| VALENTINE | My youthful travel therein made me happy,<br>Or else I had been often miserable. |
| THIRD OUTLAW | By the bare scalp of Robin Hood’s fat friar,<br>This fellow were a king for our wild faction. |
| FIRST OUTLAW | We’ll have him. Sirs, a word.<br>The Outlaws confer |
| SPEED | (to Valentine) Master, be one of them.<br>It’s an honourable kind of thievery. |
| VALENTINE | Peace, villain. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | Tell us this: have you anything to take to? |
| VALENTINE | Nothing but my fortune. |
| THIRD OUTLAW | Know, then, that some of us are gentlemen<br>Such as the fury of ungoverned youth<br>Thrust from the company of aweful men.<br>Myself was from Verona banished<br>For practising to steal away a lady,<br>An heir, and near allied unto the Duke. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | And I from Mantua, for a gentleman<br>Who, in my mood, I stabbed unto the heart. |
| FIRST OUTLAW | And I, for suchlike petty crimes as these.<br>But to the purpose, for we cite our faults<br>That they may hold excused our lawless lives.<br>And partly seeing you are beautified<br>With goodly shape, and by your own report<br>A linguist, and a man of such perfection<br>As we do in our quality much want— |
| SECOND OUTLAW | Indeed because you are a banished man,<br>Therefore above the rest we parley to you.<br>Are you content to be our general,<br>To make a virtue of necessity<br>And live as we do in this wilderness? |
| THIRD OUTLAW | What sayst thou? Wilt thou be of our consort?<br>Say ‘Ay’, and be the captain of us all.<br>We’ll do thee homage, and be ruled by thee,<br>Love thee as our commander and our king. |
| FIRST OUTLAW | But if thou scorn our courtesy, thou diest. |
| SECOND OUTLAW | Thou shalt not live to brag what we have offered. |
| VALENTINE | I take your offer, and will live with you,<br>Provided that you do no outrages<br>On silly women or poor passengers. |
| THIRD OUTLAW | No, we detest such vile, base practices.<br>Come, go with us. We’ll bring thee to our crews<br>And show thee all the treasure we have got,<br>Which, with ourselves, all rest at thy dispose. Exeunt |
