| *(didascalia)* | King Henry enters with his power. Alarum, and exeunt to the battle. Then enter the Earl of Douglas, and Sir Walter Blunt, disguised as the King |
| BLUNT | What is thy name, that in the battle thus<br>Thou crossest me? What honour dost thou seek<br>Upon my head? |
| DOUGLAS | Know then my name is Douglas,<br>And I do haunt thee in the battle thus<br>Because some tell me that thou art a king. |
| BLUNT | They tell thee true. |
| DOUGLAS | The Lord of Stafford dear today hath bought<br>Thy likeness, for instead of thee, King Harry,<br>This sword hath ended him. So shall it thee,<br>Unless thou yield thee as my prisoner. |
| BLUNT | I was not born a yielder, thou proud Scot,<br>And thou shalt find a king that will revenge<br>Lord Stafford’s death.<br>They fight. Douglas kills Blunt. Then enter Hotspur |
| HOTSPUR | O Douglas, hadst thou fought at Holmedon thus,<br>I never had triumphed upon a Scot. |
| DOUGLAS | All’s done, all’s won: here breathless lies the King. |
| HOTSPUR | Where? |
| DOUGLAS | Here. |
| HOTSPUR | This, Douglas? No, I know this face full well.<br>A gallant knight he was; his name was Blunt—<br>Semblably furnished like the King himself. |
| DOUGLAS | (to Blunt’s body)<br>A fool go with thy soul, whither it goes !<br>A borrowed title hast thou bought too dear.<br>Why didst thou tell me that thou wert a king? |
| HOTSPUR | The king hath many marching in his coats. |
| DOUGLAS | Now by my sword, I will kill all his coats.<br>I’ll murder all his wardrobe, piece by piece,<br>Until I meet the King. |
| HOTSPUR | Up and away!<br>Our soldiers stand full fairly for the day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt, leaving Blunt’s body |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter Sir John Oldcastle |
| SIR JOHN | Though I could scape shot-free at London, I fear the shot here. Here’s no scoring but upon the pate.—Soft, who are you?—Sir Walter Blunt. There’s honour for you. Here’s no vanity. I am as hot as molten lead, and as heavy too. God keep lead out of me; I need no more weight than mine own bowels. I have led my ragamuffins where they are peppered; there’s not three of my hundred and fifty left alive, and they are for the town’s end, to beg during life. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Prince Harry |
| SIR JOHN | But who comes here? |
| PRINCE HARRY | What, stand’st thou idle here? Lend me thy sword.<br>Many a noble man lies stark and stiff<br>Under the hoofs of vaunting enemies,<br>Whose deaths as yet are unrevenged. I prithee<br>Lend me thy sword. |
| SIR JOHN | O Hal, I prithee give me leave to breathe awhile.<br>Turk Gregory never did such deeds in arms<br>As I have done this day. I have paid Percy,<br>I have made him sure. |
| PRINCE HARRY | He is indeed,<br>And living to kill thee. I prithee<br>Lend me thy sword. |
| SIR JOHN | Nay, before God, Hal,<br>If Percy be alive thou gett’st not my sword;<br>But take my pistol if thou wilt. |
| PRINCE HARRY | Give it me. What, is it in the case? |
| SIR JOHN | Ay, Hal;<br>’Tis hot, ’tis hot. There’s that will sack a city.<br>The Prince draws it out, and finds it to be a bottle of sack |
| PRINCE HARRY | What, is it a time to jest and dally now?<br>He throws the bottle at him. Exit |
| SIR JOHN | Well, if Percy be alive, I’ll pierce him. If he do come in my way, so; if he do not, if I come in his willingly, let him make a carbonado of me. I like not such grinning honour as Sir Walter hath. Give me life, which if I can save, so; if not, honour comes unlocked for, and there’s an end. Exit [with Blunt’s body] |
