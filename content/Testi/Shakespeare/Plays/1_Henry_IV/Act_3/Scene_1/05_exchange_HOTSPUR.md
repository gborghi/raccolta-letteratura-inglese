| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Methinks my moiety north from Burton here In quantity equals not one of yours. See how this [[River|river]] comes me cranking in, And cuts me from the best of all my land A huge half-[[Moon|moon]], a monstrous cantle, out. I’ll have the current in this place dammed up, And here the smug and silver Trent shall run In a new channel fair and evenly. It shall not [[Wind|wind]] with such a deep indent, To rob me of so rich a [[Bottom|bottom]] here. GLYNDŴR Not [[Wind|wind]]? It shall, it must; you see it doth. |
| MORTIMER | Yea, but mark how he bears his course, and runs me up With like advantage on the other side, Gelding the opposed continent as much As on the other side it takes from you. |
| WORCESTER | Yea, but a little charge will trench him here, And on this north side win this cape of land, And then he runs straight and even. no |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I’ll have it so; a little charge will do it. GLYNDŴR I’ll not have it altered. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Will not you? GLYNDŴR No, nor you shall not. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Who shall say me nay? 115 GLYNDŴR Why, that will I. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Let me not understand you, then: speak it in Welsh. GLYNDŴR I can speak English, lord, as [[Well|well]] as you; For I was trained up in the English [[Court|court]], Where, being but young, I framed to the harp Many an English ditty lovely [[Well|well]], And gave the tongue a helpful ornament— A virtue that was never seen in you. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | Marry, and I am glad of it, with all my heart. I had rather be a kitten and cry ‘mew’ Than one of these same metre ballad-mongers. I had rather hear a brazen canstick turned, Or a dry [[Wheel|wheel]] grate on the axle-tree, And that would set my teeth nothing on edge, Nothing so much as mincing poetry. ’Tis like the forced gait of a shuffling nag. GLYNDŴR Come, you shall have Trent turned. |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I do not care. I’ll give thrice so much land To any [[Well|well]]-deserving friend; But in the way of bargain—mark ye me—135 I’ll cavil on the ninth part of a hair. Are the indentures drawn? Shall we be gone? GLYNDŴR The [[Moon|moon]] shines fair. You may away by [[Night|night]]. I’ll haste the writer, and withal Break with your wives of your departure hence. I am afraid my daughter will run mad, So much she doteth on her Mortimer. Exit |
| MORTIMER | Fie, cousin Percy, how you [[Cross|cross]] my father! |
| [[Hotspur|HOTSPUR]] | I cannot choose. Sometime he angers me With telling me of the moldwarp and the ant, Of the dreamer Merlin and his prophecies, And of a dragon and a finless fish, A clip-winged griffin and a moulten raven, A couching [[Lion|lion]] and a ramping cat, And such a deal of skimble-skamble stuff 150 As puts me from my [[Faith|faith]]. I tell you what, He held me last [[Night|night]] at the least nine hours In reckoning up the several devils’ names That were his lackeys. I cried, ‘Hum!’ and, ‘[[Well]], go to!’, But marked him not a word. O, he is as tedious As a tired [[Horse|horse]], a railing wife, Worse than a smoky [[house]]. I had rather live With [[cheese]] and garlic, in a windmill, far, Than feed on cates and have him talk to me In any [[Summer|summer]] [[house]] in Christendom. 160 |
