| SIR JOHN | No, I’ll be sworn; I make as good use of it as many a man doth of a [[Death|death]]’s head, or a [[Memento Mori|memento mori]]. I never see thy face but I think upon hell-[[Fire|fire]] and Dives that lived in purple—for there he is in his robes, burning, burning. If thou wert any way given to virtue, I would swear by thy face; my oath should be ’By this [[Fire|fire]] that’s God’s angell’ But thou art altogether given over, and wert indeed, but for the light in thy face, the son of utter darkness. When thou rannest up Gads [[Hill]] in the [[Night|night]] to catch my [[Horse|horse]], if I did not think thou hadst been an ignis fatuus or a ball of wildfire, there’s no purchase in money. O, thou art a perpetual triumph, an everlasting bonfire-light! Thou hast saved me a thousand marks in links and torches, walking with thee in the [[Night|night]] betwixt tavern and tavern—but the sack that thou hast drunk me would have bought me lights as good cheap at the dearest chandler’s in Europe. I have maintained that salamander of yours with [[Fire|fire]] any [[Time|time]] this two-and-thirty years, God reward me for it. |
