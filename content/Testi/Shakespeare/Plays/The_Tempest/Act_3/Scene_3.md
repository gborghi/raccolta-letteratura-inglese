| *(didascalia)* | Enter Alonso, Sebastian, Antonio, Gonzalo, Adrian, and Francisco |
| GONZALO | (to Alonso)<br>By’r la’kin, I can go no further, sir.<br>My old bones ache. Here’s a maze trod indeed<br>Through forthrights and meanders. By your patience,<br>I needs must rest me. |
| ALONSO | Old lord, I cannot blame thee,<br>Who am myself attached with weariness<br>To th’ dulling of my spirits. Sit down and rest.<br>Even here I will put off my hope, and keep it<br>No longer for my flatterer. He is drowned<br>Whom thus we stray to find, and the sea mocks<br>Our frustrate search on land. Well, let him go. |
| *(didascalia)* | They sit |
| ANTONIO | (aside to Sebastian)<br>I am right glad that he’s so out of hope.<br>Do not for one repulse forgo the purpose<br>That you resolved t’effect. |
| SEBASTIAN | (aside to Antonio) The next advantage<br>Will we take throughly. |
| ANTONIO | (aside to Sebastian) Let it be tonight,<br>For now they are oppressed with travel. They<br>Will not nor cannot use such vigilance<br>As when they are fresh. |
| SEBASTIAN | (aside to Antonio) I say tonight. No more.<br>Solemn and strange music. Enter Prospero on the top, invisible |
| ALONSO | What harmony is this? My good friends, hark. |
| GONZALO | Marvellous sweet music. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter spirits, in several strange shapes, bringing in a table and a banquet, and dance about it with gentle actions of salutations, and, inviting the King and his companions to eat, they depart |
| ALONSO | Give us kind keepers, heavens! What were these? |
| SEBASTIAN | A living drollery. Now I will believe<br>That there are unicorns; that in Arabia<br>There is one tree, the phoenix’ throne, one phoenix<br>At this hour reigning there. |
| ANTONIO | I’ll believe both;<br>And what does else want credit come to me,<br>And I’ll be sworn ’tis true. Travellers ne’er did lie,<br>Though fools at home condemn ’em. |
| GONZALO | If in Naples<br>I should report this now, would they believe me—<br>If I should say I saw such islanders?<br>For certes these are people of the island,<br>Who though they are of monstrous shape, yet note<br>Their manners are more gentle-kind than of<br>Our human generation you shall find<br>Many, nay, almost any. |
| PROSPERO | (aside)<br>Honest lord,<br>Thou hast said well, for some of you there present<br>Are worse than devils. |
| ALONSO | I cannot too much muse.<br>Such shapes, such gesture, and such sound,<br>expressing—<br>Although they want the use of tongue—a kind<br>Of excellent dumb discourse. |
| PROSPERO | (aside)<br>Praise in departing. |
| FRANCISCO | They vanished strangely. |
| SEBASTIAN | No matter, since<br>They have left their viands behind, for we have<br>stomachs.<br>Will’t please you taste of what is here? |
| ALONSO | Not I. |
| GONZALO | Faith, sir, you need not fear. When we were boys,<br>Who would believe that there were mountaineers<br>Dewlapped like bulls, whose throats had hanging at ’em<br>Wallets of flesh? Or that there were such men<br>Whose heads stood in their breasts? Which now we<br>find<br>Each putter-out of five for one will bring us<br>Good warrant of. |
| *(didascalia)* | ALONSO rising I will stand to and feed, |
| GONZALO | Although my last—no matter, since I feel<br>The best is past. Brother, my lord the Duke,<br>Stand to, and do as we. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alonso, Sebastian, and Antonio approach the table. Thunder and lightning. Ariel descends like a harpy, claps his wings upon the table, and, with a quaint device, the banquet vanishes |
| ARIEL | You are three men of sin, whom destiny—<br>That hath to instrument this lower world<br>And what is in’t—the never-surfeited sea<br>Hath caused to belch up you, and on this island<br>Where man doth not inhabit, you ’mongst men<br>Being most unfit to live. I have made you mad,<br>And even with suchlike valour men hang and drown<br>Their proper selves.<br>Alonso, Sebastian, and Antonio draw<br>You fools! I and my fellows<br>Are ministers of fate. The elements<br>Of whom your swords are tempered may as well<br>Wound the loud winds, or with bemocked-at stabs<br>Kill the still-closing waters, as diminish<br>One dowl that’s in my plume. My fellow ministers<br>Are like invulnerable. If you could hurt,<br>Your swords are now too massy for your strengths<br>And will not be uplifted.<br>Alonso, Sebastian, and Antonio stand amazed<br>But remember,<br>For that’s my business to you, that you three<br>From Milan did supplant good Prospero;<br>Exposed unto the sea, which hath requit it,<br>Him and his innocent child; for which foul deed,<br>The powers, delaying not forgetting, have<br>Incensed the seas and shores, yea, all the creatures,<br>Against your peace. Thee of thy son, Alonso,<br>They have bereft, and do pronounce by me<br>Ling’ring perdition—worse than any death<br>Can be at once—shatt step by step attend<br>You and your ways; whose wraths to guard you<br>from—<br>Which here in this most desolate isle else falls<br>Upon your heads—is nothing but heart’s sorrow<br>And a clear life ensuing. |
| *(didascalia)* | He ascends and vanishes in thunder. Then, to soft music, enter the spirits again, and dance with mocks and mows, and they depart, carrying out the table |
| PROSPERO | Bravely the figure of this harpy hast thou<br>Performed, my Ariel; a grace it had devouring.<br>Of my instruction hast thou nothing bated<br>In what thou hadst to say. So with good life<br>And observation strange my meaner ministers<br>Their several kinds have done. My high charms work,<br>And these mine enemies are all knit up<br>In their distractions. They now are in my power;<br>And in these fits I leave them, while I visit<br>Young Ferdinand, whom they suppose is drowned,<br>And his and mine loved darling. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Gonzalo, Adrian, and Francisco go towards the others |
| GONZALO | I’th’ name of something holy, sir, why stand you<br>In this strange stare? |
| ALONSO | O, it is monstrous, monstrous!<br>Methought the billows spoke and told me of it,<br>The winds did sing it to me, and the thunder,<br>That deep and dreadful organ-pipe, pronounced<br>The name of Prosper. It did bass my trespass.<br>Therefor my son i’th’ ooze is bedded, and<br>I’ll seek him deeper than e’er plummet sounded,<br>And with him there lie mudded. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| SEBASTIAN | But one fiend at a time,<br>I’ll fight their legions o’er. |
| ANTONIO | I’ll be thy second. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Sebastian and Antonio |
| GONZALO | All three of them are desperate. Their great guilt,<br>Like poison given to work a great time after,<br>Now ’gins to bite the spirits. I do beseech you<br>That are of suppler joints, follow them swiftly,<br>And hinder them from what this ecstasy<br>May now provoke them to. |
| ADRIAN | Follow, I pray you. Exeunt |
