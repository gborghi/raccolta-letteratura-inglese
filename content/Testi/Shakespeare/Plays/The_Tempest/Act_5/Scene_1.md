| *(didascalia)* | Enter Prospero, in his [[Magic|magic]] robes, and [[Ariel]] |
| PROSPERO | Now does my project gather to a head.<br>My charms crack not, my spirits obey, and [[Time|time]]<br>Goes upright with his carriage. How’s the day? |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | On the sixth hour; at which [[Time|time]], my lord,<br>You said our work should cease. |
| PROSPERO | I did say so<br>When first I raised the tempest. Say, my spirit,<br>How fares the [[King]] and’s followers? |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | Confined together<br>In the same fashion as you gave in charge,<br>Just as you left them; all prisoners, sir,<br>In the lime-grove which weather-fends your cell.<br>They cannot budge till your release. The [[King]],<br>His brother, and yours, abide all three distracted,<br>And the remainder mourning over them,<br>Brimful of sorrow and dismay; but chiefly<br>Him that you termed, sir, the good old lord [[Gonzalo]]:<br>His [[Tears|tears]] run down his beard like [[Winter|winter]]’s drops<br>From eaves of reeds. Your charm so strongly works ’em<br>That if you now beheld them your affections<br>Would become tender. |
| PROSPERO | Dost thou think so, spirit? |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | Mine would, sir, were I human. |
| PROSPERO | And mine shall.<br>Hast thou, which art but air, a touch, a feeling<br>Of their afflictions, and shall not myself,<br>One of their kind, that relish all as sharply<br>Passion as they, be kindlier moved than thou art?<br>Though with their high wrongs I am struck to th’<br>quick,<br>Yet with my nobler reason ’gainst my fury<br>Do I take part. The rarer action is<br>In virtue than in vengeance. They being penitent,<br>The sole drift of my purpose doth extend<br>Not a frown further. Go release them, [[Ariel]].<br>My charms I’ll break, their senses I’ll restore,<br>And they shall be themselves. |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | I’ll fetch them, sir. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Prospero draws a circle with his staff |
| PROSPERO | Ye elves of hills, brooks, standing lakes and groves,<br>And ye that on the sands with printless foot<br>Do chase the ebbing Neptune, and do fly him<br>When he comes back; you demi-puppets that<br>By moonshine do the green sour ringlets make<br>Whereof the ewe not bites; and you whose pastime<br>Is to make midnight mushrooms, that rejoice<br>To hear the solemn curfew; by whose aid,<br>Weak masters though ye be, I have bedimmed<br>The noontide sun, called forth the mutinous winds,<br>And ‘twixt the green sea and the azured vault<br>Set roaring war—to the dread rattling thunder<br>Have I given [[Fire|fire]], and rifted Jove’s stout oak<br>With his own bolt; the strong-based promontory<br>Have I made shake, and by the spurs plucked up<br>The pine and cedar; graves at my command<br>Have waked their sleepers, oped, and let ’em forth<br>By my so potent art. But this rough [[Magic|magic]]<br>I here abjure. And when I have required<br>Some heavenly music—which even now I do—<br>To work mine end upon their senses that<br>This airy charm is for, I’ll break my staff,<br>Bury it certain fathoms in the earth,<br>And deeper than did ever plummet sound<br>I’ll drown my book.<br>Solemn music. Here enters first [[Ariel]], invisible; then [[Alonso]], with a frantic gesture, attended by [[Gonzalo]]; [[Sebastian]] and [[Antonio]], in like manner, attended by Adrian and Francisco. They all enter the circle which Prospero had made, and there stand charmed; which Prospero observing, speaks<br>(To [[Alonso]]) A solemn air, and the best comforter<br>To an unsettled fancy, cure thy brains,<br>Now useless, boiled within thy skull.<br>(To [[Sebastian]] and [[Antonio]]) There stand,<br>For you are spelt-stopped.—<br>Holy [[Gonzalo]], honourable man,<br>Mine eyes, ev’n sociable to the show of thine,<br>Fall fellowly drops. (Aside) The charm dissolves apace,<br>And as the morning steals upon the [[Night|night]],<br>Melting the darkness, so their rising senses<br>Begin to chase the ignorant fumes that mantle<br>Their clearer reason.—O good [[Gonzalo]],<br>My true preserver, and a loyal sir<br>To him thou follow’st, I will pay thy graces<br>[[Home]] both in word and deed.—Most cruelly<br>Didst thou, [[Alonso]], use me and my daughter.<br>Thy brother was a furtherer in the act.—<br>Thou art pinched for’t now, [[Sebastian]].<br>(To [[Antonio]]) Flesh and [[Blood|blood]],<br>You, brother mine, that entertained [[Ambition|ambition]],<br>Expelled remorse and [[Nature|nature]], whom, with [[Sebastian]]—<br>Whose inward pinches therefore are most strong,—<br>Would here have killed your [[King|king]], I do forgive thee,<br>Unnatural though thou art. (Aside) Their understanding<br>Begins to swell, and the approaching tide<br>Will shortly fill the reasonable shores<br>That now lie foul and muddy. Not one of them<br>That yet looks on me, or would know me.—[[Ariel]],<br>Fetch me the hat and rapier in my cell.<br>I will disease me, and myself present<br>As I was sometime Milan. Quickly, spirit!<br>Thou shalt ere long be free.<br>[[Ariel]] sings and helps to attire him as [[Duke of Milan]] |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | Where the bee sucks, there suck I: In a cowslip’s bell I lie;<br>There I couch when owls do cry.<br>On the bat’s back I do fly<br>After [[Summer|summer]] merrily.<br>Merrily, merrily shall I live now<br>Under the blossom that hangs on the bough.<br>Merrily, merrily shall I live now<br>Under the blossom that hangs on the bough. |
| PROSPERO | Why, that’s my dainty [[Ariel]]! I shall miss thee,<br>But yet thou shalt have [[Freedom|freedom]].—So, so, so.—<br>To the [[King]]’s [[Ship|ship]], invisible as thou art!<br>There shalt thou find the mariners asleep<br>Under the hatches. The Master and the Boatswain<br>Being awake, enforce them to this place,<br>And presently, I prithee. |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | I drink the air before me, and return<br>Or ere your pulse twice beat. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Gonzalo|GONZALO]] | All torment, trouble, [[Wonder|wonder]], and amazement<br>Inhabits here. Some heavenly power guide us<br>Out of this fearful country! |
| PROSPERO | Behold, sir [[King]],<br>The wronged [[Duke of Milan]], Prospero.<br>For more assurance that a living prince<br>Does now speak to thee, I embrace thy [[Body|body]];<br>And to thee and thy company I bid<br>A hearty welcome.<br>He embraces [[Alonso]] |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | Whe’er thou beest he or no,<br>Or some enchanted trifle to abuse me,<br>As late I have been, I not know. Thy pulse<br>Beats as of flesh and [[Blood|blood]]; and since I saw thee<br>Th’affliction of my mind amends, with which<br>I [[Fear|fear]] a [[Madness|madness]] held me. This must crave—<br>An if this be at all—a most strange story.<br>Thy dukedom I resign, and do entreat<br>Thou pardon me my wrongs. But how should<br>Prospero<br>Be living and be here? |
| PROSPERO | (to [[Gonzalo]])<br>First, noble friend,<br>Let me embrace thine age, whose [[Honour|honour]] cannot<br>Be measured or confined.<br>He embraces [[Gonzalo]] |
| [[Gonzalo|GONZALO]] | Whether this be<br>Or be not, I’ll not swear. |
| PROSPERO | You do yet taste<br>Some subtleties o’th’ isle that will not let you<br>Believe things certain.—Welcome, my friends all.<br>(Aside to [[Sebastian]] and [[Antonio]])<br><br>But you, my brace of lords, were I so minded,<br>I here could pluck his highness’ frown upon you<br>And justify you traitors. At this [[Time|time]]<br>I will tell no tales. |
| [[Sebastian|SEBASTIAN]] | (to [[Antonio]]) The devil speaks in him. |
| PROSPERO | No.<br>(To [[Antonio]]) For you, most wicked sir, whom to call<br>brother<br>Would even infect my mouth, I do forgive<br>Thy rankest fault, all of them, and require<br>My dukedom of thee, which perforce I know<br>Thou must restore. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | If thou beest Prospero,<br>Give us particulars of thy preservation,<br>How thou hast met us here, whom three hours since<br>Were wrecked upon this shore, where I have lost—<br>How sharp the point of this remembrance is!—<br>My dear son [[Ferdinand]]. |
| PROSPERO | I am woe for’t, sir. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | Irreparable is the loss, and patience<br>Says it is past her cure. |
| PROSPERO | I rather think<br>You have not sought her help, of whose soft grace<br>For the like loss I have her sovereign aid,<br>And rest myself content. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | You the like loss? |
| PROSPERO | As great to me as late; and supportable<br>To make the dear loss have I means much weaker<br>Than you may call to comfort you, for I<br>Have lost my daughter. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | A daughter?<br>O heavens, that they were living both in Naples,<br>The [[King|king]] and [[Queen|queen]] there! That they were, I wish<br>Myself were mudded in that oozy bed<br>Where my son lies. When did you lose your daughter? |
| PROSPERO | In this last tempest. I perceive these lords<br>At this encounter do so much admire<br>That they devour their reason, and scarce think<br>Their eyes do offices of [[Truth|truth]], these words<br>Are natural breath. But howsoe’er you have<br>Been jostled from your senses, know for certain<br>That I am Prospero, and that very Duke<br>Which was thrust forth of Milan, who most strangely,<br>Upon this shore where you were wrecked, was landed<br>To be the lord on’t. No more yet of this,<br>For ’tis a chronicle of day by day,<br>Not a relation for a breakfast, nor<br>Befitting this first meeting. Welcome, sir.<br>This cell’s my [[Court|court]]. Here have I few attendants,<br>And subjects none abroad. Pray you, look in.<br>My dukedom since you have given me again,<br>I will requite you with as good a thing;<br>At least bring forth a [[Wonder|wonder]] to content ye<br>As much as me my dukedom.<br>Here Prospero discovers [[Ferdinand]] and [[Miranda]], playing at chess |
| [[Miranda|MIRANDA]] | Sweet lord, you play me false. |
| [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]] | No, my dearest [[Love|love]],<br>I would not for the world. |
| [[Miranda|MIRANDA]] | Yes, for a score of kingdoms you should wrangle,<br>An I would call it fair play. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | If this prove<br>A vision of the island, one dear son<br>Shall I twice lose. |
| [[Sebastian|SEBASTIAN]] | A most high miracle. |
| [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]] | (coming forward)<br>Though the seas threaten, they are merciful.<br>I have cursed them without cause.<br>He kneels |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | Now all the blessings<br>Of a glad father compass thee about.<br>Arise and say how thou cam’st here.<br>[[Ferdinand]] rises |
| [[Miranda|MIRANDA]] | (coming forward)<br>O [[Wonder|wonder]]!<br>How many goodly creatures are there here!<br>How beauteous mankind is! O brave new world<br>That has such people in’t! |
| PROSPERO | ’Tis new to thee. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | (to [[Ferdinand]])<br>What is this maid with whom thou wast at play?<br>Your eld’st acquaintance cannot be three hours.<br>Is she the goddess that hath severed us,<br>And brought us thus together? |
| [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]] | Sir, she is mortal;<br>But by immortal [[Providence|providence]] she’s mine.<br>I chose her when I could not ask my father<br>For his advice, nor thought I had one. She<br>Is daughter to this famous [[Duke of Milan]],<br>Of whom so often I have heard renown,<br>But never saw before; of whom I have<br>Received a second life; and second father<br>This lady makes him to me. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | I am hers.<br>But O, how oddly will it sound, that I<br>Must ask my [[Child|child]] [[Forgiveness|forgiveness]]! |
| PROSPERO | There, sir, stop.<br>Let us not burden our remembrance with<br>A heaviness that’s gone. |
| [[Gonzalo|GONZALO]] | I have inly wept,<br>Or should have spoke ere this. Look down, you gods,<br>And on this couple drop a blessed [[Crown|crown]],<br>For it is you that have chalked forth the way<br>Which brought us hither. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | I say amen, [[Gonzalo]]. |
| [[Gonzalo|GONZALO]] | Was Milan thrust from Milan, that his issue<br>Should become kings of Naples? O rejoice<br>Beyond a common joy! And set it down<br>With gold on lasting pillars: in one voyage<br>Did Claribel her husband find at Tunis,<br>And [[Ferdinand]] her brother found a wife<br>Where he himself was lost; Prospero his dukedom<br>In a poor isle; and all of us ourselves,<br>When no man was his own. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | (to [[Ferdinand]] and [[Miranda]]) Give me your hands.<br>Let grief and sorrow still embrace his heart<br>That doth not wish you joy. |
| [[Gonzalo|GONZALO]] | Be it so! Amen! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Ariel]], with the Master and Boatswain amazedly following |
| [[Gonzalo|GONZALO]] | O look, sir, look, sir, here is more of us!<br>I prophesied if a gallows were on land<br>This fellow could not drown. (To the Boatswain) Now,<br><br>blasphemy,<br>That swear’st grace o’erboard: not an oath on shore?<br>Hast thou no mouth by land? What is the news? |
| BOATSWAIN | The best news is that we have safely found<br>Our [[King]] and company. The next, our [[Ship|ship]],<br>Which but three glasses since we gave out split,<br>Is tight and yare and bravely rigged, as when<br>We first put out to sea. |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | (aside to Prospero) Sir, all this service<br>Have I done since I went. |
| PROSPERO | (aside to [[Ariel]])<br>My tricksy spirit! |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | These are not natural events; they strengthen<br>From strange to stranger. Say, how came you hither? |
| BOATSWAIN | If I did think, sir, I were [[Well|well]] awake<br>I’d strive to tell you. We were dead of [[Sleep|sleep]],<br>And—how we know not—all clapped under hatches,<br>Where but even now, with strange and several noises<br>Of roaring, shrieking, howling, jingling [[Chains|chains]],<br>And more diversity of sounds, all horrible,<br>We were awaked; straightway at liberty;<br>Where we in all her trim freshly beheld<br>Our royal, good, and gallant [[Ship|ship]], our Master<br>Cap’ring to eye her. On a trice, so please you,<br>Even in [[A Dream|a dream]], were we divided from them,<br>And were brought moping hither. |
| [[Ariel|ARIEL]] | (aside to Prospero)<br>Was’t [[Well|well]] done? |
| PROSPERO | (aside to [[Ariel]])<br>Bravely, my diligence. Thou shalt be free. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | This is as strange a maze as e’er men trod,<br>And there is in this business more than [[Nature|nature]]<br>Was ever conduct of. Some oracle<br>Must rectify our knowledge. |
| PROSPERO | Sir, my liege,<br>Do not infest your mind with beating on<br>The strangeness of this business. At picked leisure,<br>Which shall be shortly, single I’ll resolve you,<br>Which to you shall seem probable, of every<br>These happened accidents; till when be cheerful,<br>And think of each thing [[Well|well]]. (Aside to [[Ariel]]) Come<br>hither, spirit.<br>Set [[Caliban]] and his companions free.<br>Untie the spell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Ariel]] |
| PROSPERO | (To [[Alonso]]) How fares my gracious sir?<br>There are yet missing of your company<br>Some few odd lads that you remember not. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Ariel]], driving in [[Caliban]], Stefano, and [[Trinculo]], in their stolen apparel |
| STEFANO | Every man shift for all the rest, and let no man take care for himself, for all is but fortune. Coragio, bully-[[Monster|monster]], coragio! |
| [[Trinculo|TRINCULO]] | If these be true spies which I wear in my head, here’s a goodly sight. |
| [[Caliban|CALIBAN]] | O Setebos, these be brave spirits indeed!<br>How fine my master is! I am afraid<br>He will chastise me. |
| [[Sebastian|SEBASTIAN]] | Ha, ha! What things are these, my lord [[Antonio]]?<br>Will money buy ’em? |
| [[Antonio|ANTONIO]] | Very like; one of them<br>Is a plain fish, and no [[Doubt|doubt]] marketable. |
| PROSPERO | Mark but the badges of these men, my lords,<br>Then say if they be true. This misshapen knave,<br>His mother was a [[Witch|witch]], and one so strong<br>That could control the [[Moon|moon]], make flows and ebbs,<br>And deal in her command without her power.<br>These three have robbed me, and this demi-devil,<br>For he’s a bastard one, had plotted with them<br>To take my life. | [[Caliban|CALIBAN]] | I shall be pinched to [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | Is not this Stefano, my drunken butler? |
| [[Sebastian|SEBASTIAN]] | He is drunk now. Where had he wine? |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | And [[Trinculo]] is reeling ripe. Where should they<br>Find this grand liquor that hath gilded ’em?<br>(To [[Trinculo]]) How cam’st thou in this pickle? |
| [[Trinculo|TRINCULO]] | I have been in such a pickle since I saw you last that, I [[Fear|fear]] me, will never out of my bones. I shall not [[Fear|fear]] fly-blowing. |
| [[Sebastian|SEBASTIAN]] | Why, how now, Stefano? |
| STEFANO | O, touch me not! I am not Stefano, but a cramp. |
| PROSPERO | You’d be [[King|king]] o’the isle, sirrah? |
| STEFANO | I should have been a sore one, then. |
| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | (pointing to [[Caliban]]) This is a strange thing as e’er I looked on. |
| PROSPERO | He is as disproportioned in his manners<br>As in his shape. (To [[Caliban]]) Go, sirrah, to my cell.<br>Take with you your companions. As you look<br>To have my pardon, trim it handsomely. |
| [[Caliban|CALIBAN]] | Ay, that I will; and I’ll be wise hereafter,<br>And seek for grace. What a thrice-[[Double|double]] ass<br>Was I to take this drunkard for a god,<br>And worship this dull fooll |

| [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | (to Stefano and [[Trinculo]])<br>Hence, and bestow your luggage where you found it. |
| [[Sebastian|SEBASTIAN]] | Or stole it, rather. |

| PROSPERO | (to [[Alonso]])<br>Sir, I invite your highness and your train<br>To my poor cell, where you shall take your rest<br>For this one [[Night|night]]; which part of it I’ll waste<br>With such discourse as I not [[Doubt|doubt]] shall make it<br>Go quick away: the story of my life,<br>And the particular accidents gone by<br>Since I came to this isle. | [[Alonso|ALONSO]] | I long<br>To hear the story of your life, which must<br>Take the ear strangely. |
| PROSPERO | I’ll deliver all,<br>And promise you calm seas, auspicious gales,<br>And sail so expeditious that shall catch<br>Your royal fleet far off. (Aside to [[Ariel]]) My [[Ariel]], chick,<br>That is thy charge. Then to the elements<br>Be free, and fare thou [[Well|well]]. |

| PROSPERO | Please you, draw near. |

| PROSPERO | Epilogue |
| PROSPERO | Now my charms are all o’erthrown,<br>And what strength I have’s mine own,<br>Which is most faint. Now ’tis true<br>I must be here confined by you<br>Or sent to Naples. Let me not,<br>Since I have my dukedom got,<br>And pardoned the deceiver, dwell<br>In this bare island by your spell;<br>But release me from my bands<br>With the help of your good hands.<br>Gentle breath of yours my sails<br>Must fill, or else my project fails,<br>Which was to please. Now I want<br>Spirits to enforce, art to enchant;<br>And my ending is [[Despair|despair]]<br>Unless I be relieved by [[Prayer|prayer]],<br>Which pierces so, that it assaults<br>[[Mercy]] itself, and frees all faults.<br>As you from crimes would pardoned be,<br>Let your indulgence set me free.<br>He awaits applause, then exit |
| [[Cardenio|CARDENIO]] | A BRIEF ACCOUNT |
| MANY | plays acted in Shakespeare’s [[Time|time]] have failed to survive; they may easily include some that he wrote. The mystery of [[Love]]’s Labour’s Won is discussed elsewhere (pp. xxxvii, 337). Certain manuscript records of the seventeenth century suggest that at least one other play in which he had a hand may have disappeared. On 9 September 1653 the [[London]] publisher Humphrey Moseley entered in the Stationers’ Register a batch of plays including ‘The [[history|History]] of [[Cardenio]], by Mr Fletcher and Shakespeare’. [[Cardenio]] is a character in Part One of Cervantes’ [[Don Quixote]], published in English [[Translation|translation]] in 1612. Two earlier allusions suggest that the [[King]]’s Men owned a play on this subject at the [[Time|time]] that Shakespeare was collaborating with John Fletcher (1579-1625). On 20 May 1613 the Privy Council authorized payment of £20 to John Heminges, as leader of the [[King]]’s Men, for the presentation at [[Court|court]] of six plays, one listed as ‘Cardenno’. On 9 July of the same year Heminges received £6 13s. 4d. for his company’s performance of a play ‘called Cardenna’ before the ambassador of the Duke of Savoy.<br>No more information about this play survives from the seventeenth century, but in 1728 Lewis Theobald published a play based on the story of [[Cardenio]] and called [[Double]] Falsehood, or The Distrest [[lovers|Lovers]], which he claimed to have ‘revised and adapted’ from one ‘written originally by W. Shakespeare’. It had been successfully produced at Drury Lane on 13 December 1727, and was given thirteen times up to 1 May 1728. Other performances are recorded in 1740, 1741, 1767 (when it was reprinted), 1770, and 1847. In 1770 a newspaper stated that ‘the original manuscript’ was ‘treasured up in the Museum of Covent [[Garden]] Playhouse’; [[Fire|fire]] destroyed the theatre, including its library, in 1808.<br>Theobald claimed to own several manuscripts of an original play by Shakespeare, and remarked that some of his contemporaries thought the style was Fletcher’s, not Shakespeare’s. When he himself came to edit Shakespeare’s plays he did not include either [[Double]] Falsehood or the play on which he claimed to have based it; he simply edited the plays of the First Folio, not adding either [[Pericles]] or The Two Noble Kinsmen, though he believed they were partly by Shakespeare. It is quite possible that [[Double]] Falsehood is based (however distantly) on a play of Shakespeare’s [[Time|time]]; if so, the play is likely to have been the one performed by the [[King]]’s Men and ascribed by Moseley in 1653 to Fletcher and Shakespeare.<br>[[Double]] Falsehood is a [[Tragicomedy|tragicomedy]]; the characters’ names differ from those in [[Don Quixote]], and the story is varied. Henriquez rapes Violante, then falls in [[Love|love]] with Leonora, loved by his friend Julio. Her parents agree to the [[Marriage|marriage]], but Julio interrupts the ceremony. Leonora (who had intended to kill herself) swoons and later takes sanctuary in a nunnery. Julio goes mad with [[Desire|desire]] for vengeance on his false friend; and the wronged Violante, disguised as a boy, joins a group of shepherds, and is almost raped by one of them. Henriquez’s virtuous brother, Roderick, ignorant of his villainy, helps him to abduct Leonora. Leonora and Violante both denounce Henriquez to Roderick. Finally Henriquez repents and marries Violante, while Julio (now sane) marries Leonora.<br>Some of the motifs of [[Double]] Falsehood, such as the disguised heroine wronged by her [[Lover|lover]] and, particularly, the reuniting and [[Reconciliation|reconciliation]] of parents with children, recall Shakespeare’s late plays. But most of the dialogue seems un-Shakespearian. Though the play deserved its limited [[success]], it is now no more than an interesting curiosity. |
