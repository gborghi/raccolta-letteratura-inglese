| *(didascalia)* | Enter Bolingbroke Duke of Lancaster and Hereford, the [[Duke of York]], the [[Earl of Northumberland]], and soldiers, with drum and colours |
| BOLINGBROKE | So that by this intelligence we learn<br>The Welshmen are dispersed, and Salisbury<br>Is gone to meet the [[King]], who lately landed<br>With some few private friends upon this coast. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | The news is very fair and good, my lord.<br>[[Richard]] not far from hence hath hid his head. |
| YORK | It would beseem the Lord Northumberland<br>To say ‘[[King]] [[Richard]]’. Alack the heavy day<br>When such a sacred [[King|king]] should hide his head! |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Your grace mistakes. Only to be brief<br>Left I his title out. |
| YORK | The [[Time|time]] hath been,<br>Would you have been so brief with him, he would<br>Have been so brief with you to shorten you,<br>For taking so the head, your whole head’s length. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Mistake not, uncle, further than you should. |
| YORK | Take not, good cousin, further than you should,<br>Lest you mistake the heavens are over our heads. |
| BOLINGBROKE | I know it, uncle, and oppose not myself<br>Against their will. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Harry Percy and a trumpeter |
| BOLINGBROKE | But who comes here?<br>Welcome, Harry. What, will not this [[Castle|castle]] yield? |
| HARRY PERCY | The [[Castle|castle]] royally is manned, my lord,<br>Against thy entrance. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Royally?<br>Why, it contains no [[King|king]]. |
| HARRY PERCY | Yes, my good lord,<br>It doth contain a [[King|king]]. [[King]] [[Richard]] lies |
| *(didascalia)* | Within the limits of yon lime and stone, |
| HARRY PERCY | And with him are the Lord Aumerle, Lord Salisbury,<br>Sir Stephen Scrope, besides a clergyman<br>Of holy reverence; who, I cannot learn. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | O, belike it is the Bishop of Carlisle. |
| BOLINGBROKE | (to Northumberland) Noble lord,<br>Go to the rude ribs of that ancient [[Castle|castle]];<br>Through brazen trumpet send the breath of parley<br>Into his ruined ears, and thus deliver.<br>[[Henry Bolingbroke]]<br>Upon his knees doth kiss [[King]] [[Richard]]’s hand,<br>And sends allegiance and true [[Faith|faith]] of heart<br>To his most royal person, hither come<br>Even at his feet to lay my arms and power,<br>Provided that my banishment repealed<br>And lands restored again be freely granted.<br>If not, I’ll use the advantage of my power,<br>And lay the [[Summer|summer]]’s [[Dust|dust]] with showers of [[Blood|blood]]<br>Rained from the wounds of slaughtered Englishmen;<br>The which how far off from the mind of Bolingbroke<br>It is such crimson tempest should bedrench<br>The fresh green lap of fair [[King]] [[Richard]]’s land,<br>My stooping duty tenderly shall show.<br>Go, signify as much, while here we march<br>Upon the grassy carpet of this plain.<br>Let’s march without the noise of threat‘ning drum,<br>That from this [[Castle|castle]]’s tottered battlements<br>Our fair appointments may be [[Well|well]] perused.<br>Methinks [[King]] [[Richard]] and myself should meet<br>With no less terror than the elements<br>Of [[Fire|fire]] and [[Water|water]] when their thund’ring shock<br>At meeting [[Tears|tears]] the cloudy cheeks of heaven.<br>Be he the [[Fire|fire]], I’ll be the yielding [[Water|water]].<br>The rage be his, whilst on the earth I rain<br>My waters: on the earth, and not on him.—<br>March on, and mark [[King]] [[Richard]], how he looks. |
| *(didascalia)* | They march about the stage; then Bolingbroke, York, Percy, and soldiers stand at a distance from the walls; Northumberland and trumpeter advance to the walls. The trumpets sound Fa parley without, and an answer within; then a flourish within. [[King]] [[Richard]] appeareth on the walls, with the Bishop of Carlisle, the [[Duke of Aumerle]], Scrope, and the [[Earl of Salisbury]] |
| BOLINGBROKE | See, see, [[King]] [[Richard]] doth himself appear,<br>As doth the blushing discontented sun<br>From out the fiery portal of the east<br>When he perceives the envious clouds are bent<br>To dim his glory and to stain the track<br>Of his bright passage to the occident. |
| YORK | Yet looks he like a [[King|king]]. Behold, his eye,<br>As bright as is the eagle’s, lightens forth<br>Controlling majesty. Alack, alack for woe<br>That any harm should stain so fair a show! |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (to Northumberland)<br>We are amazed; and thus long have we stood<br>To watch the fearful bending of thy knee,<br>Because we thought ourself thy lawful [[King|king]].<br>An if we be, how dare thy joints forget<br>To pay their aweful duty to our presence?<br>If we be not, show us the hand of God<br>That hath dismissed us from our stewardship.<br>For [[Well|well]] we know no hand of [[Blood|blood]] and bone<br>Can grip the sacred handle of our sceptre,<br>Unless he do profane, steal, or usurp.<br>And though you think that all—as you have done—<br>Have torn their souls by turning them from us,<br>And we are barren and bereft of friends,<br>Yet know my master, God omnipotent,<br>Is mustering in his clouds on our behalf<br>Armies of pestilence; and they shall strike<br>Your children yet [[unborn]] and unbegot,<br>That lift your vassal hands against my head<br>And threat the glory of my precious [[Crown|crown]].<br>Tell Bolingbroke, for yon methinks he is,<br>That every stride he makes upon my land<br>Is dangerous treason. He is come to open<br>The purple testament of bleeding war;<br>But ere the [[Crown|crown]] he looks for live in peace<br>Ten thousand bloody crowns of mothers’ sons<br>Shall ill become the flower of England’s face,<br>Change the complexion of her maid-pale peace<br>To scarlet indignation, and bedew<br>Her pastures’ grass with faithful English [[Blood|blood]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | NORTHUMBERLAND kneeling |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | The [[King]] of heaven forbid our lord the [[King]]<br>Should so with civil and uncivil arms<br>Be rushed upon. Thy thrice-noble cousin<br>Harry Bolingbroke doth humbly kiss thy hand,<br>And by the honourable tomb he swears,<br>That stands upon your royal grandsire’s bones,<br>And by the royalties of both your bloods,<br>Currents that [[Spring|spring]] from one most gracious head,<br>And by the buried hand of warlike Gaunt,<br>And by the worth and [[Honour|honour]] of himself,<br>Comprising all that may be sworn or said, no<br>His coming hither hath no further scope<br>Than for his lineal royalties, and to beg<br>Enfranchisement immediate on his knees;<br>Which on thy royal party granted once,<br>His glittering arms he will commend to rust,<br>His barbed steeds to stables, and his heart<br>To faithful service of your majesty.<br>This swears he as he is a prince and just,<br>And as I am a gentleman I credit him. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Northumberland, say thus the [[King]] returns:<br>His noble cousin is right welcome hither,<br>And all the number of his fair demands<br>Shall be accomplished without contradiction.<br>With all the gracious utterance thou hast,<br>Speak to his gentle hearing kind commends.<br>Northumberland and the trumpeter return to Bolingbroke<br>(To Aumerle) We do debase ourself, cousin, do we not,<br>To look so poorly and to speak so fair?<br>Shall we call back Northumberland, and send<br>Defiance to the traitor, and so die? |
| AUMERLE | No, good my lord, let’s fight with gentle words<br>Till [[Time|time]] lend friends, and friends their helpful swords. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | O God, O God, that e‘er this tongue of mine,<br>That laid the sentence of dread banishment<br>On yon proud man, should take it off again<br>With words of sooth! O, that I were as great<br>As is my grief, or lesser than my name,<br>Or that I could forget what I have been,<br>Or not remember what I must be now!<br>Swell’st thou, proud heart? I’ll give thee scope to beat,<br>Since foes have scope to beat both thee and me.<br>Northumberland advances to the walls |
| AUMERLE | Northumberland comes back from Bolingbroke. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | What must the [[King]] do now? Must he submit?<br>The [[King]] shall do it. Must he be deposed?<br>The [[King]] shall be contented. Must he lose<br>The name of [[King]]? A God’s name, let it go.<br>I’ll give my jewels for a set of beads,<br>My gorgeous palace for a hermitage,<br>My gay apparel for an almsman’s gown,<br>My figured goblets for a dish of wood,<br>My sceptre for a palmer’s walking staff,<br>My subjects for a pair of carved saints,<br>And my large kingdom for a little [[Grave|grave]],<br>A little, little [[Grave|grave]], an obscure [[Grave|grave]];<br>Or I’ll be buried in the [[King]]’s highway,<br>Some way of common trade where subjects’ feet<br>May hourly trample on their sovereign’s head,<br>For on my heart they tread now, whilst I live,<br>And buried once, why not upon my head?<br>Aumerle, thou weep‘st, my tender-hearted cousin.<br>We’ll make foul weather with despised [[Tears|tears]].<br>Our sighs and they shall lodge the [[Summer|summer]] corn,<br>And make a dearth in this revolting land.<br>Or shall we play the wantons with our woes,<br>And make some pretty match with shedding [[Tears|tears]];<br>As thus to drop them still upon one place<br>Till they have fretted us a pair of graves |
| *(didascalia)* | Within the earth, and therein laid? ‘There lies |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Two kinsmen digged their graves with weeping eyes.’<br>Would not this ill do [[Well|well]]? [[Well]], [[Well|well]], I see<br>I talk but idly and you mock at me.<br>Most mighty prince, my lord Northumberland,<br>What says [[King]] Bolingbroke? Will his majesty<br>Give [[Richard]] leave to live till [[Richard]] die?<br>You make a leg, and Bolingbroke says ‘Ay’. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | My lord, in the base [[Court|court]] he doth attend<br>To speak with you. May it please you to come down? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Down, down I come like glist’ring Phaethon,<br>Wanting the manage of unruly jades.<br>In the base [[Court|court]]: base [[Court|court]] where kings grow base<br>To come at traitors’ calls, and do them grace.<br>In the base [[Court|court]], come down: down [[Court|court]], down<br>[[King]],<br>For [[Night|night]]-owls shriek where mounting larks should sing. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[King]] [[Richard]] and his party |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Northumberland returns to Bolingbroke |
| BOLINGBROKE | What says his majesty? |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Sorrow and grief of heart<br>Makes him speak fondly, like a frantic man. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] [[Richard]] and his party below |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Yet he is come. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Stand all apart,<br>And show fair duty to his majesty.<br>He kneels down<br>My gracious lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Fair cousin, you debase your princely knee<br>To make the base earth proud with kissing it.<br>Me rather had my heart might feel your [[Love|love]]<br>Than my unpleased eye see your courtesy.<br>Up, cousin, up. Your heart is up, I know,<br>Thus high at least, although your knee be low. |
| BOLINGBROKE | My gracious lord, I come but for mine own. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Your own is yours, and I am yours, and all. |
| BOLINGBROKE | So far be mine, my most redoubted lord,<br>As my true service shall deserve your [[Love|love]]. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | [[Well]] you deserve. They [[Well|well]] deserve to have<br>That know the strong’st and surest way to get. |
| *(didascalia)* | Bolingbroke rises |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (To York) Uncle, give me your hands. Nay, dry your<br>eyes.<br>[[Tears]] show their [[Love|love]], but want their remedies.<br>(To Bolingbroke) Cousin, I am too young to be your father,<br>Though you are old enough to be my heir.<br>What you will have I’ll give, and willing too;<br>For do we must what force will have us do.<br>Set on towards [[London]], cousin: is it so? |
| BOLINGBROKE | Yea, my good lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Then I must not say no. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exeunt |
