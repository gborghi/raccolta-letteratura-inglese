| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Queen]], with her two Ladies |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | What sport shall we devise here in this [[Garden|garden]],<br>To drive away the heavy thought of care? |
| *(didascalia)* | first LADY Madam, we’ll play at bowls. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | ’Twill make me think the world is full of rubs,<br>And that my fortune runs against the bias. |
| *(didascalia)* | SECOND LADY Madam, we’ll [[Dance|dance]]. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | My legs can keep no measure in delight<br>When my poor heart no measure keeps in grief;<br>Therefore no dancing, girl. Some other sport. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST LADY Madam, we’ll tell tales. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Of sorrow or of joy? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST LADY Of either, madam. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Of neither, girl.<br>For if of joy, being altogether wanting,<br>It doth remember me the more of sorrow.<br>Or if of grief, being altogether had,<br>It adds more sorrow to my want of joy.<br>For what I have I need not to repeat,<br>And what I want it boots not to complain. |
| *(didascalia)* | SECOND LADY |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Madam, I’ll sing. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | ’Tis [[Well|well]] that thou hast cause;<br>But thou shouldst please me better wouldst thou weep. |
| *(didascalia)* | SECOND LADY |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | I could weep, madam, would it do you good. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | And I could sing, would weeping do me good,<br>And never borrow any tear of thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Gardener and two Men |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | But stay; here come the gardeners.<br>Let’s step into the [[Shadow|shadow]] of these trees.<br>My wretchedness unto a row of pins<br>They will talk of state, for everyone doth so<br>Against a change. Woe is forerun with woe.<br>The [[Queen]] and her Ladies stand apart |
| *(didascalia)* | GARDENER to First Man |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Go, bind thou up young dangling apricots<br>Which, like unruly children, make their sire<br>Stoop with oppression of their prodigal weight.<br>Give some supportance to the bending twigs. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Second Man Go thou, and, like an executioner, |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Cut off the heads of too fast-growing sprays<br>That look too lofty in our commonwealth.<br>All must be even in our [[government]].<br>You thus employed, I will go root away<br>The noisome weeds which without profit suck<br>The soil’s fertility from wholesome [[Flowers|flowers]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST MAN |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Why should we, in the compass of a pale,<br>Keep law and form and due proportion,<br>Showing as in a model our firm estate,<br>When our sea-wallèd [[Garden|garden]], the whole land,<br>Is full of weeds, her fairest [[Flowers|flowers]] choked up,<br>Her fruit trees all unpruned, her hedges ruined,<br>Her knots disordered, and her wholesome herbs<br>Swarming with caterpillars? |
| GARDENER | Hold thy peace.<br>He that hath suffered this disordered [[Spring|spring]]<br>Hath now himself met with the fall of leaf.<br>The weeds which his broad spreading [[Leaves|leaves]] did<br>shelter,<br>That seemed in eating him to hold him up,<br>Are plucked up, root and all, by Bolingbroke—<br>I mean the Earl of Wiltshire, Bushy, Green. |
| *(didascalia)* | SECOND MAN |
| GARDENER | What, are they dead? |
| GARDENER | They are; and Bolingbroke<br>Hath seized the wasteful [[King]]. O, what pity is it<br>That he had not so trimmed and dressed his land<br>As we this [[Garden|garden]]! We at [[Time|time]] of year<br>Do wound the bark, the skin of our fruit trees,<br>Lest, being over-proud in sap and [[Blood|blood]],<br>With too much riches it confound itself.<br>Had he done so to great and growing men,<br>They might have lived to bear, and he to taste,<br>Their fruits of duty. Superfluous branches<br>We lop away, that bearing boughs may live.<br>Had he done so, himself had borne the [[Crown|crown]],<br>Which waste of idle hours hath quite thrown down. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST MAN |
| GARDENER | What, think you then the [[King]] shall be deposed? |
| GARDENER | Depressed he is already, and deposed<br>’Tis [[Doubt|doubt]] he will be. Letters came last [[Night|night]]<br>To a dear friend of the good [[Duke of York]]’s<br>That tell black tidings. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | O, I am pressed to [[Death|death]] through want of speaking!<br>She comes forward<br>Thou, old Adam’s likeness, set to dress this [[Garden|garden]],<br>How dares thy harsh rude tongue sound this unpleasing news?<br>What Eve, what serpent hath suggested thee<br>To make a second fall of cursed man?<br>Why dost thou say [[King]] [[Richard]] is deposed?<br>Dar‘st thou, thou little better thing than earth,<br>Divine his downfall? Say where, when, and how<br>Cam’st thou by this ill tidings? Speak, thou wretch! |
| GARDENER | Pardon me, madam. Little joy have I<br>To breathe this news, yet what I say is true.<br>[[King]] [[Richard]] he is in the mighty hold<br>Of Bolingbroke. Their fortunes both are weighed.<br>In your lord’s scale is nothing but himself<br>And some few vanities that make him light.<br>But in the balance of great Bolingbroke,<br>Besides himself, are all the English peers,<br>And with that odds he weighs [[King]] [[Richard]] down.<br>Post you to [[London]] and you will find it so.<br>I speak no more than everyone doth know. |
| [[Queen|QUEEN]] | Nimble mischance that art so light of foot,<br>Doth not thy embassage belong to me,<br>And am I last that knows it? O, thou think‘st<br>To serve me last, that I may longest keep<br>Thy sorrow in my breast. Come, ladies, go<br>To meet at [[London]] [[London]]’s [[King|king]] in woe.<br>What, was I born to this, that my sad look<br>Should grace the triumph of great Bolingbroke?<br>Gard’ner, for telling me these news of woe,<br>Pray God the plants thou graft’st may never grow. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with her Ladies |
| GARDENER | Poor [[Queen]], so that thy state might be no worse<br>I would my skill were subject to thy curse.<br>Here did she fall a tear. Here in this place<br>I’ll set a bank of rue, sour herb-of-grace.<br>Rue even for ruth here shortly shall be seen<br>In the remembrance of a weeping [[Queen|queen]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
