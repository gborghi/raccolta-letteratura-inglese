| *(didascalia)* | Enter, as to Parliament, Bolingbroke Duke of Lancaster and Hereford, the [[Duke of Aumerle]], the [[Earl of Northumberland]], Harry Percy, Lord Fitzwalter, the Duke of Surrey, the Bishop of Carlisle, and the Abbot of Westminster |
| BOLINGBROKE | Call forth Bagot. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Bagot, with officers |
| BOLINGBROKE | Now, Bagot, freely speak thy mind:<br>What thou dost know of noble [[Gloucester]]’s [[Death|death]],<br>Who wrought it with the [[King]], and who performed<br>The bloody office of his timeless end. |
| BAGOT | Then set before my face the Lord Aumerle. |
| BOLINGBROKE | (to Aumerle)<br>Cousin, stand forth, and look upon that man.<br>Aumerle stands forth |
| BAGOT | My lord Aumerle, I know your daring tongue<br>Scorns to unsay what once it hath delivered.<br>In that dead [[Time|time]] when [[Gloucester]]’s [[Death|death]] was plotted<br>I heard you say ‘Is not my arm of length,<br>That reacheth from the restful English [[Court|court]]<br>As far as Calais, to mine uncle’s head?’<br>Amongst much other talk that very [[Time|time]]<br>I heard you say that you had rather refuse<br>The offer of an hundred thousand crowns<br>Than Bolingbroke’s return to England,<br>Adding withal how blest this land would be<br>In this your cousin’s [[Death|death]]. |
| AUMERLE | Princes and noble lords,<br>What answer shall I make to this base man?<br>Shall I so much dishonour my fair [[Stars|stars]]<br>On equal terms to give him chastisement?<br>Either I must, or have mine [[Honour|honour]] soiled<br>With the attainder of his slanderous lips.<br>He throws down his gage<br>There is my gage, the manual seal of [[Death|death]]<br>That marks thee out for hell. I say thou liest,<br>And will maintain what thou hast said is false<br>In thy heart [[Blood|blood]], though being all too base<br>To stain the temper of my knightly [[Sword|sword]]. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Bagot, forbear. Thou shalt not take it up. |
| AUMERLE | Excepting one, I would he were the best<br>In all this presence that hath moved me so. |
| FITZWALTER | If that thy valour stand on sympathy,<br>There is my gage, Aumerle, in gage to thine.<br>He throws down his gage<br>By that fair sun which shows me where thou stand‘st,<br>I heard thee say, and vauntingly thou spak’st it,<br>That thou wert cause of noble [[Gloucester]]’s [[Death|death]].<br>If thou deny’st it twenty times, thou liest,<br>And I will turn thy falsehood to thy heart,<br>Where it was forged, with my rapier’s point. |
| AUMERLE | Thou dar’st not, coward, live to see that day. |
| FITZWALTER | Now by my [[Soul|soul]], I would it were this hour. |
| AUMERLE | Fitzwalter, thou art damned to hell for this. |
| HARRY PERCY | Aumerle, thou liest. His [[Honour|honour]] is as true<br>In this appeal as thou art all unjust;<br>And that thou art so, there I throw my gage<br>He throws down his gage<br>To prove it on thee to the extremest point<br>Of mortal breathing. Seize it if thou dar’st. |
| AUMERLE | An if I do not, may my hands rot off,<br>And never brandish more revengeful steel<br>Over the glittering helmet of my foe. |
| SURREY | My lord Fitzwalter, I do remember [[Well|well]]<br>The very [[Time|time]] Aumerle and you did talk. |
| FITZWALTER | ’Tis very true. You were in presence then,<br>And you can witness with me this is true. |
| SURREY | As false, by heaven, as heaven itself is true. |
| FITZWALTER | Surrey, thou liest. |
| SURREY | Dishonourable boy,<br>That lie shall lie so heavy on my [[Sword|sword]]<br>That it shall render vengeance and [[Revenge|revenge]],<br>Till thou, the lie-giver, and that lie do lie<br>In earth as quiet as thy father’s skull;<br>In proof whereof, there is my [[Honour|honour]]’s pawn.<br>He throws down his gage<br>Engage it to the trial if thou dar’st. |
| FITZWALTER | How fondly dost thou spur a forward [[Horse|horse]]!<br>If I dare eat, or drink, or breathe, or live,<br>I dare meet Surrey in a wilderness<br>And spit upon him whilst I say he lies,<br>And lies, and lies. There is my bond of [[Faith|faith]]<br>To tie thee to my strong correction.<br>As I intend to thrive in this new world,<br>Aumerle is guilty of my true appeal.<br>Besides, I heard the banished Norfolk say<br>That thou, Aumerle, didst send two of thy men<br>To execute the noble Duke at Calais. |
| AUMERLE | Some honest Christian trust me with a gage.<br>He takes another’s gage and throws it down<br>That Norfolk lies, here do I throw down this,<br>If he may be repealed, to try his [[Honour|honour]]. |
| BOLINGBROKE | These differences shall all rest under gage<br>Till Norfolk be repealed. Repealed he shall be,<br>And, though mine enemy, restored again<br>To all his lands and signories. When he is returned,<br>Against Aumerle we will enforce his trial. |
| BISHOP OF CARLISLE | That honourable day shall never be seen.<br>Many a [[Time|time]] hath banished Norfolk fought<br>For Jesu [[Christ]] in glorious Christian field,<br>Streaming the ensign of the Christian [[Cross|cross]]<br>Against black pagans, Turks, and Saracens;<br>And, toiled with works of war, retired himself<br>To [[Italy]], and there at [[Venice]] gave<br>His [[Body|body]] to that pleasant country’s earth,<br>And his pure [[Soul|soul]] unto his captain, [[Christ]],<br>Under whose colours he had fought so long. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Why, Bishop of Carlisle, is Norfolk dead? |
| BISHOP OF CARLISLE | As surely as I live, my lord. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Sweet peace conduct his sweet [[Soul|soul]] to the bosom<br>Of good old Abraham! Lords appellants,<br>Your differences shall all rest under gage<br>Till we assign you to your days of trial. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of York]] |
| YORK | Great Duke of Lancaster, I come to thee<br>From plume-plucked [[Richard]], who with willing [[Soul|soul]]<br>Adopts thee heir, and his high sceptre yields<br>To the possession of thy royal hand.<br>Ascend his throne, descending now from him,<br>And long live Henry, of that name the fourth! |
| BOLINGBROKE | In God’s name I’ll ascend the regal throne. |
| BISHOP OF CARLISLE | Marry, God forbid!<br>Worst in this royal presence may I speak,<br>Yet best beseeming me to speak the [[Truth|truth]].<br>Would God that any in this noble presence<br>Were enough noble to be upright judge<br>Of noble [[Richard]]. Then true noblesse would<br>Learn him forbearance from so foul a wrong.<br>What subject can give sentence on his [[King|king]]?<br>And who sits here that is not [[Richard]]’s subject?<br>Thieves are not judged but they are by to hear,<br>Although apparent [[Guilt|guilt]] be seen in them;<br>And shall the figure of God’s majesty,<br>His captain, steward, deputy elect,<br>Anointed, crowned, planted many years,<br>Be judged by subject and inferior breath,<br>And he himself not present? O, forfend it, God,<br>That in a Christian climate souls refined<br>Should show so heinous, black, obscene a deed!<br>I speak to subjects, and a subject speaks<br>Stirred up by God thus boldly for his [[King|king]].<br>My lord of Hereford here, whom you call [[King|king]],<br>Is a foul traitor to proud Hereford’s [[King|king]];<br>And, if you [[Crown|crown]] him, let me prophesy<br>The [[Blood|blood]] of English shall manure the ground,<br>And future ages groan for this foul act.<br>Peace shall go [[Sleep|sleep]] with Turks and infidels,<br>And in this seat of peace tumultuous wars<br>Shall kin with kin and kind with kind confound.<br>Disorder, horror, [[Fear|fear]], and mutiny<br>Shall here inhabit, and this land be called<br>The field of Golgotha and dead men’s skulls.<br>O, if you rear this [[house]] against this [[house]]<br>It will the woefullest division prove<br>That ever fell upon this cursed earth!<br>Prevent, resist it; let it not be so,<br>Lest [[Child|child]], [[Child|child]]’s children, cry against you woe. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | [[Well]] have you argued, sir, and for your pains<br>Of capital treason we arrest you here.<br>My lord of Westminster, be it your charge<br>To keep him safely till his day of trial.<br>May it please you, lords, to grant the Commons’ suit? |
| BOLINGBROKE | Fetch hither [[Richard]], that in common view<br>He may surrender. So we shall proceed<br>Without suspicion. |
| YORK | I will be his conduct. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| BOLINGBROKE | Lords, you that here are under our arrest,<br>Procure your sureties for your days of answer.<br>Little are we beholden to your [[Love|love]],<br>And little looked for at your helping hands. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Richard]] and the [[Duke of York]], with attendants bearing the [[Crown|crown]] and sceptre |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Alack, why am I sent for to a [[King|king]]<br>Before I have shook off the regal thoughts<br>Wherewith I reigned? I hardly yet have learned<br>To insinuate, flatter, bow, and bend my knee.<br>Give sorrow leave awhile to tutor me<br>To this submission. Yet I [[Well|well]] remember<br>The favours of these men. Were they not mine?<br>Did they not sometime cry ‘All haill’ to me?<br>So Judas did to [[Christ]]. But He in twelve<br>Found [[Truth|truth]] in all but one; I, in twelve thousand, none.<br>God save the [[King]] ! Will no man say ‘Amen’ ?<br>Am I both priest and clerk? [[Well]] then, Amen.<br>God save the [[King]], although I be not he.<br>And yet Amen, if heaven do think him me.<br>To do what service am I sent for hither? |
| YORK | To do that office of thine own good will<br>Which tired majesty did make thee offer:<br>The [[Resignation|resignation]] of thy state and [[Crown|crown]]<br>To [[Henry Bolingbroke]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (to an attendant)<br>Give me the [[Crown|crown]]. (To Bolingbroke) Here, cousin, seize the [[Crown|crown]].<br>Here, cousin. On this side my hand, on that side thine.<br>Now is this golden [[Crown|crown]] like a deep [[Well|well]]<br>That owes two buckets filling one another,<br>The emptier ever dancing in the air,<br>The other down, unseen, and full of [[Water|water]].<br>That bucket down and full of [[Tears|tears]] am I,<br>Drinking my griefs, whilst you mount up on high. |
| BOLINGBROKE | I thought you had been willing to resign. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | My [[Crown|crown]] I am, but still my griefs are mine.<br>You may my glories and my state depose,<br>But not my griefs; still am I [[King|king]] of those. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Part of your cares you give me with your [[Crown|crown]]. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Your cares set up do not pluck my cares down.<br>My care is loss of care by old care done;<br>Your care is gain of care by new care won.<br>The cares I give I have, though given away;<br>They ’tend the [[Crown|crown]], yet still with me they stay. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Are you contented to resign the [[Crown|crown]]? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ay, no; no, ay; for I must nothing be;<br>Therefore no, no, for I resign to thee.<br>Now mark me how I will undo myself.<br>I give this heavy weight from off my head,<br>[Bolingbroke accepts the [[Crown|crown]]]<br>And this unwieldy sceptre from my hand, |
| *(didascalia)* | Bolingbroke accepts the sceptre |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | The [[Pride|pride]] of kingly sway from out my heart.<br>With mine own [[Tears|tears]] I wash away my balm,<br>With mine own hands I give away my [[Crown|crown]],<br>With mine own tongue deny my sacred state,<br>With mine own breath release all duteous oaths.<br>All pomp and majesty I do forswear.<br>My manors, rents, revenues I forgo.<br>My acts, decrees, and statutes I deny.<br>God pardon all oaths that are broke to me.<br>God keep all vows unbroke are made to thee.<br>Make me, that nothing have, with nothing grieved,<br>And thou with all pleased, that hast all achieved.<br>Long mayst thou live in [[Richard]]’s seat to sit,<br>And soon lie [[Richard]] in an earthy pit.<br>‘God save [[King]] Henry,’ unkinged [[Richard]] says,<br>‘And send him many years of sunshine days.’<br>What more remains? |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | (giving [[Richard]] papers)<br>No more but that you read<br>These accusations and these grievous crimes<br>Committed by your person and your followers<br>Against the state and profit of this land,<br>That by confessing them, the souls of men<br>May deem that you are worthily deposed. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Must I do so? And must I ravel out<br>My weaved-up follies ? Gentle Northumberland,<br>If thy offences were upon record,<br>Would it not shame thee in so fair a troop<br>To read a lecture of them? If thou wouldst,<br>There shouldst thou find one heinous article<br>Containing the deposing of a [[King|king]]<br>And cracking the strong warrant of an oath,<br>Marked with a blot, damned in the book of heaven.<br>Nay, all of you that stand and look upon<br>Whilst that my wretchedness doth bait myself,<br>Though some of you, with Pilate, wash your hands,<br>Showing an outward pity, yet you Pitates<br>Have here delivered me to my sour [[Cross|cross]],<br>And [[Water|water]] cannot wash away your sin. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | My lord, dispatch. Read o’er these articles. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Mine eyes are full of [[Tears|tears]]; I cannot see.<br>And yet salt [[Water|water]] blinds them not so much<br>But they can see a sort of traitors here.<br>Nay, if I turn mine eyes upon myself<br>I find myself a traitor with the rest,<br>For I have given here my [[Soul|soul]]’s consent<br>T’undeck the pompous [[Body|body]] of a [[King|king]],<br>Made glory base and sovereignty a slave,<br>Proud majesty a subject, state a peasant. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | My lord— |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | No lord of thine, thou haught-insulting man,<br>Nor no man’s lord. I have no name, no title,<br>No, not that name was given me at the font,<br>But ’tis usurped. Alack the heavy day,<br>That I have worn so many winters out<br>And know not now what name to call myself!<br>O, that I were a mockery [[King|king]] of [[Snow|snow]],<br>Standing before the sun of Bolingbroke<br>To melt myself away in [[Water|water]]-drops !<br>Good [[King|king]], great [[King|king]]—and yet not greatly good—<br>An if my word be sterling yet in England,<br>Let it command a [[Mirror|mirror]] hither straight,<br>That it may show me what a face I have,<br>Since it is bankrupt of his majesty. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Go some of you and fetch a looking-glass. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one or more |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Read o’er this paper while the glass doth come. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Fiend, thou torment’st me ere I come to hell. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Urge it no more, my lord Northumberland. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | The Commons will not then be satisfied. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | They shall be satisfied. I’ll read enough<br>When I do see the very book indeed<br>Where all my sins are writ, and that’s myself. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter one with a glass |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Give me that glass, and therein will I read.<br>[[Richard]] takes the glass and looks in it<br>No deeper wrinkles yet? Hath sorrow struck<br>So many blows upon this face of mine<br>And made no deeper wounds? O flatt’ring glass,<br>Like to my followers in prosperity,<br>Thou dost beguile me! Was this face the face<br>That every day under his household roof<br>Did keep ten thousand men? Was this the face<br>That like the sun did make beholders wink?<br>Is this the face which faced so many follies,<br>That was at last outfaced by Bolingbroke?<br>A brittle glory shineth in this face.<br>As brittle as the glory is the face,<br>He shatters the glass<br>For there it is, cracked in an hundred shivers.<br>Mark, silent [[King]], the moral of this sport:<br>How soon my sorrow hath destroyed my face. |
| BOLINGBROKE | The [[Shadow|shadow]] of your sorrow hath destroyed<br>The [[Shadow|shadow]] of your face. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Say that again:<br>‘The [[Shadow|shadow]] of my sorrow’—ha, let’s see.<br>‘Tis very true: my grief lies all within,<br>And these external manner of laments<br>Are merely shadows to the unseen grief<br>That swells with [[Silence|silence]] in the tortured [[Soul|soul]].<br>There lies the substance, and I thank thee, [[King]],<br>For thy great bounty that not only giv’st<br>Me cause to wail, but teachest me the way<br>How to lament the cause. I’ll beg one boon,<br>And then be gone and trouble you no more.<br>Shall I obtain it? |
| BOLINGBROKE | Name it, fair cousin. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Fair cousin? I am greater than a [[King|king]];<br>For when I was a [[King|king]] my flatterers<br>Were then but subjects; being now a subject,<br>I have a [[King|king]] here to my flatterer.<br>Being so great, I have no need to beg. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Yet ask. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | And shall I have? |
| BOLINGBROKE | You shall. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Then give me leave to go. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Whither? |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Whither you will, so I were from your sights. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Go some of you, convey him to the Tower. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | O good, ‘convey’! Conveyors are you all,<br>That rise thus nimbly by a true [[King|king]]’s fall. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit, guarded |
| BOLINGBROKE | On Wednesday next we solemnly set down<br>Our coronation. Lords, prepare yourselves. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but the Abbot of Westminster, the Bishop of Carlisle, and Aumerle |
| ABBOT OF WESTMINSTER | A woeful pageant have we here beheld. |
| BISHOP OF CARLISLE | The woe’s to come, the children yet [[unborn]]<br>Shall feel this day as sharp to them as thorn. |
| AUMERLE | You holy clergymen, is there no plot<br>To rid the realm of this pernicious blot? |
| ABBOT OF WESTMINSTER | My lord, before I freely speak my mind herein,<br>You shall not only take the sacrament<br>To bury mine intents, but also to effect<br>Whatever I shall happen to devise.<br>I see your brows are full of discontent,<br>Your hearts of sorrow, and your eyes of [[Tears|tears]].<br>Come [[Home|home]] with me to supper. I will lay<br>A plot shall show us all a merry day. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
