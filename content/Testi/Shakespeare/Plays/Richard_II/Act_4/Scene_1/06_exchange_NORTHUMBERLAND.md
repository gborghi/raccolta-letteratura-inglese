| NORTHUMBERLAND | (giving [[Richard]] papers) No more but that you read These accusations and these grievous crimes Committed by your person and your followers Against the state and profit of this land, That by confessing them, the souls of men May deem that you are worthily deposed. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Must I do so? And must I ravel out My weaved-up follies ? Gentle Northumberland, If thy offences were upon record, Would it not shame thee in so fair a troop To read a lecture of them? If thou wouldst, There shouldst thou find one heinous article Containing the deposing of a [[King|king]] And cracking the strong warrant of an oath, Marked with a blot, damned in the book of heaven. Nay, all of you that stand and look upon Whilst that my wretchedness doth bait myself, Though some of you, with Pilate, wash your hands, Showing an outward pity, yet you Pitates Have here delivered me to my sour [[Cross|cross]], And [[Water|water]] cannot wash away your sin. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | My lord, dispatch. Read o’er these articles. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Mine eyes are full of [[Tears|tears]]; I cannot see. And yet salt [[Water|water]] blinds them not so much But they can see a sort of traitors here. Nay, if I turn mine eyes upon myself I find myself a traitor with the rest, For I have given here my [[Soul|soul]]’s consent T’undeck the pompous [[Body|body]] of a [[King|king]], Made glory base and sovereignty a slave, Proud majesty a subject, state a peasant. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | My lord— |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | No lord of thine, thou haught-insulting man, Nor no man’s lord. I have no name, no title, No, not that name was given me at the font, But ’tis usurped. Alack the heavy day, That I have worn so many winters out And know not now what name to call myself! O, that I were a mockery [[King|king]] of [[Snow|snow]], Standing before the sun of Bolingbroke To melt myself away in [[Water|water]]-drops ! Good [[King|king]], great [[King|king]]—and yet not greatly good— An if my word be sterling yet in England, Let it command a [[Mirror|mirror]] hither straight, That it may show me what a face I have, Since it is bankrupt of his majesty. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Go some of you and fetch a looking-glass. Exit one or more |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | Read o’er this paper while the glass doth come. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Fiend, thou torment’st me ere I come to hell. |
| BOLINGBROKE | Urge it no more, my lord Northumberland. |
| NORTHUMBERLAND | The Commons will not then be satisfied. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | They shall be satisfied. I’ll read enough When I do see the very book indeed Where all my sins are writ, and that’s myself. Enter one with a glass Give me that glass, and therein will I read. [[Richard]] takes the glass and looks in it No deeper wrinkles yet? Hath sorrow struck So many blows upon this face of mine And made no deeper wounds? O flatt’ring glass, Like to my followers in prosperity, Thou dost beguile me! Was this face the face That every day under his household roof Did keep ten thousand men? Was this the face That like the sun did make beholders wink? Is this the face which faced so many follies, That was at last outfaced by Bolingbroke? A brittle glory shineth in this face. As brittle as the glory is the face, He shatters the glass For there it is, cracked in an hundred shivers. Mark, silent [[King]], the moral of this sport: How soon my sorrow hath destroyed my face. |
