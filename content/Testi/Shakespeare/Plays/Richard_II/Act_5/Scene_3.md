| *(didascalia)* | Enter Bolingbroke, crowned [[King]] Henry, with Harry Percy, and other nobles |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Can no man tell of my unthrifty son?<br>‘Tis full three months since I did see him last.<br>If any plague hang over us, ’tis he.<br>I would to God, my lords, he might be found.<br>Enquire at [[London]] ’mongst the taverns there,<br>For there, they say, he daily doth frequent<br>With unrestrained loose companions—<br>Even such, they say, as stand in narrow lanes<br>And beat our watch and rob our passengers—<br>Which he, young wanton and effeminate boy,<br>Takes on the point of [[Honour|honour]] to support<br>So dissolute a crew. |
| HARRY PERCY | My lord, some two days since, I saw the Prince,<br>And told him of these triumphs held at [[Oxford]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | And what said the gallant? |
| HARRY PERCY | His answer was he would unto the stews,<br>And from the common’st creature pluck a glove,<br>And wear it as a favour, and with that<br>He would unhorse the lustiest challenger. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | As dissolute as desperate. Yet through both<br>I see some sparks of better [[Hope|hope]], which elder days<br>May happily bring forth. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Duke of Aumerle]], amazed |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | But who comes here? |
| AUMERLE | Where is the [[King]]? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | What means our cousin that he stares and looks so wildly? |
| AUMERLE | (kneeling)<br>God save your grace! I do beseech your majesty<br>To have some conference with your grace [[Alone|alone]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to lords)<br>Withdraw yourselves, and leave us here [[Alone|alone]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[King]] Henry and Aumerle |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | What is the matter with our cousin now? |
| AUMERLE | For ever may my knees grow to the earth,<br>My tongue cleave to the roof within my mouth,<br>Unless a pardon ere I rise or speak. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Intended or committed was this fault?<br>If on the first, how heinous e’er it be,<br>To win thy after-[[Love|love]] I pardon thee. |
| AUMERLE | (rising)<br>Then give me leave that I may turn the key,<br>That no man enter till my tale be done. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Have thy [[Desire|desire]].<br>Aumerle locks the door.<br>The [[Duke of York]] knocks at the door and crieth |
| YORK | (within) My liege, beware! Look to thyself!<br>Thou hast a traitor in thy presence there.<br>[[King]] Henry draws his [[Sword|sword]] |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to Aumerle) [[Villain]], I’ll make thee safe. |
| AUMERLE | Stay thy revengeful hand! Thou hast no cause to [[Fear|fear]]. |
| YORK | (knocking within)<br>Open the door, secure foolhardy [[King]]!<br>Shall I for [[Love|love]] speak treason to thy face?<br>Open the door, or I will break it open. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King]] Henry opens the door. Enter the [[Duke of York]] |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | What is the matter, uncle? Speak,<br>Recover breath, tell us how near is danger,<br>That we may arm us to encounter it. |
| YORK | Peruse this writing here, and thou shalt know<br>The treason that my haste forbids me show.<br>He gives [[King]] Henry the paper |
| AUMERLE | Remember, as thou read’st, thy promise past.<br>I do repent me. Read not my name there.<br>My heart is not confederate with my hand. |
| YORK | It was, [[Villain|villain]], ere thy hand did set it down.<br>I tore it from the traitor’s bosom, [[King]].<br>[[Fear]], and not [[Love|love]], begets his penitence.<br>Forget to pity him, lest pity prove<br>A serpent that will sting thee to the heart. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | O, heinous, strong, and bold conspiracy!<br>O loyal father of a treacherous son!<br>Thou sheer, immaculate, and silver fountain,<br>From whence this stream through muddy passages<br>Hath held his current and defiled himself,<br>Thy overflow of good converts to bad,<br>And thy abundant goodness shall excuse<br>This deadly blot in thy digressing son. |
| YORK | So shall my virtue be his vice’s bawd,<br>And he shall spend mine [[Honour|honour]] with his shame,<br>As thriftless sons their scraping fathers’ gold.<br>Mine [[Honour|honour]] lives when his dishonour dies,<br>Or my shamed life in his dishonour lies.<br>Thou kill’st me in his life: giving him breath<br>The traitor lives, the true man’s put to [[Death|death]]. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | (within)<br>What ho, my liege, for God’s sake let me in! |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | What shrill-voiced suppliant makes this eager cry? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | (within)<br>A woman, and thy aunt, great [[King]]; ’tis I.<br>Speak with me, pity me! Open the door!<br>A beggar begs that never begged before. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Our scene is altered from a serious thing,<br>And now changed to ‘The Beggar and the [[King]]’.<br>My dangerous cousin, let your mother in.<br>I know she is come to pray for your foul sin.<br>Aumerle opens the door. Enter the Duchess of York |
| YORK | If thou do pardon, whosoever pray,<br>More sins for this [[Forgiveness|forgiveness]] prosper may.<br>This festered joint cut off, the rest rest sound.<br>This let [[Alone|alone]] will all the rest confound. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | (kneeling)<br>O [[King]], believe not this hard-hearted man.<br>[[Love]] loving not itself, none other can. |
| YORK | Thou frantic woman, what dost thou make here?<br>Shall thy old dugs once more a traitor rear? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Sweet York, be patient.—Hear me, gentle liege. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Rise up, good aunt. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Not yet, I thee beseech.<br>Forever will I kneel upon my knees,<br>And never see day that the happy sees,<br>Till thou give joy, until thou bid me joy<br>By pardoning Rutland, my transgressing boy. |
| AUMERLE | (kneeling)<br>Unto my mother’s prayers I bend my knee. |
| YORK | (kneeling)<br>Against them both my true joints bended be.<br>Ill mayst thou thrive if thou grant any grace. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Pleads he in earnest? Look upon his face.<br>His eyes do drop no [[Tears|tears]], his prayers are in jest.<br>His words come from his mouth; ours from our<br>breast.<br>He prays but faintly, and would be denied;<br>We pray with heart and [[Soul|soul]], and all beside.<br>His weary joints would gladly rise, I know;<br>Our knees shall kneel till to the ground they grow.<br>His prayers are full of false hypocrisy;<br>Ours of true zeal and deep integrity.<br>Our prayers do outpray his; then let them have<br>That [[Mercy|mercy]] which true [[prayer]] ought to have. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[King|KING]] HENRY |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Good aunt, stand up. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Nay, do not say ‘Stand up’.<br>Say ‘Pardon’ first, and afterwards ‘Stand up’.<br>An if I were thy [[Nurse|nurse]], thy tongue to teach,<br>‘Pardon’ should be the first word of thy speech.<br>I never longed to hear a word till now.<br>Say ‘Pardon’, [[King]]. Let pity teach thee how.<br>The word is short, but not so short as sweet;<br>No word like ’Pardon’ for kings’ mouths so meet. |
| YORK | Speak it in French, [[King]]: say ‘Pardonnez-moi’. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Dost thou teach pardon pardon to destroy?<br>Ah, my sour husband, my hard-hearted lord<br>That sets the word itself against the word!<br>Speak ‘Pardon’ as ’tis current in our land;<br>The chopping French we do not understand.<br>Thine eye begins to speak; set thy tongue there;<br>Or in thy piteous heart plant thou thine ear,<br>That hearing how our plaints and prayers do pierce,<br>Pity may move thee ’Pardon’ to rehearse. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Good aunt, stand up. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | I do not sue to stand.<br>Pardon is all the suit I have in hand. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | I pardon him as God shall pardon me. |
| *(didascalia)* | York and Aumerle rise |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | O, happy vantage of a kneeling knee!<br>Yet am I sick for [[Fear|fear]]. Speak it again.<br>Twice saying pardon doth not pardon twain,<br>But makes one pardon strong. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | I pardon him<br>With all my heart. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | (rising) A god on earth thou art. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | But for our trusty brother-in-law and the Abbot,<br>With all the rest of that consorted crew,<br>Destruction straight shall dog them at the heels.<br>Good uncle, help to order several powers<br>To [[Oxford]], or where’er these traitors are.<br>They shall not live within this world, I swear,<br>But I will have them if I once know where.<br>Uncle, farewell; and cousin, so adieu.<br>Your mother [[Well|well]] hath prayed; and prove you true. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Come, my old son. I pray God make thee new. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[King]] Henry at one door; York, the Duchess of York, and Aumerle at another door |
