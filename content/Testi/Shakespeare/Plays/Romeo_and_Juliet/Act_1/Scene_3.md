| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Capulet]]’s Wife and the [[Nurse]] |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | [[Nurse]], where’s my daughter? Call her forth to me. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Now, by my maidenhead at twelve year old,<br>I bade her come. What, [[Lamb|lamb]], what, ladybird—<br>God forbid—where is this girl? What, [[Juliet]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Juliet]] |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | How now, who calls? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Your mother. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Madam, I am here. What is your will? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | This is the matter.—[[Nurse]], give leave a while.<br>We must talk in secret.—[[Nurse]], come back again.<br>I have remembered me, thou s’ hear our counsel.<br>Thou knowest my daughter’s of a pretty age. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | [[Faith]], I can tell her age unto an hour. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | She’s not fourteen. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | I’ll lay fourteen of my teeth—and yet, to my teen be it spoken, I have but four—she’s not fourteen. How long is it now to Lammastide? |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | A fortnight and odd days. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Even or odd, of all days in the year<br>Come Lammas Eve at [[Night|night]] shall she be fourteen.<br>Susan and she—God rest all Christian souls!—<br>Were of an age. [[Well]], Susan is with God;<br>She was too good for me. But, as I said,<br>On Lammas Eve at [[Night|night]] shall she be fourteen,<br>That shall she, marry, I remember it [[Well|well]].<br>‘Tis since the earthquake now eleven years,<br>And she was weaned—I never shall forget it–<br>Of all the days of the year upon that day,<br>For I had then laid wormwood to my dug,<br>Sitting in the sun under the dovehouse wall.<br>My lord and you were then at Mantua.<br>Nay, I do bear a brain! But, as I said,<br>When it did taste the wormwood on the nipple<br>Of my dug and felt it bitter, pretty [[Fool|fool]],<br>To see it tetchy and fall out wi’th’ dug!<br>‘Shake’, quoth the dove-housed‘Twas no need, I trow,<br>To bid me trudge;<br>And since that [[Time|time]] it is eleven years,<br>For then she could stand high-lone. Nay, by th’ rood,<br>She could have run and waddled all about,<br>For even the day before, she broke her brow,<br>And then my husband—God be with his [[Soul|soul]],<br>A was a merry man!—took up the [[Child|child]].<br>‘Yea,’ quoth he, ‘dost thou fall upon thy face?<br>Thou wilt fall backward when thou hast more wit,<br>Wilt thou not, Jule?’ And, by my halidom,<br>The pretty wretch left crying and said ‘Ay’.<br>To see now how a jest shall come about!<br>I warrant an I should live a thousand years<br>I never should forget it. ‘Wilt thou not, Jule?’ quoth he,<br>And, pretty [[Fool|fool]], it stinted and said ‘Ay’. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Enough of this. I pray thee hold thy peace. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Yes, madam. Yet I cannot choose but laugh<br>To think it should leave crying and say ‘Ay’.<br>And yet, I warrant, it had upon it brow<br>A bump as big as a young cock‘rel’s stone.<br>A perilous knock, and it cried bitterly.<br>‘Yea,’ quoth my husband, ‘fall’st upon thy face?<br>Thou wilt fall backward when thou com’st to age,<br>Wilt thou not, Jule?’ It stinted and said ‘Ay’. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | And stint thou too, I pray thee, [[Nurse]], say I. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Peace, I have done. God mark thee to his grace,<br>Thou wast the prettiest babe that e’er I nursed.<br>An I might live to see thee married once,<br>I have my wish. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | Marry, that ’marry’ is the very theme<br>I came to talk of. Tell me, daughter [[Juliet]],<br>How stands your dispositions to be married? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | It is an [[Honour|honour]] that I dream not of. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | ‘An [[Honour|honour]]’! Were not I thine only [[Nurse|nurse]],<br>I would say thou hadst sucked wisdom from thy teat. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | [[Well]], think of [[Marriage|marriage]] now. Younger than you<br>Here in [[Verona]], ladies of esteem,<br>Are made already mothers. By my count<br>I was your mother much upon these years<br>That you are now a maid. Thus then, in brief:<br>The valiant [[Paris]] seeks you for his [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | A man, young lady, lady, such a man<br>As all the world—why, he’s a man of wax. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | [[Verona]]’s [[Summer|summer]] hath not such a flower. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Nay, he’s a flower, in [[Faith|faith]], a very flower. |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | (to [[Juliet]])<br>What say you ? Can you [[Love|love]] the gentleman ?<br>This [[Night|night]] you shall behold him at our feast.<br>Read o‘er the volume of young [[Paris]]’ face,<br>And find delight writ there with [[Beauty|beauty]]’s pen.<br>Examine every married lineament,<br>And see how one another lends content;<br>And what obscured in this fair volume lies<br>Find written in the margin of his eyes.<br>This precious book of [[Love|love]], this unbound [[Lover|lover]],<br>To beautify him only lacks a cover.<br>The fish lives in the sea, and ’tis much [[Pride|pride]]<br>For fair without the fair within to hide.<br>That book in many’s eyes doth share the glory<br>That in gold clasps locks in the golden story.<br>So shall you share all that he doth possess<br>By having him, making yourself no less. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | No less, nay, bigger. Women grow by men.<br>[[Capulet|CAPULET]]’s WIFE (to [[Juliet]])<br>Speak briefly: can you like of [[Paris]]’ [[Love|love]]? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | I’ll look to like, if looking liking move;<br>But no more deep will I endart mine eye<br>Than your consent gives strength to make it fly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Peter |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Madam, the guests are come, supper served up, |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | you called, my young lady asked for, the [[Nurse]] cursed<br>in the pantry, and everything in extremity. I must hence<br>to wait. I beseech you follow straight. |
| *(didascalia)* | We follow thee. Exit Peter |
| [[Capulet|CAPULET]]’S WIFE | [[Juliet]], the County stays. |
