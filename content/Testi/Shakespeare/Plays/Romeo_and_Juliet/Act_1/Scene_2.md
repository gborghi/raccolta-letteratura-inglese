| *(didascalia)* | Enter Capulet, Paris, and Peter, a servingman |
| CAPULET | But Montague is bound as well as I,<br>In penalty alike, and ’tis not hard, I think,<br>For men so old as we to keep the peace. |
| PARIS | Of honourable reckoning are you both,<br>And pity ’tis you lived at odds so long.<br>But now, my lord: what say you to my suit? |
| CAPULET | But saying o’er what I have said before.<br>My child is yet a stranger in the world;<br>She hath not seen the change of fourteen years.<br>Let two more summers wither in their pride<br>Ere we may think her ripe to be a bride. |
| PARIS | Younger than she are happy mothers made. |
| CAPULET | And too soon marred are those so early made.<br>But woo her, gentle Paris, get her heart;<br>My will to her consent is but a part,<br>And, she agreed, within her scope of choice<br>Lies my consent and fair-according voice.<br>This night I hold an old-accustomed feast<br>Whereto I have invited many a guest<br>Such as I love, and you among the store,<br>One more most welcome, makes my number more.<br>At my poor house look to behold this night<br>Earth-treading stars that make dark heaven light.<br>Such comfort as do lusty young men feel<br>When well-apparelled April on the heel<br>Of limping winter treads—even such delight<br>Among fresh female buds shall you this night<br>Inherit at my house; hear all, all see,<br>And like her most whose merit most shall be,<br>Which on more view of many, mine, being one,<br>May stand in number, though in reck’ning none. |
| *(didascalia)* | Come, go with me. (Giving Peter a paper) Go, sirrah, |
| CAPULET | trudge about;<br>Through fair Verona find those persons out<br>Whose names are written there, and to them say<br>My house and welcome on their pleasure stay. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Capulet and Paris |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Find them out whose names are written here? It |
| CAPULET | is written that the shoemaker should meddle with his<br>yard and the tailor with his last, the fisher with his<br>pencil and the painter with his nets; but I am sent to<br>find those persons whose names are here writ, and can<br>never find what names the writing person hath here<br>writ. I must to the learned. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Benvolio and Romeo |
| CAPULET | In good time. |
| BENVOLIO | (to Romeo)<br>Tut, man, one fire burns out another’s burning,<br>One pain is lessened by another’s anguish.<br>Turn giddy, and be holp by backward turning.<br>One desperate grief cures with another’s languish.<br>Take thou some new infection to thy eye,<br>And the rank poison of the old will die. |
| ROMEO | Your plantain leaf is excellent for that. |
| BENVOLIO | For what, I pray thee? |
| ROMEO | For your broken shin. |
| BENVOLIO | Why, Romeo, art thou mad? |
| ROMEO | Not mad, but bound more than a madman is;<br>Shut up in prison, kept without my food, |
| *(didascalia)* | Whipped and tormented and—(to Peter Good e’en, |
| ROMEO | good fellow. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER |
| ROMEO | God gi‘good e’en. I pray, sir, can you read? |
| ROMEO | Ay, mine own fortune in my misery. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER erhaps you have learned it without book. But I pray, can you read anything you see? |
| ROMEO | Ay, if I know the letters and the language. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Ye say honestly. Rest you merry. |
| ROMEO | Stay, fellow, I can read.<br>He reads the letter<br>‘Signor Martino and his wife and daughters,<br>County Anselme and his beauteous sisters,<br>The lady widow of Vitruvio,<br>Signor Placentio and his lovely nieces,<br>Mercutio and his brother Valentine,<br>Mine uncle Capulet, his wife and daughters,<br>My fair niece Rosaline and Livia,<br>Signor Valentio and his cousin Tybalt,<br>Lucio and the lively Helena.’<br>A fair assembly. Whither should they come? |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Up. |
| ROMEO | Whither? |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER To supper to our house. |
| ROMEO | Whose house? |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER My master’s. |
| ROMEO | Indeed, I should have asked thee that before. |
| *(didascalia)* | PETER Now I’ll tell you without asking. My master is |
| ROMEO | the great rich Capulet, and if you be not of the house<br>of Montagues, I pray come and crush a cup of wine.<br>Rest you merry. Exit |
| BENVOLIO | At this same ancient feast of Capulet’s<br>Sups the fair Rosaline, whom thou so loves,<br>With all the admirèd beauties of Verona.<br>Go thither, and with unattainted eye<br>Compare her face with some that I shall show,<br>And I will make thee think thy swan a crow. |
| ROMEO | When the devout religion of mine eye<br>Maintains such falsehood, then turn tears to fires;<br>And these who, often drowned, could never die,<br>Transparent heretics, be burnt for liars.<br>One fairer than my love !—the all-seeing sun<br>Ne’er saw her match since first the world begun. |
| BENVOLIO | Tut, you saw her fair, none else being by,<br>Herself poised with herself in either eye;<br>But in that crystal scales let there be weighed<br>Your lady’s love against some other maid<br>That I will show you shining at this feast,<br>And she shall scant show well that now seems best. |
| ROMEO | I’ll go along, no such sight to be shown,<br>But to rejoice in splendour of mine own. Exeunt |
