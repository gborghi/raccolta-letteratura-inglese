| *(didascalia)* | Enter Capulet, his Wife, and Paris |
| CAPULET | Things have fall’n out, sir, so unluckily<br>That we have had no time to move our daughter.<br>Look you, she loved her kinsman Tybalt dearly,<br>And so did I. Well, we were born to die.<br>’Tis very late. She’ll not come down tonight.<br>I promise you, but for your company<br>I would have been abed an hour ago. |
| PARIS | These times of woe afford no times to woo.<br>Madam, good night. Commend me to your daughter. |
| CAPULET’S WIFE | I will, and know her mind early tomorrow.<br>Tonight she’s mewed up to her heaviness. |
| *(didascalia)* | Paris offers to go in, and Capulet calls him again |
| CAPULET | Sir Paris, I will make a desperate tender<br>Of my child’s love. I think she will be ruled<br>In all respects by me. Nay, more, I doubt it not.<br>Wife, go you to her ere you go to bed.<br>Acquaint her here of my son Paris’ love,<br>And bid her-mark you me?—on Wednesday next—<br>But soft—what day is this? |
| PARIS | Monday, my lord. |
| CAPULET | Monday. Ha, ha! Well, Wednesday is too soon.<br>O’ Thursday let it be. 0’ Thursday, tell her,<br>She shall be married to this noble earl.<br>Will you be ready? Do you like this haste?<br>We’ll keep no great ado—a friend or two.<br>For hark you, Tybalt being slain so late,<br>It may be thought we held him carelessly,<br>Being our kinsman, if we revel much.<br>Therefore we’ll have some half a dozen friends,<br>And there an end. But what say you to Thursday? |
| PARIS | My lord, I would that Thursday were tomorrow. |
| CAPULET | Well, get you gone. O’ Thursday be it, then.<br>(To his Wife) Go you to Juliet ere you go to bed.<br>Prepare her, wife, against this wedding day.—<br>Farewell, my lord.—Light to my chamber, ho!—<br>Afore me, it is so very late that we<br>May call it early by and by. Good night. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Capulet and his wife at one door, Paris at another door |
