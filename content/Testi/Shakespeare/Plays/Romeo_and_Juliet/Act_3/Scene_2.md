| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Juliet]] |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Gallop apace, you fiery-footed steeds,<br>Towards Phoebus’ lodging. Such a waggoner<br>As Phaeton would whip you to the west<br>And bring in cloudy [[Night|night]] immediately.<br>Spread thy close curtain, [[Love|love]]-performing [[Night|night]],<br>That runaways’ eyes may wink, and [[Romeo]]<br>Leap to these arms untalked of and unseen.<br>[[lovers|Lovers]] can see to do their amorous rites<br>By their own beauties; or, if [[Love|love]] be blind,<br>It best agrees with [[Night|night]]. Come, civil [[Night|night]],<br>Thou sober-suited matron all in black,<br>And learn me how to lose a winning match<br>Played for a pair of stainless maidenhoods.<br>Hood my unmanned [[Blood|blood]], bating in my cheeks,<br>With thy black mantle till strange [[Love|love]] grown bold<br>Think true [[Love|love]] acted simple modesty.<br>Come [[Night|night]], come [[Romeo]]; come, thou day in [[Night|night]],<br>For thou wilt lie upon the wings of [[Night|night]]<br>Whiter than new [[Snow|snow]] on a raven’s back.<br>Come, gentle [[Night|night]]; come, loving, black-browed [[Night|night]],<br>Give me my [[Romeo]], and when I shall die<br>Take him and cut him out in little [[Stars|stars]],<br>And he will make the face of heaven so fine<br>That all the world will be in [[Love|love]] with [[Night|night]]<br>And pay no worship to the garish sun.<br>O, I have bought the mansion of a [[Love|love]]<br>But not possessed it, and though I am sold,<br>Not yet enjoyed. So tedious is this day<br>As is the [[Night|night]] before some festival<br>To an impatient [[Child|child]] that hath new robes<br>And may not wear them. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Nurse]], wringing her hands, with the ladder of cords in her lap |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | O, here comes my [[Nurse]],<br>And she brings news, and every tongue that speaks<br>But [[Romeo]]’s name speaks heavenly eloquence.<br>Now, [[Nurse]], what news ? What, hast thou there<br>The cords that [[Romeo]] bid thee fetch? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Nurse|NURSE]] putting down the cords Ay, ay, the cords. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Ay me, what news? Why dost thou wring thy hands? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Ah, welladay! He’s dead, he’s dead, he’s dead!<br>We are undone, lady, we are undone.<br>Alack the day, he’s gone, he’s killed, he’s dead! |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Can heaven be so envious? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | [[Romeo]] can,<br>Though heaven cannot. O [[Romeo]], [[Romeo]],<br>Who ever would have thought it [[Romeo]]? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | What devil art thou that dost torment me thus?<br>This torture should be roared in dismal hell.<br>Hath [[Romeo]] slain himself? Say thou but ‘Ay’,<br>And that bare vowel ‘I’ shall [[Poison|poison]] more<br>Than the [[Death|death]]-darting eye of cockatrice.<br>I am not I if there be such an ‘Ay’,<br>Or those eyes shut that makes thee answer ‘Ay’.<br>If he be slain, say ‘Ay’; or if not, ‘No’.<br>Brief sounds determine of my weal or woe. |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | I saw the wound, I saw it with mine eyes,<br>God save the mark, here on his manly breast—<br>A piteous corpse, a bloody, piteous corpse—<br>Pale, pale as ashes, all bedaubed in [[Blood|blood]],<br>All in gore [[Blood|blood]]; I swooned at the sight. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | O, break, my heart, poor bankrupt, break at once!<br>To [[Prison|prison]], eyes; ne’er look on liberty.<br>Vile earth, to earth resign; end motion here,<br>And thou and [[Romeo]] press one heavy bier! |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | O [[Tybalt]], [[Tybalt]], the best friend I had!<br>O courteous [[Tybalt]], honest gentleman,<br>That ever I should live to see thee dead! |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | What [[Storm|storm]] is this that blows so contrary?<br>Is [[Romeo]] slaughtered, and is [[Tybalt]] dead?<br>My dearest cousin and my dearer lord?<br>Then, dreadful trumpet, sound the general doom,<br>For who is living if those two are gone? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | [[Tybalt]] is gone and [[Romeo]] banished.<br>[[Romeo]] that killed him—he is banished. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | O God, did [[Romeo]]’s hand shed [[Tybalt]]’s [[Blood|blood]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Nurse|NURSE]] |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | It did, it did, alas the day, it did. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Juliet|JULIET]] |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | O serpent heart hid with a flow‘ring face!<br>Did ever dragon keep so fair a [[Cave|cave]]?<br>Beautiful [[Tyrant|tyrant]], fiend angelical!<br>Dove-feathered raven, wolvish-ravening [[Lamb|lamb]]!<br>Despisèd substance of divinest show!<br>Just opposite to what thou justly seem’st—<br>A damnèd saint, an honourable [[Villain|villain]].<br>O [[Nature|nature]], what hadst thou to do in hell<br>When thou didst bower the spirit of a fiend<br>In mortal paradise of such sweet flesh?<br>Was ever book containing such vile matter<br>So fairly bound? O, that deceit should dwell<br>In such a gorgeous palace! |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | There’s no trust, no [[Faith|faith]], no honesty in men;<br>All perjured, all forsworn, all naught, dissemblers all.<br>Ah, where’s my man? Give me some aqua vitae.<br>These griefs, these woes, these sorrows make me old.<br>Shame come to [[Romeo]]! |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Blistered be thy tongue<br>For such a wish! He was not born to shame.<br>Upon his brow shame is ashamed to sit,<br>For ’tis a throne where [[Honour|honour]] may be crowned<br>Sole monarch of the universal earth.<br>O, what a beast was I to chide at him! |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Will you speak [[Well|well]] of him that killed your cousin? |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Shall I speak ill of him that is my husband?<br>Ah, poor my lord, what tongue shall smooth thy name<br>When I, thy three-hours wife, have mangled it?<br>But wherefore, [[Villain|villain]], didst thou kill my cousin?<br>That [[Villain|villain]] cousin would have killed my husband.<br>Back, foolish [[Tears|tears]], back to your native [[spring]]!<br>Your tributary drops belong to woe,<br>Which you, mistaking, offer up to joy.<br>My husband lives, that [[Tybalt]] would have slain;<br>And [[Tybalt]]’s dead, that would have slain my husband.<br>All this is comfort. Wherefore weep I then?<br>Some word there was, worser than [[Tybalt]]’s [[Death|death]],<br>That murdered me. I would forget it fain,<br>But O, it presses to my [[Memory|memory]]<br>Like damned guilty deeds to sinners’ minds!<br>‘[[Tybalt]] is dead, and [[Romeo]] banished.’<br>That ‘banishèd’, that one word ‘banishèd’<br>Hath slain ten thousand Tybalts. [[Tybalt]]’s [[Death|death]]<br>Was woe enough, if it had ended there;<br>Or, if sour woe delights in fellowship<br>And needly will be ranked with other griefs,<br>Why followed not, when she said ‘[[Tybalt]]’s dead’,<br>‘Thy father’, or ‘thy mother’, nay, or both,<br>Which modern lamentation might have moved?<br>But with a rearward following [[Tybalt]]’s [[Death|death]],<br>‘[[Romeo]] is banishèd‘-to speak that word<br>Is father, mother, [[Tybalt]], [[Romeo]], [[Juliet]],<br>All slain, all dead. ‘[[Romeo]] is banishèd’—<br>There is no end, no limit, measure, bound,<br>In that word’s [[Death|death]]. No words can that woe sound.<br>Where is my father and my mother, [[Nurse]]? |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | Weeping and wailing over [[Tybalt]]’s corpse.<br>Will you go to them? I will bring you thither. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | Wash they his wounds with [[Tears|tears]]; mine shall be spent<br>When theirs are dry, for [[Romeo]]’s banishment.<br>Take up those cords. Poor ropes, you are beguiled,<br>Both you and I, for [[Romeo]] is exiled.<br>He made you for a highway to my bed,<br>But I, a maid, die maiden-widowed.<br>Come, cords; come, [[Nurse]]; I’ll to my wedding bed,<br>And [[Death|death]], not [[Romeo]], take my maidenhead! |
| [[Nurse|NURSE]] | (taking up the cords)<br>Hie to your chamber. I’ll find [[Romeo]]<br>To comfort you. I wot [[Well|well]] where he is.<br>Hark ye, your [[Romeo]] will be here at [[Night|night]].<br>I’ll to him. He is hid at Laurence’ cell. |
| [[Juliet|JULIET]] | (giving her a ring)<br>O, find him! Give this ring to my true knight,<br>And bid him come to take his last farewell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
