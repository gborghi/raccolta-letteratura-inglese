| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Capulet, his Wife, the Nurse, and two Servingmen |
| CAPULET | (giving a Servingman a paper)<br>So many guests invite as here are writ. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Servingman |
| CAPULET | (To the other Servingman) Sirrah, go hire me twenty<br>cunning cooks. |
| SERVINGMAN | You shall have none ill, sir, for I’ll try if they can lick their fingers. |
| CAPULET | How canst thou try them so? |
| SERVINGMAN | Marry, sir, ’tis an ill cook that cannot lick his own fingers, therefore he that cannot lick his fingers goes not with me. |
| *(didascalia)* | CAPULET Go, be gone. Exit Servingman |
| SERVINGMAN | We shall be much unfurnished for this time.<br>(To the Nurse) What, is my daughter gone to Friar<br>Laurence? |
| NURSE | Ay, forsooth. |
| CAPULET | Well, he may chance to do some good on her.<br>A peevish, self-willed harlotry it is. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Juliet |
| NURSE | See where she comes from shrift with merry look. |
| CAPULET | (to Juliet)<br>How now, my headstrong, where have you been gadding ? |
| JULIET | Where I have learned me to repent the sin<br>Of disobedient opposition<br>To you and your behests, and am enjoined<br>By holy Laurence to fall prostrate here<br>To beg your pardon. (Kneeling) Pardon, I beseech you.<br>Henceforward I am ever ruled by you. |
| *(didascalia)* | CAPULET to the Nurse |
| JULIET | Send for the County; go tell him of this.<br>I’ll have this knot knit up tomorrow morning. |
| JULIET | I met the youthful lord at Laurence’ cell,<br>And gave him what becoming love I might,<br>Not stepping o’er the bounds of modesty. |
| CAPULET | Why, I am glad on’t. This is well. Stand up.<br>Juliet rises<br>This is as’t should be. Let me see the County. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Nurse Ay, marry, go, I say, and fetch him hither. |
| CAPULET | Now, afore God, this reverend holy friar,<br>All our whole city is much bound to him. |
| JULIET | Nurse, will you go with me into my closet<br>To help me sort such needful ornaments<br>As you think fit to furnish me tomorrow? |
| CAPULET’S WIFE | No, not till Thursday. There is time enough. |
| CAPULET | Go, Nurse, go with her. We’ll to church tomorrow. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Juliet and Nurse |
| CAPULET’S WIFE | We shall be short in our provision.<br>’Tis now near night. |
| CAPULET | Tush, I will stir about,<br>And all things shall be well, I warrant thee, wife.<br>Go thou to Juliet, help to deck up her.<br>I’ll not to bed tonight. Let me alone.<br>I’ll play the housewife for this once. What, ho!<br>They are all forth. Well, I will walk myself<br>To County Paris to prepare up him<br>Against tomorrow. My heart is wondrous light,<br>Since this same wayward girl is so reclaimed. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
