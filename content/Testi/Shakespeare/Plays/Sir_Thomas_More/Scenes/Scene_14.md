| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Warders of the Tower, with halberds |
| FIRST WARDER | Ho, make a guard there! |
| SECOND WARDER | Master Lieutenant gives a strait command<br>The people be avoided from the bridge. |
| THIRD WARDER | From whence is he committed, who can tell? |
| FIRST WARDER | From Durham [[house|House]], I hear. |
| SECOND WARDER | The guard were waiting there an hour ago. |
| THIRD WARDER | If he stay long, he’ll not get near the wharf,<br>There’s such a crowd of boats upon the Thames. |
| FIRST WARDER | [[Well]], be it spoken without offence to any,<br>A wiser or more virtuous gentleman<br>Was never bred in England. |
| SECOND WARDER | I think the poor will bury him in [[Tears|tears]].<br>I never heard a man since I was born<br>So generally bewailed of everyone. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a poor Woman [with others in a crowd] |
| THIRD WARDER | What means this woman?—Whither dost thou press? |
| FIRST WARDER | This woman will be trod to [[Death|death]] anon. |
| SECOND WARDER | [to the Woman] What makest thou here? |
| WOMAN | To speak with that good man [[Sir Thomas More]]. |
| FIRST WARDER | To speak with him? He’s not Lord Chancellor. |
| WOMAN | The more’s the pity, sir, if it pleased God. |
| FIRST WARDER | Therefore if thou hast a petition to deliver<br>Thou mayst keep it now, for anything I know. |
| WOMAN | I am a poor woman, and have had, God knows,<br>A suit this two year in the Chancery,<br>And he hath all the evidence I have,<br>Which should I lose I am utterly undone. |
| FIRST WARDER | [[Faith]], and I [[Fear|fear]] thou‘It hardly come by ’em now.<br>I am sorry for thee even with all my heart. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Lords [of Shrewsbury and Surrey], with [[Sir Thomas More]], and attendants; and enter Lieutenant and Gentleman Porter |
| SECOND WARDER | Woman, stand back. You must avoid this place.<br>The lords must pass this way into the Tower. |
| MORE | I thank your lordships for your pains thus far<br>To my strong-[[house]]. |
| WOMAN | Now good [[Sir Thomas More]], for [[Christ]]’s dear sake<br>Deliver me my writings back again<br>That do concern my title. |
| MORE | What, my old client, art thou got hither too?<br>Poor silly wretch, I must confess indeed<br>I had such writings as concern thee near,<br>But the [[King]]<br>Has ta’en the matter into his own hand;<br>He has all I had. Then, woman, sue to him.<br>I cannot help thee. Thou must bear with me. |
| WOMAN | Ah, gentle heart, my [[Soul|soul]] for thee is sad.<br>Farewell, the best friend that the poor e’er had. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| GENTLEMAN PORTER | Before you enter through the Tower gate,<br>Your upper garment, sir, belongs to me. |
| MORE | Sir, you shall have it. There it is.<br>He gives him his cap |
| GENTLEMAN PORTER | The upmost on your back, sir. You mistake me. |
| MORE | Sir, now I understand ye very [[Well|well]].<br>But that you name my back,<br>Sure else my cap had been the uppermost. |
| SHREWSBURY | Farewell, kind lord. God send us merry meeting. |
| MORE | Amen, my lord. |
| SURREY | Farewell, dear friend. I [[Hope|hope]] your safe return. |
| MORE | My lord, and my dear fellow in the Muses,<br>Farewell. Farewell, most noble poet. |
| LIEUTENANT | Adieu, most honoured lords. Exeunt Lords |
| MORE | Fair [[Prison|prison]], welcome. Yet methinks<br>For thy fair building ‘tis too foul a name.<br>Many a guilty [[Soul|soul]], and many an [[Innocent|innocent]],<br>Have breathed their farewell to thy hollow rooms.<br>I oft have entered into thee this way,<br>Yet, I thank God, ne’er with a clearer [[Conscience|conscience]]<br>Than at this hour.<br>This is my comfort yet: how hard soe’er<br>My lodging prove, the cry of the poor suitor,<br>Fatherless [[Orphan|orphan]], or distressèd widow<br>Shall not disturb me in my quiet [[Sleep|sleep]].<br>On then, i’ God’s name, to our close abode.<br>God is as strong here as he is abroad. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
