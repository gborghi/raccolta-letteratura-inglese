| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Bishop of Rochester, Surrey, Shrewsbury, Lieutenant of the Tower, and warders with weapons |
| ROCHESTER | Your kind persuasions, honourable lords,<br>I can but thank ye for, but in this breast<br>There lives a soul that aims at higher things<br>Than temporary pleasing earthly kings.<br>God bless his highness, even with all my heart.<br>We shall meet one day, though that now we part. |
| SURREY | We not misdoubt your wisdom can discern<br>What best befits it; yet in love and zeal<br>We could entreat it might be otherwise. |
| SHREWSBURY | [to Rochester]<br>No doubt your fatherhood will by yourself<br>Consider better of the present case,<br>And grow as great in favour as before. |
| ROCHESTER | For that, as pleaseth God, in my restraint<br>From worldly causes I shall better see<br>Into myself than at proud liberty.<br>The Tower and I will privately confer<br>Of things wherein at freedom I may err.<br>But I am troublesome unto your honours,<br>And hold ye longer than becomes my duty.<br>Master Lieutenant, I am now your charge;<br>And, though you keep my body, yet my love<br>Waits on my king and you while Fisher lives. |
| SURREY | Farewell, my lord of Rochester. We’ll pray<br>or your release, and labour’t as we may. |
| SHREWSBURY | [to Rochester]<br>Thereof assure yourself. So do we leave ye,<br>And to your happy private thoughts bequeath ye. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Lords |
| ROCHESTER | Now, Master Lieutenant, on; i’ God’s name, go;<br>And with as glad a mind go I with you<br>As ever truant bade the school adieu. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
