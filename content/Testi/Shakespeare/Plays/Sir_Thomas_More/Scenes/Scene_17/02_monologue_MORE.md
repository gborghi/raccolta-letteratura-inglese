| MORE | O, is this the place? I promise ye, it is a goodly scaffold. In sooth, I am come about a headless errand, For I have not much to say, now I am here. Well, let’s ascend, i’ God’s name. In troth, methinks your stair is somewhat weak. [To the Hangman] I prithee, honest friend, lend me thy hand To help me up. As for my coming down, Let me alone, I’ll look to that myself. As he is going up the stairs, enters the Earls of Surrey and Shrewsbury My lords of Surrey and of Shrewsbury, give me your hands yet before we part. Ye see, though it pleaseth the King to raise me thus high, yet I am not proud; for the higher I mount the better I can see my friends about me. I am now on a far voyage, and this strange wooden horse must bear me thither. Yet I perceive by your looks you like my bargain so ill that there’s not one of ye all dare venture with me. (Walking) Truly, here’s a most sweet gallery. I like the air of it better than my garden at Chelsea. By your patience, good people that have pressed thus into my bedchamber, if you’ll not trouble me I’ll take a sound sleep here. |
