| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Lady More, her two Daughters, [one of them Roper’s Wife,] and Master Roper, as walking |
| ROPER | Madam, what ails ye for to look so sad? |
| LADY MORE | Troth, son, I know not what. I am not sick,<br>And yet I am not [[Well|well]]. I would be merry,<br>But somewhat lies so heavy on my heart<br>I cannot choose but sigh. You are a scholar.<br>I pray ye tell me, may one credit [[Dreams|dreams]]? |
| ROPER | Why ask you that, dear madam? |
| LADY MORE | Because tonight I had the strangest dream<br>That e‘er my [[Sleep|sleep]] was troubled with.<br>Methought ’twas [[Night|night]],<br>And that the [[King]] and [[Queen]] went on the Thames<br>In barges to hear music. My lord and I<br>Were in a little boat, methought—Lord, Lord,<br>What strange things live in slumbers!—and being near,<br>We grappled to the barge that bare the [[King]];<br>But after many pleasing voices spent<br>In that still-moving music [[house]], methought<br>The violence of the stream did sever us<br>Quite from the golden fleet and hurried us<br>Unto the Bridge which, with unused horror,<br>We entered at full tide; thence some flight shoot<br>Being carried by the waves, our boat stood still<br>Just opposite the Tower; and there it turned<br>And turned about, as when a whirlpool sucks<br>The circled waters. Methought that we both cried,<br>Till that we sunk, where arm in arm we died. |
| ROPER | Give no respect, dear madam, to fond [[Dreams|dreams]].<br>They are but slight illusions of the [[Blood|blood]]. |
| LADY MORE | Tell me not all are so, for often [[Dreams|dreams]]<br>Are true diviners, either of good or ill.<br>I cannot be in quiet till I hear<br>How my lord fares. |
| ROPER | (aside)<br>Nor I.—Come hither, wife.<br>I will not fright thy mother to interpret<br>The [[Nature|nature]] of [[A Dream|a dream]]; but, trust me, sweet,<br>This [[Night|night]] I have been troubled with thy father<br>Beyond all thought. |
| ROPER’S WIFE | [aside to Roper] Truly, and so have I.<br>Methought I saw him here in Chelsea church,<br>Standing upon the rood-loft, now defaced;<br>And whilst he kneeled and prayed before the image<br>It fell with him into the upper choir,<br>Where my poor father lay all stained in [[Blood|blood]]. |
| ROPER | [aside to his Wife]<br>Our [[Dreams|dreams]] all meet in one conclusion,<br>Fatal, I [[Fear|fear]]. |
| LADY MORE | What’s that you talk? I pray ye let me know it. |
| ROPER’S WIFE | Nothing, good mother. |
| LADY MORE | This is your fashion still: I must know nothing.<br>Call Master Catesby; he shall straight to [[Court|court]]<br>And see how my lord does. I shall not rest<br>Until my heart lean panting on his breast. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Sir Thomas More]], merrily, servants attending |
| MORE’S OTHER DAUGHTER | See where my father comes, joyful and merry. |
| MORE | As seamen, having passed a troubled [[Storm|storm]],<br>[[Dance]] on the pleasant shore, so I—O, I could speak<br>Now like a poet! Now, afore God, I am passing light.<br>Wife, give me kind welcome.<br>[He kisses her]<br>Thou wast wont to blame<br>My kissing when my beard was in the stubble;<br>But I have been trimmed of late: I have had<br>A smooth [[Court|court]] shaving, in good [[Faith|faith]], I have.<br>Daughters<br>kneel<br>[To Daughters] God bless ye.—Son Roper, give me your<br>hand. |
| ROPER | Your [[Honour|honour]]’s welcome [[Home|home]]. |
| MORE | [[Honour]]? Ha, ha!<br>And how dost, wife? |
| ROPER | [aside] He bears himself most strangely. |
| LADY MORE | Will your lordship in? |
| MORE | Lordship? No, wife, that’s gone.<br>The ground was slight that we did lean upon. |
| LADY MORE | Lord, that your [[Honour|honour]] ne’er will leave these jests!<br>In [[Faith|faith]], it ill becomes ye. |
| MORE | O good wife,<br>[[Honour]] and jests are both together fled.<br>The merriest councillor of England’s dead. |
| LADY MORE | Who’s that, my lord? |
| MORE | Still ‘lord’? The Lord Chancellor, wife. |
| LADY MORE | That’s you. |
| MORE | Certain, but I have changed my life.<br>Am I not leaner than I was before?<br>The fat is gone. My title’s only ‘More’.<br>Contented with one style, I’ll live at rest.<br>They that have many names are not still best.<br>I have resigned mine office. Count’st me not wise? |
| LADY MORE | O God! |
| MORE | Come, breed not female children in your eyes.<br>The [[King]] will have it so. |
| LADY MORE | What’s the offence? |
| MORE | Tush, let that pass; we’ll talk of that anon.<br>The [[King]] seems a physician to my fate.<br>His princely mind would train me back to state. |
| ROPER | Then be his patient, my most honoured father. |
| MORE | O son Roper,<br>Ubi turpis est medicina, sanari piget.<br><br>No, wife, be merry, and be merry all.<br>You smiled at rising; weep not at my fall.<br>Let’s in, and here joy like to private friends,<br>Since days of pleasure have repentant ends.<br>The light of [[Greatness|greatness]] is with triumph borne;<br>It sets at midday oft, with public scorn. Exeunt |
