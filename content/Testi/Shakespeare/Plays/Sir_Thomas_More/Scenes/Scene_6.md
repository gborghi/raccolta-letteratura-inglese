| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lincoln]], Doll, Clown [Betts,] George Betts, Williamson, [Sherwin,] others: [Citizens,] [armed] |
| *(didascalia)* | [Addition II (playhouse scribe)]<br>[Addition II (Shakespeare)] |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | Peace, hear me! He that will not see a red herring at a Harry groat, butter at eleven pence a pound, meal at nine shillings a bushel, and beef at four nobles a stone, list to me. |
| OTHER GEORGE BETTS | It will come to that pass if strangers be suffered. Mark him. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | Our country is a great eating country; argo they eat more in our country than they do in their own. |
| OTHER CLOWN BETTS | By a halfpenny loaf a day, troy weight. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | They bring in strange roots, which is merely to the undoing of poor prentices. For what’s a sorry parsnip to a good heart? |
| OTHER WILLIAMSON | Trash, trash. They breed sore eyes, and ’tis enough to infect the [[City|city]] with the palsy. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | Nay, it has infected it with the palsy, for these bastards of dung—as you know, they grow in dung—have infected us, and it is our infection will make the [[City|city]] shake. Which partly comes through the eating of parsnips. |
| CLOWN BETTS | True, and pumpkins together. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Sergeant-at-arms |
| SERGEANT | What say ye to the [[Mercy|mercy]] of the [[King]]?<br>Do you refuse it? |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | You would have us upon th’ hip, would you? No, marry, do we not. We accept of the [[King]]’s [[Mercy|mercy]], but we will show no [[Mercy|mercy]] upon the strangers. |
| SERGEANT | You are the simplest things that ever stood In such a question. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | How say you now, prentices? Prentices ‘simple’? Down with him! |
| ALL CITIZENS | Prentices simple? Prentices simple? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the [[Lord Mayor]], Surrey, Shrewsbury, [More, Palmer] |
| MAYOR | Hold, in the [[King]]’s name, hold! |
| SURREY | Friends, masters, countrymen— |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | Peace ho, peace! I charge you keep the peace. |
| SHREWSBURY | My masters, countrymen— |
| WILLIAMSON | The noble Earl of Shrewsbury! Let’s hear him. |
| GEORGE BETTS | We’ll hear the [[Earl of Surrey]]. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | The Earl of Shrewsbury! |
| GEORGE BEETS | We’ll hear both. |
| ALL CITIZENS | Both, both, both, both! |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | Peace, I say, peace! Are you men of wisdom, or what are you? |
| SURREY | What you will have them, but not men of wisdom. |
| SOME CITIZENS | We’ll not hear my lord of Surrey. |
| OTHER CITIZENS | No, no, no, no, no! Shrewsbury, Shrewsbury! |
| MORE | Whiles they are o’er the bank of their obedience<br>Thus will they bear down all things. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | Sheriff More speaks. Shall we hear Sheriff More speak? |
| DOLL | Let’s hear him. A keeps a plentiful shrievaltry, and a made my brother, [[arthur|Arthur]] Watchins, Sergeant Safe’s yeoman. Let’s hear Sheriff More! |
| ALL CITIZENS | Sheriff More, More, More, Sheriff More! |
| MORE | Even by the rule you have among yourselves,<br>Command still audience. |
| SOME CITIZENS | Surrey, Surrey! |
| OTHER CITIZENS | More, More! |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | and GEORGE BETTS Peace, peace, [[Silence|silence]], peace! |
| MORE | You that have voice and credit with the number,<br>Command them to a stillness. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | A plague on them, they will not hold their peace. The devil cannot rule them. |
| MORE | Then what a rough and riotous charge have you<br>To lead those that the devil cannot rule.—<br>Good masters, hear me speak. |
| DOLL | Ay, by th’ mass will we, More. Thou’rt a good housekeeper, and I thank thy good worship for my brother [[arthur|Arthur]] Watchins. |
| ALL THE OTHER CITIZENS | Peace, peace! |
| MORE | Look what you do offend you cry upon;<br>That is, the peace. Not one of you here present,<br>Had there such fellows lived when you were babes<br>That could have topped the peace as now you would,<br>The peace wherein you have till now grown up<br>Had been ta’en from you, and the bloody times<br>Could not have brought you to the state of men.<br>Alas, poor things! What is it you have got<br>Although we grant you get the thing you seek? |
| GEORGE BETTS | Marry, the removing of the strangers, which cannot choose but much advantage the poor handicrafts of the [[City|city]]. |
| MORE | Grant them removed, and grant that this your noise<br>Hath chid down all the majesty of England.<br>Imagine that you see the wretched strangers,<br>Their babies at their backs, with their poor luggage,<br>Plodding to th’ ports and coasts for transportation,<br>And that you sit as kings in your desires,<br>Authority quite silenced by your brawl,<br>And you in ruff of your opinions clothed:<br>What had you got? I’ll tell you: you had taught<br>How insolence and strong hand should prevail,<br>How order should be quelled. And by this pattern<br>Not one of you should live an aged man;<br>For other ruffians, as their fancies wrought,<br>With selfsame hand, [[Self|self]] reasons, and [[Self|self]] right,<br>Would shark on you, and men, like ravenous fishes,<br>Would feed on one another. |
| DOLL | Before God, that’s as true as the gospel. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | Nay, this’ a sound fellow, I tell you. Let’s mark him. |
| MORE | Let me set up before your thoughts, good friends,<br>One supposition, which if you will mark<br>You shall perceive how horrible a shape<br>Your innovation bears. First, ‘tis a sin<br>Which oft th’apostle did forewarn us of,<br>Urging obedience to authority;<br>And ‘twere no error if I told you all<br>You were in arms ’gainst God. |
| ALL CITIZENS | Marry, God forbid thatl |
| MORE | Nay, certainly you are. no<br>For to the [[King|king]] God hath His office lent<br>Of dread, of justice, power, and command;<br>Hath bid him rule, and willed you to obey.<br>And, to add ampler majesty to this,<br>He hath not only lent the [[King|king]] His figure,<br>His throne and [[Sword|sword]], but given him His own name:<br>Calls him a god on earth. What do you, then,<br>Rising ‘gainst him that God Himself installs,<br>But rise ’gainst God? What do you to your souls<br>In doing this? O, desperate as you are,<br>Wash your foul minds with [[Tears|tears]], and those same hands<br>That you, like rebels, lift against the peace,<br>Lift up for peace; and your unreverent knees,<br>Make them your feet. To kneel to be forgiven<br>Is safer wars than ever you can make<br><br>Whose discipline is riot.<br><br>In, in, to your obedience! Why, even your hurly<br><br>Cannot proceed but by obedience.<br><br>Tell me but this: What [[Rebel|rebel]] captain,<br>As mutinies are incident, by his name<br>Can still the rout? Who will obey a traitor?<br>Or how can [[Well|well]] that proclamation sound<br>When there is no addition but ‘a [[Rebel|rebel]]’<br>To qualify a [[Rebel|rebel]]? You’ll put down strangers,<br>Kill them, cut their throats, possess their houses,<br>And lead the majesty of law in lyam<br>To slip him like a hound. Alas, alas! Say now the [[King]],<br>As he is clement if th’offender mourn,<br>Should so much come too short of your great trespass<br>As but to banish you: whither would you go?<br>What country, by the [[Nature|nature]] of your error,<br>Should give you harbour? Go you to France or<br>Flanders,<br>To any German province, Spain or Portugal,<br>Nay, anywhere that not adheres to England:<br>Why, you must needs be strangers. Would you be<br>pleased<br>To find a nation of such barbarous temper<br>That, breaking out in hideous violence,<br>Would not afford you an abode on earth,<br>Whet their detested knives against your throats,<br>Spurn you like [[dogs]], and like as if that God<br>Owed not nor made not you, nor that the elements<br>Were not all appropriate to your comforts,<br>But chartered unto them? What would you think<br>To be thus used? This is the strangers’ case,<br>And this your mountainish inhumanity. |
| ALL CITIZENS | [[Faith]], a says true. Let us do as we may be done by. |
| ALL CITIZENS [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | We’ll be ruled by you, Master More, if you’ll stand our friend to procure our pardon. |
| MORE | Submit you to these noble gentlemen,<br>Entreat their mediation to the [[King]],<br>Give up yourself to form, obey the magistrate,<br>And there’s no [[Doubt|doubt]] but [[Mercy|mercy]] may be found<br>If you so seek it.<br><br>[Addition 11 (Shakespeare)]<br>[Original Text (Munday)] |
| ALL CITIZENS | We yield, and [[Desire|desire]] his highness’ [[Mercy|mercy]]. They lay by their weapons |
| MORE | No [[Doubt|doubt]] his majesty will grant it you.<br>But you must yield to go to several prisons<br>Till that his highness’ will be further known. |
| ALL CITIZENS | Most willingly, whither you will have us. |
| SHREWSBURY | [[Lord Mayor]], let them be sent to several prisons,<br>And there, in any case, be [[Well|well]] entreated.<br>My lord of Surrey, please you to take [[Horse|horse]]<br>And ride to Cheapside, where the aldermen<br>Are with their several companies in arms.<br>Will them to go unto their several wards,<br>Both for the stay of further mutiny<br>And for the apprehending of such persons<br>As shall contend. |
| SURREY | I go, my noble lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| SHREWSBURY | We’ll straight go tell his highness these good news.<br>Withal, Sheriff More, I’ll tell him how your breath<br>Hath ransomed many a subject from sad [[Death|death]]. Exit |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | [[Lincoln]] and Sherwin, you shall both to Newgate,<br>The rest unto the Counters. |
| PALMER | Go, guard them hence. A little breath [[Well|well]] spent<br>Cheats expectation in his fair’st event. |
| DOLL | [[Well]], Sheriff More, thou hast done more with thy good words than all they could with their weapons. Give me thy hand. Keep thy promise now for the [[King]]’s pardon, or, by the Lord, I’ll call thee a plain cony-catcher. |
| [[Lincoln|LINCOLN]] | Farewell, Sheriff More. And as we yield by thee<br>So make our peace; then thou deal’st honestly. |
| CLOWN BETTS | Ay, and save us from the gallows, else a deals [[Double|double]] honestly.<br>[The Citizens] are led away |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | Master Sheriff More, you have preserved the [[City|city]]<br>From a most dangerous fierce commotion.<br>For if this limb of riot here in St Martin’s<br>Had joined with other branches of the [[City|city]]<br>That did begin to kindle, ’twould have bred<br>Great rage. That rage much murder would have fed. |
| PALMER | Not steel but eloquence hath wrought this good.<br><br>You have redeemed us from much threatened [[Blood|blood]]. |
| MORE | My lord, and brethren, what I here have spoke<br>My country’s [[Love|love]] and, next, the [[City|city]]’s care<br>Enjoined me to; which since it thus prevails,<br>Think God hath made weak More His instrument<br>To thwart sedition’s violent intent.<br>I think ’twere best, my lord, some two hours hence<br>We meet at the Guildhall, and there determine<br>That thorough every ward the watch be clad<br>In armour. But especially provide<br>That at the [[City|city]] gates selected men,<br>Substantial citizens, do ward tonight,<br>For [[Fear|fear]] of further mischief. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | It shall be so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Shrewsbury |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | But yon, methinks, my lord of Shrewsbury. |
| SHREWSBURY | My lord, his majesty sends loving thanks<br>To you, your brethren, and his faithful subjects<br>Your careful citizens. But Master More, to you<br>A rougher yet as kind a salutation.<br>Your name is yet too short. Nay, you must kneel.<br>A knight’s creation is this knightly steel. |
| *(didascalia)* | More kneels |
| SHREWSBURY | Rise up [[Sir Thomas More]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | He knights More |
| *(didascalia)* | MORE rising |
| SHREWSBURY | I thank his highness for thus honouring me. |
| SHREWSBURY | This is but first taste of his princely favour,<br>For it hath pleased his high majesty,<br>Noting your wisdom and deserving merit,<br>To put this staff of [[Honour|honour]] in your hand,<br>For he hath chose you of his Privy Council. |
| *(didascalia)* | He gives More a staff of office |
| MORE | My lord, for to deny my sovereign’s bounty<br>Were to drop precious stones into the heaps<br>Whence first they came.<br>To urge my imperfections in excuse<br>Were all as stale as custom. No, my lord,<br>My service is my [[King|king]]’s. Good reason why,<br>Since life or [[Death|death]] hangs on our sovereign’s eye. |
| [[Lord Mayor|LORD MAYOR]] | His majesty hath honoured much the [[City|city]]<br>In this his princely choice. |
| MORE | My lord and brethren,<br>Though I depart for [[Court|court]], my [[Love|love]] shall rest<br>〈 〉<br>I now must [[Sleep|sleep]] in [[Court|court]], sound sleeps forbear.<br>The chamberlain to state is public care.<br>Yet in this rising of my private [[Blood|blood]]<br>My studious thoughts shall tend the [[City|city]]’s good. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Croft |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Crofts |
| SHREWSBURY | How now, Crofts? What news? |
| CROFTS | My lord, his highness sends express command<br>That a record be entered of this riot,<br>And that the chief and capital offenders<br>Be thereon straight arraigned; for himself intends<br>To sit in person on the rest tomorrow<br>At Westminster. |
| SHREWSBURY | [[Lord Mayor]], you hear your charge.<br>Come, good [[Sir Thomas More]], to [[Court|court]] let’s hie.<br>You are th’appeaser of this mutiny. |
| MORE | My lord, farewell. New days begets new tides.<br>Life whirls ’bout fate, then to a [[Grave|grave]] it slides. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
