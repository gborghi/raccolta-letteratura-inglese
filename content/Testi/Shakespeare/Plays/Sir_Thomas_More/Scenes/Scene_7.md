| *(didascalia)* | Enter Master Sheriff, and meet a Messenger |
| SHERIFF | Messenger, what news? |
| MESSENGER | Is execution yet performed? |
| SHERIFF | Not yet. The carts stand ready at the stairs,<br>And they shall presently away to Tyburn. |
| MESSENGER | Stay, Master Sheriff. It is the Council’s pleasure,<br>For more example in so bad a case,<br>A gibbet be erected in Cheapside<br>Hard by the Standard, whither you must bring<br>Lincoln, and those that were the chief with him,<br>To suffer death, and that immediately. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Officers |
| SHERIFF | It shall be done, sir. Exit Messenger<br>Officers, be speedy.<br>Call for a gibbet, see it be erected.<br>Others make haste to Newgate; bid them bring<br>The prisoners hither, for they here must die.<br>Away, I say, and see no time be slacked. |
| OFFICERS | We go, sir. |
| SHERIFF | That’s well said, fellows. Now you do your duty. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt some Officers severally. Others set up the gibbet |
| SHERIFF | God for his pity help these troublous times!<br><br>The street’s stopped up with gazing multitudes.<br><br>Command our armed officers with halberds<br><br>Make way for entrance of the prisoners.<br><br>Let proclamation once again be made<br><br>That every householder, on pain of death,<br><br>Keep in his prentices, and every man<br><br>Stand with a weapon ready at his door,<br><br>As he will answer to the contrary. |
| FIRST OFFICER | I’ll see it done, sir. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Offices |
| SHERIFF | Bring them away to execution.<br><br>The writ is come above two hours since.<br><br>The city will be fined for this neglect. |
| SECOND OFFICER | There’s such a press and multitude at Newgate<br>They cannot bring the carts unto the stairs<br>To take the prisoners in. |
| SHERIFF | Then let them come on foot.<br>We may not dally time with great command. |
| SECOND OFFICER | Some of the Bench, sir, think it very fit<br>That stay be made, and give it out abroad<br>The execution is deferred till morning;<br>And when the streets shall be a little cleared<br>To chain them up, and suddenly dispatch it. |
| *(didascalia)* | The prisoners are brought in, amongst them Lincoln, |
| *(didascalia)* | Doll, Williamson, Clown Betts, and Sherwin, well |
| *(didascalia)* | guarded, and the Executioner |
| SHERIFF | Stay, in meantime methinks they come along.<br>See, they are coming. So, ’tis very well.<br>Bring Lincoln there the first unto the tree. |
| CLOWN BETTS | Ay, for I cry lag, sir. |
| LINCOLN | I knew the first, sir, did belong to me.<br>This the old proverb now complete doth make:<br>That ‘Lincoln should be hanged for London’s sake’. |
| *(didascalia)* | I’ God’s name, let’s to work. To Executioner Fellow, dispatch. |
| LINCOLN | He goes up<br>I was the foremost man in this rebellion,<br>And I the foremost that must die for it. |
| DOLL | Bravely, John Lincoln, let thy death express<br>That, as thou lived‘st a man, thou died’st no less. |
| LINCOLN | Doll Williamson, thine eyes shall witness it.<br>Then to all you that come to view mine end<br>I must confess I had no ill intent<br>But against such as wronged us overmuch.<br>And now I can perceive it was not fit<br>That private men should carve out their redress<br>Which way they list. No, learn it now by me:<br>Obedience is the best in each degree.<br>And, asking mercy meekly of my king,<br>I patiently submit me to the law.<br>But God forgive them that were cause of it;<br>And, as a Christian, truly from my heart,<br>I likewise crave they would forgive me too,<br>〈 〉<br>That others by example of the same<br>Henceforth be warned to attempt the like<br>’Gainst any alien that repaireth hither.<br>Fare ye well all. The next time that we meet<br>I trust in heaven we shall each other greet. He leaps off |
| DOLL | Farewell, John Lincoln. Say all what they can,<br>Thou lived‘st a good fellow, and died’st an honest man. |
| CLOWN BETTS | Would I were so far on my journey. The first stretch is the worst, methinks. |
| SHERIFF | Bring Williamson there forward. |
| DOLL | Good Master Sheriff, I have an earnest suit,<br>And, as you are a man, deny’t me not. |
| SHERIFF | Woman, what is it? Be it in my power,<br>Thou shalt obtain it. |
| DOLL | Let me die next, sir, that is all I crave.<br>You know not what a comfort you shall bring<br>To my poor heart to die before my husband. |
| SHERIFF | Bring her to death. She shall have her desire. |
| CLOWN BETTS | Sir, and I have a suit to you too. |
| SHERIFF | What is it? |
| CLOWN BETTS | That, as you have hanged Lincoln first and will hang her next, so you will not hang me at all. |
| SHERIFF | Nay, you set ope the Counter gates, and you must hang chiefly. |
| CLOWN BETTS | Well then, so much for that! |
| *(didascalia)* | DOLL to Sherif |
| CLOWN BETTS | Sir, your free bounty much contents my mind.<br>Commend me to that good sheriff Master More,<br>And tell him had’t not been for his persuasion<br>John Lincoln had not hung here as he does.<br>We would first have locked up in Leaden Hall,<br>And there been burned to ashes with the roof. |
| SHERIFF | Woman, what Master More did was a subject’s duty,<br>And hath so pleased our gracious lord the King<br>That he is hence removed to higher place<br>And made of Council to his majesty. |
| DOLL | Well is he worthy of it, by my troth:<br>An honest, wise, well-spoken gentleman.<br>Yet would I praise his honesty much more<br>If he had kept his word and saved our lives.<br>But let that pass. Men are but men, and so<br>Words are but words, and pays not what men owe.<br>Now, husband, since perhaps the world may say<br>That through my means thou com’st thus to thy end,<br>Here I begin this cup of death to thee,<br>Because thou shalt be sure to taste no worse<br>Than I have taken that must go before thee.<br>What though I be a woman? That’s no matter.<br>I do owe God a death, and I must pay him.<br>Husband, give me thy hand. Be not dismayed.<br>This chore being chored, then all our debt is paid.<br>Only two little babes we leave behind us,<br>And all I can bequeath them at this time<br>Is but the love of some good honest friend<br>To bring them up in charitable sort.<br>What, masters?—He goes upright that never halts,<br>And they may live to mend their parents’ faults. |
| WILLIAMSON | Why, well said, wife. I‘faith, thou cheer’st my heart.<br>Give me thy hand. Let’s kiss, and so let’s part.<br>He kisses her on the ladder |
| DOLL | The next kiss, Williamson, shall be in heaven.<br>Now cheerly, lads! George Betts, a hand with thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Clown Betts And thine too, Ralph. And thine, good |
| DOLL | honest Sherwin.<br>Now let me tell the women of this town<br>No stranger yet brought Doll to lying down.<br>So long as I an Englishman can see,<br>Nor French nor Dutch shall get a kiss of me.<br>And when that I am dead, for me yet say<br>I died in scorn to be a stranger’s prey. |
| *(didascalia)* | A great shout and noise within |
| VOICES WITHIN | Pardon, pardon, pardon, pardon!<br>Room for the Earl of Surreyl Room there, room! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Surrey |
| SURREY | Save the man’s life, if it be possible! |
| SHERIFF | It is too late, my lord, he’s dead already. |
| SURREY | I tell ye, Master Sheriff, you are too forward<br>To make such haste with men unto their death.<br>I think your pains will merit little thanks,<br>Since that his highness is so merciful<br>As not to spill the blood of any subject. |
| SHERIFF | My noble lord, would we so much had known!<br>The Council’s warrant hastened our dispatch.<br>It had not else been done so suddenly. |
| SURREY | Sir Thomas More humbly upon his knee<br>Did beg the lives of all, since on his word<br>They did so gently yield. The King hath granted it,<br>And made him Lord High Chancellor of England,<br>According as he worthily deserves.<br>Since Lincoln’s life cannot be had again,<br>Then for the rest, from my dread sovereign’s lips,<br>I here pronounce free pardon for them all— |
| ALL | (flinging up caps)<br>God save the King! God save the King,<br>My good Lord Chancellor and the Earl of Surreyl |
| DOLL | And Doll desires it from her very heart<br>More’s name may live for this right noble part;<br>And whensoe’er we talk of Ill May Day<br>Praise More, whose honest words our falls did stay. |
| SURREY | In hope his highness’ clemency and mercy,<br>Which in the arms of mild and meek compassion<br>Would rather clip you, as the loving nurse<br>Oft doth the wayward infant, than to leave you<br>To the sharp rod of justice; so to draw you<br>To shun such lewd assemblies as beget<br>Unlawful riots and such traitorous acts<br>That, striking with the hand of private hate,<br>Maim your dear country with a public wound.<br>O God, that mercy, whose majestic brow<br><br>Should be unwrinkled, and that awe-full justice,<br><br>Which looketh through a veil of sufferance<br><br>Upon the frailty of the multitude,<br><br>Should with the clamours of outrageous wrongs<br><br>Be stirred and wakened thus to punishment!<br><br>But your deserved death he doth forgive.<br>Who gives you life, pray all he long may live. |
| ALL | God save the King! God save the King,<br>My good Lord Chancellor, and the Earl of Surreyl |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
