| *(didascalia)* | Alarums. Enter [[Cassius]] with an ensign, and Titinius |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | O look, Titinius, look: the villains fly.<br>Myself have to mine own turned enemy:<br>This ensign here of mine was turning back;<br>I slew the coward, and did take it from him. |
| TITINIUS | O [[Cassius]], [[Brutus]] gave the word too early,<br>Who, having some advantage on Octavius,<br>Took it too eagerly. His soldiers fell to spoil,<br>Whilst we by Antony are all enclosed. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pindarus |
| PINDARUS | Fly further off, my lord, fly further off!<br>[[Mark Antony]] is in your tents, my lord;<br>Fly therefore, noble [[Cassius]], fly farre off. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | This [[Hill|hill]] is far enough. Look, look, Titinius,<br>Are those my tents where I perceive the [[Fire|fire]]? |
| TITINIUS | They are, my lord. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Titinius, if thou lovest me,<br>Mount thou my [[Horse|horse]], and hide thy spurs in him<br>Till he have brought thee up to yonder troops<br>And here again, that I may rest assured<br>Whether yon troops are friend or enemy. |
| TITINIUS | I will be here again even with a thought. Exit |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Go, Pindarus, get higher on that [[Hill|hill]].<br>My sight was ever thick. Regard, Titinius,<br>And tell me what thou not’st about the field. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Pindarus |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | This day I breathed first. [[Time]] is come round,<br>And where I did begin, there shall I end.<br>My life is run his compass. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pindarus above |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Sirrah, what news? |
| PINDARUS | O my lord! |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | What news? |
| PINDARUS | Titinius is enclosed round about<br>With horsemen, that make to him on the spur.<br>Yet he spurs on. Now they are almost on him.<br>Now Titinius. Now some light. O, he lights too.<br>He’s ta’en. |
| *(didascalia)* | Shout within |
| PINDARUS | And hark, they shout for joy. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Come down; behold no more. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Pindarus |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | O coward that I am, to live so long<br>To see my best friend ta’en before my face! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pindarus below |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Come hither, sirrah. In Parthia did I take thee prisoner,<br>And then I swore thee, saving of thy life,<br>That whatsoever I did bid thee do<br>Thou shouldst attempt it. Come now, keep thine oath.<br>Now be a freeman, and, with this good [[Sword|sword]]<br>That ran through Caesar’s bowels, search this bosom.<br>Stand not to answer. Here, take thou the hilts,<br>Pindarus takes the [[Sword|sword]]<br>And when my face is covered, as ’tis now,<br>Guide thou the [[Sword|sword]].<br>Pindarus stabs him<br>Caesar, thou art revenged,<br>Even with the [[Sword|sword]] that killed thee.<br>He dies |
| PINDARUS | So, I am free, yet would not so have been<br>Durst I have done my will. O [[Cassius]]!<br>Far from this country Pindarus shall run,<br>Where never Roman shall take note of him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Titinius, wearing a wreath of victory, and Messala |
| MESSALA | It is but change, Titinius, for Octavius<br>Is overthrown by noble [[Brutus]]’ power,<br>As [[Cassius]]’ legions are by Antony. |
| TITINIUS | These tidings will [[Well|well]] comfort [[Cassius]]. |
| MESSALA | Where did you leave him? |
| TITINIUS | All disconsolate,<br>With Pindarus his bondman, on this [[Hill|hill]]. |
| MESSALA | Is not that he that lies upon the ground? |
| TITINIUS | He lies not like the living.—O my heart! |
| MESSALA | Is not that he?<br>TITINlUS No, this was he, Messala;<br>But [[Cassius]] is no more. O setting sun,<br>As in thy red rays thou dost sink tonight,<br>So in his red [[Blood|blood]] [[Cassius]]’ day is set.<br>The sun of Rome is set. Our day is gone.<br>Clouds, dews, and dangers come. Our deeds are done.<br>Mistrust of my [[success]] hath done this deed. |
| MESSALA | Mistrust of good [[success]] hath done this deed.<br>O hateful Error, [[Melancholy]]’s [[Child|child]],<br>Why dost thou show to the apt thoughts of men<br>The things that are not? O Error, soon conceived,<br>Thou never com‘st unto a happy birth,<br>But kill’st the mother that engendered thee. |
| TITINIUS | What, Pindarus! Where art thou, Pindarus? |
| MESSALA | Seek him, Titinius, whilst I go to meet<br>The noble [[Brutus]], thrusting this report<br>Into his ears. I may say ‘thrusting’ it,<br>For piercing steel and darts envenomed<br>Shall be as welcome to the ears of [[Brutus]]<br>As tidings of this sight. |
| TITINIUS | Hie you, Messala,<br>And I will seek for Pindarus the while. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Messala |
| TITINIUS | Why didst thou send me forth, brave [[Cassius]]?<br>Did I not meet thy friends, and did not they<br>Put on my brows this wreath of victory,<br>And bid me give it thee? Didst thou not hear their<br>shouts?<br>Alas, thou hast misconstrued everything.<br>But hold thee, take this garland on thy brow.<br>Thy [[Brutus]] bid me give it thee, and I<br>Will do his bidding. [[Brutus]], come apace,<br>And see how I regarded Caius [[Cassius]].<br>By your leave, gods, this is a Roman’s part:<br>Come [[Cassius]]’ [[Sword|sword]], and find Titinius’ heart.<br>He stabs himself, and dies |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter [[Brutus]], Messala, young Cato, |
| *(didascalia)* | Strato, Volumnius, Lucillius, Labio, and Flavio |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Where, where, Messala, doth his [[Body|body]] lie? |
| MESSALA | Lo yonder, and Titinius mourning it. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Titinius’ face is upward. |
| CATO | He is slain. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O [[Julius Caesar]], thou art mighty yet.<br>Thy spirit walks abroad, and turns our swords<br>In our own proper entrails.<br>Low Alarums |
| CATO | Brave Titinius,<br>Look whe’er he have not crowned dead [[Cassius]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Are yet two Romans living such as these?<br>The last of all the Romans, fare thee [[Well|well]].<br>It is impossible that ever Rome<br>Should breed thy fellow. Friends, I owe more [[Tears|tears]]<br>To this dead man than you shall see me pay.—<br>I shall find [[Time|time]], [[Cassius]], I shall find [[Time|time]].—<br>Come, therefore, and to Thasos send his [[Body|body]].<br>His funerals shall not be in our camp,<br>Lest it discomfort us. Lucillius, come;<br>And come, young Cato. Let us to the field.<br>Labio and Flavio, set our battles on.<br>‘Tis three o’[[Clock|clock]], and, Romans, yet ere [[Night|night]]<br>We shall try fortune in a second fight. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt with the bodies |
