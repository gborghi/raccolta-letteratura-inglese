| MURELLUS | Wherefore rejoice? What conquest brings he [[Home|home]]? What tributaries follow him to Rome To grace in captive bonds his chariot wheels? You blocks, you stones, you worse than senseless things! O, you hard hearts, you cruel men of Rome, Knew you not [[Pompey]]? Many a [[Time|time]] and oft Have you climbed up to walls and battlements, To towers and windows, yea to chimney-tops, Your infants in your arms, and there have sat The livelong day with patient expectation To see great [[Pompey]] pass the streets of Rome. And when you saw his chariot but appear, Have you not made an universal shout, That Tiber trembled underneath her banks To hear the replication of your sounds Made in her concave shores? And do you now put on your best attire? And do you now cull out a holiday? And do you now strew [[Flowers|flowers]] in his way That comes in triumph over [[Pompey]]’s [[Blood|blood]]? Be gone! Run to your houses, fall upon your knees, Pray to the gods to intermit the plague That needs must light on this ingratitude. |
