| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Yet I [[Fear|fear]] him; For in the engrafted [[Love|love]] he bears to Caesar— |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Alas, good [[Cassius]], do not think of him. If he [[Love|love]] Caesar, all that he can do Is to himself: take thought, and die for Caesar. And that were much he should, for he is given To sports, to wildness, and much company. |
| TREBONIUS | There is no [[Fear|fear]] in him. Let him not die; For he will live, and laugh at this hereafter. [[Clock]] strikes |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Peace, count the [[Clock|clock]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | The [[Clock|clock]] hath stricken three. |
| TREBONIUS | ’Tis [[Time|time]] to part. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | But it is doubtful yet Whether Caesar will come forth today or no; For he is superstitious grown of late, Quite from the main opinion he held once Of fantasy, of [[Dreams|dreams]] and ceremonies. It may be these apparent prodigies, The unaccustomed terror of this [[Night|night]], And the [[Persuasion|persuasion]] of his augurers, May hold him from the Capitol today. |
| DECIUS | Never [[Fear|fear]] that. If he be so resolved I can o’ersway him; for he loves to hear That unicorns may be betrayed with trees, And bears with glasses, elephants with holes, Lions with toils, and men with flatterers; But when I tell him he hates flatterers; He says he does, being then most flattered. Let me work, For I can give his [[Humour|humour]] the true bent, And I will bring him to the Capitol. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Nay, we will all of us be there to fetch him. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | By the eighth hour. Is that the uttermost? |
| CINNA | Be that the uttermost, and fail not then. |
| METELLUS | Caius Ligarius doth bear Caesar hard, Who rated him for speaking [[Well|well]] of [[Pompey]]. I [[Wonder|wonder]] none of you have thought of him. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Now good Metellus, go along by him. He loves me [[Well|well]], and I have given him reasons. Send him but hither, and I’ll fashion him. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | The morning comes upon’s. We’ll leave you, [[Brutus]]. And, friends, disperse yourselves; but all remember What you have said, and show yourselves true Romans. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Good gentlemen, look fresh and merrily. Let not our looks put on our purposes; But bear it as our Roman actors do, With untired spirits and formal constancy. And so good morrow to you every one. Exeunt all but [[Brutus]] Boy, [[Lucius]]!—Fast asleep? It is no matter. Enjoy the honey-heavy dew of slumber. Thou hast no figures nor no fantasies Which busy care draws in the brains of men; Therefore thou [[Sleep|sleep]]’st so sound. Enter [[Portia]] |
| [[Portia|PORTIA]] | [[Brutus]], my lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Portia]], what mean you? Wherefore rise you now? It is not for your health thus to commit Your weak condition to the raw cold morning. |
| [[Portia|PORTIA]] | Nor for yours neither. You’ve ungently, [[Brutus]], Stole from my bed; and yesternight at supper You suddenly arose, and walked about Musing and sighing, with your arms across; And when I asked you what the matter was, You stared upon me with ungentle looks. I urged you further; then you scratched your head, And too impatiently stamped with your foot. Yet I insisted; yet you answered not, But with an angry wafture of your hand Gave sign for me to leave you. So I did, Fearing to strengthen that impatience Which seemed too much enkindled, and withal Hoping it was but an effect of [[Humour|humour]], Which sometime hath his hour with every man. It will not let you eat, nor talk, nor [[Sleep|sleep]]; And could it work so much upon your shape As it hath much prevailed on your condition, I should not know you [[Brutus]]. Dear my lord, Make me acquainted with your cause of grief. |
