| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Brutus]] in his orchard |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | What, [[Lucius]], ho!—<br>I cannot by the progress of the [[Stars|stars]]<br>Give guess how near to day.—[[Lucius]], I say!—<br>I would it were my fault to [[Sleep|sleep]] so soundty.—<br>When, [[Lucius]], when? Awake, I say! What, [[Lucius]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]] |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Called you, my lord? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Get me a taper in my study, [[Lucius]]. When it is lighted, come and call me here. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | I will, my lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | It must be by his [[Death|death]]. And for my part<br>I know no personal cause to spurn at him,<br>But for the general. He would be crowned.<br>How that might change his [[Nature|nature]], there’s the<br>question.<br>It is the bright day that brings forth the adder,<br>And that craves wary walking. [[Crown]] him: that!<br>And then I grant we put a sting in him<br>That at his will he may do danger with.<br>Th‘abuse of [[Greatness|greatness]] is when it disjoins<br>Remorse from power. And to speak [[Truth|truth]] of Caesar,<br>I have not known when his affections swayed<br>More than his reason. But ’tis a common proof<br>That lowliness is young [[Ambition|ambition]]’s ladder,<br>Whereto the climber-upward turns his face;<br>But when he once attains the upmost round,<br>He then unto the ladder turns his back,<br>Looks in the clouds, scorning the base degrees<br>By which he did ascend. So Caesar may.<br>Then lest he may, prevent. And since the quarrel<br>Will bear no colour for the thing he is,<br>Fashion it thus: that what he is, augmented,<br>Would run to these and these extremities;<br>And therefore think him as a serpent’s egg,<br>Which, hatched, would as his kind grow mischievous,<br>And kill him in the shell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]], with a letter |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | The taper burneth in your closet, sir.<br>Searching the [[Window|window]] for a flint, I found<br>This paper, thus sealed up, and I am sure<br>It did not lie there when I went to bed.<br>He gives him the letter |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Get you to bed again; it is not day.<br>Is not tomorrow, boy, the ides of March? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | I know not, sir. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Look in the calendar and bring me word. |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | I will, sir. Exit |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | The exhalations whizzing in the air<br>Give so much light that I may read by them.<br>He opens the letter and reads<br><br>‘[[Brutus]], thou [[Sleep|sleep]]’st. Awake, and see thyself.<br>Shall Rome, et cetera? Speak, strike, redress.‘—<br>‘[[Brutus]], thou [[Sleep|sleep]]‘st. Awake.’<br>Such instigations have been often dropped<br>Where I have took them up.<br>‘Shall Rome, et cetera?’ Thus must I piece it out:<br>Shall Rome stand under one man’s awe? What,<br>Rome?<br>My ancestors did from the streets of Rome<br>The [[Tarquin]] drive when he was called a [[King|king]].<br>‘Speak, strike, redress.’ Am I entreated<br>To speak and strike? O Rome, I make thee promise,<br>If the redress will follow, thou receivest<br>Thy full petition at the hand of [[Brutus]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]] |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Sir, March is wasted fifteen days. Knock within |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | ‘Tis good. Go to the gate; somebody knocks. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Lucius]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Since [[Cassius]] first did whet me against Caesar<br>I have not slept.<br>Between the acting of a dreadful thing<br>And the first motion, all the interim is<br>Like a phantasma or a hideous dream.<br>The genius and the mortal instruments<br>Are then in counsel, and the state of man,<br>Like to a little kingdom, suffers then<br>The [[Nature|nature]] of an insurrection. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]] |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Sir, ’tis your brother [[Cassius]] at the door,<br>Who doth [[Desire|desire]] to see you. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Is he [[Alone|alone]]? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | No, sir, there are more with him. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Do you know them? |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | No, sir; their hats are plucked about their ears,<br>And half their faces buried in their cloaks,<br>That by no means I may discover them<br>By any mark of favour. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Let ’em enter. Exit [[Lucius]]<br>They are the faction. O conspiracy,<br>Sham‘st thou to show thy dang’rous brow by [[Night|night]],<br>When evils are most free? O then by day<br>Where wilt thou find a cavern dark enough<br>To mask thy monstrous visage? Seek none, conspiracy.<br>Hide it in smiles and affability;<br>For if thou put thy native semblance on,<br>Not Erebus itself were dim enough<br>To hide thee from prevention. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the conspirators, muffled: [[Cassius]], [[Casca]], Decius, Cinna, Metellus, and Trebonius |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | I think we are too bold upon your rest.<br>Good morrow, [[Brutus]]. Do we trouble you? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I have been up this hour, awake all [[Night|night]].<br>Know I these men that come along with you? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Yes, every man of them; and no man here<br>But honours you; and every one doth wish<br>You had but that opinion of yourself<br>Which every noble Roman bears of you.<br>This is Trebonius. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He is welcome hither. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | This, Decius [[Brutus]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | He is welcome too. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | This, [[Casca]]; Cinna, this; and this, Metellus Cimber. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | They are all welcome.<br>What watchful cares do interpose themselves<br>Betwixt your eyes and [[Night|night]]? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Shall I entreat a word? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Cassius]] and [[Brutus]] stand aside and whisper |
| DECIUS | Here lies the east. Doth not the day break here? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | No. |
| CINNA | O pardon, sir, it doth; and yon grey lines<br>That fret the clouds are messengers of day. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | You shall confess that you are both deceived.<br>He points his [[Sword|sword]]<br><br>Here, as I point my [[Sword|sword]], the sun arises,<br>Which is a great way growing on the south,<br>Weighing the youthful season of the year.<br>Some two months hence up higher toward the north<br>He first presents his [[Fire|fire]], and the high east<br>Stands, as the Capitol, directly here. |
| *(didascalia)* | He points his [[Sword|sword]]. [[Brutus]] and [[Cassius]] join the other conspirators |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Give me your hands all over, one by one.<br>He shakes their hands |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And let us swear our resolution. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | No, not an oath. If not the face of men,<br>The sufferance of our souls, the [[Time|time]]’s abuse—<br>If these be motives weak, break off betimes,<br>And every man hence to his idle bed.<br>So let high-sighted tyranny range on<br>Till each man drop by lottery. But if these,<br>As I am sure they do, bear [[Fire|fire]] enough<br>To kindle cowards and to steel with valour<br>The melting spirits of women, then, countrymen,<br>What need we any spur but our own cause<br>To prick us to redress? What other bond<br>Than secret Romans, that have spoke the word<br>And will not palter? And what other oath<br>Than honesty to honesty engaged<br>That this shall be or we will fall for it?<br>Swear priests and cowards and men cautelous,<br>Old feeble carrions, and such [[Suffering|suffering]] souls<br>That welcome wrongs; unto bad causes swear<br>Such creatures as men [[Doubt|doubt]]; but do not stain<br>The even virtue of our enterprise,<br>Nor th’insuppressive mettle of our spirits,<br>To think that or our cause or our performance<br>Did need an oath, when every drop of [[Blood|blood]]<br>That every Roman bears, and nobly bears,<br>Is guilty of a several bastardy<br>If he do break the smallest particle<br>Of any promise that hath passed from him. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | But what of Cicero? Shall we sound him?<br>I think he will stand very strong with us. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Let us not leave him out. |
| CINNA | No, by no means. |
| METELLUS | O, let us have him, for his silver hairs<br>Will purchase us a good opinion,<br>And buy men’s voices to commend our deeds.<br>It shall be said his judgement ruled our hands.<br>Our youths and wildness shall no whit appear,<br>But all be buried in his gravity. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O, name him not! Let us not break with him,<br>For he will never follow anything<br>That other men begin. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Then leave him out. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Indeed he is not fit. |
| DECIUS | Shall no man else be touched, but only Caesar? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Decius, [[Well|well]] urged. I think it is not meet<br>[[Mark Antony]], so [[Well|well]] beloved of Caesar,<br>Should outlive Caesar. We shall find of him<br>A shrewd contriver. And you know his means,<br>If he improve them, may [[Well|well]] stretch so far<br>As to annoy us all; which to prevent,<br>Let Antony and Caesar fall together. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Our course will seem too bloody, Caius [[Cassius]],<br>To cut the head off and then hack the limbs,<br>Like wrath in [[Death|death]] and envy afterwards—<br>For Antony is but a limb of Caesar.<br>Let’s be sacrificers, but not butchers, Caius.<br>We all stand up against the spirit of Caesar,<br>And in the spirit of men there is no [[Blood|blood]].<br>O, that we then could come by Caesar’s spirit,<br>And not dismember Caesar! But, alas,<br>Caesar must bleed for it. And, gentle friends,<br>Let’s kill him boldly, but not wrathfully.<br>Let’s carve him as a dish fit for the gods,<br>Not hew him as a carcass fit for hounds.<br>And let our hearts, as subtle masters do,<br>Stir up their servants to an act of rage,<br>And after seem to chide ’em. This shall make<br>Our purpose necessary, and not envious;<br>Which so appearing to the common eyes,<br>We shall be called purgers, not murderers.<br>And for [[Mark Antony]], think not of him,<br>For he can do no more than Caesar’s arm<br>When Caesar’s head is off. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Yet I [[Fear|fear]] him;<br>For in the engrafted [[Love|love]] he bears to Caesar— |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Alas, good [[Cassius]], do not think of him.<br>If he [[Love|love]] Caesar, all that he can do<br>Is to himself: take thought, and die for Caesar.<br>And that were much he should, for he is given<br>To sports, to wildness, and much company. |
| TREBONIUS | There is no [[Fear|fear]] in him. Let him not die;<br>For he will live, and laugh at this hereafter.<br>[[Clock]] strikes |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Peace, count the [[Clock|clock]]. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | The [[Clock|clock]] hath stricken three. |
| TREBONIUS | ’Tis [[Time|time]] to part. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | But it is doubtful yet<br>Whether Caesar will come forth today or no;<br>For he is superstitious grown of late,<br>Quite from the main opinion he held once<br>Of fantasy, of [[Dreams|dreams]] and ceremonies.<br>It may be these apparent prodigies,<br>The unaccustomed terror of this [[Night|night]],<br>And the [[Persuasion|persuasion]] of his augurers,<br>May hold him from the Capitol today. |
| DECIUS | Never [[Fear|fear]] that. If he be so resolved<br>I can o’ersway him; for he loves to hear<br>That unicorns may be betrayed with trees,<br>And bears with glasses, elephants with holes,<br>Lions with toils, and men with flatterers;<br>But when I tell him he hates flatterers;<br>He says he does, being then most flattered. Let me<br>work,<br>For I can give his [[Humour|humour]] the true bent,<br>And I will bring him to the Capitol. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Nay, we will all of us be there to fetch him. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | By the eighth hour. Is that the uttermost? |
| CINNA | Be that the uttermost, and fail not then. |
| METELLUS | Caius Ligarius doth bear Caesar hard,<br>Who rated him for speaking [[Well|well]] of [[Pompey]].<br>I [[Wonder|wonder]] none of you have thought of him. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Now good Metellus, go along by him.<br>He loves me [[Well|well]], and I have given him reasons.<br>Send him but hither, and I’ll fashion him. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | The morning comes upon’s. We’ll leave you, [[Brutus]].<br>And, friends, disperse yourselves; but all remember<br>What you have said, and show yourselves true Romans. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Good gentlemen, look fresh and merrily.<br>Let not our looks put on our purposes;<br>But bear it as our Roman actors do,<br>With untired spirits and formal constancy.<br>And so good morrow to you every one. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Brutus]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Boy, [[Lucius]]!—Fast asleep? It is no matter.<br>Enjoy the honey-heavy dew of slumber.<br>Thou hast no figures nor no fantasies<br>Which busy care draws in the brains of men;<br>Therefore thou [[Sleep|sleep]]’st so sound. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Portia]] |
| [[Portia|PORTIA]] | [[Brutus]], my lord. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Portia]], what mean you? Wherefore rise you now?<br>It is not for your health thus to commit<br>Your weak condition to the raw cold morning. |
| [[Portia|PORTIA]] | Nor for yours neither. You’ve ungently, [[Brutus]],<br>Stole from my bed; and yesternight at supper<br>You suddenly arose, and walked about<br>Musing and sighing, with your arms across;<br>And when I asked you what the matter was,<br>You stared upon me with ungentle looks.<br>I urged you further; then you scratched your head,<br>And too impatiently stamped with your foot.<br>Yet I insisted; yet you answered not,<br>But with an angry wafture of your hand<br>Gave sign for me to leave you. So I did,<br>Fearing to strengthen that impatience<br>Which seemed too much enkindled, and withal<br>Hoping it was but an effect of [[Humour|humour]],<br>Which sometime hath his hour with every man.<br>It will not let you eat, nor talk, nor [[Sleep|sleep]];<br>And could it work so much upon your shape<br>As it hath much prevailed on your condition,<br>I should not know you [[Brutus]]. Dear my lord,<br>Make me acquainted with your cause of grief. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I am not [[Well|well]] in health, and that is all. |
| [[Portia|PORTIA]] | [[Brutus]] is wise, and were he not in health<br>He would embrace the means to come by it. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Why, so I do. Good [[Portia]], go to bed. |
| [[Portia|PORTIA]] | Is [[Brutus]] sick? And is it physical<br>To walk unbracèd and suck up the humours<br>Of the dank morning? What, is [[Brutus]] sick?<br>And will he steal out of his wholesome bed<br>To dare the vile contagion of the [[Night|night]],<br>And tempt the rheumy and unpurgèd air<br>To add unto his sickness? No, my [[Brutus]],<br>You have some sick offence within your mind,<br>Which by the right and virtue of my place<br>I ought to know of. (Kneeling) And upon my knees,<br>I charm you by my once-commended [[Beauty|beauty]],<br>By all your vows of [[Love|love]], and that great vow<br>Which did incorporate and make us one,<br>That you unfold to me, your [[Self|self]], your half,<br>Why you are heavy, and what men tonight<br>Have had resort to you—for here have been<br>Some six or seven, who did hide their faces<br>Even from darkness. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Kneel not, gentle [[Portia]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Portia|PORTIA]] rising |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | I should not need if you were gentle [[Brutus]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Within the bond of [[Marriage|marriage]], tell me, [[Brutus]], |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Is it excepted I should know no [[Secrets|secrets]]<br>That appertain to you? Am I your [[Self|self]]<br>But as it were in sort or limitation?<br>To keep with you at meals, comfort your bed,<br>And talk to you sometimes? Dwell I but in the<br>suburbs<br>Of your good pleasure? If it be no more,<br>[[Portia]] is [[Brutus]]’ harlot, not his wife. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | You are my true and honourable wife,<br>As dear to me as are the ruddy drops<br>That visit my sad heart. |
| [[Portia|PORTIA]] | If this were true, then should I know this secret.<br>I grant I am a woman, but withal<br>A woman that Lord [[Brutus]] took to wife.<br>I grant I am a woman, but withal<br>A woman [[Well|well]] reputed, Cato’s daughter.<br>Think you I am no stronger than my sex,<br>Being so fathered and so husbanded?<br>Tell me your counsels; I will not disclose ’em.<br>I have made strong proof of my constancy,<br>Giving myself a voluntary wound<br>Here in the thigh. Can I bear that with patience,<br>And not my husband’s [[Secrets|secrets]]? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O ye gods,<br>Render me worthy of this noble wife! |
| *(didascalia)* | Knocking within |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Hark, hark, one knocks. [[Portia]], go in a while,<br>And by and by thy bosom shall partake<br>The [[Secrets|secrets]] of my heart.<br>All my engagements I will construe to thee,<br>All the charactery of my sad brows.<br>Leave me with haste. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Portia]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | [[Lucius]], who’s that knocks? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lucius]], and Ligarius, with a kerchief round his head |
| [[Lucius|LUCIUS]] | Here is a sick man that would speak with you. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Caius Ligarius, that Metellus spake of.—<br>Boy, stand aside. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Lucius]] |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Caius Ligarius, how? |
| LIGARIUS | Vouchsafe good morrow from a feeble tongue. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | O, what a [[Time|time]] have you chose out, brave Caius,<br>To wear a kerchief! Would you were not sick! |
| LIGARIUS | I am not sick if [[Brutus]] have in hand<br>Any exploit worthy the name of [[Honour|honour]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Such an exploit have I in hand, Ligarius,<br>Had you a healthful ear to hear of it. |
| LIGARIUS | By all the gods that Romans bow before,<br>I here discard my sickness.<br>He pulls off his kerchief [[Soul]] of Rome,<br>Brave son derived from honourable loins,<br>Thou like an exorcist hast conjured up<br>My mortifièd spirit. Now bid me run,<br>And I will strive with things impossible,<br>Yea, get the better of them. What’s to do? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | A piece of work that will make sick men whole. |
| LIGARIUS | But are not some whole that we must make sick? |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | That must we also. What it is, my Caius,<br>I shall unfold to thee as we are going<br>To whom it must be done. |
| LIGARIUS | Set on your foot,<br>And with a heart new-fired I follow you<br>To do I know not what; but it sufficeth<br>That [[Brutus]] leads me on. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Follow me then. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
