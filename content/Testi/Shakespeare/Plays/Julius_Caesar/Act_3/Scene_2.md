| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Then follow me, and give me audience, friends.<br>(Aside to [[Cassius]]) [[Cassius]], go you into the other street,<br>And part the numbers.<br>(To the Plebeians)<br>Those that will hear me speak, let ’em stay here;<br>Those that will follow [[Cassius]], go with him;<br>And public reasons shall be rendered<br>Of Caesar’s [[Death|death]].<br>[[Brutus]] ascends to the pulpit |

| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Brutus]] abovein the pulpit |
| THIRD PLEBEIAN | The noble [[Brutus]] is ascended. [[Silence]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Be patient till the last.<br>Romans, countrymen, and [[lovers]], hear me for my<br>cause, and be silent that you may hear. Believe me for<br>mine [[Honour|honour]], and have respect to mine [[Honour|honour]], that<br>you may believe. Censure me in your wisdom, and<br>awake your senses, that you may the better judge. If<br>there be any in this assembly, any dear friend of<br>Caesar’s, to him I say that [[Brutus]]’ [[Love|love]] to Caesar was<br>no less than his. If then that friend demand why [[Brutus]]<br>[[Rose|rose]] against Caesar, this is my answer: not that I loved<br>Caesar less, but that I loved Rome more. Had you<br>rather Caesar were living, and die all slaves, than that<br>Caesar were dead, to live all free men? As Caesar loved<br>me, I weep for him. As he was fortunate, I rejoice at<br>it. As he was valiant, I [[Honour|honour]] him. But as he was<br>ambitious, I slew him. There is [[Tears|tears]] for his [[Love|love]], joy<br>for his fortune, [[Honour|honour]] for his valour, and [[Death|death]] for<br>his [[Ambition|ambition]]. Who is here so base that would be a<br>bondman? If any, speak, for him have I offended. Who<br>is here so rude that would not be a Roman? If any,<br>speak, for him have I offended. Who is here so vile that<br>will not [[Love|love]] his country? If any, speak, for him have<br>I offended. I pause for a reply. |
| ALL THE PLEBEIANS | None, [[Brutus]], none. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Then none have I offended. I have done no more to Caesar than you shall do to [[Brutus]]. The question of his [[Death|death]] is enrolled in the Capitol, his glory not extenuated wherein he was worthy, nor his offences enforced for which he suffered [[Death|death]]. |

With this I depart: that as I slew my best [[Lover|lover]] for the good of Rome, I have the same dagger for myself when it shall please my country to need my [[Death|death]]. |

| *(didascalia)* | FIFTH PLEBEIAN |

| *(didascalia)* | Enter Antony in the pulpit |
| *(didascalia)* | FOURTH PLEBEIAN |
| THIRD PLEBEIAN | Peace, let us hear what Antony can say. |
| ANTONY | You gentle Romans. ALL THE PLEBEIANS Peace, ho! Let us hear him. |
| ANTONY | Friends, Romans, countrymen, lend me your ears.<br>I come to bury Caesar, not to praise him.<br>The evil that men do lives after them;<br>The good is oft interred with their bones.<br>So let it be with Caesar. The noble [[Brutus]]<br>Hath told you Caesar was ambitious.<br>If it were so, it was a grievous fault,<br>And grievously hath Caesar answered it.<br>Here, under leave of [[Brutus]] and the rest—<br>For [[Brutus]] is an honourable man,<br>So are they all, all honourable men—<br>Come I to speak in Caesar’s funeral.<br>He was my friend, faithful and just to me.<br>But [[Brutus]] says he was ambitious,<br>And [[Brutus]] is an honourable man.<br>He hath brought many captives [[Home|home]] to Rome,<br>Whose ransoms did the general coffers fill.<br><br>Did this in Caesar seem ambitious?<br>When that the poor have cried, Caesar hath wept.<br>[[Ambition]] should be made of sterner stuff.<br>Yet [[Brutus]] says he was ambitious,<br>And [[Brutus]] is an honourable man.<br>You all did see that on the Lupercal<br>I thrice presented him a kingly [[Crown|crown]],<br>Which he did thrice refuse. Was this [[Ambition|ambition]]?<br>Yet [[Brutus]] says he was ambitious,<br>And sure he is an honourable man.<br>I speak not to disprove what [[Brutus]] spoke,<br>But here I am to speak what I do know.<br>You all did [[Love|love]] him once, not without cause.<br>What cause withholds you then to mourn for him?<br>O judgement, thou art fled to brutish beasts,<br>And men have lost their reason!<br>He weeps Bear with me.<br>My heart is in the coffin there with Caesar,<br>And I must pause till it come back to me. |

| ANTONY | But yesterday the word of Caesar might<br>Have stood against the world. I rather choose<br>To wrong the dead, to wrong myself and you,<br>Than I will wrong such honourable men.<br>But here’s a parchment with the seal of Caesar.<br>I found it in his closet. ’Tis his will.<br>Let but the commons hear this testament—<br>Which, pardon me, I do not mean to read—<br>And they would go and kiss dead Caesar’s wounds,<br>And dip their napkins in his sacred [[Blood|blood]],<br>Yea, beg a hair of him for [[Memory|memory]],<br>And, dying, mention it within their wills,<br>Bequeathing it as a rich legacy<br>Unto their issue. |

| ANTONY | If you have [[Tears|tears]], prepare to shed them now.<br>You all do know this mantle. | ANTONY | Good friends, sweet friends, let me not stir you up<br>To such a sudden flood of mutiny.<br>They that have done this deed are honourable.<br>What private griefs they have, alas, I know not,<br>That made them do it. They are wise and honourable,<br>And will no [[Doubt|doubt]] with reasons answer you.<br>I come not, friends, to steal away your hearts.<br>I am no orator as [[Brutus]] is,<br>But, as you know me all, a plain blunt man<br>That [[Love|love]] my friend; and that they know full [[Well|well]]<br>That gave me public leave to speak of him.<br>For I have neither wit, nor words, nor worth,<br>Action, nor utterance, nor the power of speech,<br>To stir men’s [[Blood|blood]]. I only speak right on.<br>I tell you that which you yourselves do know,<br>Show you sweet Caesar’s wounds, poor poor dumb<br>mouths,<br>And bid them speak for me. But were I [[Brutus]],<br>And [[Brutus]] Antony, there were an Antony<br>Would ruffle up your spirits, and put a tongue<br>In every wound of Caesar that should move<br>The stones of Rome to rise and mutiny. |

| ANTONY | Yet hear me, countrymen, yet hear me speak. |
| ALL THE PLEBEIANS | Peace, ho! Hear Antony, most noble Antony. |
| ANTONY | Why, friends, you go to do you know not what.<br>Wherein hath Caesar thus deserved your loves?<br>Alas, you know not. I must tell you’ then.<br>You have forgot the will I told you of. |
| ALL THE PLEBEIANS | Most true. The will. Let’s stay and hear the will. |
| ANTONY | Here is the will, and under Caesar’s seal.<br>To every Roman citizen he gives—<br>To every several man—seventy-five drachmas. |
| *(didascalia)* | FOURTH PLEBEIAN |
| ANTONY | Most noble Caesar! We’ll [[Revenge|revenge]] his [[Death|death]]. |
| THIRD PLEBEIAN | O royal Caesar! |
| ANTONY | Hear me with patience. |
| ALL THE PLEBEIANS | Peace, ho! |
| ANTONY | Moreover he hath left you all his walks,<br>His private arbours, and new-planted orchards,<br>On this side Tiber. He hath left them you,<br>And to your heirs for ever—common pleasures<br>To walk abroad and recreate yourselves.<br>Here was a Caesar. When comes such another? |
