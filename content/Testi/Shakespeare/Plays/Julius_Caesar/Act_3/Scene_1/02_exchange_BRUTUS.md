| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | (coming forward and kneeling) I kiss thy hand, but not in flattery, Caesar, Desiring thee that Publius Cimber may Have an immediate [[Freedom|freedom]] of repeal. |
| CAESAR | What, [[Brutus]]? |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | (coming forward and kneeling) Pardon, Caesar; Caesar, pardon. As low as to thy foot doth [[Cassius]] fall To beg enfranchisement for Publius Cimber. |
| CAESAR | I could be [[Well|well]] moved if I were as you. If I could pray to move, prayers would move me. But I am constant as the Northern Star, Of whose true fixed and resting quality There is no fellow in the firmament. The skies are painted with unnumbered sparks; They are all [[Fire|fire]], and every one doth shine; But there’s but one in all doth hold his place. So in the world: ‘tis furnished [[Well|well]] with men, And men are flesh and [[Blood|blood]], and apprehensive; Yet in the number I do know but one That unassailable holds on his rank, Unshaked of motion; and that I am he Let me a little show it even in this— That I was constant Cimber should be banished, And constant do remain to keep him so. |
| CINNA | (coming forward and kneeling) O Caesar! |
| CAESAR | Hence! Wilt thou lift up Olympus? |
| DECIUS | (coming forward With Ligarius and kneeling) Great Caesar! |
| CAESAR | Doth not [[Brutus]] bootless kneel? |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | (coming forward and kneeling) Speak hands for me. They stab Caesar, [[Casca]] first, [[Brutus]] last |
| CAESAR | Et tu, Bruté?—Then fall Caesar. He dies |
| CINNA | Liberty! [[Freedom]]! Tyranny is dead! Run hence, proclaim, cry it about the streets. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Some to the common pulpits, and cry out ‘Liberty, [[Freedom|freedom]], and enfranchisement!’ |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | People and senators, be not affrighted. Exeunt in a tumult [[Lepidus]], Popillius, other senators, Artemidorus, Soothsayer, and citizen Fly not! Stand still! [[Ambition]]’s debt is paid. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Go to the pulpit, [[Brutus]]. |
| DECIUS | And [[Cassius]] too. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Where’s Publius? |
| CINNA | Here, quite confounded with this mutiny. |
| METELLUS | Stand fast together, lest some friend of Caesar’s Should chance—- |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Talk not of standing.—Pubtius, good cheer! There is no harm intended to your person, Nor to no Roman else—so tell them, Publius. |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | And leave us, Publius, lest that the people, Rushing on us, should do your age some mischief. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Do so; and let no man abide this deed But we the doers. Exit Publius Enter Trebonius |
| [[Cassius|CASSIUS]] | Where is Antony? |
| TREBONIUS | Fled to his [[house]], amazed. Men, wives, and children stare, cry out, and run, As it were doomsday. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Fates, we will know your pleasures. That we shall die, we know; ‘tis but the [[Time|time]] And drawing days out that men stand upon. |
| [[Casca|CASCA]] | Why, he that cuts off twenty years of life Cuts off so many years of fearing [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Brutus|BRUTUS]] | Grant that, and then is [[Death|death]] a benefit. So are we Caesar’s friends, that have abridged His [[Time|time]] of fearing [[Death|death]]. Stoop, Romans, stoop, And let us bathe our hands in Caesar’s [[Blood|blood]] Up to the elbows, and besmear our swords; Then walk we forth even to the market-place, And, waving our red weapons o‘er our heads, Let’s all cry ‘peace, [[Freedom|freedom]], and liberty!’ |
