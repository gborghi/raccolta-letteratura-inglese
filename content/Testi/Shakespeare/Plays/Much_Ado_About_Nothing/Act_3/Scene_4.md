| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Hero]], Margaret, and Ursula |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | Good Ursula, wake my cousin [[Beatrice]], and [[Desire|desire]] her to rise. |
| URSULA | I will, lady. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | And bid her come hither. |
| URSULA | [[Well]]. Exit |
| MARGARET | Troth, I think your other rebato were better. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | No, pray thee, good Meg, I’ll wear this. |
| MARGARET | By my troth, ’s not so good, and I warrant your cousin will say so. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | My cousin’s a [[Fool|fool]], and thou art another: I’ll wear none but this. |
| MARGARET | I like the new tire within excellently, if the hair were a thought browner. And your gown’s a most rare fashion, i‘[[Faith|faith]]. I saw the Duchess of Milan’s gown that they praise so. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | O, that exceeds, they say. |
| MARGARET | By my troth, ’s but a [[Night|night]]-gown in respect of yours—cloth o’ gold, and cuts, and laced with silver, set with pearls, down sleeves, side sleeves, and skirts round underborne with a bluish tinsel. But for a fine, quaint, graceful, and excellent fashion, yours is worth ten on’t. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | God give me joy to wear it, for my heart is exceeding heavy. |
| MARGARET | ’Twill be heavier soon by the weight of a man. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | Fie upon thee, art not ashamed? |
| MARGARET | Of what, lady? Of speaking honourably ? Is not [[Marriage|marriage]] honourable in a beggar? Is not your lord honourable without [[Marriage|marriage]]? I think you would have me say ‘saving your reverence, a husband’. An bad thinking do not wrest true speaking, I’ll offend nobody. Is there any harm in ‘the heavier for a husband’? None, I think, an it be the right husband and the right wife—otherwise ’tis light and not heavy. Ask my Lady [[Beatrice]] else. Here she comes. Enter [[Beatrice]] |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | Good morrow, coz. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | Good morrow, sweet [[Hero]]. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | Why, how now? Do you speak in the sick tune? [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] I am out of all other tune, methinks. MARGARET Clap ’s into ‘Light o’ [[Love|love]]’. That goes without a burden. Do you sing it, and I’ll [[Dance|dance]] it. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | Ye light o’ [[Love|love]] with your heels. Then if your husband have stables enough, you’ll see he shall lack no barns. |
| MARGARET | O illegitimate construction! I scorn that with my heels. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | (to [[Hero]]) ‘Tis almost five o’[[Clock|clock]], cousin. ’Tis [[Time|time]] you were ready. By my troth, I am exceeding ill. Heigh-ho! |
| MARGARET | For a hawk, a [[Horse|horse]], or a husband? |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | For the letter that begins them all—h. |
| MARGARET | [[Well]], an you be not turned Turk, there’s no more sailing by the star. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | What means the [[Fool|fool]], trow? |
| MARGARET | Nothing, I. But God send everyone their heart’s [[Desire|desire]]. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | These gloves the Count sent me, they are an excellent perfume. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | I am stuffed, cousin. I cannot smell. |
| MARGARET | A maid, and stuffed! There’s goodly catching of cold. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | O, God help me, God help me. How long have you professed apprehension? |
| MARGARET | Ever since you left it. Doth not my wit become me rarely? |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | It is not seen enough. You should wear it in your cap. By my troth, I am sick. |
| MARGARET | Get you some of this distilled carduus benedictus, and lay it to your heart. It is the only thing for a qualm. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | There thou prickest her with a thistle. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | Benedictus—why Benedictus? You have some moral in this Benedictus. |
| MARGARET | Moral? No, by my troth, I have no moral meaning. I meant plain holy-thistle. You may think perchance that I think you are in [[Love|love]]. Nay, by’r Lady, I am not such a [[Fool|fool]] to think what I list, nor I list not to think what I can, nor indeed I cannot think, if I would think my heart out of thinking, that you are in [[Love|love]], or that you will be in [[Love|love]], or that you can be in [[Love|love]]. Yet [[Benedick]] was such another, and now is he become a man. He swore he would never marry, and yet now in despite of his heart he eats his meat without grudging. And how you may be converted I know not, but methinks you look with your eyes, as other women do. |
| [[Beatrice|BEATRICE]] | What pace is this that thy tongue keeps? |
| MARGARET | Not a false gallop. Enter Ursula |
| URSULA | (to [[Hero]]) Madam, withdraw. The Prince, the Count, Signor [[Benedick]], [[Don John]], and all the gallants of the town are come to fetch you to church. |
| [[Hero|HERO]] | Help to dress me, good coz, good Meg, good Ursula. Exeunt |
