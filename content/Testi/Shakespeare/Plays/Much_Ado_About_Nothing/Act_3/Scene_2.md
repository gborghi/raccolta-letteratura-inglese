| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Don Pedro]] the Prince, [[Claudio]], [[Benedick]], and [[Leonato]] |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | I do but stay till your [[Marriage|marriage]] be consummate, and then go I toward Aragon. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | I’ll bring you thither, my lord, if you’ll vouchsafe me. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Nay, that would be as great a soil in the new<br>gloss of your [[Marriage|marriage]] as to show a [[Child|child]] his new coat<br>and forbid him to wear it. I will only be bold with<br>[[Benedick]] for his company, for from the [[Crown|crown]] of his<br>head to the sole of his foot he is all mirth. He hath<br>twice or thrice cut Cupid’s bow-string, and the little<br>hangman dare not shoot at him. He hath a heart as<br>sound as a bell, and his tongue is the clapper, for what<br>his heart thinks his tongue speaks. |
| [[Benedick|BENEDICK]] | Gallants, I am not as I have been. |
| [[Leonato|LEONATO]] | So say I. Methinks you are sadder. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | I [[Hope|hope]] he be in [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Hang him, truant! There’s no true drop of [[Blood|blood]] in him to be truly touched with [[Love|love]]. If he be sad, he wants money. |
| [[Benedick|BENEDICK]] | I have the toothache. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Draw it. |
| [[Benedick|BENEDICK]] | Hang it. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | You must hang it first and draw it afterwards. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | What? Sigh for the toothache? |
| [[Leonato|LEONATO]] | Where is but a [[Humour|humour]] or a worm. |
| [[Benedick|BENEDICK]] | [[Well]], everyone can master a grief but he that has it. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | Yet say I he is in [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | There is no appearance of fancy in him, unless it be a fancy that he hath to strange disguises, as to be a Dutchman today, a Frenchman tomorrow, or in the shape of two countries at once, as a German from the waist downward, all slops, and a Spaniard from the hip upward, no doublet. Unless he have a fancy to this foolery, as it appears he hath, he is no [[Fool|fool]] for fancy, as you would have it appear he is. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | If he be not in [[Love|love]] with some woman there is no believing old signs. A brushes his hat o’ mornings, what should that bode? |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Hath any man seen him at the barber’s? |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | No, but the barber’s man hath been seen with him, and the old ornament of his cheek hath already stuffed tennis balls. |
| [[Leonato|LEONATO]] | Indeed, he looks younger than he did by the loss of a beard. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Nay, a rubs himself with civet. Can you smell him out by that? |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | That’s as much as to say the sweet youth’s in [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | The greatest note of it is his [[Melancholy|melancholy]]. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | And when was he wont to wash his face? |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Yea, or to paint himself?—for the which I hear what they say of him. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | Nay, but his jesting spirit, which is now crept into a lute-string, and now governed by stops. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Indeed, that tells a heavy tale for him. Conclude, conclude, he is in [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | Nay, but I know who loves him. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | That would I know, too. I warrant, one that knows him not. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | Yes, and his ill conditions, and in despite of all, dies for him. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | She shall be buried with her face upwards. |
| [[Benedick|BENEDICK]] | Yet is this no charm for the toothache. Old signor, walk aside with me. I have studied eight or nine wise words to speak to you which these hobby-horses must not hear. Exeunt [[Benedick]] and [[Leonato]] |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | For my life, to break with him about [[Beatrice]]. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | ’Tis even so. [[Hero]] and Margaret have by this played their parts with [[Beatrice]], and then the two bears will not bite one another when they meet. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Don John]] the bastard |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | My lord, and brother, God save you. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Good-e’en, brother. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | If your leisure served I would speak with you. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | In private? |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | If it please you. Yet Count [[Claudio]] may hear, for what I would speak of concerns him. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | What’s the matter? |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | (to [[Claudio]]) Means your lordship to be married tomorrow ? |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | You know he does. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | I know not that when he knows what I know. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | If there be any impediment, I pray you discover it. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | You may think I [[Love|love]] you not. Let that appear hereafter, and aim better at me by that I now will manifest. For my brother, I think he holds you [[Well|well]] and in dearness of heart hath holp to effect your ensuing [[Marriage|marriage]]—surely suit ill spent, and labour ill bestowed. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | Why, what’s the matter? |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | I came hither to tell you, and, circumstances shortened—for she has been too long a-talking of—the lady is disloyal. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | Who, [[Hero]]? |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | Even she. [[Leonato]]’s [[Hero]], your [[Hero]], every man’s [[Hero]]. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | Disloyal? |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | The word is too good to paint out her wickedness. I could say she were worse. Think you of a worse title, and I will fit her to it. [[Wonder]] not till further warrant. Go but with me tonight, you shall see her chamber [[Window|window]] entered, even the [[Night|night]] before her wedding day. If you [[Love|love]] her then, tomorrow wed her. But it would better fit your [[Honour|honour]] to change your mind. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | May this be so? |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | I will not think it. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | If you dare not trust that you see, confess not that you know. If you will follow me I will show you enough, and when you have seen more and heard more, proceed accordingly. |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | If I see anything tonight why I should not marry her, tomorrow, in the congregation where I should wed, there will I shame her. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | And as I wooed for thee to obtain her, I will join with thee to disgrace her. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | I will disparage her no farther till you are my witnesses. Bear it coldly but till midnight, and let the issue show itself. |
| [[Don Pedro|DON PEDRO]] | O day untowardly turned! |
| [[Claudio|CLAUDIO]] | O mischief strangely thwarting! |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | O plague right [[Well|well]] prevented!—So will you say when you have seen the sequel. Exeunt |
