| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Don John]] the bastard and Conrad, his companion |
| CONRAD | What the goodyear, my lord, why are you thus out of measure sad? |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | There is no measure in the occasion that breeds it, therefore the sadness is without limit. |
| CONRAD | You should hear reason. [[Don John|DON JOHN]] And when I have heard it, what blessing brings it? |
| CONRAD | If not a present remedy, at least a patient sufferance. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | I [[Wonder|wonder]] that thou—being, as thou sayst thou art, born under [[Saturn]]—goest about to apply a moral medicine to a mortifying mischief. I cannot hide what I am. I must be sad when I have cause, and smile at no man’s jests; eat when I have stomach, and wait for no man’s leisure; [[Sleep|sleep]] when I am drowsy, and tend on no man’s business; laugh when I am merry, and claw no man in his [[Humour|humour]]. |
| CONRAD | Yea, but you must not make the full show of this till you may do it without controlment. You have of late stood out against your brother, and he hath ta’en you newly into his grace, where it is impossible you should take true root but by the fair weather that you make yourself. It is needful that you frame the season for your own harvest. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | I had rather be a canker in a hedge than a [[Rose|rose]] in his grace, and it better fits my [[Blood|blood]] to be disdained of all than to fashion a carriage to rob [[Love|love]] from any. In this, though I cannot be said to be a flattering honest man, it must not be denied but I am a plain-dealing [[Villain|villain]]. I am trusted with a muzzle, and enfranchised with a clog. Therefore I have decreed not to sing in my cage. If I had my mouth I would bite. If I had my liberty I would do my liking. In the mean [[Time|time]], let me be that I am, and seek not to alter me. |
| CONRAD | Can you make no use of your discontent? |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | I make all use of it, for I use it only. Who comes here? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Borachio |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | What news, Borachio? |
| BORACHIO | I came yonder from a great supper. The Prince your brother is royally entertained by [[Leonato]], and I can give you intelligence of an intended [[Marriage|marriage]]. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | Will it serve for any model to build mischief on? What is he for a [[Fool|fool]] that betroths himself to unquietness? |
| BORACHIO | Marry, it is your brother’s right hand. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | Who, the most exquisite [[Claudio]]? |
| BORACHIO | Even he. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | A proper squire. And who, and who? Which way looks he? |
| BORACHIO | Marry, on [[Hero]], the daughter and heir of [[Leonato]]. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | A very forward March chick. How came you to this? |
| BORACHIO | Being entertained for a perfumer, as I was smoking a musty room comes me the Prince and [[Claudio]] hand in hand, in sad conference. I whipped me behind the arras, and there heard it agreed upon that the Prince should woo [[Hero]] for himself and, having obtained her, give her to Count [[Claudio]]. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | Come, come, let us thither. This may prove food to my displeasure. That young start-up hath all the glory of my overthrow. If I can [[Cross|cross]] him any way I bless myself every way. You are both sure, and will assist me? |
| CONRAD | To the [[Death|death]], my lord. |
| [[Don John|DON JOHN]] | Let us to the great supper. Their cheer is the greater that I am subdued. Would the cook were o’ my mind. Shall we go prove what’s to be done? |
| BORACHIO | We’ll wait upon your lordship. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
