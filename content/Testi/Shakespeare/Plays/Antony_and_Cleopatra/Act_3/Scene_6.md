| *(didascalia)* | Enter Agrippa, Maecenas, and Caesar |
| CAESAR | Contemning Rome, he has done all this and more<br>In Alexandria. Here’s the manner of’t:<br>I’th’ market place on a tribunal silvered,<br>[[Cleopatra]] and himself in chairs of gold<br>Were publicly enthroned. At the feet sat<br>Caesarion, whom they call my father’s son,<br>And all the unlawful issue that their lust<br>Since then hath made between them. Unto her<br>He gave the stablishment of [[Egypt]]; made her<br>Of lower Syria, Cyprus, Lydia,<br>Absolute [[Queen|queen]]. |
| MAECENAS | This in the public eye? |
| CAESAR | I‘th’ common showplace, where they exercise.<br>His sons he there proclaimed the kings of kings;<br>Great Media, Parthia, and Armenia<br>He gave to Alexander. To Ptolemy he assigned<br>Syria, Cilicia, and Phoenicia. She<br>In th’habiliments of the goddess Isis<br>That day appeared, and oft before gave audience,<br>As ’tis reported, so. |
| MAECENAS | Let Rome be thus informed. |
| AGRIPPA | Who, queasy with his insolence already,<br>Will their good thoughts call from him. |
| CAESAR | The people knows it,<br>And have now received his accusations. |
| AGRIPPA | Who does he accuse? |
| CAESAR | Caesar, and that having in Sicily<br>Sextus Pompeius spoiled, we had not rated him<br>His part o’th’ isle. Then does he say he lent me<br>Some shipping, unrestored. Lastly, he frets<br>That [[Lepidus]] of the triumvirate<br>Should be deposed; and being, that we detain<br>All his revenue. |
| AGRIPPA | Sir, this should be answered. |
| CAESAR | ’Tis done already, and the messenger gone.<br>I have told him [[Lepidus]] was grown too cruel,<br>That he his high authority abused<br>And did deserve his change. For what I have<br>conquered,<br>I grant him part; but then in his Armenia,<br>And other of his conquered kingdoms,<br>I demand the like. |
| MAECENAS | He’ll never yield to that. |
| CAESAR | Nor must not then be yielded to in this. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Octavia]] with her train |
| [[Octavia|OCTAVIA]] | Hail, Caesar, and my lord; hail, most dear Caesar! |
| CAESAR | That ever I should call thee castaway! |
| [[Octavia|OCTAVIA]] | You have not called me so, nor have you cause. |
| CAESAR | Why have you stol’n upon us thus? You come not<br>Like Caesar’s sister. The wife of Antony<br>Should have an army for an usher, and<br>The neighs of [[Horse|horse]] to tell of her approach<br>Long ere she did appear. The trees by th’ way<br>Should have borne men, and expectation fainted,<br>[[Longing]] for what it had not. Nay, the [[Dust|dust]]<br>Should have ascended to the roof of heaven,<br>Raised by your populous troops. But you are come<br>A market maid to Rome, and have prevented<br>The ostentation of our [[Love|love]]; which, left unshown,<br>Is often left unloved. We should have met you<br>By sea and land, supplying every stage<br>With an augmented greeting. |
| [[Octavia|OCTAVIA]] | Good my lord,<br>To come thus was I not constrained, but did it<br>On my free will. My lord, [[Mark Antony]],<br>Hearing that you prepared for war, acquainted<br>My grieved ear withal, whereon I begged<br>His pardon for return. |
| CAESAR | Which soon he granted,<br>Being an obstruct ’tween his lust and him. |
| [[Octavia|OCTAVIA]] | Do not say so, my lord. |
| CAESAR | I have eyes upon him,<br>And his affairs come to me on the [[Wind|wind]].<br>Where is he now? |
| [[Octavia|OCTAVIA]] | My lord, in Athens. |
| CAESAR | No, my most wronged sister. [[Cleopatra]]<br>Hath nodded him to her. He hath given his empire<br>Up to a whore; who now are levying<br>The kings o’th’ earth for war. He hath assembled<br>Bocchus, the [[King]] of Libya; Archelaus<br>Of Cappadocia; Philadelphos, [[King]]<br>Of Paphlagonia; the Thracian [[King]] Adallas;<br>[[King]] Malchus of Arabia; [[King]] of Pont;<br>Herod of Jewry; Mithridates, [[King]]<br>Of Comagene; Polemon and Amyntas,<br>The Kings of Mede and Lycaonia;<br>With a more larger list of sceptres. |
| [[Octavia|OCTAVIA]] | Ay me most wretched,<br>That have my heart parted betwixt two friends<br>That does afflict each other! |
| CAESAR | Welcome hither.<br>Your letters did withhold our breaking forth<br>Till we perceived both how you were wrong led<br>And we in negligent danger. Cheer your heart.<br>Be you not troubled with the [[Time|time]], which drives<br>O’er your content these strong necessities;<br>But let determined things to destiny<br>Hold unbewailed their way. Welcome to Rome;<br>Nothing more dear to me. You are abused<br>Beyond the mark of thought, and the high gods,<br>To do you justice, makes their ministers<br>Of us and those that [[Love|love]] you. Best of comfort,<br>And ever welcome to us. |
| AGRIPPA | Welcome, lady. |
| MAECENAS | Welcome, dear madam.<br>Each heart in Rome does [[Love|love]] and pity you.<br>Only th’adulterous Antony, most large<br>In his abominations, turns you off,<br>And gives his potent regiment to a trull<br>That noises it against us. |
| [[Octavia|OCTAVIA]] | Is it so, sir? |
| CAESAR | Most certain. Sister, welcome. Pray you<br>Be ever known to patience. My dear’st sister! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
