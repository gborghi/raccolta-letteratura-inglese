| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Antony and Cleopatra, with Charmian and others |
| ANTONY | (calling)<br>Eros, mine armour, Eros! |
| CLEOPATRA | Sleep a little. |
| ANTONY | No, my chuck. Eros, come, mine armour, Eros! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Eros with armour |
| ANTONY | Come, good fellow, put thine iron on.<br>If fortune be not ours today, it is<br>Because we brave her. Come. |
| CLEOPATRA | Nay, I’ll help, too.<br>What’s this for? |
| ANTONY | Ah, let be, let be! Thou art<br>The armourer of my heart. False, false! This, this! |
| CLEOPATRA | Sooth, la, I’ll help. Thus it must be.<br>She helps Antony to arm |
| ANTONY | Well, well,<br>We shall thrive now. Seest thou, my good fellow?<br>Go put on thy defences. |
| EROS | Briefly, sir. |
| CLEOPATRA | Is not this buckled well? |
| ANTONY | Rarely, rarely.<br>He that unbuckles this, till we do please<br>To doff’t for our repose, shall hear a storm.<br>Thou fumblest, Eros, and my queen’s a squire<br>More tight at this than thou. Dispatch. O love,<br>That thou couldst see my wars today, and knew’st<br>The royal occupation! Thou shouldst see<br>A workman in’t. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter an armed Soldier |
| ANTONY | Good morrow to thee. Welcome.<br>Thou look’st like him that knows a warlike charge.<br>To business that we love we rise betime,<br>And go to’t with delight. |
| SOLDIER | A thousand, sir,<br>Early though’t be, have on their riveted trim,<br>And at the port expect you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Shout within. Trumpets flourish. Enter Captains and Soldiers |
| CAPTAIN | The morn is fair. Good morrow, General. |
| SOLDIERS | Good morrow, General. |
| ANTONY | ’Tis well blown, lads.<br>This morning, like the spirit of a youth<br>That means to be of note, begins betimes.<br>So, so. Come, give me that. This way. Well said.<br>Fare thee well, dame. Whate’er becomes of me,<br>This is a soldier’s kiss.<br>He kisses Cleopatra<br>Rebukable<br>And worthy shameful check it were to stand<br>On more mechanic compliment. I’ll leave thee<br>Now like a man of steel. You that will fight,<br>Follow me close. I’ll bring you to’t. Adieu. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Cleopatra and Charmian |
| CHARMIAN | Please you retire to your chamber? |
| CLEOPATRA | Lead me.<br>He goes forth gallantly. That he and Caesar might<br>Determine this great war in single fight!<br>Then, Antony—but now! Well, on. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
