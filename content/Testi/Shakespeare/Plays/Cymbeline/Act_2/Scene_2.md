| *(didascalia)* | A trunk and arras. A bed is thrust forth with Innogen in it, reading a book. Enter to her Helen, a lady |
| INNOGEN | Who’s there? My woman Helen? |
| HELEN | Please you, madam. |
| INNOGEN | What hour is it? |
| HELEN | Almost midnight, madam. |
| INNOGEN | I have read three hours then. Mine eyes are weak.<br>Fold down the leaf where I have left. To bed.<br>Take not away the taper; leave it burning,<br>And if thou canst awake by four o’th’ clock,<br>I prithee call me. Sleep hath seized me wholly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Helen |
| INNOGEN | To your protection I commend me, gods.<br>From fairies and the tempters of the night<br>Guard me, beseech ye.<br>She sleeps.<br>Giacomo comes from the trunk |
| GIACOMO | The crickets sing, and man’s o‘er-laboured sense<br>Repairs itself by rest. Our Tarquin thus<br>Did softly press the rushes ere he wakened<br>The chastity he wounded. Cytherea,<br>How bravely thou becom’st thy bed! Fresh lily,<br>And whiter than the sheets! That I might touch,<br>But kiss, one kiss! Rubies unparagoned,<br>How dearly they do‘t! ’Tis her breathing that<br>Perfumes the chamber thus. The flame o‘th’ taper<br>Bows toward her, and would underpeep her lids,<br>To see th’enclosed lights, now canopied<br>Under these windows, white and azure-laced<br>With blue of heaven’s own tinct. But my design-<br>To note the chamber. I will write all down.<br>He writes in his tables<br>Such and such pictures, there the window, such<br>Th‘adornment of her bed, the arras, figures,<br>Why, such and such; and the contents o’th’ story.<br>Ah, but some natural notes about her body<br>Above ten thousand meaner movables<br>Would testify t’enrich mine inventory.<br>O sleep, thou ape of death, lie dull upon her,<br>And be her sense but as a monument<br>Thus in a chapel lying. Come off, come off;<br>As slippery as the Gordian knot was hard.<br>He takes the bracelet from her arm<br>‘Tis mine, and this will witness outwardly,<br>As strongly as the conscience does within,<br>To th’ madding of her lord. On her left breast<br>A mole, cinque-spotted, like the crimson drops<br>I’th’ bottom of a cowslip. Here’s a voucher<br>Stronger than ever law could make. This secret<br>Will force him think I have picked the lock and<br>ta’en<br>The treasure of her honour. No more. To what end?<br>Why should I write this down that’s riveted,<br>Screwed to my memory? She hath been reading late,<br>The tale of Tereus. Here the leaf’s turned down<br>Where Philomel gave up. I have enough.<br>To th’ trunk again, and shut the spring of it.<br>Swift, swift, you dragons of the night, that dawning<br>May bare the raven’s eye! I lodge in fear.<br>Though this’ a heavenly angel, hell is here.<br>Clock strikes<br>One, two, three. Time, time! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit into the trunk. The bed and trunk are removed |
