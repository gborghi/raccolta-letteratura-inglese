| *(didascalia)* | Enter Belarius, Guiderius, Arviragus, and Innogen dressed as a man, from the cave |
| BELARIUS | (to Innogen)<br>You are not well. Remain here in the cave.<br>We’ll come to you from hunting. |
| ARVIRAGUS | (to Innogen)<br>Brother, stay here.<br>Are we not brothers? |
| INNOGEN | So man and man should be,<br>But clay and clay differs in dignity,<br>Whose dust is both alike. I am very sick. |
| GUIDERIUS | (to Belarius and Arviragus)<br>Go you to hunting. I’ll abide with him. |
| INNOGEN | So sick I am not, yet I am not well;<br>But not so citizen a wanton as<br>To seem to die ere sick. So please you, leave me.<br>Stick to your journal course. The breach of custom<br>Is breach of all. I am ill, but your being by me<br>Cannot amend me. Society is no comfort<br>To one not sociable. I am not very sick,<br>Since I can reason of it. Pray you, trust me here.<br>I’ll rob none but myself; and let me die,<br>Stealing so poorly. |
| GUIDERIUS | I love thee: I have spoke it;<br>How much the quantity, the weight as much,<br>As I do love my father. |
| BELARIUS | What, how, how? |
| ARVIRAGUS | If it be sin to say so, sir, I yoke me<br>In my good brother’s fault. I know not why<br>I love this youth, and I have heard you say<br>Love’s reason’s without reason. The bier at door<br>And a demand who is’t shall die, I’d say<br>‘My father, not this youth’. |
| BELARIUS | (aside)<br>O noble strain!<br>O worthiness of nature, breed of greatness!<br>Cowards father cowards, and base things sire base.<br>Nature hath meal and bran, contempt and grace.<br>I’m not their father, yet who this should be<br>Doth miracle itself, loved before me.<br>(Aloud) ‘Tis the ninth hour o’th’ morn. |
| ARVIRAGUS | (to Innogen)<br>Brother, farewell. |
| INNOGEN | I wish ye sport. |
| ARVIRAGUS | You health.—So please you, sir. |
| INNOGEN | (aside)<br>These are kind creatures. Pisanio,<br>I’ll now taste of thy drug. |

| INNOGEN | Well or ill,<br>I am bound to you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |

| *(didascalia)* | BELARIUS |

| *(didascalia)* | Enter Belarius and Arviragus |
| BELARIUS | No company’s abroad? |
| ARVIRAGUS | None in the world. You did mistake him, sure. |
| BELARIUS | I cannot tell. Long is it since I saw him,<br>But time hath nothing blurred those lines of favour<br>Which then he wore. The snatches in his voice<br>And burst of speaking were as his. I am absolute<br>’Twas very Cloten. |
| ARVIRAGUS | In this place we left them.<br>I wish my brother make good time with him,<br>You say he is so fell. |
| BELARIUS | Being scarce made up,<br>I mean to man, he had not apprehension<br>Of roaring terrors; for defect of judgement<br>Is oft the cause of fear. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Guiderius with Cloten’s head |
| BELARIUS | But see, thy brother. |
| GUIDERIUS | This Cloten was a fool, an empty purse,<br>There was no money in’t. Not Hercules<br>Could have knocked out his brains, for he had none.<br>Yet I not doing this, the fool had borne<br>My head as I do his. |
| BELARIUS | What hast thou done? |
| GUIDERIUS | I am perfect what: cut off one Cloten’s head,<br>Son to the Queen after his own report,<br>Who called me traitor, mountaineer, and swore<br>With his own single hand he’d take us in,<br>Displace our heads where—thanks, ye gods—they<br>grow,<br>And set them on Lud’s town. |
| BELARIUS | We are all undone. |
| GUIDERIUS | Why, worthy father, what have we to lose<br>But that he swore to take, our lives? The law<br>Protects not us: then why should we be tender<br>To let an arrogant piece of flesh threat us,<br>Play judge and executioner all himself,<br>For we do fear the law? | BELARIUS | No single soul<br>Can we set eye on, but in all safe reason<br>He must have some attendants. | ARVIRAGUS | Let ord’nance<br>Come as the gods foresay it; howsoe’er,<br>My brother hath done well. |
| BELARIUS | I had no mind<br>To hunt this day. The boy Fidele’s sickness<br>Did make my way long forth.<br>GUIDERlUS<br>With his own sword,<br>Which he did wave against my throat, I have ta’en<br>His head from him. | *(didascalia)* | Exit with Cloten’s head |
| BELARIUS | I fear ’twill be revenged.<br>Would, Polydore, thou hadst not done’t, though<br>valour<br>Becomes thee well enough. |
| ARVIRAGUS | Would I had done’t,<br>So the revenge alone pursued me. Polydore,<br>I love thee brotherly, but envy much<br>Thou hast robbed me of this deed. I would revenges<br>That possible strength might meet would seek us<br>through<br>And put us to our answer. |

| ARVIRAGUS | Poor sick Fidele!<br>I’ll willingly to him. To gain his colour<br>I’d let a parish of such Clotens blood,<br>And praise myself for charity. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit into the cave |
| BELARIUS | O thou goddess,<br>Thou divine Nature, how thyself thou blazon‘st<br>In these two princely boys! They are as gentle<br>As zephyrs blowing below the violet,<br>Not wagging his sweet head; and yet as rough,<br>Their royal blood enchafed, as the rud’st wind<br>That by the top doth take the mountain pine<br>And make him stoop to th’ vale. ’Tis wonder<br>That an invisible instinct should frame them<br>To royalty unlearned, honour untaught,<br>Civility not seen from other, valour<br>That wildly grows in them, but yields a crop<br>As if it had been sowed. Yet still it’s strange<br>What Cloten’s being here to us portends,<br>Or what his death will bring us. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Guiderius |
| GUIDERIUS | Where’s my brother?<br>I have sent Cloten’s clotpoll down the stream<br>In embassy to his mother. His body’s hostage<br>For his return.<br>Solemn music |

| GUIDERIUS | Is he at home? |
| BELARIUS | He went hence even now. |
| GUIDERIUS | What does he mean? Since death of my dear’st mother<br>It did not speak before. All solemn things<br>Should answer solemn accidents. The matter?<br>Triumphs for nothing and lamenting toys<br>Is jollity for apes and grief for boys.<br>Is Cadwal mad? |

| GUIDERIUS | (to Innogen) O sweetest, fairest lily!<br>My brother wears thee not one half so well<br>As when thou grew’st thyself. |

| GUIDERIUS | Where? |
| ARVIRAGUS | O’th’ floor,<br>His arms thus leagued. I thought he slept, and put<br>My clouted brogues from off my feet, whose rudeness<br>Answered my steps too loud. |
| GUIDERIUS | Why, he but sleeps.<br>If he be gone he’ll make his grave a bed.<br>With female fairies will his tomb be haunted,<br>(To Innogen) And worms will not come to thee. |

| GUIDERIUS | Prithee, have done,<br>And do not play in wench-like words with that<br>Which is so serious. Let us bury him,<br>And not protract with admiration what<br>Is now due debt. To th’ grave. |
| ARVIRAGUS | Say, where shall ’s lay him? |
| GUIDERIUS | By good Euriphile, our mother. |
| ARVIRAGUS | Be’t SO,<br>And let us, Polydore, though now our voices<br>Have got the mannish crack, sing him to th’ ground<br>As once our mother; use like note and words,<br>Save that ‘Euriphile’ must be ‘Fidele’. |
| GUIDERIUS | Cadwal,<br>I cannot sing. I’ll weep, and word it with thee,<br>For notes of sorrow out of tune are worse<br>Than priests and fanes that lie. |
| ARVIRAGUS | We’ll speak it then. |
| BELARIUS | Great griefs, I see, medicine the less, for Cloten<br>Is quite forgot. He was a queen’s son, boys,<br>And though he came our enemy, remember<br>He was paid for that. Though mean and mighty<br>rotting<br>Together have one dust, yet reverence,<br>That angel of the world, doth make distinction<br>Of place ’tween high and low. | GUIDERIUS | Pray you, fetch him hither.<br>Thersites’ body is as good as Ajax’<br>When neither are alive. |
| ARVIRAGUS | (to Belarius) If you’ll go fetch him,<br>We’ll say our song the whilst. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Belarius |
| ARVIRAGUS | Brother, begin. |
| GUIDERIUS | Nay, Cadwal, we must lay his head to th’east.<br>My father hath a reason for’t. |
| ARVIRAGUS | ’Tis true. |
| GUIDERIUS | Come on, then, and remove him. |
| ARVIRAGUS | So, begin. |
| GUIDERIUS | Fear no more the heat o‘th’ sun,<br>Nor the furious winter’s rages.<br>Thou thy worldly task hast done,<br>Home art gone and ta’en thy wages.<br>Golden lads and girls all must,<br>As chimney-sweepers, come to dust. |
| ARVIRAGUS | Fear no more the frown o’th’ great,<br>Thou art past the tyrant’s stroke.<br>Care no more to clothe and eat,<br>To thee the reed is as the oak.<br>The sceptre, learning, physic, must<br>All follow this and come to dust. |
| GUIDERIUS | Fear no more the lightning flash, |
| ARVIRAGUS | Nor th’all-dreaded thunder-stone. |
| GUIDERIUS | Fear not slander, censure rash. |
| ARVIRAGUS | Thou hast finished joy and moan. |
| GUIDERIUS | and ARVIRAGUS<br>All lovers young, all lovers must<br>Consign to thee and come to dust. |
| GUIDERIUS | No exorcisor harm thee, |
| ARVIRAGUS | Nor no witchcraft charm thee. |
| GUIDERIUS | Ghost unlaid forbear thee. |
| ARVIRAGUS | Nothing ill come near thee. |
| GUIDERIUS | and ARVIRAGUS<br>Quiet consummation have,<br>And renowned be thy grave. |
