| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pisanio, and Innogen in a riding-suit |
| INNOGEN | Thou told‘st me when we came from horse the place<br>Was near at hand. Ne’er longed my mother so<br>To see me first as I have now. Pisanio, man,<br>Where is Posthumus? What is in thy mind<br>That makes thee stare thus? Wherefore breaks that<br>sigh<br>From th’inward of thee? One but painted thus<br>Would be interpreted a thing perplexed<br>Beyond self-explication. Put thyself<br>Into a haviour of less fear, ere wildness<br>Vanquish my staider senses. What’s the matter?<br>Pisanio gives her a letter<br>Why tender‘st thou that paper to me with<br>A look untender? If’t be summer news,<br>Smile to’t before; if winterly, thou need’st<br>But keep that count’nance still. My husband’s hand?<br>That drug-damned Italy hath out-craftied him,<br>And he’s at some hard point. Speak, man. Thy tongue<br>May take off some extremity which to read<br>Would be even mortal to me. |
| PISANIO | Please you read,<br>And you shall find me, wretched man, a thing<br>The most disdained of fortune. |
| INNOGEN | (reads) ‘Thy mistress, Pisanio, hath played the strumpet in my bed, the testimonies whereof lies bleeding in me. I speak not out of weak surmises but from proof as strong as my grief and as certain as I expect my revenge. That part thou, Pisanio, must act for me, if thy faith be not tainted with the breach of hers. Let thine own hands take away her life. I shall give thee opportunity at Milford Haven. She hath my letter for the purpose, where if thou fear to strike and to make me certain it is done, thou art the pander to her dishonour and equally to me disloyal.’ |
| PISANIO | (aside)<br>What shall I need to draw my sword? The paper<br>Hath cut her throat already. No, ’tis slander,<br>Whose edge is sharper than the sword, whose tongue<br>Outvenoms all the worms of Nile, whose breath<br>Rides on the posting winds and doth belie<br>All corners of the world. Kings, queens, and states,<br>Maids, matrons, nay, the secrets of the grave<br>This viperous slander enters. (To Innogen) What cheer,<br>madam? |
| INNOGEN | False to his bed? What is it to be false?<br>To lie in watch there and to think on him?<br>To weep ’twixt clock and clock? If sleep charge nature,<br>To break it with a fearful dream of him<br>And cry myself awake? That’s false to ’s bed, is it? |
| PISANIO | Alas, good lady. |
| INNOGEN | I false? Thy conscience witness, Giacomo,<br>Thou didst accuse him of incontinency.<br>Thou then lookedst like a villain; now, methinks,<br>Thy favour’s good enough. Some jay of Italy,<br>Whose mother was her painting, hath betrayed him.<br>Poor I am stale, a garment out of fashion,<br>And for I am richer than to hang by th’ walls<br>I must be ripped. To pieces with me! O,<br>Men’s vows are women’s traitors. All good seeming,<br>By thy revolt, O husband, shall be thought<br>Put on for villainy; not born where’t grows,<br>But worn a bait for ladies. |
| PISANIO | Good madam, hear me. |
| INNOGEN | True honest men being heard like false Aeneas<br>Were in his time thought false, and Sinon’s weeping<br>Did scandal many a holy tear, took pity<br>From most true wretchedness. So thou, Posthumus,<br>Wilt lay the leaven on all proper men.<br>Goodly and gallant shall be false and perjured<br>From thy great fail. (To Pisanio) Come, fellow, be thou<br>honest,<br>Do thou thy master’s bidding. When thou seest<br>him,<br>A little witness my obedience. Look,<br>I draw the sword myself. Take it, and hit<br>The innocent mansion of my love, my heart.<br>Fear not, ‘tis empty of all things but grief.<br>Thy master is not there, who was indeed<br>The riches of it. Do his bidding; strike.<br>Thou mayst be valiant in a better cause,<br>But now thou seem’st a coward. |
| PISANIO | Hence, vile instrument,<br>Thou shalt not damn my hand! |
| INNOGEN | Why, I must die,<br>And if I do not by thy hand thou art<br>No servant of thy master’s. Against self-slaughter<br>There is a prohibition so divine<br>That cravens my weak hand. Come, here’s my heart.<br>Something’s afore’t. Soft, soft, we’ll no defence;<br>Obedient as the scabbard. What is here?<br>She takes letters from her bosom<br>The scriptures of the loyal Leonatus,<br>All turned to heresy? Away, away,<br>Corrupters of my faith, you shall no more<br>Be stomachers to my heart. Thus may poor fools<br>Believe false teachers. Though those that are betrayed<br>Do feel the treason sharply, yet the traitor<br>Stands in worse case of woe. And thou, Posthumus,<br>That didst set up my disobedience ’gainst the King<br>My father, and make me put into contempt the suits<br>Of princely fellows, shalt hereafter find<br>It is no act of common passage but<br>A strain of rareness; and I grieve myself<br>To think, when thou shalt be disedged by her<br>That now thou tirest on, how thy memory<br>Will then be panged by me. (To Pisanio) Prithee,<br>dispatch.<br>The lamb entreats the butcher. Where’s thy knife?<br>Thou art too slow to do thy master’s bidding<br>When I desire it too. |
| PISANIO | O gracious lady,<br>Since I received command to do this business<br>I have not slept one wink. |
| INNOGEN | Do’t, and to bed, then. |
| PISANIO | I’ll wake mine eyeballs out first. |
| INNOGEN | Wherefore then<br>Didst undertake it? Why hast thou abused<br>So many miles with a pretence?—this place,<br>Mine action, and thine own? Our horses’ labour,<br>The time inviting thee? The perturbed court,<br>For my being absent, whereunto I never<br>Purpose return? Why hast thou gone so far<br>To be unbent when thou hast ta‘en thy stand,<br>Th’elected deer before thee? |
| PISANIO | But to win time<br>To lose so bad employment, in the which<br>I have considered of a course. Good lady,<br>Hear me with patience. |
| INNOGEN | Talk thy tongue weary. Speak.<br>I have heard I am a strumpet, and mine ear,<br>Therein false struck, can take no greater wound,<br>Nor tent to bottom that. But speak. |
| PISANIO | Then, madam,<br>I thought you would not back again. |
| INNOGEN | Most like,<br>Bringing me here to kill me. |
| PISANIO | Not so, neither.<br>But if I were as wise as honest, then<br>My purpose would prove well. It cannot be<br>But that my master is abused. Some villain,<br>Ay, and singular in his art, hath done you both<br>This cursed injury. |
| INNOGEN | Some Roman courtesan. |
| PISANIO | No, on my life.<br>I’ll give but notice you are dead, and send him<br>Some bloody sign of it, for ’tis commanded<br>I should do so. You shall be missed at court,<br>And that will well confirm it. |
| INNOGEN | Why, good fellow,<br>What shall I do the while, where bide, how live,<br>Or in my life what comfort when I am<br>Dead to my husband? |
| PISANIO | If you’ll back to th’ court— |
| INNOGEN | No court, no father, nor no more ado<br>With that harsh, churlish, noble, simple nothing,<br>That Cloten, whose love suit hath been to me<br>As fearful as a siege. |
| PISANIO | If not at court,<br>Then not in Britain must you bide. |
| INNOGEN | Where then?<br>Hath Britain all the sun that shines? Day, night,<br>Are they not but in Britain? I‘th’ world’s volume<br>Our Britain seems as of it but not in’t,<br>In a great pool a swan’s nest. Prithee, think<br>There’s livers out of Britain. |
| PISANIO | I am most glad<br>You think of other place. Th‘ambassador,<br>Lucius the Roman, comes to Milford Haven<br>Tomorrow. Now if you could wear a mind<br>Dark as your fortune is, and but disguise<br>That which t’appear itself must not yet be<br>But by self-danger, you should tread a course<br>Pretty and full of view; yea, haply near<br>The residence of Posthumus; so nigh, at least,<br>That though his actions were not visible, yet<br>Report should render him hourly to your ear<br>As truly as he moves. |
| INNOGEN | O, for such means,<br>Though peril to my modesty, not death on’t,<br>I would adventure. |
| PISANIO | Well then, here’s the point:<br>You must forget to be a woman; change<br>Command into obedience, fear and niceness—<br>The handmaids of all women, or more truly<br>Woman it pretty self—into a waggish courage,<br>Ready in gibes, quick-answered, saucy and<br>As quarrelous as the weasel. Nay, you must<br>Forget that rarest treasure of your cheek,<br>Exposing it—but O, the harder heart!—<br>Alack, no remedy—to the greedy touch<br>Of common-kissing Titan, and forget<br>Your laboursome and dainty trims wherein<br>You made great Juno angry. |
| INNOGEN | Nay, be brief.<br>I see into thy end, and am almost<br>A man already. |
| PISANIO | First, make yourself but like one.<br>Forethinking this, I have already fit—<br>’Tis in my cloak-bag—doublet, hat, hose, all<br>That answer to them. Would you in their serving,<br><br>And with what imitation you can borrow<br>From youth of such a season, fore noble Lucius<br>Present yourself, desire his service, tell him<br>Wherein you’re happy—which will make him know<br>If that his head have ear in music—doubtless<br>With joy he will embrace you, for he’s honourable,<br>And, doubling that, most holy. Your means abroad—<br>You have me, rich, and I will never fail<br>Beginning nor supplyment. |
| INNOGEN | Thou art all the comfort<br>The gods will diet me with. Prithee away.<br>There’s more to be considered, but we’ll even<br>All that good time will give us. This attempt<br>I am soldier to, and will abide it with<br>A prince’s courage. Away, I prithee. |
| PISANIO | Well, madam, we must take a short farewell<br>Lest, being missed, I be suspected of<br>Your carriage from the court. My noble mistress,<br>Here is a box. I had it from the Queen.<br>What’s in’t is precious. If you are sick at sea<br>Or stomach-qualmed at land, a dram of this<br>Will drive away distemper. To some shade,<br>And fit you to your manhood. May the gods<br>Direct you to the best. |
| INNOGEN | Amen. I thank thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
| *(didascalia)* | 3.5 Flourish. Enter Cymbeline, the Queen, Cloten, Lucius, and lords |
| CYMBELINE | (to Lucius)<br>Thus far, and so farewell. |
| LUCIUS | Thanks, royal sir.<br>My emperor hath wrote I must from hence;<br>And am right sorry that I must report ye<br>My master’s enemy. |
| CYMBELINE | Our subjects, sir,<br>Will not endure his yoke, and for ourself<br>To show less sovereignty than they must needs<br>Appear unkinglike. |
| LUCIUS | So, sir, I desire of you<br>A conduct over land to Milford Haven. |
| *(didascalia)* | (To the Queen) Madam, all joy befall your grace, to Cloten and you. |
| CYMBELINE | My lords, you are appointed for that office.<br>The due of honour in no point omit.<br>So farewell, noble Lucius. |
| LUCIUS | Your hand, my lord. |
| CLOTEN | Receive it friendly, but from this time forth<br>I wear it as your enemy. |
| LUCIUS | Sir, the event<br>Is yet to name the winner. Fare you well. |
| CYMBELINE | Leave not the worthy Lucius, good my lords,<br>Till he have crossed the Severn. Happiness. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Lucius and lords |
| QUEEN | He goes hence frowning, but it honours us<br>That we have given him cause. |
| CLOTEN | ’Tis all the better.<br>Your valiant Britons have their wishes in it. |
| CYMBELINE | Lucius hath wrote already to the Emperor<br>How it goes here. It fits us therefore ripely<br>Our chariots and our horsemen be in readiness.<br>The powers that he already hath in Gallia<br>Will soon be drawn to head, from whence he moves<br>His war for Britain. |
| QUEEN | ’Tis not sleepy business,<br>But must be looked to speedily and strongly. |
| CYMBELINE | Our expectation that it would be thus<br>Hath made us forward. But, my gentle queen,<br>Where is our daughter? She hath not appeared<br>Before the Roman, nor to us hath tendered<br>The duty of the day. She looks us like<br>A thing more made of malice than of duty.<br>We have noted it. Call her before us, for<br>We have been too slight in sufferance. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one or more |
| QUEEN | Royal Sir,<br>Since the exile of Posthumus most retired<br>Hath her life been, the cure ,whereof, my lord,<br>’Tis time must do. Beseech your majesty<br>Forbear sharp speeches to her. She’s a lady<br>So tender of rebukes that words are strokes,<br>And strokes death to her. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| CYMBELINE | Where is she, sir? How<br>Can her contempt be answered? |
| MESSENGER | Please you, sir,<br>Her chambers are all locked, and there’s no answer<br>That will be given to th’ loud’st of noise we make. |
| QUEEN | My lord, when last I went to visit her<br>She prayed me to excuse her keeping close,<br>Whereto constrained by her infirmity,<br>She should that duty leave unpaid to you<br>Which daily she was bound to proffer. This<br>She wished me to make known, but our great<br>court<br>Made me to blame in memory. |
| CYMBELINE | Her doors locked?<br>Not seen of late? Grant heavens that which I<br>Fear prove false. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| QUEEN | on, I say, follow the King. |
| CLOTEN | That man of hers, Pisanio, her old servant,<br>I have not seen these two days. |
| QUEEN | Go, look after. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Cloten |
| QUEEN | Pisanio, thou that stand’st so for Posthumus!<br>He hath a drug of mine. I pray his absence<br>Proceed by swallowing that, for he believes<br>It is a thing most precious. But for her,<br>Where is she gone? Haply despair hath seized her,<br>Or, winged with fervour of her love, she’s flown<br>To her desired Posthumus. Gone she is<br>To death or to dishonour, and my end<br>Can make good use of either. She being down,<br>I have the placing of the British crown. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Cloten |
| QUEEN | How now, my son? |
| CLOTEN | ’Tis certain she is fled.<br>Go in and cheer the King. He rages, none<br>Dare come about him. |
| QUEEN | All the better. May<br>This night forestall him of the coming day. Exit |
| CLOTEN | I love and hate her. For she’s fair and royal,<br>And that she hath all courtly parts more exquisite<br>Than lady, ladies, woman—from every one<br>The best she hath, and she, of all compounded,<br>Outsells them all—I love her therefore; but<br>Disdaining me, and throwing favours on<br>The low Posthumus, slanders so her judgement<br>That what’s else rare is choked; and in that point<br>I will conclude to hate her, nay, indeed,<br>To be revenged upon her. For when fools<br>Shall— |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pisanio |
| CLOTEN | Who is here? What, are you packing, sirrah?<br>Come hither. Ah, you precious pander! Villain,<br>Where is thy lady? In a word, or else<br>Thou art straightway with the fiends. |
| PISANIO | O good my lord! |
| CLOTEN | Where is thy lady?—or, by Jupiter,<br>I will not ask again. Close villain,<br>I’ll have this secret from thy tongue or rip<br>Thy heart to find it. Is she with Posthumus,<br>From whose so many weights of baseness cannot<br>A dram of worth be drawn? |
| PISANIO | Alas, my lord,<br>How can she be with him? When was she missed?<br>He is in Rome. |
| CLOTEN | Where is she, sir? Come nearer.<br>No farther halting. Satisfy me home<br>What is become of her. |
| PISANIO | O my all-worthy lord! |
| CLOTEN | All-worthy villain,<br>Discover where thy mistress is at once,<br>At the next word. No more of ‘worthy lord’.<br>Speak, or thy silence on the instant is<br>Thy condemnation and thy death. |
| PISANIO | Then, sir,<br>This paper is the history of my knowledge<br>Touching her flight.<br>He gives Cloten a letter |
| CLOTEN | Let’s see’t. I will pursue her<br>Even to Augustus’ throne. |
| *(didascalia)* | PISANIO aside |
| CLOTEN | Or this or perish.<br>She’s far enough, and what he learns by this<br>May prove his travel, not her danger. |
| CLOTEN | Hum! |
| PISANIO | (aside)<br>I’ll write to my lord she’s dead. O Innogen,<br>Safe mayst thou wander, safe return again! |
| CLOTEN | Sirrah, is this letter true? |
| PISANIO | Sir, as I think. |
| CLOTEN | It is Posthumus’ hand; I know’t. Sirrah, if thou wouldst not be a villain but do me true service, undergo those employments wherein I should have cause to use thee with a serious industry—that is, what villainy soe’er I bid thee do, to perform it directly and truly—I would think thee an honest man. Thou shouldst neither want my means for thy relief nor my voice for thy preferment. |
| PISANIO | Well, my good lord. |
| CLOTEN | Wilt thou serve me? For since patiently and constantly thou hast stuck to the bare fortune of that beggar Posthumus, thou canst not in the course of gratitude but be a diligent follower of mine. Wilt thou serve me? 121 |
| PISANIO | Sir, I will. |
| CLOTEN | Give me thy hand. Here’s my purse. Hast any of thy late master’s garments in thy possession? |
| PISANIO | I have, my lord, at my lodging the same suit he wore when he took leave of my lady and mistress. |
| CLOTEN | The first service thou dost me, fetch that suit hither. Let it be thy first service. Go. |
| PISANIO | I shall, my lord. Exit |
| CLOTEN | Meet thee at Milford Haven! I forgot to ask him one thing; I’ll remember’t anon. Even there, thou villain Posthumus, will I kill thee. I would these garments were come. She said upon a time—the bitterness of it I now belch from my heart—that she held the very garment of Posthumus in more respect than my noble and natural person, together with the adornment of my qualities. With that suit upon my back will I ravish her—first kill him, and in her eyes; there shall she see my valour, which will then be a torment to her contempt. He on the ground, my speech of insultment ended on his dead body, and when my lust hath dined—which, as I say, to vex her I will execute in the clothes that she so praised—to the court I’ll knock her back, foot her home again. She hath despised me rejoicingly, and I’ll be merry in my revenge. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pisanio with Posthumus’ suit |
| CLOTEN | Be those the garments? |
| PISANIO | Ay, my noble lord. |
| CLOTEN | How long is’t since she went to Milford Haven? |
| PISANIO | She can scarce be there yet. |
| CLOTEN | Bring this apparel to my chamber. That is the second thing that I have commanded thee. The third is that thou wilt be a voluntary mute to my design. Be but duteous, and true preferment shall tender itself to thee. My revenge is now at Milford. Would I had wings to follow it. Come, and be true. Exit |
| PISANIO | Thou bidd’st me to my loss, for true to thee<br>Were to prove false, which I will never be<br>To him that is most true. To Milford go,<br>And find not her whom thou pursuest. Flow, flow,<br>You heavenly blessings, on her. This fool’s speed<br>Be crossed with slowness; labour be his meed. Exit |
