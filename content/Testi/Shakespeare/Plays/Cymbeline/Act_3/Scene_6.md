| *(didascalia)* | Enter Innogen, dressed as a man, before the cave |
| INNOGEN | I see a man’s life is a tedious one.<br>I have tired myself, and for two nights together<br>Have made the ground my bed. I should be sick,<br>But that my resolution helps me. Milford,<br>When from the mountain-top Pisanio showed thee,<br>Thou wast within a ken. O Jove, I think<br>Foundations fly the wretched—such, I mean,<br>Where they should be relieved. Two beggars told me<br>I could not miss my way. Will poor folks lie,<br>That have afflictions on them, knowing ‘tis<br>A punishment or trial? Yes. No wonder,<br>When rich ones scarce tell true. To lapse in fullness<br>Is sorer than to lie for need, and falsehood<br>Is worse in kings than beggars. My dear lord,<br>Thou art one o’th’ false ones. Now I think on thee<br>My hunger’s gone, but even before I was<br>At point to sink for food. But what is this?<br>Here is a path to’t. ‘Tis some savage hold.<br>I were best not call; I dare not call; yet famine,<br>Ere clean it o’erthrow nature, makes it valiant.<br>Plenty and peace breeds cowards, hardness ever<br>Of hardiness is mother. Ho! Who’s here?<br>If anything that’s civil, speak; if savage,<br>Take or lend. Ho! No answer? Then I’ll enter.<br>Best draw my sword, and if mine enemy<br>But fear the sword like me he’ll scarcely look on’t.<br>Such a foe, good heavens! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit into the cave |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Belarius, Guiderius, and Arviragus |
| BELARIUS | You, Polydore, have proved best woodman and<br>Are master of the feast. Cadwal and I<br>Will play the cook and servant; ‘tis our match.<br>The sweat of industry would dry and die<br>But for the end it works to. Come, our stomachs<br>Will make what’s homely savoury. Weariness<br>Can snore upon the flint when resty sloth<br>Finds the down pillow hard. Now peace be here,<br>Poor house, that keep’st thyself. |
| GUIDERIUS | I am throughly weary. |
| ARVIRAGUS | I am weak with toil yet strong in appetite. |
| GUIDERIUS | There is cold meat i’th’ cave. We’ll browse on that<br>Whilst what we have killed be cooked. |
| BELARIUS | (looking into the cave) Stay, come not in.<br>But that it eats our victuals I should think<br>Here were a fairy. |
| GUIDERIUS | What’s the matter, sir? |
| BELARIUS | By Jupiter, an angel—or, if not,<br>An earthly paragon. Behold divineness<br>No elder than a boy. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Innogen from the cave, dressed as a man |
| INNOGEN | Good masters, harm me not.<br>Before I entered here I called, and thought<br>To have begged or bought what I have took. Good<br>truth,<br>I have stol’n naught, nor would not, though I had<br>found<br>Gold strewed i’th’ floor. Here’s money for my meat.<br>I would have left it on the board so soon<br>As I had made my meal, and parted<br>With prayers for the provider. |
| GUIDERIUS | Money, youth? |
| ARVIRAGUS | All gold and silver rather turn to dirt,<br>As ’tis no better reckoned but of those<br>Who worship dirty gods. |
| INNOGEN | I see you’re angry.<br>Know, if you kill me for my fault, I should<br>Have died had I not made it. |
| BELARIUS | Whither bound? |
| INNOGEN | To Milford Haven. |
| BELARIUS | What’s your name? |
| INNOGEN | Fidele, sir. I have a kinsman who<br>Is bound for Italy. He embarked at Milford,<br>To whom being going, almost spent with hunger,<br>I am fall’n in this offence. |
| BELARIUS | Prithee, fair youth,<br>Think us no churls, nor measure our good minds<br>By this rude place we live in. Well encountered.<br>’Tis almost night. You shall have better cheer<br>Ere you depart, and thanks to stay and eat it.<br>Boys, bid him welcome. |
| GUIDERIUS | Were you a woman, youth,<br>I should woo hard but be your groom in honesty,<br>Ay, bid for you as I’d buy. |
| ARVIRAGUS | I’ll make’t my comfort<br>He is a man, I’ll love him as my brother.<br>(To Innogen) And such a welcome as I’d give to him<br>After long absence, such is yours. Most welcome.<br>Be sprightly, for you fall ’mongst friends. |
| INNOGEN | ’Mongst friends<br>If brothers. (Aside) Would it had been so that they<br>Had been my father’s sons. Then had my price<br>Been less, and so more equal ballasting<br>To thee, Posthumus.<br>The three men speak apart |
| BELARIUS | He wrings at some distress. |
| GUIDERIUS | Would I could free’t. |
| ARVIRAGUS | Or I, whate’er it be,<br>What pain it cost, what danger. Gods! |
| BELARIUS | Hark, boys.<br>They whisper |
| INNOGEN | (aside) Great men<br>That had a court no bigger than this cave,<br>That did attend themselves and had the virtue<br>Which their own conscience sealed them, laying by<br>That nothing-gift of differing multitudes,<br>Could not outpeer these twain. Pardon me, gods,<br>I’d change my sex to be companion with them,<br>Since Leonatus’ false. |
| BELARIUS | It shall be so.<br>Boys, we’ll go dress our hunt. Fair youth, come in.<br>Discourse is heavy, fasting. When we have supped<br>We’ll mannerly demand thee of thy story,<br>So far as thou wilt speak it. |
| GUIDERIUS | Pray draw near. |
| ARVIRAGUS | The night to th’ owl and morn to th’ lark less welcome. |
| INNOGEN | Thanks, sir. |
| ARVIRAGUS | I pray draw near. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt into the cave |
