| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Retreat. Enter the [[Duke of York]], his sons Edward and [[Richard]], and soldiers, including a drummer and some bearing colours |
| YORK | (to Edward and [[Richard]]) How now, boys! Fortunate this fight hath been, I [[Hope|hope]], to us and ours for England’s good And our great [[Honour|honour]], that so long we lost Whilst faint-heart Henry did usurp our rights. Of Salisbury, who can report of him? That [[Winter|winter]] [[Lion|lion]] who in rage forgets Aged contusions and all brush of [[Time|time]], And, like a gallant in the brow of youth, Repairs him with occasion. This happy day Is not itself, nor have we won one foot If Salisbury be lost. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | My noble father, Three times today I holp him to his [[Horse|horse]]; Three times bestrid him; thrice I led him off, Persuaded him from any further act; But still where danger was, still there I met him, And like rich hangings in a homely [[house]], So was his will in his old feeble [[Body|body]]. Enter the Earls of Salisbury and Warwick |
| EDWARD | (to York) See, noble father, where they both do come— The only props unto the [[house]] of York! |
| SALISBURY | Now, by my [[Sword|sword]], [[Well|well]] hast thou fought today; By th’ mass, so did we all. I thank you, [[Richard]]. God knows how long it is I have to live, And it hath pleased him that three times today You have defended me from imminent [[Death|death]]. [[Well]], lords, we have not got that which we have— ’Tis not enough our foes are this [[Time|time]] fled, Being opposites of such repairing [[Nature|nature]]. |
| YORK | I know our safety is to follow them, For, as I hear, the [[King]] is fled to [[London]], To call a present [[Court|court]] of Parliament. Let us pursue him ere the writs go forth. What says Lord Warwick, shall we after them? |
| WARWICK | After them? Nay, before them if we can! Now by my hand, lords, ’twas a glorious day! Saint Albans battle won by famous York Shall be eternized in all age to come. Sound drums and trumpets, and to [[London]] all, And more such days as these to us befall! Flourish. Exeunt |
| ADDITIONAL PASSAGES | A. We adopt the 1594 Quarto version of the [[Queen]]’s initial speech, 1.1.24—9; the Folio version, which follows, is probably the author’s original draft. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Great [[King]] of England, and my gracious lord, The mutual conference that my mind hath had— By day, by [[Night|night]]; waking, and in my [[Dreams|dreams]]; In courtly company, or at my beads— With you, mine alder liefest sovereign, Makes me the bolder to salute my [[King|king]] With ruder terms, such as my wit affords And overjoy of heart doth minister. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | B. For 1.4.39―40.2 the Quarto substitutes the following; it may report a revision made in rehearsal to cover the Spirit’s descent. The Spirit sinks down again |
| BOLINGBROKE | Then down, I say, unto the damned pool Where Pluto in his fiery wagon sits Riding, amidst the singed and parched smokes, The [[Road|road]] of Ditis by the [[River]] Styx. There howl and burn for ever in those flames. Rise, Jordan, rise, and stay thy charming spells— Zounds, we are betrayed! |
