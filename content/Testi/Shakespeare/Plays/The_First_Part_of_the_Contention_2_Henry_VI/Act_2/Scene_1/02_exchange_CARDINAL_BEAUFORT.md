| [[Cardinal Beaufort|CARDINAL BEAUFORT]] | (aside to [[Gloucester]]) I am with you. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why, how now, uncle [[Gloucester]]? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Talking of hawking, nothing else, my lord. (Aside to the Cardinal) Now, by God’s mother, priest, I’ll shave your [[Crown|crown]] for this, Or all my fence shall fail. |
| [[Cardinal Beaufort|CARDINAL BEAUFORT]] | (aside to [[Gloucester]]) Medice, teipsum— Protector, see to’t [[Well|well]]; protect yourself. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | The winds grow high; so do your stomachs, lords. How irksome is this music to my heart ! When such strings jar, what [[Hope|hope]] of harmony? I pray, my lords, let me compound this strife. Enter one crying ‘a miracle’ |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | What means this noise? Fellow, what miracle dost thou proclaim? |
| ONE | A miracle, a miracle! |
| SUFFOLK | Come to the [[King]]—tell him what miracle. |
| ONE | (to [[King]] Henry) Forsooth, a blind man at Saint Alban’s shrine Within this half-hour hath received his sight— A man that ne’er saw in his life before. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Now God be praised, that to believing souls Gives light in darkness, comfort in [[Despair|despair]]! Enter the Mayor and aldermen of Saint Albans, with music, bearing the man, Simpcox, between two in a chair. Enter Simpcox’s Wife and other townsmeni with them |
| [[Cardinal Beaufort|CARDINAL BEAUFORT]] | Here comes the townsmen on procession To present your highness with the man. The townsmen kneel |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Great is his comfort in this earthly vale, Although by sight his sin be multiplied. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to the townsmen) Stand by, my masters, bring him near the [[King]]. His highness’ pleasure is to talk with him. They  rise and  bear Simpcox before the [[King]] |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to Simpcox) Good fellow, tell us here the circumstance, That we for thee may glorify the Lord. What, hast thou been long blind and now restored? |
| SIMPCOX | Born blind, an’t please your grace. |
| SIMPCOX’S WIFE | Ay, indeed, was he. |
| SUFFOLK | What woman is this? |
| SIMPCOX’S WIFE | His wife, an’t like your worship. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Hadst thou been his mother Thou couldst have better told. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | (to Simpcox) Where wert thou born? |
| SIMPCOX | At Berwick, in the north, an’t like your grace. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Poor [[Soul|soul]], God’s goodness hath been great to thee. Let never day nor [[Night|night]] unhallowed pass, But still remember what the Lord hath done. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (to Simpcox) Tell me, good fellow, cam’st thou here by chance, Or of devotion to this holy shrine? |
| SIMPCOX | God knows, of pure devotion, being called A hundred times and oftener, in my [[Sleep|sleep]], By good Saint Alban, who said, ‘Simon, come; Come offer at my shrine and I will help thee.’ |
| SIMPCOX’S WIFE | Most true, forsooth, and many [[Time|time]] and oft Myself have heard a voice to call him so. |
| [[Cardinal Beaufort|CARDINAL BEAUFORT]] | (to Simpcox) What, art thou lame ? |
| SIMPCOX | Ay, God almighty help me. |
| SUFFOLK | How cam’st thou so? |
| SIMPCOX | A fall off of a tree. |
| SIMPCOX’S WIFE | (to Suffolk) A plum tree, master. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | How long hast thou been blind? |
| SIMPCOX | O, born so, master. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | What, and wouldst climb a tree? SIMPCOX But that in all my life, when I was a youth. |
