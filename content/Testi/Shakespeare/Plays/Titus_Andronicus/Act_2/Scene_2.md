| *(didascalia)* | Enter Titus Andronicus and his three sons (Quintus, Lucius, and Martius), and Marcus, making a noise with hounds and horns |
| TITUS | The hunt is up, the morn is bright and grey,<br>The fields are fragrant and the woods are green.<br>Uncouple here, and let us make a bay<br>And wake the Emperor and his lovely bride,<br>And rouse the Prince, and ring a hunter’s peal,<br>That all the court may echo with the noise.<br>Sons, let it be your charge, as it is ours,<br>To attend the Emperor’s person carefully.<br>I have been troubled in my sleep this night,<br>But dawning day new comfort hath inspired.<br>Here a cry of hounds, and wind horns in a peal;<br><br>then enter Saturninus, Tamora, Bassianus, Lavinia,<br><br>Chiron, Demetrius, and their attendants<br>Many good-morrows to your majesty.<br>Madam, to you as many, and as good.<br>I promised your grace a hunter’s peal. |
| SATURNINUS | And you have rung it lustily, my lords,<br>Somewhat too early for new-married ladies. |
| BASSIANUS | Lavinia, how say you? |
| LAVINIA | I say no.<br>I have been broad awake two hours and more. |
| SATURNINUS | Come on then, horse and chariots let us have,<br>And to our sport. (To Tamora) Madam, now shall ye see<br>Our Roman hunting. |
| MARCUS | I have dogs, my lord,<br>Will rouse the proudest panther in the chase,<br>And climb the highest promontory top. |
| TITUS | And I have horse will follow where the game<br>Makes way, and run like swallows o’er the plain. |
| DEMETRIUS | (aside)<br>Chiron, we hunt not, we, with horse nor hound,<br>But hope to pluck a dainty doe to ground. Exeunt |
