| *(didascalia)* | Enter Edgar disguised as a peasant, with a staff, guiding the blind Duke of Gloucester |
| GLOUCESTER | When shall we come to th’ top of that same hill? |
| EDGAR | You do climb up it now. Look how we labour. |
| GLOUCESTER | Methinks the ground is even. |
| EDGAR | Horrible steep.<br>Hark, do you hear the sea? |
| GLOUCESTER | No, truly. |
| EDGAR | Why, then your other senses grow imperfect<br>By your eyes’ anguish. |
| GLOUCESTER | So may it be indeed.<br>Methinks thy voice is altered, and thou speak’st<br>With better phrase and matter than thou didst. |
| EDGAR | You’re much deceived. In nothing am I changed<br>But in my garments. |
| GLOUCESTER | Methinks you’re better spoken. |
| EDGAR | Come on, sir, here’s the place. Stand still. How fearful<br>And dizzy ’tis to cast one’s eyes so low!<br>The crows and choughs that wing the midway air<br>Show scarce so gross as beetles. Halfway down<br>Hangs one that gathers samphire, dreadful trade!<br>Methinks he seems no bigger than his head.<br>The fishermen that walk upon the beach<br>Appear like mice, and yon tall anchoring barque<br>Diminished to her cock, her cock a buoy<br>Almost too small for sight. The murmuring surge<br>That on the unnumbered idle pebble chafes<br>Cannot be heard, it’s so high. I’ll look no more,<br>Lest my brain turn and the deficient sight<br>Topple down headlong. |
| GLOUCESTER | Set me where you stand. |
| EDGAR | Give me your hand. You are now within a foot<br>Of th’extreme verge. For all beneath the moon<br>Would I not leap upright. |
| GLOUCESTER | Let go my hand.<br>Here, friend, ’s another purse; in it a jewel<br>Well worth a poor man’s taking. Fairies and gods<br>Prosper it with thee! Go thou farther off.<br>Bid me farewell, and let me hear thee going. |
| EDGAR | Now fare you well, good sir.<br>He stands aside |
| GLOUCESTER | With all my heart. |
| EDGAR | (aside)<br>Why I do trifle thus with his despair<br>Is done to cure it. |
| GLOUCESTER | O you mighty gods,<br>He kneels<br>This world I do renounce, and in your sights<br>Shake patiently my great affliction off!<br>If I could bear it longer, and not fall<br>To quarrel with your great opposeless wills,<br>My snuff and loathed part of nature should<br>Burn itself out. If Edgar live, O bless him!—<br>Now, fellow, fare thee well. |
| EDGAR | Gone, sir. Farewell.<br>Gloucester falls forward<br>(Aside) And yet I know not how conceit may rob<br>The treasury of life, when life itself<br>Yields to the theft. Had he been where he thought,<br>By this had thought been past.—Ative or dead?<br>(To Gloucester) Ho you, sir; hear you, sir? Speak.<br>(Aside) Thus might he pass indeed. Yet he revives.<br>(To Gloucester) What are you, sir? |
| GLOUCESTER | Away, and let me die. |
| EDGAR | Hadst thou been aught but goss‘mer, feathers, air,<br>So many fathom down precipitating<br>Thou hadst shivered like an egg. But thou dost breathe,<br>Hast heavy substance, bleed’st not, speak’st, art sound.<br>Ten masts a-length make not the altitude<br>Which thou hast perpendicularly fell.<br>Thy life’s a miracle. Speak yet again. |
| GLOUCESTER | But have I fallen, or no? |
| EDGAR | From the dread summit of this chalky bourn.<br>Look up a-height. The shrill-gorged lark so far<br>Cannot be seen or heard. Do but look up. |
| GLOUCESTER | Alack, I have no eyes.<br>Is wretchedness deprived that benefit<br>To end itself by death? ’Twas yet some comfort<br>When misery could beguile the tyrant’s rage<br>And frustrate his proud will. |
| EDGAR | Give me your arm.<br>Up. So, how now? Feel you your legs? You stand. |
| GLOUCESTER | Too well, too well. |
| EDGAR | This is above all strangeness.<br>Upon the crown of the cliff what thing was that<br>Which parted from you? |
| GLOUCESTER | A poor unfortunate beggar. |
| EDGAR | As I stood here below, methoughts his eyes<br>Were two full moons. A had a thousand noses,<br>Horns whelked and waved like the enridged sea.<br>It was some fiend. Therefore, thou happy father,<br>Think that the clearest gods, who made their honours<br>Of men’s impossibilities, have preserved thee. |
| GLOUCESTER | I do remember now. Henceforth I’ll bear<br>Affliction till it do cry out itself<br>‘Enough, enough,’ and die. That thing you speak of,<br>I took it for a man. Often would it say<br>‘The fiend, the fiend!’ He led me to that place. |
| EDGAR | Bear free and patient thoughts. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter King Lear mad, crowned with weeds and |
| *(didascalia)* | flowers |
| EDGAR | But who comes here?<br>The safer sense will ne’er accommodate<br>His master thus. |
| LEAR | No, they cannot touch me for coining. I am the King himself. |
| EDGAR | O thou side-piercing sight! |
| LEAR | Nature is above art in that respect. There’s your press-money. That fellow handles his bow like a crow-keeper. Draw me a clothier’s yard. Look, look, a mouse! Peace, peace, this toasted cheese will do it. There’s my gauntlet. I’ll prove it on a giant. Bring up the brown bills. O, well flown, bird, in the air. Hal Give the word. |
| EDGAR | Sweet marjoram. |
| LEAR | Pass. |
| GLOUCESTER | I know that voice. |
| LEAR | Ha, Gonorill Ha, Regan! They flattered me like a dog, and told me I had white hairs in my beard ere the black ones were there. To say ‘ay’ and ‘no’ to everything I said ‘ay’ and ‘no’ to was no good divinity. When the rain came to wet me once, and the wind to make me chatter, when the thunder would not peace at my bidding, there I found them, there I smelt them out. Go to, they are not men of their words. They told me I was everything; ’tis a lie, I am not ague-proof. |
| GLOUCESTER | The trick of that voice I do well remember.<br>Is’t not the King? |
| LEAR | Ay, every inch a king. |
| *(didascalia)* | Gloucester kneels |
| LEAR | When I do stare, see how the subject quakes!<br>I pardon that man’s life. What was thy cause?<br>Adultery? Thou shalt not die for adultery.<br>No, the wren goes to‘t, and the small gilded fly<br>Does lecher in my sight.<br>Let copulation thrive, for Gloucester’s bastard son<br>Was kinder to his father than my daughters<br>Got ’tween the lawful sheets. To‘t, luxury, pell-mell,<br>For I lack soldiers. Behold yon simp’ring dame,<br>Whose face between her forks presageth snow,<br>That minces virtue, and does shake the head<br>To hear of pleasure’s name:<br>The fitchew nor the soiled horse goes to’t<br>With a more riotous appetite. Down from the waist<br>They’re centaurs, though women all above.<br>But to the girdle do the gods inherit;<br>Beneath is all the fiend’s. There’s hell, there’s<br>darkness,<br>There’s the sulphury pit, burning, scalding,<br>Stench, consummation. Fie, fie, fie; pah, pah!<br>Give me an ounce of civet, good apothecary,<br>To sweeten my imagination.<br>There’s money for thee. |
| GLOUCESTER | O, let me kiss that hand! |
| LEAR | Here, wipe it first; it smells of mortality. |
| GLOUCESTER | O ruined piece of nature! This great world<br>Shall so wear out to naught. Do you know me? |
| LEAR | I remember thy eyes well enough. Dost thou squiny on me?<br>No, do thy worst, blind Cupid, I’ll not love.<br>Read thou that challenge. Mark the penning of’t. |
| GLOUCESTER | Were all the letters suns, I could not see one. |
| EDGAR | (aside)<br>I would not take this from report; it is,<br>And my heart breaks at it. |
| LEAR | (to Gloucester) Read. |
| GLOUCESTER | What—with the case of eyes? |
| LEAR | O ho, are you there with me? No eyes in your head, nor no money in your purse? Your eyes are in a heavy case, your purse in a light; yet you see how this world goes. |
| GLOUCESTER | I see it feelingly. |
| LEAR | What, art mad? A man may see how the world goes with no eyes; look with thy ears. See how yon justice rails upon yon simple thief. Hark in thy ear: handy-dandy, which is the thief, which is the justice? Thou hast seen a farmer’s dog bark at a beggar? |
| GLOUCESTER | Ay, sir. |
| LEAR | An the creature run from the cur, there thou mightst behold the great image of authority. A dog’s obeyed in office.<br>Thou rascal beadle, hold thy bloody hand.<br>Why dost thou lash that whore? Strip thine own back.<br>Thy blood as hotly lusts to use her in that kind<br>For which thou whip’st her. The usurer hangs the<br>cozener.<br>Through tattered rags small vices do appear;<br>Robes and furred gowns hides all. Get thee glass eyes,<br>And, like a scurvy politician, seem<br>To see the things thou dost not. No tears, now.<br>Pull off my boots. Harder, harder! So. |
| EDGAR | (aside)<br>O, matter and impertinency mixed—<br>Reason in madness! |
| LEAR | If thou wilt weep my fortune, take my eyes.<br>I know thee well enough: thy name is Gloucester.<br>Thou must be patient. We came crying hither.<br>Thou know’st the first time that we smell the air<br>We wail and cry. I will preach to thee. Mark me. |
| GLOUCESTER | Alack, alack, the day! |
| *(didascalia)* | LEAR removing his crown of weeds |
| GLOUCESTER | When we are born, we cry that we are come<br>To this great stage of fools. This’ a good block.<br>It were a delicate stratagem to shoe<br>A troop of horse with felt; and when I have stole<br>upon<br>These son-in-laws, then kill, kill, kill, kill, kill, kill! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter three Gentlemen |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| GLOUCESTER | O, here he is. Lay hands upon him, sirs.<br>(To Lear) Your most dear— |
| LEAR | No rescue? What, a prisoner? I am e’en<br>The natural fool of fortune. Use me well.<br>You shall have ransom. Let me have a surgeon;<br>I am cut to the brains. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN You shall have anything. |
| LEAR | No seconds? All myself?<br>Why, this would make a man a man of salt,<br>To use his eyes for garden water-pots,<br>Ay, and laying autumn’s dust. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN Good sir— |
| LEAR | I will die bravely, like a bridegroom.<br>What, I will be jovial. Come, come,<br>I am a king, my masters, know you that? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| LEAR | You are a royal one, and we obey you. |
| LEAR | Then there’s life in’t. Nay, an you get it, you shall get it with running. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit running, pursued by two Gentlemen |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| LEAR | A sight most pitiful in the meanest wretch,<br>Past speaking in a king. Thou hast one daughter<br>Who redeems nature from the general curse<br>Which twain hath brought her to. |
| EDGAR | Hail, gentle sir. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN Sir, speed you. What’s your will? |
| EDGAR | Do you hear aught of a battle toward? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| EDGAR | Most sure and vulgar, everyone hears that<br>That can distinguish sense. |
| EDGAR | But, by your favour,<br>How near’s the other army? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| EDGAR | Near and on speedy foot, the main; descriers<br>Stands on the hourly thoughts. |
| EDGAR | I thank you, sir. That’s all. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| EDGAR | Though that the Queen on special cause is here,<br>Her army is moved on. |
| EDGAR | I thank you, sir. Exit Gentleman |
| GLOUCESTER | You ever gentle gods, take my breath from me.<br>Let not my worser spirit tempt me again<br>To die before you please. |
| EDGAR | Well pray you, father. |
| GLOUCESTER | Now, good sir, what are you? |
| EDGAR | A most poor man, made lame by fortune’s blows,<br>Who by the art of known and feeling sorrows<br>Am pregnant to good pity. Give me your hand,<br>I’ll lead you to some biding. |
| *(didascalia)* | GLOUCESTER rising Hearty thanks. |
| EDGAR | The bounty and the benison of heaven<br>To send thee boot to boot. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Oswald the steward |
| OSWALD | A proclaimed prize! Most happy!<br>That eyeless head of thine was first framed flesh<br>To raise my fortunes. Thou most unhappy traitor,<br>Briefly thyself remember. The sword is out<br>That must destroy thee. |
| GLOUCESTER | Now let thy friendly hand<br>Put strength enough to’t. |
| OSWALD | (to Edgar) Wherefore, bold peasant,<br>Durst thou support a published traitor? Hence,<br>Lest the infection of his fortune take<br>Like hold on thee. Let go his arm. |
| EDGAR | ‘Chill not let go, sir, without ’cagion. |
| OSWALD | Let go, slave, or thou diest. |
| EDGAR | Good gentleman, go your gate. Let poor volk pass. An ‘chud have been swaggered out of my life, it would not have been so long by a vortnight. Nay, come not near the old man. Keep out, ’che vor’ ye, or I’ll try whether your costard or my baton be the harder; I’ll be plain with you. |
| OSWALD | Out, dunghill!<br>They fight |
| EDGAR | ’Chill pick your teeth, sir. Come, no matter for your foins. |
| *(didascalia)* | Edgar knocks him down |
| OSWALD | Slave, thou hast slain me. Villain, take my purse.<br>If ever thou wilt thrive, bury my body,<br>And give the letters which thou find’st about me<br>To Edmund, Earl of Gloucester. Seek him out<br>Upon the British party. O untimely death! Death!<br>He dies |
| EDGAR | I know thee well—a serviceable villain,<br>As duteous to the vices of thy mistress<br>As badness would desire. |
| GLOUCESTER | What, is he dead? |
| EDGAR | Sit you down, father. Rest you.<br>Gloucester sits<br>Let’s see his pockets. These letters that he speaks of<br>May be my friends. He’s dead; I am only sorrow<br>He had no other deathsman. Let us see.<br>Leave, gentle wax; and manners, blame us not.<br>To know our enemies’ minds we’d rip their hearts;<br>Their papers is more lawful.<br>He reads a letter<br>‘Let your reciprocal vows be remembered. You have<br>many opportunities to cut him off. If your will want<br>not, time and place will be fruitfully offered. There is<br>nothing done if he return the conqueror; then am I<br>the prisoner, and his bed my jail, from the loathed<br>warmth whereof, deliver me, and supply the place for<br>your labour.<br>Your—wife, so I would say—your affectionate<br>servant, and for you her own for venture,<br>Gonoril.’<br>O indistinguished space of woman’s wit—<br>A plot upon her virtuous husband’s life,<br>And the exchange my brother!—Here in the sands<br>Thee I’ll rake up, the post unsanctified<br>Of murderous lechers, and in the mature time<br>With this ungracious paper strike the sight<br>Of the death-practised Duke. For him ’tis well<br>That of thy death and business I can tell.<br>[Exit with the body] |
| GLOUCESTER | The King is mad. How stiff is my vile sense,<br>That I stand up and have ingenious feeling<br>Of my huge sorrows! Better I were distraught;<br>So should my thoughts be fenced from my griefs,<br>And woes by wrong imaginations lose<br>The knowledge of themselves.<br>A drum afar off. [Enter Edgar] |
| EDGAR | Give me your hand.<br>Far off methinks I hear the beaten drum.<br>Come, father, I’ll bestow you with a friend. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Edgar guiding Gloucester |
