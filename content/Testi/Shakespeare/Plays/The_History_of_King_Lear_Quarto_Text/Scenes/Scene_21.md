| CORDELIA | O thou good Kent,<br>How shall I live and work to match thy goodness?<br>My life will be too short, and every measure fail me. |
| KENT | To be acknowledged, madam, is o’erpaid.<br>All my reports go with the modest truth,<br>Nor more, nor clipped, but so. |
| CORDELIA | Be better suited.<br>These weeds are memories of those worser hours.<br>I prithee put them off. |
| KENT | Pardon me, dear madam.<br>Yet to be known shortens my made intent.<br>My boon I make it that you know me not<br>Till time and I think meet. |
| CORDELIA | Then be’t so, my good lord.<br>[Enter the Doctor and First Gentleman]<br>How does the King? |
| DOCTOR | Madam, sleeps still. |
| CORDELIA | O you kind gods,<br>Cure this great breach in his abused nature;<br>The untuned and hurrying senses O wind up<br>Of this child-changed father! |
| DOCTOR | So please your majesty<br>That we may wake the King? He hath slept long. |
| CORDELIA | Be governed by your knowledge, and proceed<br>I’th’ sway of your own will. Is he arrayed?<br>[FIRST GENTLEMAN]<br>Ay, madam. In the heaviness of his sleep<br>We put fresh garments on him.<br>[DOCTOR]<br>Good madam, be by when we do awake him.<br>I doubt not of his temperance. |
| CORDELIA | Very well. |
| DOCTOR | Please you draw near. Louder the music there!<br>King Lear is [discovered] asleep |
| CORDELIA | O my dear father, restoration hang<br>Thy medicine on my lips, and let this kiss<br>Repair those violent harms that my two sisters<br>Have in thy reverence made! |
| KENT | Kind and dear princess! |
| CORDELIA | Had you not been their father, these white flakes<br>Had challenged pity of them. Was this a face<br>To be exposed against the warring winds,<br>To stand against the deep dread-bolted thunder<br>In the most terrible and nimble stroke<br>Of quick cross-lightning, to watch—poor perdu—<br><br>With this thin helm? Mine injurer’s mean‘st dog,<br>Though he had bit me, should have stood that night<br>Against my fire. And wast thou fain, poor father,<br>To hovel thee with swine and rogues forlorn<br>In short and musty straw? Alack, alack,<br>’Tis wonder that thy life and wits at once<br>Had not concluded all! (To the Doctor) He wakes.<br>Speak to him. |
| DOCTOR | Madam, do you; ’tis fittest. |
| CORDELIA | (to Lear)<br>How does my royal lord? How fares your majesty? |
| LEAR | You do me wrong to take me out o’th’ grave.<br>Thou art a soul in bliss, but I am bound<br>Upon a wheel of fire, that mine own tears<br>Do scald like molten lead. |
| CORDELIA | Sir, know me. |
| LEAR | You’re a spirit, I know. Where did you die? |
| CORDELIA | (to the Doctor) Still, still far wide! |
| DOCTOR | He’s scarce awake. Let him alone a while. |
| LEAR | Where have I been? Where am I? Fair daylight?<br>I am mightily abused. I should e’en die with pity<br>To see another thus. I know not what to say.<br>I will not swear these are my hands. Let’s see:<br>I feel this pin prick. Would I were assured<br>Of my condition. |
| CORDELIA | (kneeling) O look upon me, sir,<br>And hold your hands in benediction o’er me.<br>No, sir, you must not kneel. |
| LEAR | Pray do not mock.<br>I am a very foolish, fond old man,<br>Fourscore and upward, and to deal plainly,<br>I fear I am not in my perfect mind.<br>Methinks I should know you, and know this man;<br>Yet I am doubtful, for I am mainly ignorant<br>What place this is; and all the skill I have<br>Remembers not these garments; nor I know not<br>Where I did lodge last night. Do not laugh at me,<br>For as I am a man, I think this lady<br>To be my child, Cordelia. |
| CORDELIA | And so I am. |
| LEAR | Be your tears wet? Yes, faith. I pray, weep not.<br>If you have poison for me, I will drink it.<br>I know you do not love me; for your sisters<br>Have, as I do remember, done me wrong.<br>You have some cause; they have not. |
| CORDELIA | No cause, no cause. |
| LEAR | Am I in France? |
| KENT | In your own kingdom, sir. |
| LEAR | Do not abuse me. |
| DOCTOR | Be comforted, good madam. The great rage<br>You see is cured in him, and yet it is danger<br>To make him even o’er the time he has lost.<br>Desire him to go in; trouble him no more<br>Till further settling. |
| CORDELIA | (to Lear) Will’t please your highness walk? |
| LEAR | You must bear with me.<br>Pray now, forget and forgive. I am old<br>And foolish. Exeunt all but Kent and [First] Gentleman<br>[FIRST] GENTLEMAN Holds it true, sir, that the Duke Of Cornwall was so slain? |
| KENT | Most certain, sir.<br>[FIRST] GENTLEMAN<br>Who is conductor of his people? |
| KENT | As ’tis said,<br>The bastard son of Gloucester.<br>[FIRST] GENTLEMAN They say Edgar,<br>His banished son, is with the Earl of Kent<br>In Germany. |
| KENT | Report is changeable.<br>’Tis time to look about. The powers of the kingdom 90<br>Approach apace.<br>[FIRST] GENTLEMAN The arbitrement is<br>Like to be bloody. Fare you well, sir. Exit |
| KENT | My point and period will be throughly wrought,<br>Or well or ill, as this day’s battle’s fought. Exit |
