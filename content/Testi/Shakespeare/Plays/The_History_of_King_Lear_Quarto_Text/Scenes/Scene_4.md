| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of [[Kent]], disguised |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | If but as [[Well|well]] I other accents borrow<br>That can my speech diffuse, my good intent<br>May carry through itself to that full issue<br>For which I razed my likeness. Now, banished [[Kent]],<br>If thou canst serve where thou dost stand condemned,<br>Thy master, whom thou lov’st, shall find thee full of<br>labour. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King Lear]] and servants from hunting |
| LEAR | Let me not stay a jot for dinner. Go get it ready. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one |
| LEAR | (To [[Kent]]) How now, what art thou? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | A man, sir. |
| LEAR | What dost thou profess? What wouldst thou with us? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I do profess to be no less than I seem, to serve him truly that will put me in trust, to [[Love|love]] him that is honest, to converse with him that is wise and says little, to [[Fear|fear]] judgement, to fight when I cannot choose, and to eat no fish. |
| LEAR | What art thou? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | A very honest-hearted fellow, and as poor as the [[King]]. |
| LEAR | If thou be as poor for a subject as he is for a [[King|king]], thou’rt poor enough. What wouldst thou? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Service. |
| LEAR | Who wouldst thou serve? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | You. |
| LEAR | Dost thou know me, fellow? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | No, sir, but you have that in your countenance which I would fain call master. |
| LEAR | What’s that? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Authority. |
| LEAR | What services canst do? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I can keep honest counsel, ride, run, mar a curious tale in telling it, and deliver a plain message bluntly. That which ordinary men are fit for I am qualified in; and the best of me is diligence. |
| LEAR | How old art thou? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Not so young to [[Love|love]] a woman for singing, nor so old to dote on her for anything. I have years on my back forty-eight. |
| LEAR | Follow me. Thou shalt serve me, if I like thee no worse after dinner. I will not part from thee yet.—Dinner, ho, dinner! Where’s my knave, my [[Fool|fool]]? Go you and call my [[Fool|fool]] hither. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Oswald the steward |
| LEAR | You, sirrah, where’s my daughter? |
| OSWALD | So please you— |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| LEAR | What says the fellow there? Call the clotpoll back. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Servant and [[Kent]] |
| LEAR | Where’s my [[Fool|fool]]? Ho, I think the world’s asleep. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of [[Kent]] and a Servant |
| LEAR | How now, where’s that mongrel? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | He says, my lord, your daughter is not [[Well|well]]. |
| LEAR | Why came not the slave back to me when I called him? |
| SERVANT | Sir, he answered me in the roundest manner he would not. |
| LEAR | A would not? |
| SERVANT | My lord, I know not what the matter is, but to my judgement your highness is not entertained with that ceremonious affection as you were wont. There’s a great abatement appears as [[Well|well]] in the general dependants as in the Duke himself also, and your daughter. |
| LEAR | Ha, sayst thou so? |
| SERVANT | I beseech you pardon me, my lord, if I be mistaken, for my duty cannot be silent when I think your highness wronged. |
| LEAR | Thou but rememberest me of mine own conception. I have perceived a most faint neglect of late, which I have rather blamed as mine own jealous curiosity than as a very pretence and purport of unkindness. I will look further into’t. But where’s this [[Fool|fool]]? I have not seen him these two days. |
| SERVANT | Since my young lady’s going into France, sir, the [[Fool|fool]] hath much pined away. |
| *(didascalia)* | LEAR No more of that, I have noted it. Go you and tell my daughter I would speak with her. Exit one Go you, call hither my [[Fool|fool]]. Exit one |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Oswald the steward crossing the stage |
| SERVANT | O you, sir, you, sir, come you hither. Who am I, sir? |
| OSWALD | My lady’s father. |
| LEAR | My lady’s father? My lord’s knave, you whoreson dog, you slave, you cur! |
| OSWALD | I am none of this, my lord, I beseech you pardon me. |
| LEAR | Do you bandy looks with me, you rascal? |
| *(didascalia)* | Lear strikes him |
| OSWALD | I’ll not be struck, my lord— |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (tripping him) Nor tripped neither, you base football player. |
| LEAR | (to [[Kent]]) I thank thee, fellow. Thou serv’st me, and I’ll [[Love|love]] thee. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Oswald) Come, sir, I’ll teach you differences. Away, away. If you will measure your lubber’s length again, tarry; but away if you have wisdom. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Oswald |
| LEAR | Now, friendly knave, I thank thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lear’s [[Fool]] |
| LEAR | There’s earnest of thy service.<br>He gives [[Kent]] money |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Let me hire him, too. (To [[Kent]]) Here’s my coxcomb. |
| LEAR | How now, my pretty knave, how dost thou? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | (to [[Kent]]) Sirrah, you were best take my coxcomb. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Why, [[Fool|fool]]? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Why, for taking one’s part that’s out of favour. Nay, an thou canst not smile as the [[Wind|wind]] sits, thou’lt catch cold shortly. There, take my coxcomb. Why, this fellow hath banished two on’s daughters and done the third a blessing against his will. If thou follow him, thou must needs wear my coxcomb. (To Lear) How now, nuncle? Would I had two coxcombs and two daughters. |
| LEAR | Why, my boy? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | If I gave them my living I’d keep my coxcombs myself. There’s mine; beg another off thy daughters. |
| LEAR | Take heed, sirrah—the whip. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | [[Truth]] is a dog that must to kennel. He must be whipped out when Lady the brach may stand by the [[Fire|fire]] and stink. |
| LEAR | A pestilent gall to me! |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Fool|FOOL]] to [[Kent]] Sirrah, I’ll teach thee a speech. |
| LEAR | Do. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Mark it, uncle.<br>Have more than thou showest,<br>Speak less than thou knowest,<br>Lend less than thou owest,<br>Ride more than thou goest,<br>Learn more than thou trowest,<br>Set less than thou throwest,<br>Leave thy drink and thy whore,<br>And keep in-a-door,<br>And thou shalt have more<br>Than two tens to a score. |
| LEAR | This is nothing, [[Fool|fool]]. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Then, like the breath of an unfee’d lawyer, you gave me nothing for’t. Can you make no use of nothing, uncle? |
| LEAR | Why no, boy. Nothing can be made out of nothing. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | (to [[Kent]]) Prithee, tell him so much the rent of his land comes to. He will not believe a [[Fool|fool]]. 130 |
| LEAR | A bitter [[Fool|fool]]. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Dost know the difference, my boy, between a bitter [[Fool|fool]] and a sweet [[Fool|fool]]? |
| LEAR | No, lad. Teach me. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Fool|FOOL]] sings |
| LEAR | That lord that counselled thee To give away thy land,<br>Come, place him here by me;<br>Do thou for him stand.<br>The sweet and bitter [[Fool|fool]]<br>Will presently appear,<br>The one in motley here,<br>The other found out there. |
| LEAR | Dost thou call me [[Fool|fool]], boy? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | All thy other titles thou hast given away. That thou wast born with. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Lear) This is not altogether [[Fool|fool]], my lord. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | No, [[Faith|faith]]; lords and great men will not let me. If I had a monopoly out, they would have part on’t, and ladies too, they will not let me have all the [[Fool|fool]] to myself—they’ll be snatching. Give me an egg, nuncle, and I’ll give thee two crowns. |
| LEAR | What two crowns shall they be? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Why, after I have cut the egg in the middle and eat up the meat, the two crowns of the egg. When thou clovest thy [[Crown|crown]] i‘th’ middle and gavest away both parts, thou borest thy ass o’th’ back o’er the dirt. Thou hadst little wit in thy bald [[Crown|crown]] when thou gavest thy golden one away. If I speak like myself in this, let him be whipped that first finds it so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Fools had ne’er less wit in a year,<br>For wise men are grown foppish.<br>They know not how their wits do wear,<br>Their manners are so apish. |
| LEAR | When were you wont to be so full of [[songs]], sirrah? |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | I have used it, nuncle, ever since thou madest thy daughters thy mother; for when thou gavest them the rod and puttest down thine own breeches, |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Then they for sudden joy did weep, And I for sorrow sung,<br>That such a [[King|king]] should play bo-peep<br>And go the fools among.<br>Prithee, nuncle, keep a schoolmaster that can teach thy [[Fool|fool]] to lie. I would fain learn to lie. |
| LEAR | An you lie, we’ll have you whipped. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | I marvel what kin thou and thy daughters are. They’ll have me whipped for speaking true, thou wilt have me whipped for lying, and sometime I am whipped for holding my peace. I had rather be any kind of thing than a [[Fool|fool]]; and yet I would not be thee, nuncle. Thou hast pared thy wit o’ both sides and left nothing in the middle. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Gonoril |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Here comes one of the parings. |
| LEAR | How now, daughter, what makes that frontlet on?<br>Methinks you are too much o’ late i’th’ frown. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Fool|FOOL]] Thou wast a pretty fellow when thou hadst no need to care for her frown. Now thou art an O without a figure. I am better than thou art, now. I am a [[Fool|fool]]; thou art nothing. To Gonoril Yes, forsooth, I will hold my tongue; so your face bids me, though you say nothing. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| LEAR | Mum, mum. He that keeps neither crust nor crumb,<br>Weary of all, shall want some.<br>That’s a shelled peascod. |
| GONORIL | (to Lear)<br>Not only, sir, this your all-licensed [[Fool|fool]],<br>But other of your insolent retinue<br>Do hourly carp and quarrel, breaking forth<br>In rank and not-to-be-endured riots.<br>Sir, I had thought by making this [[Well|well]] known unto<br>you<br>To have found a safe redress, but now grow fearful,<br>By what yourself too late have spoke and done,<br>That you protect this course, and put it on<br>By your allowance; which if you should, the fault<br>Would not scape censure, nor the redress [[Sleep|sleep]]<br>Which in the tender of a wholesome weal<br>Might in their working do you that offence,<br>That else were shame, that then necessity<br>Must call discreet proceedings. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | (to Lear) For, you trow, nuncle, |
| *(didascalia)* | Sings |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | The hedge-sparrow fed the cuckoo so long<br>That it had it head bit off by it young;<br>so out went the candle, and we were left darkling. |
| LEAR | (to Gonoril) Are you our daughter? |
| GONORIL | Come, sir, I would you would make use of that good<br>wisdom<br>Whereof I know you are fraught, and put away<br>These dispositions that of late transform you<br>From what you rightly are. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Fool|FOOL]] May not an ass know when the cart draws the [[Horse|horse]]? Sings ‘Whoop, jug, I [[Love|love]] thee!’ |
| LEAR | Doth any here know me? Why, this is not Lear.<br>Doth Lear walk thus, speak thus? Where are his eyes?<br>Either his notion weakens, or his discernings<br>Are lethargied. Sleeping or waking, ha?<br>Sure, ’tis not so.<br>Who is it that can tell me who I am?<br>Lear’s [[Shadow|shadow]]? I would learn that, for by the marks<br>Of sovereignty, knowledge, and reason<br>I should be false persuaded I had daughters. |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Which they will make an obedient father. |
| LEAR | (to Gonoril)<br>Your name, fair gentlewoman? |
| GONORIL | Come, sir,<br>This admiration is much of the savour<br>Of other your new pranks. I do beseech you<br>Understand my purposes aright,<br>As you are old and reverend, should be wise.<br>Here do you keep a hundred knights and squires,<br>Men so disordered, so debauched and bold<br>That this our [[Court|court]], infected with their manners,<br>Shows like a riotous inn, epicurism<br>And lust make more like to a tavern, or brothel,<br>Than a great palace. The shame itself doth speak<br>For instant remedy. Be thou desired,<br>By her that else will take the thing she begs,<br>A little to disquantity your train,<br>And the remainder that shall still depend<br>To be such men as may besort your age,<br>That know themselves and you. |
| LEAR | Darkness and devils!<br>Saddle my horses, call my train together!— |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one or more |
| LEAR | Degenerate bastard, I’ll not trouble thee.<br>Yet have I left a daughter. |
| GONORIL | You strike my people, and your disordered rabble<br>Make servants of their betters. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Duke of Albany |
| LEAR | We that too late repent‘s—O sir, are you come?<br>Is it your will that we—prepare my horses. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit one or more |
| LEAR | Ingratitude, thou marble-hearted fiend,<br>More hideous when thou show’st thee in a [[Child|child]]<br>Than the sea-[[Monster|monster]]—(to Gonoril) detested kite, thou<br>liest.<br>My train are men of choice and rarest parts,<br>That all particulars of duty know,<br>And in the most exact regard support<br>The worships of their name. O most small fault,<br>How ugly didst thou in [[Cordelia]] show,<br>That, like an engine, wrenched my frame of [[Nature|nature]]<br>From the fixed place, drew from my heart all [[Love|love]],<br>And added to the gall! O Lear, Lear!<br>Beat at this gate that let thy folly in<br>And thy dear judgement out.—Go, go, my people! |
| ALBANY | My lord, I am guiltless as I am ignorant. |
| LEAR | It may be so, my lord. Hark, [[Nature|nature]], hear:<br>Dear goddess, suspend thy purpose if<br>Thou didst intend to make this creature fruitful.<br>Into her womb convey sterility.<br>Dry up in her the organs of increase,<br>And from her derogate [[Body|body]] never [[Spring|spring]]<br>A babe to [[Honour|honour]] her. If she must teem,<br>Create her [[Child|child]] of spleen, that it may live<br>And be a thwart disnatured torment to her.<br>Let it stamp wrinkles in her brow of youth,<br>With cadent [[Tears|tears]] fret channels in her cheeks,<br>Turn all her mother’s pains and benefits<br>To laughter and contempt, that she may feel—<br>That she may feel<br>How sharper than a serpent’s tooth it is<br>To have a thankless [[Child|child]].—Go, go, my people! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Lear, [[Kent]], [[Fool]], and servants |
| ALBANY | Now, gods that we adore, whereof comes this? |
| GONORIL | Never afflict yourself to know the cause,<br>But let his disposition have that scope<br>That dotage gives it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King Lear]] and his [[Fool]] |
| LEAR | What, fifty of my followers at a clap? |
| *(didascalia)* | Within a fortnight? |
| ALBANY | What is the matter, sir? |
| LEAR | I’ll tell thee. (To Gonoril) Life and [[Death|death]]! I am<br>ashamed<br>That thou hast power to shake my manhood thus,<br>That these hot [[Tears|tears]], that break from me perforce<br>And should make thee—worst blasts and fogs upon<br>thee!<br>Untented woundings of a father’s curse<br>Pierce every sense about thee! Old fond eyes,<br>Beweep this cause again I’ll pluck you out<br>And cast you, with the waters that you make,<br>To temper clay. Yea,<br>Is’t come to this? Yet have I left a daughter<br>Whom, I am sure, is kind and comfortable.<br>When she shall hear this of thee, with her nails<br>She’ll flay thy wolvish visage. Thou shalt find<br>That I’ll resume the shape which thou dost think<br>I have cast off for ever; thou shalt, I warrant thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| GONORIL | Do you mark that, my lord? |
| ALBANY | I cannot be so partial, Gonoril,<br>To the great [[Love|love]] I bear you— |
| GONORIL | Come, sir, no more.—<br>You, more knave than [[Fool|fool]], after your master! |
| [[Fool|FOOL]] | Nuncle Lear, nuncle Lear, tarry, and take the [[Fool|fool]] with thee.<br>A fox when one has caught her,<br>And such a daughter,<br>Should sure to the slaughter,<br>If my cap would buy a halter.<br>So, the [[Fool|fool]] follows after. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| GONORIL | What, Oswald, ho! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Oswald |
| OSWALD | Here, madam. |
| GONORIL | What, have you writ this letter to my sister? |
| OSWALD | Yes, madam. |
| GONORIL | Take you some company, and away to [[Horse|horse]].<br>Inform her full of my particular fears,<br>And thereto add such reasons of your own<br>As may compact it more. Get you gone,<br>And after, your retinue. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Oswald |
| GONORIL | Now, my lord,<br>This milky gentleness and course of yours,<br>Though I dislike not, yet under pardon<br>You’re much more ataxed for want of wisdom<br>Than praised for harmful mildness. |
| ALBANY | How far your eyes may pierce I cannot tell.<br>Striving to better aught, we mar what’s [[Well|well]]. |
| GONORIL | Nay, then— |
| ALBANY | [[Well]], [[Well|well]], the event. Exeunt |
