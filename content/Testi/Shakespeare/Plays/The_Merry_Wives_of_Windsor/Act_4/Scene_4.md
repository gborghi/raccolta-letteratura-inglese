| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Master Page]], [[Master Ford]], [[Mistress Page]], [[Mistress Ford]], and Sir Hugh Evans |
| EVANS | ‘Tis one of the best discretions of a ’oman as ever I did look upon. |
| PAGE | And did he send you both these letters at an instant? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Within a quarter of an hour. |
| FORD | Pardon me, wife. Henceforth do what thou wilt.<br>I rather will suspect the sun with cold<br>Than thee with wantonness. Now doth thy [[Honour|honour]]<br>stand,<br>In him that was of late an heretic,<br>As firm as [[Faith|faith]]. |
| PAGE | ’Tis [[Well|well]], ’tis [[Well|well]]; no more.<br>Be not as extreme in submission<br>As in offence.<br>But let our plot go forward. Let our wives<br>Yet once again, to make us public sport,<br>Appoint a meeting with this old fat fellow,<br>Where we may take him and disgrace him for it. |
| FORD | There is no better way than that they spoke of. |
| PAGE | How, to send him word they’ll meet him in the Park<br>At midnight? Fie, fie, he’ll never come. |
| EVANS | You say he has been thrown in the rivers, and has been grievously peaten as an old ’oman. Methinks there should be terrors in him, that he should not come. Methinks his flesh is punished; he shall have no desires. |
| PAGE | So think I too. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] |
| PAGE | Devise but how you’ll use him when he comes,<br>And let us two devise to bring him thither. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | There is an old tale goes that Herne the hunter,<br>Sometime a keeper here in Windsor Forest,<br>Doth all the [[Winter|winter]] [[Time|time]] at still midnight<br>Walk round about an oak with great ragg’d horns;<br>And there he blasts the trees, and takes the cattle,<br>And makes milch-kine yield [[Blood|blood]], and shakes a chain<br>In a most hideous and dreadful manner.<br>You have heard of such a spirit, and [[Well|well]] you know<br>The superstitious idle-headed eld<br>Received, and did deliver to our age,<br>This tale of Herne the hunter for a [[Truth|truth]]. |
| PAGE | Why, yet there want not many that do [[Fear|fear]]<br>In deep of [[Night|night]] to walk by this Herne’s Oak.<br>But what of this? |
| [[Mistress Ford|MISTRESS FORD]] | Marry, this is our device:<br>That [[Falstaff]] at that oak shall meet with us,<br>Disguised like Herne, with huge horns on his head. |
| PAGE | [[Well]], let it not be doubted but he’ll come,<br>And in this shape. When you have brought him<br>thither<br>What shall be done with him? What is your plot? |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | That likewise have we thought upon, and thus.<br>Nan Page my daughter, and my little son,<br>And three or four more of their growth, we’ll dress<br>Like urchins, oafs, and fairies, green and white,<br>With rounds of waxen tapers on their heads,<br>And rattles in their hands. Upon a sudden,<br>As [[Falstaff]], she, and I are newly met,<br>Let them from forth a saw-pit rush at once,<br>With some diffused [[Song|song]]. Upon their sight<br>We two in great amazèdness will fly.<br>Then let them all encircle him about,<br>And, fairy-like, to pinch the unclean knight,<br>And ask him why, that hour of fairy revel,<br>In their so sacred paths he dares to tread<br>In shape profane.<br>[mistress! FORD And till he tell the [[Truth|truth]],<br>Let the supposed fairies pinch him sound,<br>And burn him with their tapers. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | The [[Truth|truth]] being known,<br>We’ll all present ourselves, dis-horn the spirit,<br>And mock him [[Home|home]] to Windsor. |
| FORD | The children must<br>Be practised [[Well|well]] to this, or they’ll ne’er do’t. |
| EVANS | I will teach the children their behaviours, and I will be like a jackanapes also, to burn the knight with my taber. |
| FORD | That will be excellent. I’ll go buy them vizors. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | My Nan shall be the [[Queen]] of all the Fairies,<br>Finely attired in a robe of white. |
| PAGE | That silk will I go buy—(aside) and in that tire<br>Shall Master Slender steal my Nan away,<br>And marry her at Eton. (To [[Mistress Page]]) Go send to<br>[[Falstaff]] straight. |
| FORD | Nay, I’ll to him again in name of Brooke.<br>He’ll tell me all his purpose. Sure he’ll come. |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | [[Fear]] not you that. (To Page, Ford, and Evans) Go get us<br>properties<br>And tricking for our fairies. |
| EVANS | Let us about it. It is admirable pleasures, and fery honest knaveries. Exeunt Ford, Page, and Evans |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | Go, [[Mistress Ford]],<br>Send quickly to Sir John, to know his mind. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Mistress Ford]] |
| [[Mistress Page|MISTRESS PAGE]] | I’ll to the Doctor. He hath my good will,<br>And none but he, to marry with Nan Page.<br>That Slender, though [[Well|well]] landed, is an idiot;<br>And he my husband best of all affects.<br>The Doctor is [[Well|well]] moneyed, and his friends<br>Potent at [[Court|court]]. He, none but he, shall have her,<br>Though twenty thousand worthier come to crave her. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
