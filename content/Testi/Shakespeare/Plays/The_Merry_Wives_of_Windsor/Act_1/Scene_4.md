| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Mistress Quickly]] and Simple |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | What, John Rugby! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter John Rugby |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | I pray thee, go to the casement and see if you can see<br>my master, Master [[Doctor Caius]], coming. If he do,<br>i’[[Faith|faith]], and find anybody in the [[house]], here will be an<br>old abusing of God’s patience and the [[King]]’s English. |
| RUGBY | I’ll go watch. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Go; and we’ll have a posset for’t soon at [[Night|night]], in [[Faith|faith]], at the latter end of a seacoal [[Fire|fire]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Rugby |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | An honest, willing, kind fellow as ever servant shall come in [[house]] withal; and, I warrant you, no telltale, nor no breedbate. His worst fault is that he is given to [[prayer]]; he is something peevish that way—but nobody but has his fault. But let that pass. Peter Simple you say your name is? |
| SIMPLE | Ay, for fault of a better. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | And Master Slender’s your master ? |
| SIMPLE | Ay, forsooth. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Does he not wear a great round beard, like a glover’s paring-knife? |
| SIMPLE | No, forsooth; he hath but a little whey face, with a little yellow beard, a [[Cain]]-coloured beard. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | A softly spirited man, is he not? |
| SIMPLE | Ay, forsooth; but he is as tall a man of his hands as any is between this and his head. He hath fought with a warrener. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | How say you?—O, I should remember him: does he not hold up his head, as it were, and strut in his gait? |
| SIMPLE | Yes, indeed does he. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | [[Well]], heaven send [[Anne Page]] no worse fortune! Tell Master Parson Evans I will do what I can for your master. Anne is a good girl, and I wish— |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Rugby |
| *(didascalia)* | RUGBY Out, alas, here comes my master! Exit |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | We shall all be shent. Run in here, good young man; for God’s sake, go into this closet. He will not stay long.<br>Simple steps into the closet<br>What, John Rugby! John! What, John, I say! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Rugby |
| *(didascalia)* | Speaking loudly Go, John, go enquire for my master. I |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | [[Doubt|doubt]] he be not [[Well|well]], that he comes not [[Home|home]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Rugby |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | (Singing) ’And down, down, adown-a’ (etc.) |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Doctor Caius]] |
| CAIUS | Vat is you sing? I do not like dese toys. Pray you go and vetch me in my closet un boîtier vert—a box, a green-a box. Do intend vat I speak? A green-a box. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Ay, forsooth, I’ll fetch it you. (Aside) I am glad he went not in himself. If he had found the young man, he would have been horn-mad.<br>She goes to fetch the box |
| CAIUS | Fe, fe, fe, fe! Ma foi, il fait fort chaud! Je m’en vais à la cour. La grande affaire. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Is it this, sir? |
| CAIUS | Oui. Mets-le à ma pochette. Dépêche, quickly! Vere is dat knave Rugby? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | What, John Rugby! John! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Rugby |
| RUGBY | Here, sir. |
| CAIUS | You are John Rugby, and you are Jack Rugby. Come, take-a your rapier, and come after my heel to the [[Court|court]]. |
| RUGBY | ’Tis ready, sir, here in the porch.<br>He fetches the rapier |
| CAIUS | By my trot, I tarry too long. ’Od’s me, qu’ai-j’oublié? Dere is some simples in my closet dat I vill not for the varld I shall leave behind. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | (aside) Ay me, he’ll find the young man there, and be mad. |
| CAIUS | (discovering Simple) O diable, diable! Vat is in my closet? Villainy, larron! Rugby, my rapier!<br>He takes the rapier |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Good master, be content. |
| CAIUS | Wherefore shall I be content-a? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | The young man is an honest man. |
| CAIUS | What shall de honest man do in my closet? Dere is no honest man dat shall come in my closet. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | I beseech you, be not so phlegmatic. Hear the [[Truth|truth]] of it. He came of an errand to me from Parson Hugh. |
| CAIUS | Vell. |
| SIMPLE | Ay, forsooth, to [[Desire|desire]] her to— |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Peace, I pray you. |
| CAIUS | Peace-a your tongue. (To Simple) Speak-a your tale. |
| SIMPLE | To [[Desire|desire]] this honest gentlewoman, your maid, to speak a good word to Mistress [[Anne Page]] for my master in the way of [[Marriage|marriage]]. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | This is all, indeed, la; but I’ll ne’er put my finger in the [[Fire|fire]] an need not. |
| CAIUS | Sir Hugh send-a you?—Rugby, baile me some paper.<br>Rugby brings paper<br>(To Simple) Tarry you a little-a while.<br>Caius writes |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | (aside to Simple) I am glad he is so quiet. If he had been throughly moved, you should have heard him so loud and so [[Melancholy|melancholy]]. But notwithstanding, man, I’ll do your master what good I can. And the very yea and the no is, the French doctor, my master—I may call him my master, look you, for I keep his [[house]], and I wash, wring, brew, bake, scour, dress meat and drink, make the beds, and do all myself— |
| SIMPLE | (aside to [[Mistress Quickly]]) ’Tis a great charge to come under one [[Body|body]]’s hand. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | (aside to Simple) Are you advised o’ that? You shall find it a great charge—and to be up early, and down late. But notwithstanding, to tell you in your ear—I would have no words of it—my master himself is in [[Love|love]] with Mistress [[Anne Page]]. But notwithstanding that, I know Anne’s mind: that’s neither here nor there. |
| CAIUS | (giving the letter to Simple) You, jack’nape, give-a this letter to Sir Hugh. By Gar, it is a shallenge. I will cut his troat in de Park, and I will teach a scurvy jackanape priest to meddle or make. You may be gone. It is not good you tarry here. By Gar, I will cut all his two stones. By Gar, he shall not have a stone to throw at his dog. Exit Simple |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Alas, he speaks but for his friend. CAIUS It is no matter-a ver dat. Do not you tell-a me dat I shall have [[Anne Page]] for myself? By Gar, I vill kill de jack-priest. And I have appointed mine Host of de Jarteer to measure our weapon. By Gar, I will myself have [[Anne Page]]. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Sir, the maid loves you, and all shall be [[Well|well]]. We must give folks leave to prate, what the goodyear! |
| CAIUS | Rugby, come to the [[Court|court]] with me. (To [[Mistress Quickly]]) By Gar, if I have not [[Anne Page]], I shall turn your head out of my door. Follow my heels, Rugby. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | You shall have Anne— |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Caius and Rugby |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | —ass-head of your own. No, I know Anne’s mind for<br>that. Never a woman in Windsor knows more of Anne’s<br>mind than I do, nor can do more than I do with her,<br>I thank heaven. |
| [[Fenton|FENTON]] | (within) Who’s within there, ho! |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Who’s there, I trow?—Come near the [[house]], I pray you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Master [[Fenton]] |
| [[Fenton|FENTON]] | How now, good woman, how dost thou? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | The better that it pleases your good worship to ask. |
| [[Fenton|FENTON]] | What news? How does pretty Mistress Anne? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | In [[Truth|truth]], sir, and she is pretty, and honest, and gentle, and one that is your friend. I can tell you that by the way, I praise heaven for it. |
| [[Fenton|FENTON]] | Shall I do any good, thinkest thou? Shall I not lose my suit? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Troth, sir, all is in His hands above. But notwithstanding, Master [[Fenton]], I’ll be sworn on a book she loves you. Have not your worship a wart above your eye? |
| [[Fenton|FENTON]] | Yes, marry, have I. What of that? |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | [[Well]], thereby hangs a tale. Good [[Faith|faith]], it is such another Nan!—But I detest, an honest maid as ever broke bread.—We had an hour’s talk of that wart. I shall never laugh but in that maid’s company.—But indeed she is given too much to allicholy and musing.—But for you—[[Well|well]]—go to! |
| [[Fenton|FENTON]] | [[Well]], I shall see her today. Hold, there’s money for thee. Let me have thy voice in my behalf. If thou seest her before me, commend me. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Will I? I’[[Faith|faith]], that I will. And I will tell your worship more of the wart the next [[Time|time]] we have confidence, and of other wooers. |
| [[Fenton|FENTON]] | [[Well]], farewell. I am in great haste now. |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Farewell to your worship. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Fenton]] |
| [[Mistress Quickly|MISTRESS QUICKLY]] | Truly, an honest gentleman; but Anne loves him not,<br>for I know Anne’s mind as [[Well|well]] as another does.—Out<br>upon’t, what have I forgot? Exit |
