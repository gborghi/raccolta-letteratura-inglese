| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| PERICLES | The god of this great vast rebuke these surges<br>Which wash both heav’n and hell; and thou that hast<br>Upon the winds command, bind them in brass,<br>Having called them from the deep. O still<br>Thy deaf‘ning dreadful thunders, gently quench<br>Thy nimble sulph’rous flashes.—O, ho, Lychorida!<br>How does my queen?—Thou stormest venomously.<br>Wilt thou spit all thyself The seaman’s whistle<br>Is as a whisper in the ears of death,<br>Unheard.—Lychorida!—Lucina, O!<br>Divinest patroness, and midwife gentle<br>To those that cry by night, convey thy deity<br>Aboard our dancing boat, make swift the pangs<br>Of my queen’s travails!—Now, Lychorida. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lychorida with an infant |
| LYCHORIDA | Here is a thing too young for such a place,<br>Who, if it had conceit, would die, as I<br>Am like to do. Take in your arms this piece<br>Of your dead queen. |
| PERICLES | How, how, Lychorida? |
| LYCHORIDA | Patience, good sir, do not assist the storm.<br>Here’s all that is left living of your queen,<br>A little daughter. For the sake of it<br>Be manly, and take comfort. |
| PERICLES | O you gods!<br>Why do you make us love your goodly gifts,<br>And snatch them straight away? We here below<br>Recall not what we give, and therein may<br>Use honour with you. |
| LYCHORIDA | Patience, good sir,<br>E’en for this charge. |
| *(didascalia)* | She gives him the infant. Pericles, looking mournfully upon it, shakes his head, and weeps |
| PERICLES | Now mild may be thy life,<br>For a more blust‘rous birth had never babe;<br>Quiet and gentle thy conditions, for<br>Thou art the rudeliest welcome to this world<br>That e’er was prince’s child; happy what follows.<br>Thou hast as chiding a nativity<br>As fire, air, water, earth, and heav’n can make<br>To herald thee from th’ womb. Poor inch of nature,<br>Ev’n at the first thy loss is more than can<br>Thy partage quit with all thou canst find here.<br>Now the good gods throw their best eyes upon’t. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Master and a Sailor |
| *(didascalia)* | MASTER What, courage, sir! God save you. |
| PERICLES | Courage enough, I do not fear the flaw;<br>It hath done to me its worst. Yet for the love<br>Of this poor infant, this fresh new seafarer,<br>I would it would be quiet. |
| *(didascalia)* | MASTER (calling) Slack the bow-lines, there.—Thou wilt not, wilt thou? Blow, and split thyself. |
| SAILOR | But searoom, an the brine and cloudy billow kiss the moon, I care not. |
| *(didascalia)* | MASTER (to Pericles) Sir, your queen must overboard. The sea works high, the wind is loud, and will not lie till the ship be cleared of the dead. |
| PERICLES | That’s but your superstition. |
| *(didascalia)* | MASTER Pardon us, sir; with us at sea it hath been still observed, and we are strong in custom. Therefore briefly yield ’er, for she must overboard straight. |
| PERICLES | As you think meet. Most wretched queen! |
| LYCHORIDA | Here she lies, sir. |
| *(didascalia)* | She draws the curtains and discovers the body of Thaisa in a bed. Pericles gives Lychorida the infant |
| PERICLES | (to Thaisa)<br>A terrible childbed hast thou had, my dear,<br>No light, no fire. Th‘unfriendly elements<br>Forgot thee utterly, nor have I time<br>To give thee hallowed to thy grave, but straight<br>Must cast thee, scarcely coffined, in the ooze,<br>Where, for a monument upon thy bones<br>And aye-remaining lamps, the belching whale<br>And humming water must o’erwhelm thy corpse,<br>Lying with simple shetts.—O Lychorida,<br>Bid Nestor bring me spices, ink, and paper,<br>My casket and my jewels, and bid Nicander<br>Bring me the satin coffer. Lay the babe<br>Upon the pillow. Hie thee whiles I say<br>A priestly farewell to her. Suddenly, woman. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Lychorida |
| *(didascalia)* | SAILOR Sir, we have a chest beneath the hatches caulked and bitumed ready. |
| *(didascalia)* | I thank thee. To the Master Mariner, say, what coast is this? |
| *(didascalia)* | MASTER |
| PERICLES | We are near Tarsus. |
| PERICLES | Thither, gentle mariner, |
| *(didascalia)* | Alter thy course from Tyre. When canst thou reach it? MASTER |
| PERICLES | By break of day, if the wind cease. |
| PERICLES | Make for Tarsus.<br>There will I visit Cleon, for the babe<br>Cannot hold out to Tyrus. There I’ll leave it<br>At careful nursing. Go thy ways, good mariner.<br>I’ll bring the body presently. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Master at one door and Sailor beneath the hatches. Exit Pericles to Thaisa, closing the curtains |
