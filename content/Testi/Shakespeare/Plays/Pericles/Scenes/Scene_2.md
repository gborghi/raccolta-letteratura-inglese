| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Pericles]], distempered, with his lords |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Let none disturb us. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt lords |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Why should this change of thoughts,<br>The sad companion, dull-eyed [[Melancholy|melancholy]],<br>Be my so used a guest as not an hour<br>In the day’s glorious walk or peaceful [[Night|night]],<br>The tomb where grief should [[Sleep|sleep]], can breed me<br>quiet? 5<br>Here pleasures [[Court|court]] mine eyes, and mine eyes shun<br>them,<br>And danger, which I feared, ’s at Antioch,<br>Whose arm seems far too short to hit me here.<br>Yet neither pleasure’s art can joy my spirits,<br>Nor yet care’s author’s distance comfort me.<br>Then it is thus: the passions of the mind,<br>That have their first conception by misdread,<br>Have after-nourishment and life by care,<br>And what was first but [[Fear|fear]] what might be done<br>Grows elder now, and cares it be not done.<br>And so with me. The great [[Antiochus]],<br>‘Gainst whom I am too little to contend,<br>Since he’s so great can make his will his act,<br>Will think me speaking though I swear to [[Silence|silence]],<br>Nor boots it me to say I [[Honour|honour]] him<br>If he suspect I may dishonour him.<br>And what may make him blush in being known,<br>He’ll stop the course by which it might be known.<br>With hostile forces he’ll o’erspread the land,<br>And with th‘ostent of war will look so huge<br>Amazement shall drive [[Courage|courage]] from the state,<br>Our men be vanquished ere they do resist,<br>And subjects punished that ne’er thought offence,<br>Which care of them, not pity of myself,<br>Who am no more but as the tops of trees<br>Which fence the roots they grow by and defend them,<br>Makes both my [[Body|body]] pine and [[Soul|soul]] to languish,<br>And punish that before that he would punish. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter all the Lords, among them old Helicanus, to [[Pericles]] |
| FIRST LORD | Joy and all comfort in your sacred breast! |
| SECOND LORD | And keep your mind peaceful and comfortable. |
| HELICANUS | Peace, peace, and give experience tongue.<br>(To [[Pericles]]) You do not [[Well|well]] so to abuse yourself,<br>To waste your [[Body|body]] here with pining sorrow,<br>Upon whose safety doth depend the lives<br>And the prosperity of a whole kingdom.<br>‘Tis ill in you to do it, and no less<br>ll in your council not to contradict it.<br>They do abuse the [[King]] that flatter him,<br>For flatt’ry is the bellows blows up sin;<br>The thing the which is flattered, but a spark,<br>To which that [[Wind|wind]] gives heat and stronger glowing;<br>Whereas reproof, obedient and in order,<br>Fits kings as they are men, for they may err.<br>When Signor Sooth here does proclaim a peace<br>He flatters you, makes war upon your life. |
| *(didascalia)* | He kneels |
| HELICANUS | Prince, pardon me, or strike me if you please.<br>I cannot be much lower than my knees. |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | All leave us else; but let your cares o’erlook<br>What shipping and what lading’s in our haven,<br>And then return to us. Exeunt Lords<br>Helicane, thou<br>Hast moved us. What seest thou in our looks? |
| HELICANUS | An angry brow, dread lord. |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | If there be such a dart in princes’ frowns,<br>How durst thy tongue move anger to our brows? |
| HELICANUS | How dares the plants look up to heav’n from whence<br>They have their nourishment? |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Thou knowest I have pow’r to take thy life from thee. |
| HELICANUS | I have ground the axe myself; do you but strike the blow. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Pericles|PERICLES]] lifting him up |
| HELICANUS | Rise, prithee, rise. Sit down. Thou art no flatterer,<br>I thank thee for it, and the heav‘ns forbid<br>That kings should let their ears hear their faults hid.<br>Fit counsellor and servant for a prince,<br>Who by thy wisdom mak’st a prince thy servant,<br>What wouldst thou have me do? |
| HELICANUS | To bear with patience<br>Such griefs as you do lay upon yourself. |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Thou speak‘st like a physician, Helicanus,<br>That ministers a potion unto me<br>That thou wouldst tremble to receive thyself.<br>Attend me, then. I went to Antioch,<br>Where, as thou know’st, against the face of [[Death|death]]<br>I sought the purchase of a glorious [[Beauty|beauty]]<br>From whence an issue I might propagate,<br>As children are heav‘n’s blessings: to parents,<br>objects;<br>Are arms to princes, and bring joys to subjects.<br>Her face was to mine eye beyond all [[Wonder|wonder]],<br>The rest—hark in thine ear—as black as incest,<br>Which by my knowledge found, the sinful father<br>Seemed not to strike, but smooth. But thou know’st<br>this,<br>‘Tis [[Time|time]] to [[Fear|fear]] when tyrants seems to kiss;<br>Which [[Fear|fear]] so grew in me I hither fled<br>Under the covering of careful [[Night|night]],<br>Who seemed my good protector, and being here<br>Bethought me what was past, what might succeed.<br>I knew him tyrannous, and tyrants’ fears<br>Decrease not, but grow faster than the years.<br>And should he [[Doubt|doubt]]—as [[Doubt|doubt]] no [[Doubt|doubt]] he doth—<br>That I should open to the list’ning air<br>How many worthy princes’ bloods were shed<br>To keep his bed of blackness unlaid ope,<br>To lop that [[Doubt|doubt]] he’ll fill this land with arms,<br>And make pretence of wrong that I have done him,<br>When all for mine—if I may call—offence<br>Must feel war’s blow, who spares not [[innocence]];<br>Which [[Love|love]] to all, of which thyself art one,<br>Who now reproved’st me for’t— |
| HELICANUS | Alas, sir. |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Drew [[Sleep|sleep]] out of mine eyes, [[Blood|blood]] from my cheeks,<br>Musings into my mind, with thousand doubts,<br>How I might stop this tempest ere it came,<br>And, finding little comfort to relieve them,<br>I thought it princely [[Charity|charity]] to grieve them. |
| HELICANUS | [[Well]], my lord, since you have giv’n me leave to speak,<br>Freely will I speak. [[Antiochus]] you [[Fear|fear]],<br>And justly too, I think, you [[Fear|fear]] the [[Tyrant|tyrant]],<br>Who either by public war or private treason<br>Will take away your life.<br>Therefore, my lord, go travel for a while,<br>Till that his rage and anger be forgot,<br>Or destinies do cut his thread of life.<br>Your rule direct to any; if to me,<br>Day serves not light more faithful than I’ll be. |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | I do not [[Doubt|doubt]] thy [[Faith|faith]],<br>But should he in my absence wrong thy liberties? |
| HELICANUS | We’ll mingle our bloods together in the earth<br>From whence we had our being and our birth. |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Tyre, I now look from thee then, and to Tarsus<br>Intend my travel, where I’ll hear from thee,<br>And by whose letters I’ll dispose myself.<br>The care I had and have of subjects’ good<br>On thee I lay, whose wisdom’s strength can bear it.<br>I’ll take thy word for [[Faith|faith]], not ask thine oath;<br>Who shuns not to break one will sure crack both.<br>But in our orbs we’ll live so round and safe<br>That [[Time|time]] of both this [[Truth|truth]] shall ne’er convince:<br>Thou showed’st a subject’s shine, I a true prince. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
