| *(didascalia)* | Enter Gower as Prologue |
| GOWER | To sing a song that old was sung<br>From ashes ancient Gower is come,<br>Assuming man’s infirmities<br>To glad your ear and please your eyes.<br>It hath been sung at festivals,<br>On ember-eves and holy-ales,<br>And lords and ladies in their lives<br>Have read it for restoratives.<br>The purchase is to make men glorious,<br>Et bonum quo antiquius eo melius.<br><br>If you, born in these latter times<br>When wit’s more ripe, accept my rhymes,<br>And that to hear an old man sing<br>May to your wishes pleasure bring,<br>I life would wish, and that I might<br>Waste it for you like taper-light.<br>This’ Antioch, then; Antiochus the Great<br>Built up this city for his chiefest seat,<br>The fairest in all Syria.<br>I tell you what mine authors say.<br>This king unto him took a fere<br>Who died, and left a female heir<br>So buxom, blithe, and full of face<br>As heav’n had lent her all his grace,<br>With whom the father liking took,<br>And her to incest did provoke.<br>Bad child, worse father, to entice his own<br>To evil should be done by none.<br>By custom what they did begin<br>Was with long use account’ no sin.<br>The beauty of this sinful dame<br>Made many princes thither frame<br>To seek her as a bedfellow,<br>In marriage pleasures playfellow,<br>Which to prevent he made a law<br>To keep her still, and men in awe,<br>That whoso asked her for his wife,<br>His riddle told not, lost his life.<br>So for her many a wight did die, |
| *(didascalia)* | A row of heads is revealed |
| GOWER | As yon grim looks do testify.<br>What now ensues, to th’ judgement of your eye<br>I give, my cause who best can justify. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Sennet. Enter King Antiochus, Prince Pericles, and lords and peers in their richest ornaments |
| ANTIOCHUS | Young Prince of Tyre, you have at large received<br>The danger of the task you undertake. |
| PERICLES | I have, Antiochus, and with a soul<br>Emboldened with the glory of her praise<br>Think death no hazard in this enterprise. |
| ANTIOCHUS | Music! |
| *(didascalia)* | Music sounds |
| ANTIOCHUS | Bring in our daughter, clothèd like a bride<br>Fit for th’embracements ev’n of Jove himself,<br>At whose conception, till Lucina reigned,<br>Nature this dowry gave to glad her presence:<br>The senate-house of planets all did sit,<br>In her their best perfections to knit. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Antiochus’ Daughter |
| PERICLES | See where she comes, apparelled like the spring,<br>Graces her subjects, and her thoughts the king<br>Of ev’ry virtue gives renown to men;<br>Her face the book of praises, where is read<br>Nothing but curious pleasures, as from thence<br>Sorrow were ever razed and testy wrath<br>Could never be her mild companion.<br>You gods that made me man, and sway in love,<br>That have inflamed desire in my breast<br>To taste the fruit of yon celestial tree<br>Or die in the adventure, be my helps,<br>As I am son and servant to your will,<br>To compass such a boundless happiness. |
| ANTIOCHUS | Prince Pericles— |
| PERICLES | That would be son to great Antiochus. |
| ANTIOCHUS | Before thee stands this fair Hesperides,<br>With golden fruit, but dang’rous to be touched, |
| *(didascalia)* | He gestures towards the heads |
| ANTIOCHUS | For death-like dragons here affright thee hard. |
| *(didascalia)* | He gestures towards his daughter |
| ANTIOCHUS | Her heav‘n-like face enticeth thee to view<br>Her countless glory, which desert must gain;<br>And which without desert, because thine eye<br>Presumes to reach, all the whole heap must die.<br>Yon sometimes famous princes, like thyself<br>Drawn by report, advent’rous by desire,<br>Tell thee with speechless tongues and semblants<br>bloodless<br>That without covering save yon field of stars<br>Here they stand, martyrs slain in Cupid’s wars,<br>And with dead cheeks advise thee to desist<br>From going on death’s net, whom none resist. |
| PERICLES | Antiochus, I thank thee, who hath taught<br>My frail mortality to know itself,<br>And by those fearful objects to prepare<br>This body, like to them, to what I must;<br>For death remembered should be like a mirror<br>Who tells us life’s but breath, to trust it error.<br>I’ll make my will then, and, as sick men do,<br>Who know the world, see heav‘n, but feeling woe<br>Grip not at earthly joys as erst they did,<br>So I bequeath a happy peace to you<br>And all good men, as ev’ry prince should do;<br>My riches to the earth from whence they came,<br>(To the Daughter) But my unspotted fire of love to you.<br>(To Antiochus) Thus ready for the way of life or death,<br>I wait the sharpest blow, Antiochus. |
| ANTIOCHUS | Scorning advice, read the conclusion then, |
| *(didascalia)* | He angrily throws down the riddle |
| ANTIOCHUS | Which read and not expounded, ’tis decreed,<br>As these before thee, thou thyself shalt bleed. |
| DAUGHTER | (to Pericles)<br>Of all ‘sayed yet, mayst thou prove prosperous;<br>Of all ’sayed yet, I wish thee happiness. |
| PERICLES | Like a bold champion I assume the lists,<br>Nor ask advice of any other thought<br>But faithfulness and courage. |
| *(didascalia)* | He takes up and reads aloud the riddle |
| PERICLES | I am no viper, yet I feed<br>On mother’s flesh which did me breed.<br>I sought a husband, in which labour<br>I found that kindness in a father.<br>He’s father, son, and husband mild;<br>I mother, wife, and yet his child.<br>How this may be and yet in two,<br>As you will live resolve it you. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sharp physic is the last. Aside But O, you powers |
| PERICLES | That gives heav’n countless eyes to view men’s acts,<br>Why cloud they not their sights perpetually<br>If this be true which makes me pale to read it? |
| *(didascalia)* | He gazes on the Daughter |
| PERICLES | Fair glass of light, I loved you, and could still,<br>Were not this glorious casket stored with ill.<br>But I must tell you now my thoughts revolt,<br>For he’s no man on whom perfections wait<br>That, knowing sin within, will touch the gate.<br>You’re a fair viol, and your sense the strings<br>Who, fingered to make man his lawful music,<br>Would draw heav’n down and all the gods to hearken,<br>But, being played upon before your time,<br>Hell only danceth at so harsh a chime.<br>Good sooth, I care not for you. |
| ANTIOCHUS | Prince Pericles, touch not, upon thy life,<br>For that’s an article within our law<br>As dang’rous as the rest. Your time’s expired.<br>Either expound now, or receive your sentence. |
| PERICLES | Great King,<br>Few love to hear the sins they love to act.<br>‘Twould braid yourself too near for me to tell it.<br>Who has a book of all that monarchs do,<br>He’s more secure to keep it shut than shown,<br>For vice repeated, like the wand’ring wind,<br>Blows dust in others’ eyes to spread itself;<br>And yet the end of all is bought thus dear,<br>The breath is gone, and the sore eyes see clear<br>To stop the air would hurt them. The blind mole casts<br>Copped hills towards heav’n to tell the earth is thronged<br>By man’s oppression, and the poor worm doth die for’t.<br>Kings are earth’s gods; in vice their law’s their will,<br>And if Jove stray, who dares say Jove doth ill?<br>It is enough you know, and it is fit,<br>What being more known grows worse, to smother it.<br>All love the womb that their first being bred;<br>Then give my tongue like leave to love my head. |
| ANTIOCHUS | (aside)<br>Heav’n, that I had thy head! He’s found the meaning.<br>But I will gloze with him.—Young Prince of Tyre,<br>Though by the tenor of our strict edict,<br>Your exposition misinterpreting,<br>We might proceed to cancel of your days,<br>Yet hope, succeeding from so fair a tree<br>As your fair self, doth tune us otherwise.<br>Forty days longer we do respite you,<br>If by which time our secret be undone,<br>This mercy shows we’ll joy in such a son.<br>And until then your entertain shall be<br>As doth befit your worth and our degree. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish.Exeunt all but Pericles |
| PERICLES | How courtesy would seem to cover sin<br>When what is done is like an hypocrite,<br>The which is good in nothing but in sight.<br>If it be true that I interpret false,<br>Then were it certain you were not so bad<br>As with foul incest to abuse your soul,<br>Where now you’re both a father and a son<br>By your uncomely claspings with your child—<br>Which pleasures fits a husband, not a father—<br>And she, an eater of her mother’s flesh,<br>By the defiling of her parents’ bed,<br>And both like serpents are, who though they feed<br>On sweetest flowers, yet they poison breed.<br>Antioch, farewell, for wisdom sees those men<br>Blush not in actions blacker than the night<br>Will ’schew no course to keep them from the light.<br>One sin, I know, another doth provoke.<br>Murder’s as near to lust as flame to smoke.<br>Poison and treason are the hands of sin,<br>Ay, and the targets to put off the shame.<br>Then, lest my life be cropped to keep you clear,<br>By flight I’ll shun the danger which I fear. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Antiochus |
| ANTIOCHUS | He hath found the meaning, for the which we mean<br>To have his head. He must not live<br>To trumpet forth my infamy, nor tell the world<br>Antiochus doth sin in such a loathèd manner,<br>And therefore instantly this prince must die,<br>For by his fall my honour must keep high.<br>Who attends us there? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Thaliart |
| THALIART | Doth your highness call? |
| ANTIOCHUS | Thaliart, you are of our chamber, Thaliart,<br>And to your secrecy our mind partakes<br>Her private actions. For your faithfulness<br>We will advance you, Thaliart. Behold,<br>Here’s poison, and here’s gold.<br>We hate the Prince of Tyre, and thou must kill him.<br>It fits thee not to ask the reason. Why?<br>Because we bid it. Say, is it done? |
| THALIART | My lord, ’tis done. |
| ANTIOCHUS | Enough. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger hastily |
| ANTIOCHUS | Let your breath cool yourself, telling your haste. |
| *(didascalia)* | Your majesty, Prince Pericles is fled. Exit |
| ANTIOCHUS | (to Thaliart)<br>As thou wilt live, fly after; like an arrow<br>Shot from a well-experienced archer hits<br>The mark his eye doth level at, so thou<br>Never return unless it be to say<br>‘Your majesty, Prince Pericles is dead.’ |
| THALIART | If I can get him in my pistol’s length<br>I’ll make him sure enough. Farewell, your highness. |
| ANTIOCHUS | Thaliart, adieu. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Thaliart |
| ANTIOCHUS | Till Pericles be dead<br>My heart can lend no succour to my head. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit. The heads are concealed |
