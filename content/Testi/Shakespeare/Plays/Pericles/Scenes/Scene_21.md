| SAILOR OF TYRE | (to Sailor of Mytilene)<br>Lord Helicanus can resolve you, sir.<br>(To Helicanus) There is a barge put off from Mytilene.<br>In it, Lysimachus, the governor,<br>Who craves to come aboard. What is your will? |
| HELICANUS | That he have his. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Sailor of Mytilene at first door Call up some gentlemen. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Helicanus above |
| *(didascalia)* | SAILOR OF TYRE |
| HELICANUS | Ho, my lord calls! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter from below the stage two or three Gentlemen; to them, enter Helicanus |
| FIRST GENTLEMAN | What is your lordship’s pleasure? |
| HELICANUS | Gentlemen, some of worth would come aboard.<br>I pray you, greet him fairly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lysimachus at first door, with the Sailor and Lords of Mytilene |
| *(didascalia)* | SAILOR OF MYTILENE (to Lysimachus) |
| HELICANUS | This is the man that can in aught resolve you. |
| LYSIMACHUS | (to Helicanus)<br>Hail, reverend sir; the gods preserve you! |
| HELICANUS | And you, sir, to outlive the age I am,<br>And die as I would do. |
| LYSIMACHUS | You wish me well.<br>I am the governor of Mytilene;<br>Being on shore, honouring of Neptune’s triumphs,<br>Seeing this goodly vessel ride before us,<br>I made to it to know of whence you are. |
| HELICANUS | Our vessel is of Tyre, in it our king,<br>A man who for this three months hath not spoken<br>To anyone, nor taken sustenance<br>But to prorogue his grief. |
| LYSIMACHUS | Upon what ground grew his distemp’rature? |
| HELICANUS | ’Twould be too tedious to tell it over,<br>But the main grief springs from the precious loss<br>Of a belovèd daughter and a wife. |
| LYSIMACHUS | May we not see him? |
| HELICANUS | See him, sir, you may,<br>But bootless is your sight. He will not speak<br>To any. |
| LYSIMACHUS | Let me yet obtain my wish. |
| HELICANUS | Behold him.<br>Helicanus draws a curtain, revealing Pericles lying upon a couch with a long overgrown beard, diffused hair, undecent nails on his fingers, and attired in sack-cloth<br>This was a goodly person<br>Till the disaster of one mortal night<br>Drove him to this. |
| LYSIMACHUS | (to Pericles)<br>Sir, King, all hail. Hail, royal sir. |
| *(didascalia)* | Pericles shrinks himself down upon his pillow |
| HELICANUS | It is in vain. He will not speak to you. |
| LORD OF MYTILENE | Sir, we have a maid in Mytilene I durst wager<br>Would win some words of him. |
| LYSIMACHUS | ’Tis well bethought.<br>She questionless, with her sweet harmony<br>And other choice attractions, would alarum<br>And make a batt‘ry through his deafened ports,<br>Which now are midway stopped. She in all happy,<br>As the fair’st of all, among her fellow maids<br>Dwells now i’th’ leafy shelter that abuts<br>Against the island’s side. Go fetch her hither. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Lord |
| HELICANUS | Sure, all effectless; yet nothing we’ll omit<br>That bears recov’ry’s name. But since your kindness<br>We have stretched thus far, let us beseech you<br>That for our gold we may provision have,<br>Wherein we are not destitute for want,<br>But weary for the staleness. |
| LYSIMACHUS | O sir, a courtesy<br>Which if we should deny, the most just gods<br>For every graft would send a caterpillar,<br>And so inflict our province. Yet once more<br>Let me entreat to know at large the cause<br>Of your king’s sorrow. |
| HELICANUS | Sit, sir. I will recount it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord with Marina and another maid |
| HELICANUS | But see, I am prevented. |
| LYSIMACHUS | O, here’s the lady that I sent for.—<br>Welcome, fair one.—Is’t not a goodly presence? HELICANUS She’s a gallant lady. |
| LYSIMACHUS | She’s such a one that, were I well assured<br>Came of gentle kind or noble stock, I’d wish<br>No better choice to think me rarely wed.—<br>Fair one, all goodness that consists in bounty<br>Expect e’en here, where is a kingly patient;<br>If that thy prosperous and artificial feat<br>Can draw him but to answer thee in aught,<br>Thy sacred physic shall receive such pay<br>As thy desires can wish. |
| MARINA | Sir, I will use<br>My utmost skill in his recure, provided<br>That none but I and my companion maid<br>Be suffered to come near him. |
| LYSIMACHUS | (to the others) Let us leave her, |
| *(didascalia)* | And the gods prosper her. The men stand aside |
| LYSIMACHUS | The Song |
| *(didascalia)* | LYSIMACHUS coming forward Marked he your music? MAID |
| LYSIMACHUS | No, nor looked on us. |
| LYSIMACHUS | (to the others) See, she will speak to him. |
| MARINA | (to Pericles)<br>Hail, sir; my lord, lend ear. |
| PERICLES | Hmh, ha! |
| *(didascalia)* | He roughly repulses her |
| MARINA | I am a maid,<br>My lord, that ne‘er before invited eyes,<br>But have been gazed on like a comet. She speaks,<br>My lord, that maybe hath endured a grief<br>Might equal yours, if both were justly weighed.<br>Though wayward fortune did malign my state,<br>My derivation was from ancestors<br>Who stood equivalent with mighty kings,<br>But time hath rooted out my parentage,<br>And to the world and awkward casualties<br>Bound me in servitude. (Aside) I will desist.<br>But there is something glows upon my cheek,<br>And whispers in mine ear ‘Stay till he speak.’ |
| PERICLES | My fortunes, parentage, good parentage,<br>To equal mine? Was it not thus? What say you? |
| MARINA | I said if you did know my parentage,<br>My lord, you would not do me violence. |
| PERICLES | I do think so. Pray you, turn your eyes upon me.<br>You’re like something that—what countrywoman?<br>Here of these shores? |
| MARINA | No, nor of any shores,<br>Yet I was mortally brought forth, and am<br>No other than I seem. |
| *(didascalia)* | PERICLES aside |
| MARINA | I am great with woe, and shall deliver weeping.<br>My dearest wife was like this maid, and such<br>My daughter might have been. My queen’s square<br>brows,<br>Her stature to an inch, as wand-like straight,<br>As silver-voiced, her eyes as jewel-like,<br>And cased as richly, in pace another Juno,<br>Who starves the ears she feeds, and makes them<br>hungry<br>The more she gives them speech.—Where do you live? |
| MARINA | Where I am but a stranger. From the deck<br>You may discern the place. |
| PERICLES | Where were you bred, And how achieved you these endowments which You make more rich to owe? |
| MARINA | If I should tell My history, it would seem like lies Disdained in the reporting. |
| PERICLES | Prithee speak.<br>Falseness cannot come from thee, for thou look‘st<br>Modest as justice, and thou seem’st a palace<br>For the crowned truth to dwell in. I will believe thee,<br>And make my senses credit thy relation<br>To points that seem impossible. Thou show‘st<br>Like one I loved indeed. What were thy friends?<br>Didst thou not say, when I did push thee back—<br>Which was when I perceived thee—that thou cam’st<br>From good descending? |
| MARINA | So indeed I did. |
| PERICLES | Report thy parentage. I think thou said‘st<br>Thou hadst been tossed from wrong to injury,<br>And that thou thought’st thy griefs might equal mine,<br>If both were opened. |
| MARINA | Some such thing I said,<br>And said no more but what my circumstance<br>Did warrant me was likely. |
| PERICLES | Tell thy story.<br>If thine considered prove the thousandth part<br>Of my endurance, thou art a man, and I<br>Have suffered like a girl. Yet thou dost look<br>Like patience gazing on kings’ graves, and smiling<br>Extremity out of act. What were thy friends?<br>How lost thou them? Thy name, my most kind virgin?<br>Recount, I do beseech thee. Come, sit by me.<br>She sits |
| MARINA | My name, sir, is Marina. |
| PERICLES | O, I am mocked,<br>And thou by some incensed god sent hither<br>To make the world to laugh at me. |
| MARINA | Patience, good sir,<br>Or here I’ll cease. |
| PERICLES | Nay, I’ll be patient.<br>Thou little know’st how thou dost startle me<br>To call thyself Marina. |
| MARINA | The name<br>Was given me by one that had some power:<br>My father, and a king. |
| PERICLES | How, a king’s daughter, And called Marina? |
| MARINA | You said you would believe me, But not to be a troubler of your peace I will end here. |
| PERICLES | But are you flesh and blood?<br>Have you a working pulse and are no fairy?<br>Motion as well? Speak on. Where were you born,<br>And wherefore called Marina? |
| MARINA | Called Marina<br>For I was born at sea. |
| PERICLES | At sea? What mother? |
| MARINA | My mother was the daughter of a king,<br>Who died when I was born, as my good nurse<br>Lychorida hath oft recounted weeping. |
| *(didascalia)* | O, stop there a little! Aside This is the rarest dream |
| PERICLES | That e’er dulled sleep did mock sad fools withal.<br>This cannot be my daughter, buried. Well.<br>(To Marina) Where were you bred? I’ll hear you more<br>to th’ bottom<br>Of your story, and never interrupt you. |
| MARINA | You will scarce believe me. ‘Twere best I did give o’er. |
| PERICLES | I will believe you by the syllable<br>Of what you shall deliver. Yet give me leave.<br>How came you in these parts? Where were you bred? |
| MARINA | The King my father did in Tarsus leave me,<br>Till cruel Cleon, with his wicked wife,<br>Did seek to murder me, and wooed a villain<br>To attempt the deed; who having drawn to do’t,<br>A crew of pirates came and rescued me.<br>To Mytilene they brought me. But, good sir,<br>What will you of me? Why do you weep? It may be<br>You think me an impostor. No, good faith,<br>I am the daughter to King Pericles,<br>If good King Pericles be. |
| PERICLES | rising Ho, Helicanus! |
| HELICANUS | (coming forward) Calls my lord? |
| PERICLES | Thou art a grave and noble counsellor,<br>Most wise in gen’ral. Tell me if thou canst<br>What this maid is, or what is like to be,<br>That thus hath made me weep. |
| HELICANUS | I know not.<br>But here’s the regent, sir, of Mytilene<br>Speaks nobly of her. |
| LYSIMACHUS | She would never tell<br>Her parentage. Being demanded that,<br>She would sit still and weep. |
| PERICLES | O Helicanus, strike me, honoured sir,<br>Give me a gash, put me to present pain,<br>Lest this great sea of joys rushing upon me<br>O‘erbear the shores of my mortality<br>And drown me with their sweetness! (To Marina) O,<br>come hither, |
| *(didascalia)* | Marina stands |
| PERICLES | Thou that begett’st him that did thee beget,<br>Thou that wast born at sea, buried at Tarsus,<br>And found at sea again!—O Helicanus,<br>Down on thy knees, thank the holy gods as loud<br>As thunder threatens us, this is Marina!<br>(To Marina) What was thy mother’s name? Tell me<br>but that,<br>For truth can never be confirmed enough,<br>Though doubts did ever sleep. |
| MARINA | First, sir, I pray, What is your title? |
| PERICLES | I am Pericles<br>Of Tyre. But tell me now my drowned queen’s name.<br>As in the rest thou hast been godlike perfect,<br>So prove but true in that, thou art my daughter,<br>The heir of kingdoms, and another life<br>To Pericles thy father. |
| *(didascalia)* | MARINA kneeling Is it no more |
| PERICLES | To be your daughter than to say my mother’s name?<br>Thaisa was my mother, who did end<br>The minute I began. |
| PERICLES | Now blessing on thee! Rise. Thou art my child. |
| *(didascalia)* | Marina stands. He kisses her |
| *(didascalia)* | To attendants Give me fresh garments.—Mine own, |
| PERICLES | Helicanus!<br>Not dead at Tarsus, as she should have been<br>By savage Cleon. She shall tell thee all,<br>When thou shalt kneel and justify in knowledge<br>She is thy very princess. Who is this? |
| HELICANUS | Sir, ’tis the governor of Mytilene,<br>Who, hearing of your melancholy state,<br>Did come to see you. |
| PERICLES | (to Lysimachus) I embrace you, sir.—<br>Give me my robes. |
| *(didascalia)* | He is attired in fresh robes |
| PERICLES | I am wild in my beholding.<br>O heavens, bless my girl! |
| *(didascalia)* | Celestial music |
| PERICLES | But hark, what music?<br>Tell Helicanus, my Marina, tell him<br>O’er point by point, for yet he seems to doubt,<br>How sure you are my daughter. But what music? |
| HELICANUS | My lord, I hear none. |
| PERICLES | None? The music of the spheres! List, my Marina. |
| LYSIMACHUS | (aside to the others)<br>It is not good to cross him. Give him way. |
| PERICLES | Rar’st sounds. Do ye not hear? |
| LYSIMACHUS | Music, my lord? |
| PERICLES | I hear most heav’nly music.<br>It raps me unto list’ning, and thick slumber<br>Hangs upon mine eyelids. Let me rest.<br>He sleeps |
| LYSIMACHUS | A pillow for his head. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Marina and others Companion friends, |
| LYSIMACHUS | If this but answer to my just belief<br>I’ll well remember you. So leave him all. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Pericles |
| *(didascalia)* | Diana descends from the heavens |
| DIANA | My temple stands in Ephesus. Hie thee thither,<br>And do upon mine altar sacrifice.<br>There when my maiden priests are met together,<br>At large discourse thy fortunes in this wise:<br>With a full voice before the people all,<br>Reveal how thou at sea didst lose thy wife.<br>To mourn thy crosses, with thy daughter‘s, call<br>And give them repetition to the life.<br>Perform my bidding, or thou liv’st in woe;<br>Do’t, and rest happy, by my silver bow.<br>Awake, and tell thy dream. |
| *(didascalia)* | Diana ascends into the heavens |
| PERICLES | Celestial Dian, goddess argentine,<br>I will obey thee. (Calling) Helicanus! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Helicanus, Lysimachus, and Marina |
| HELICANUS | Sir? |
| PERICLES | My purpose was for Tarsus, there to strike<br>Th’inhospitable Cleon, but I am<br>For other service first. Toward Ephesus<br>Turn our blown sails. Eftsoons I’ll tell thee why. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Helicanus |
| PERICLES | Shall we refresh us, sir, upon your shore,<br>And give you gold for such provision<br>As our intents will need? |
| LYSIMACHUS | With all my heart, sir,<br>And when you come ashore I have a suit. |
| PERICLES | You shall prevail, were it to woo my daughter,<br>For it seems you have been noble towards her. |
| LYSIMACHUS | Sir, lend me your arm. |
| PERICLES | Come, my Marina. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Pericles with Lysimachus at one arm, Marina at the other |
