| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Cleon]], the Governor of Tarsus, with [[Dionyza]] his wife, and others |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | My [[Dionyza]], shall we rest us here<br>And, by relating tales of others’ griefs,<br>See if ’twill teach us to forget our own? |
| [[Dionyza|DIONYZA]] | That were to blow at [[Fire|fire]] in [[Hope|hope]] to quench it,<br>For who digs hills because they do aspire<br>Throws down one mountain to cast up a higher.<br>O my distressed lord, e’en such our griefs are;<br>Here they’re but felt and seen with midges’ eyes,<br>But like to groves, being topped they higher rise. |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | O [[Dionyza]],<br>Who wanteth food and will not say he wants it,<br>Or can conceal his hunger till he famish?<br>Our tongues our sorrows dictate to sound deep<br>Our woes into the air, our eyes to weep<br>Till lungs fetch breath that may proclaim them louder,<br>That, if heav’n slumber while their creatures want,<br>They may awake their helps to comfort them.<br>I’ll then discourse our woes, felt sev’ral years,<br>And, wanting breath to speak, help me with [[Tears|tears]]. |
| [[Dionyza|DIONYZA]] | As you think best, sir. |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | This Tarsus o‘er which I have the [[government]],<br>A [[City|city]] o’er whom plenty held full hand,<br>For riches strewed herself ev’n in the streets,<br>Whose tow‘rs bore heads so high they kissed the clouds,<br>And strangers ne’er beheld but wondered at,<br>Whose men and dames so jetted and adorned<br>Like one another’s glass to trim them by;<br>Their tables were stored full to glad the sight,<br>And not so much to feed on as delight.<br>All [[Poverty|poverty]] was scorned, and [[Pride|pride]] so great<br>The name of help grew odious to repeat. |
| [[Dionyza|DIONYZA]] | O, ’tis too true. |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | But see what heav’n can do by this our change.<br>Those mouths who but of late earth, sea, and air<br>Were all too little to content and please,<br>Although they gave their creatures in abundance,<br>As houses are defiled for want of use,<br>They are now starved for want of exercise.<br>Those palates who, not yet two summers younger,<br>Must have inventions to delight the taste<br>Would now be glad of bread and beg for it.<br>Those mothers who to nuzzle up their babes<br>Thought naught too curious are ready now<br>To eat those little darlings whom they loved.<br>So sharp are hunger’s teeth that man and wife<br>Draw lots who first shall die to lengthen life.<br>Here weeping stands a lord, there lies a lady dying,<br>Here many sink, yet those which see them fall<br>Have scarce strength left to give them burial.<br>Is not this true? |
| [[Dionyza|DIONYZA]] | Our cheeks and hollow eyes do witness it. |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | O, let those cities that of plenty’s cup<br>And her prosperities so largely taste<br>With their superfluous riots, heed these [[Tears|tears]]!<br>The misery of Tarsus may be theirs. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a faintingLord of Tarsus slowly |
| LORD | Where’s the Lord Governor? |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | Here. Speak out thy sorrows which thou bring‘st in<br>haste,<br>For comfort is too far for us t’expect. |
| LORD | We have descried upon our neighbouring shore<br>A portly sail of ships make hitherward. |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | I thought as much.<br>One sorrow never comes but brings an heir<br>That may succeed as his inheritor,<br>And so in ours. Some neighbour nation,<br>Taking advantage of our misery,<br>Hath stuffed these hollow vessels with their power<br>To beat us down, the which are down already,<br>And make a conquest of unhappy men,<br>Whereas no glory’s got to overcome. |
| LORD | That’s the least [[Fear|fear]], for by the semblance<br>Of their white flags displayed they bring us peace,<br>And come to us as favourers, not foes. |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | Thou speak‘st like him’s untutored to repeat;<br>Who makes the fairest show means most deceit.<br>But bring they what they will and what they can,<br>What need we [[Fear|fear]]?<br>Our [[Grave|grave]]’s the low’st, and we are half-way there.<br>Go tell their gen’ral we attend him here<br>To know for what he comes, and whence he comes. |
| LORD | I go, my lord. Exit |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | Welcome is peace, if he on peace consist;<br>If wars, we are unable to resist. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Lord again conducting [[Pericles]] with attendants |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | (to [[Cleon]])<br>Lord Governor, for so we hear you are,<br>Let not our ships and number of our men<br>Be like a beacon fixed t’amaze your eyes.<br>We have heard your miseries as far as Tyre,<br>Since entering your unshut gates have witnessed<br>The widowed desolation of your streets;<br>Nor come we to add sorrow to your hearts,<br>But to relieve them of their heavy load;<br>And these our ships, you happily may think<br>Are like the Trojan [[Horse|horse]] was fraught within<br>With bloody veins importing overthrow,<br>Are stored with corn to make your needy bread,<br>And give them life whom hunger starved half dead. |
| *(didascalia)* | ALL OF TARSUS fallingon their knees and weeping |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | The gods of Greece protect you, and we’ll pray for you! |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Arise, I pray you, rise.<br>We do not look for reverence but for [[Love|love]],<br>And harbourage for me, my ships and men. |
| [[Cleon|CLEON]] | The which when any shall not gratify,<br>Or pay you with unthankfulness in thought,<br>Be it our wives, our children, or ourselves,<br>The curse of heav’n and men succeed their evils!<br>Till when—the which I [[Hope|hope]] shall ne’er be seen—<br>Your grace is welcome to our town and us. |
| [[Pericles|PERICLES]] | Which welcome we’ll accept, feast here a while,<br>Until our [[Stars|stars]] that frown lend us a smile. Exeunt |
