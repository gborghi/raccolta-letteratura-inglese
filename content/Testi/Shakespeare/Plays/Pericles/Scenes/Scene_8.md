| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Helicanus and Aeschines |
| HELICANUS | No, Aeschines, know this of me:<br>Antiochus from incest lived not free,<br>For which the most high gods, not minding longer<br>To hold the vengeance that they had in store<br>Due to this heinous capital offence,<br>Even in the height and pride of all his glory,<br>When he was seated in a chariot<br>Of an inestimable value, and<br>His daughter with him, both apparelled all in jewels,<br>A fire from heaven came and shrivelled up<br>Their bodies e’en to loathing, for they so stunk<br>That all those eyes adored them ere their fall<br>Scorn now their hands should give them burial. |
| AESCHINES | ’Twas very strange. |
| HELICANUS | And yet but justice, for though<br>This king were great, his greatness was no guard To bar heav’n’s shaft, but sin had his reward. |
| AESCHINES | ’Tis very true. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter three Lords, and stand aside |
| FIRST LORD | See, not a man in private conference<br>Or council has respect with him but he. |
| SECOND LORD | It shall no longer grieve without reproof. |
| THIRD LORD | And cursed be he that will not second it. |
| FIRST LORD | Follow me, then.—Lord Helicane, a word. |
| HELICANUS | With me? And welcome. Happy day, my lords. |
| FIRST LORD | Know that our griefs are risen to the top,<br>And now at length they overflow their banks. |
| HELICANUS | Your griefs? For what? Wrong not your prince you love. |
| FIRST LORD | Wrong not yourself, then, noble Helicane,<br>But if the prince do live, let us salute him<br>Or know what ground’s made happy by his step,<br>And be resolved he lives to govern us,<br>Or dead, give ’s cause to mourn his funeral<br>And leave us to our free election. |
| SECOND LORD | Whose death indeed’s the strongest in our censure,<br>And knowing this—kingdoms without a head,<br>Like goodly buildings left without a roof,<br>Soon fall to utter ruin—your noble self,<br>That best know how to rule and how to reign,<br>We thus submit unto as sovereign. |
| *(didascalia)* | ALL kneeling Live, noble Helicane! |
| HELICANUS | By honour’s cause, forbear your suffrages.<br>If that you love Prince Pericles, forbear. |
| *(didascalia)* | The lords rise |
| HELICANUS | Take I your wish I leap into the seas<br>Where’s hourly trouble for a minute’s ease,<br>But if I cannot win you to this love,<br>A twelvemonth longer then let me entreat you<br>Further to bear the absence of your king;<br>If in which time expired he not return,<br>I shall with aged patience bear your yoke.<br>Go, seek your noble prince like noble subjects,<br>And in your search spend your adventurous worth,<br>Whom if you find and win unto return,<br>You shall like diamonds sit about his crown. |
| FIRST LORD | To wisdom he’s a fool that will not yield,<br>And since Lord Helicane enjoineth us,<br>We with our travels will endeavour us.<br>If in the world he live we’ll seek him out;<br>If in his grave he rest, we’ll find him there. |
| HELICANUS | Then you love us, we you, and we’ll clasp hands.<br>When peers thus knit, a kingdom ever stands. Exeunt<br><br>Sc. 8a |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Pericles with Gentlemen with lights |
| FIRST GENTLEMAN | Here is your lodging, sir. |
| PERICLES | Pray leave me private.<br>Only for instant solace pleasure me<br>With some delightful instrument, with which,<br>And with my former practice, I intend<br>To pass away the tediousness of night, 5<br>Though slumbers were more fitting. |
| FIRST GENTLEMAN | Presently. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit First Gentleman |
| SECOND GENTLEMAN | Your will’s obeyed in all things, for our master<br>Commanded you be disobeyed in nothing. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter First Gentleman with a stringed instrument |
| PERICLES | I thank you. Now betake you to your pillows,<br>And to the nourishment of quiet sleep. 10 |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Gentlemen |
| PERICLES | Pericles plays and sings<br>Day—that hath still that sovereignty to draw back<br>The empire of the night, though for a while<br>In darkness she usurp—brings morning on.<br>I will go give his grace that salutation<br>Morning requires of me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with instrument |
