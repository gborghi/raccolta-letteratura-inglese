| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] Charles the Sixth of France, the [[Dauphin]], the Constable, the Duke of Bourbon, and others |
| [[King|KING]] CHARLES | ‘Tis certain he hath passed the [[River]] Somme. |
| CONSTABLE | And if he be not fought withal, my lord,<br>Let us not live in France; let us quit all<br>And give our vineyards to a barbarous people. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | O Dieu vivant! Shall a few sprays of us,<br>The emptying of our fathers’ luxury,<br>Our scions, put in wild and savage stock,<br>Spirt up so suddenly into the clouds<br>And over-look their grafters? |
| *(didascalia)* | BOURBON |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Normans, but bastard Normans, Norman bastards!<br>Mort de ma vie, if they march along<br>Unfought withal, but I will sell my dukedom<br>To buy a slobb’ry and a dirty farm<br>In that nook-shotten isle of Albion. |
| CONSTABLE | Dieu de batailles! Where have they this mettle?<br>Is not their climate foggy, raw, and dull,<br>On whom as in despite the sun looks pale,<br>Killing their fruit with frowns? Can sodden [[Water|water]],<br>A drench for sur-reined jades—their barley-broth—<br>Decoct their cold [[Blood|blood]] to such valiant heat?<br>And shall our quick [[Blood|blood]], spirited with wine,<br>Seem frosty? O for [[Honour|honour]] of our land<br>Let us not hang like roping icicles<br>Upon our houses’ thatch, whiles a more frosty people<br>Sweat drops of gallant youth in our rich fields—<br>‘Poor’ may we call them, in their native lords. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | By [[Faith|faith]] and [[Honour|honour]],<br>Our madams mock at us and plainly say<br>Our mettle is bred out, and they will give<br>Their bodies to the lust of English youth,<br>To new-store France with bastard warriors. |
| *(didascalia)* | BOURBON |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | They bid us, ‘To the English dancing-schools,<br>And teach lavoltas high and swift corantos’—<br>Saying our grace is only in our heels,<br>And that we are most lofty runaways. |
| [[King|KING]] CHARLES | Where is Montjoy the herald? [[Speed]] him hence.<br>Let him greet England with our sharp defiance.<br>Up, princes, and with spirit of [[Honour|honour]] edged<br>More sharper than your swords, hie to the field.<br>Charles Delabret, High Constable of France,<br>You Dukes of Orléans, Bourbon, and of Berri,<br>Alençon, Brabant, Bar, and Burgundy,<br>[[Jaques]] Châtillion, Rambures, Vaudemont,<br>Beaumont, Grandpré, Roussi, and Fauconbridge,<br>Foix, Lestrelles, Boucicault, and Charolais,<br>High dukes, great princes, barons, lords, and knights,<br>For your great seats now quit you of great shames.<br>Bar Harry England, that sweeps through our land<br>With pennons painted in the [[Blood|blood]] of Harfleur;<br>Rush on his host, as doth the melted [[Snow|snow]]<br>Upon the valleys, whose low vassal seat<br>The Alps doth spit and void his rheum upon.<br>Go down upon him, you have power enough,<br>And in a captive chariot into Rouen<br>Bring him our prisoner. |
| CONSTABLE | This becomes the great.<br>Sorry am I his numbers are so few,<br>His soldiers sick and famished in their march,<br>For I am sure when he shall see our army<br>He’ll drop his heart into the sink of [[Fear|fear]]<br>And, fore achievement, offer us his ransom. |
| [[King|KING]] CHARLES | Therefore, Lord Constable, haste on Montjoy,<br>And let him say to England that we send<br>To know what willing ransom he will give.—<br>Prince [[Dauphin]], you shall stay with us in Rouen. |
| [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Not so, I do beseech your majesty. |
| [[King|KING]] CHARLES | Be patient, for you shall remain with us.—<br>Now forth, Lord Constable, and princes all,<br>And quickly bring us word of England’s fall. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
