| *(didascalia)* | Enter Captain [[Gower]] and Captain [[Fluellen]], with a leek in his cap and a cudgel |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Nay, that’s right. But why wear you your leek today? Saint Davy’s day is past. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | There is occasions and causes why and wherefore in all things. I will tell you, ass my friend, Captain [[Gower]]. The rascally scald beggarly lousy pragging knave Pistot—which you and yourself and all the world know to be no petter than a fellow, look you now, of no merits—he is come to me, and prings me pread and salt yesterday, look you, and bid me eat my leek. It was in a place where I could not breed no contention with him, but I will be so bold as to wear it in my cap till I see him once again, and then I will tell him a little piece of my desires. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Ensign [[Pistol]] |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Why, here a comes, swelling like a turkey-cock. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | ‘Tis no matter for his swellings nor his turkey-cocks. —God pless you Ensign [[Pistol]], you scurvy lousy knave, God pless you. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Ha, art thou bedlam? Dost thou thirst, base Trojan,<br>To have me fold up Parca’s fatal web?<br>Hence! I am qualmish at the smell of leek. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | I peseech you heartily, scurvy lousy knave, at my desires and my requests and my petitions, to eat, look you, this leek. Because, look you, you do not [[Love|love]] it, nor your affections and your appetites and your digestions does not agree with it, I would [[Desire|desire]] you to eat it. 26 |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Not for Cadwallader and all his goats. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | There is one goat for you. (He strikes [[Pistol]]) Will you be so good, scald knave, as eat it? |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Base Trojan, thou shalt die. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | You say very true, scald knave, when God’s will is. I will [[Desire|desire]] you to live in the mean [[Time|time]], and eat your victuals. Come, there is sauce for it. (He strikes him) You called me yesterday ‘mountain-squire’, but I will make you today a ‘squire of low degree’. I pray you, fall to. If you can mock a leek you can eat a leek. |
| *(didascalia)* | He strikes him |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Enough, captain, you have astonished him. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | By Jesu, I will make him eat some part of my leek, or I will peat his pate four days and four nights.—Bite, I pray you. It is good for your green wound and your ploody coxcomb. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Must I bite? |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Yes, certainly, and out of [[Doubt|doubt]] and out of question too, and ambiguities. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | By this leek, I will most horribly [[Revenge|revenge]]— |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Fluellen]] threatens him I eat and eat—I swear— |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Eat, I pray you. Will you have some more sauce to your leek? There is not enough leek to swear by. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Quiet thy cudgel, thou dost see I eat. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Much good do you, scald knave, heartily. Nay, pray you throw none away. The skin is good for your broken coxcomb. When you take occasions to see leeks hereafter, I pray you mock at ’em, that is all. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Good. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Ay, leeks is good. Hold you, there is a groat to heal your pate. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Me, a groat? |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Yes, verity, and in [[Truth|truth]] you shall take it, or I have another leek in my pocket which you shall eat. |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | I take thy groat in earnest of [[Revenge|revenge]]. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | If I owe you anything, I will pay you in cudgels. You shall be a woodmonger, and buy nothing of me but cudgels. God b’wi’ you, and keep you, and heal your pate. Exit |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | All hell shall stir for this. |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Go, go, you are a counterfeit cowardly knave. Will you mock at an ancient tradition, begun upon an honourable respect and worn as a memorable trophy of predeceased valour, and dare not avouch in your deeds any of your words? I have seen you gleeking and galling at this gentleman twice or thrice. You thought, because he could not speak English in the native garb, he could not therefore handle an English cudgel. You find it otherwise. And henceforth let a Welsh correction teach you a good English condition. Fare ye [[Well|well]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Pistol|PISTOL]] | Doth Fortune play the hussy with me now?<br>News have I that my Nell is dead<br>I’th’ spital of a malady of France,<br>And there my rendezvous is quite cut off.<br>Old I do wax, and from my weary limbs<br>[[Honour]] is cudgelled. [[Well]], bawd I’ll turn,<br>And something lean to cutpurse of quick hand.<br>To England will I steal, and there I’ll steal,<br>And patches will I get unto these cudgelled scars,<br>And swear I got them in the Gallia wars. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
