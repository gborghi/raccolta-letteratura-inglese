| *(didascalia)* | Enter at one door [[King]] Harry, the Dukes of Exeter and [[Clarence]], the [[Earl of Warwick]], and other lords; at another, [[King]] Charles the Sixth of France, [[Queen Isabel]], the Duke of Burgundy, and other French, among them [[Princess Catherine]] and Alice |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Peace to this meeting, wherefor we are met. Unto our brother France and to our sister, Health and fair [[Time|time]] of day. joy and good wishes To our most fair and princely cousin Catherine; And as a branch and member of this royalty, By whom this great assembly is contrived, We do salute you, Duke of Burgundy. And princes French, and peers, health to you all. |
| [[King|KING]] CHARLES | Right joyous are we to behold your face. Most worthy brother England, fairly met. So are you, princes English, every one. |
| [[Queen Isabel|QUEEN ISABEL]] | So happy be the issue, brother England, Of this good day and of this gracious meeting, As we are now glad to behold your eyes— Your eyes which hitherto have borne in them, Against the French that met them in their bent, The fatal balls of murdering basilisks. The venom of such looks we fairly [[Hope|hope]] Have lost their quality, and that this day Shall change all griefs and quarrels into [[Love|love]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | To cry amen to that, thus we appear. |
| [[Queen Isabel|QUEEN ISABEL]] | You English princes all, I do salute you. |
