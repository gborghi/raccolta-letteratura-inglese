| [[Chorus|CHORUS]] | Now entertain conjecture of a [[Time|time]]<br>When creeping murmur and the poring dark<br>Fills the wide vessel of the universe.<br>From camp to camp through the foul womb of [[Night|night]]<br>The hum of either army stilly sounds,<br>That the fixed sentinels almost receive<br>The secret whispers of each other’s watch.<br>[[Fire]] answers [[Fire|fire]], and through their paly flames<br>Each battle sees the other’s umbered face.<br>Steed threatens steed, in high and boastful neighs<br>Piercing the [[Night|night]]’s dull ear, and from the tents<br>The armourers, accomplishing the knights,<br>With busy hammers closing rivets up,<br>Give dreadful note of preparation.<br>The country cocks do crow, the clocks do toll<br>And the third hour of drowsy morning name.<br>Proud of their numbers and secure in [[Soul|soul]],<br>The confident and overlusty French<br>Do the low-rated English play at dice,<br>And chide the cripple tardy-gaited [[Night|night]],<br>Who like a foul and ugly [[Witch|witch]] doth limp<br>So tediously away. The poor condemned English,<br>Like sacrifices, by their watchful fires<br>Sit patiently and inly ruminate<br>The morning’s danger; and their gesture sad,<br>Investing lank lean cheeks and war-worn coats,<br>Presented them unto the gazing [[Moon|moon]]<br>So many horrid [[Ghosts|ghosts]]. O now, who will behold<br>The royal captain of this ruined band<br>Walking from watch to watch, from tent to tent,<br>Let him cry, ‘Praise and glory on his head!’<br>For forth he goes and visits all his host,<br>Bids them good morrow with a modest smile<br>And calls them brothers, friends, and countrymen.<br>Upon his royal face there is no note<br>How dread an army hath enrounded him;<br>Nor doth he dedicate one jot of colour<br>Unto the weary and all-watchèd [[Night|night]],<br>But freshly looks and overbears attaint<br>With cheerful semblance and sweet majesty,<br>That every wretch, pining and pale before,<br>Beholding him, plucks comfort from his looks.<br>A largess universal, like the sun,<br>His liberal eye doth give to everyone,<br>Thawing cold [[Fear|fear]], that mean and gentle all<br>Behold, as may unworthiness define,<br>A little touch of Harry in the [[Night|night]].<br>And so our scene must to the battle fly,<br>Where O for pity, we shall much disgrace,<br>With four or five most vile and ragged foils,<br>Right ill-disposed in brawl ridiculous,<br>The name of Agincourt. Yet sit and see,<br>Minding true things by what their mock’ries be. Exit |
