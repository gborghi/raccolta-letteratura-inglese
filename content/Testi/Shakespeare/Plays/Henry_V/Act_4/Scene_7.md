| *(didascalia)* | Enter Captains [[Fluellen]] and [[Gower]] |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Kill the poys and the luggage! ‘Tis expressly against the law of arms. ’Tis as arrant a piece of knavery, mark you now, as can be offert. In your [[Conscience|conscience]] now, is it not? |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | ‘Tis certain there’s not a boy left alive. And the cowardly rascals that ran from the battle ha’ done this slaughter. Besides, they have burned and carried away all that was in the [[King]]’s tent; wherefore the [[King]] most worthily hath caused every soldier to cut his prisoner’s throat. O ’tis a gallant [[King|king]]. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Ay, he was porn at Monmouth. Captain [[Gower]], what call you the town’s name where Alexander the Pig was born? |
| GLOWER | Alexander the Great. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Why I pray you, is not ‘pig’ great? The pig or the great or the mighty or the huge or the magnanimous are all one reckonings, save the phrase is a little variations. |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | I think Alexander the Great was born in Macedon. His father was called Philip of Macedon, as I take it. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | I think it is e‘en Macedon where Alexander is porn. I tell you, captain, if you look in the maps of the world I warrant you sail find, in the comparisons between Macedon and Monmouth, that the situations, look you, is both alike. There is a [[River|river]] in Macedon, and there is also moreover a [[River|river]] at Monmouth. It is called Wye at Monmouth, but it is out of my prains what is the name of the other [[River|river]]—but ’tis all one, ’tis alike as my fingers is to my fingers, and there is salmons in both. If you mark Alexander’s life [[Well|well]], Harry of Monmouth’s life is come after it indifferent [[Well|well]]. For there is figures in all things. Alexander, God knows, and you know, in his rages and his furies and his wraths and his cholers and his moods and his displeasures and his indignations, and also being a little intoxicates in his prains, did in his ales and his angers, look you, kill his best friend Cleitus— |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Our [[King]] is not like him in that. He never killed any of his friends. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | It is not [[Well|well]] done, mark you now, to take the tales out of my mouth ere it is made an end and finished. I speak but in the figures and comparisons of it. As Alexander killed his friend Cleitus, being in his ales and his cups, so also Harry Monmouth, being in his right wits and his good judgements, turned away the fat knight with the great-belly doublet—he was full of jests and gipes and knaveries and mocks—I have forgot his name. |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Sir John [[Falstaff]]. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | That is he. I’ll tell you, there is good men porn at Monmouth. |
| [[Gower|GOWER]] | Here comes his majesty. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter [[King]] Harry and the English army, with the Duke of Bourbon, the Duke of Orléans, and other prisoners. Flourish |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I was not angry since I came to France<br>Until this instant. Take a trumpet, herald;<br>Ride thou unto the horsemen on yon [[Hill|hill]].<br>If they will fight with us, bid them come down,<br>Or void the field: they do offend our sight.<br>If they’ll do neither, we will come to them,<br>And make them skirr away as swift as stones<br>Enforced from the old Assyrian slings.<br>Besides, we’ll cut the throats of those we have,<br>And not a man of them that we shall take<br>Shall taste our [[Mercy|mercy]]. Go and tell them so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Montjoy |
| EXETER | Here comes the herald of the French, my liege. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | His eyes are humbler than they used to be. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | How now, what means this, herald? Know‘st thou<br>not<br>That I have fined these bones of mine for ransom?<br>Com’st thou again for ransom? |
| MONTJOY | No, great [[King]].<br>I come to thee for charitable licence,<br>That we may wander o’er this bloody field<br>To book our dead and then to bury them,<br>To sort our nobles from our common men—<br>For many of our princes, woe the while,<br>Lie drowned and soaked in mercenary [[Blood|blood]].<br>So do our vulgar drench their peasant limbs<br>In [[Blood|blood]] of princes, and our wounded steeds<br>Fret fetlock-deep in gore, and with wild rage<br>Jerk out their armed heels at their dead masters,<br>Killing them twice. O give us leave, great [[King]],<br>To view the field in safety, and dispose<br>Of their dead bodies. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I tell thee truly, herald,<br>I know not if the day be ours or no,<br>For yet a many of your horsemen peer<br>And gallop o’er the field. |
| MONTJOY | The day is yours. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Praised be God, and not our strength, for it.<br>What is this [[Castle|castle]] called that stands hard by? |
| MONTJOY | They call it Agincourt. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Then call we this the field of Agincourt,<br>Fought on the day of Crispin Crispian. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Your grandfather of famous [[Memory|memory]], an’t please your majesty, and your great-uncle Edward the Plack Prince of Wales, as I have read in the chronicles, fought a most prave pattle here in France. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | They did, [[Fluellen]]. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Your majesty says very true. If your majesties is remembered of it, the Welshmen did good service in a [[Garden|garden]] where leeks did grow, wearing leeks in their Monmouth caps, which your majesty know to this hour is an honourable badge of the service. And I do believe your majesty takes no scorn to wear the leek upon Saint Tavy’s day. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | I wear it for a memorable [[Honour|honour]],<br>For I am Welsh, you know, good countryman. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | All the [[Water|water]] in Wye cannot wash your majesty’s Welsh plood out of your pody, I can tell you that. God pless it and preserve it, as long as it pleases his grace, and his majesty too. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Thanks, good my countryman. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | By Jeshu, I am your majesty’s countryman. I care not who know it, I will confess it to all the world. I need not to be ashamed of your majesty, praised be God, so long as your majesty is an honest man. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | God keep me so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Williams with a glove in his cap |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Our heralds go with him.<br>Bring me just notice of the numbers dead On both our parts. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Montjoy, [[Gower]],and an English herald |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Call yonder fellow hither. |
| EXETER | (to Williams) Soldier, you must come to the [[King]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Soldier, why wearest thou that glove in thy cap? |
| WILLIAM | An’t please your majesty, ’tis the gage of one that I should fight withal, if he be alive. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | An Englishman? |
| WILLIAMS | An’t please your majesty, a rascal, that swaggered with me last [[Night|night]]—who, if a live, and ever dare to challenge this glove, I have sworn to take him a box o’th’ ear; or if I can see my glove in his cap—which he swore, as he was a soldier, he would wear if a lived—I will strike it out soundly. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | What think you, Captain [[Fluellen]]? Is it fit this soldier keep his oath? |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | He is a craven and a [[Villain|villain]] else, an’t please your majesty, in my [[Conscience|conscience]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | It may be his enemy is a gentleman of great sort, quite from the answer of his degree. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Though he be as good a gentleman as the devil is, as [[Lucifer]] and Beelzebub himself, it is necessary, look your grace, that he keep his vow and his oath. If he be perjured, see you now, his reputation is as arrant a [[Villain|villain]] and a Jack-sauce as ever his black shoe trod upon God’s ground and his earth, in my [[Conscience|conscience]], law. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Then keep thy vow, sirrah, when thou meetest the fellow. |
| WILLIAMS | So I will, my liege, as I live. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Who serv’st thou under? |
| WILLIAM | Under Captain [[Gower]], my liege. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | [[Gower]] is a good captain, and is good knowledge and literatured in the wars. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Call him hither to me, soldier. |
| WILLIAM | I will, my liege. Exit |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | (giving him Williams’s other glove) Here, [[Fluellen]], wear thou this favour for me and stick it in thy cap. When Alençon and myself were down together, I plucked this glove from his helm. If any man challenge this, he is a friend to Alençon and an enemy to our person. If thou encounter any such, apprehend him, an thou dost me [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | Your grace does me as great honours as can be desired in the hearts of his subjects. I would fain see the man that has but two legs that shall find himself aggriefed at this glove, that is all; but I would fain see it once. An’t please God of his grace, that I would see. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Know’st thou [[Gower]]? |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | He is my dear friend, an’t please you. |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | Pray thee, go seek him and bring him to my tent. 165 |
| [[Fluellen|FLUELLEN]] | I will fetch him. Exit |
| [[King|KING]] HARRY | My lord of Warwick and my brother [[Gloucester]],<br>Follow [[Fluellen]] closely at the heels.<br>The glove which I have given him for a favour<br>May haply purchase him a box o’th’ ear.<br>It is the soldier’s. I by bargain should<br>Wear it myself. Follow, good cousin Warwick.<br>If that the soldier strike him, as I judge<br>By his blunt bearing he will keep his word,<br>Some sudden mischief may arise of it,<br>For I do know [[Fluellen]] valiant<br>And touched with choler, hot as gunpowder,<br>And quickly will return an injury.<br>Follow, and see there be no harm between them.<br>Go you with me, uncle of Exeter. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
