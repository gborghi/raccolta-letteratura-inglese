| *(didascalia)* | Enter Paulina, a Gentleman, and attendants |
| PAULINA | The keeper of the prison, call to him.<br>Let him have knowledge who I am. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Gentleman |
| PAULINA | Good lady,<br>No court in Europe is too good for thee.<br>What dost thou then in prison? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Jailer and Gentleman |
| PAULINA | Now, good sir,<br>You know me, do you not? |
| JAILER | For a worthy lady,<br>And one who much I honour. |
| PAULINA | Pray you then,<br>Conduct me to the Queen. |
| JAILER | I may not, madam. To the contrary<br>I have express commandment. |
| PAULINA | Here’s ado,<br>To lock up honesty and honour from<br>Th’access of gentle visitors. Is’t lawful, pray you,<br>To see her women? Any of them? Emilia? |
| JAILER | So please you, madam,<br>To put apart these your attendants,<br>Shall bring Emilia forth. |
| PAULINA | I pray now call her.—<br>Withdraw yourselves. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Gentleman and attendants |
| JAILER | And, madam,<br>I must be present at your conference. |
| PAULINA | Well, be’t so, prithee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Jailer |
| PAULINA | Here’s such ado, to make no stain a stain<br>As passes colouring. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Jailer and Emilia |
| PAULINA | Dear gentlewoman,<br>How fares our gracious lady? |
| EMILIA | As well as one so great and so forlorn<br>May hold together. On her frights and griefs,<br>Which never tender lady hath borne greater,<br>She is, something before her time, delivered. |
| PAULINA | A boy? |
| EMILIA | A daughter, and a goodly babe,<br>Lusty, and like to live. The Queen receives<br>Much comfort in’t; says, ‘My poor prisoner,<br>I am innocent as you.’ |
| PAULINA | I dare be sworn.<br>These dangerous, unsafe lunes i‘th’ King, beshrew<br>them!<br>He must be told on’t, and he shall. The office<br>Becomes a woman best. I’ll take’t upon me.<br>If I prove honey-mouthed, let my tongue blister,<br>And never to my red-looked anger be<br>The trumpet any more. Pray you, Emilia,<br>Commend my best obedience to the Queen.<br>If she dares trust me with her little babe<br>I’ll show’t the King, and undertake to be<br>Her advocate to th’ loud‘st. We do not know<br>How he may soften at the sight o’th’ child.<br>The silence often of pure innocence<br>Persuades when speaking fails. |
| EMILIA | Most worthy madam,<br>Your honour and your goodness is so evident<br>That your free undertaking cannot miss<br>A thriving issue. There is no lady living<br>So meet for this great errand. Please your ladyship<br>To visit the next room, I’ll presently<br>Acquaint the Queen of your most noble offer,<br>Who but today hammered of this design<br>But durst not tempt a minister of honour<br>Lest she should be denied. |
| PAULINA | Tell her, Emilia,<br>I’ll use that tongue I have. If wit flow from’t<br>As boldness from my bosom, let’t not be doubted<br>I shall do good. |
| EMILIA | Now be you blest for it!<br>I’ll to the Queen. Please you come something nearer. |
| JAILER | Madam, if’t please the Queen to send the babe<br>I know not what I shall incur to pass it,<br>Having no warrant. |
| PAULINA | You need not fear it, sir.<br>This child was prisoner to the womb, and is<br>By law and process of great nature thence<br>Freed and enfranchised, not a party to<br>The anger of the King, nor guilty of—<br>If any be—the trespass of the Queen. |
| JAILER | I do believe it. |
| PAULINA | Do not you fear. Upon mine honour,<br>I will stand twixt you and danger. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
| PAULINA | 2.3 Enter Leontes |
| LEONTES | Nor night nor day, no rest! It is but weakness<br>To bear the matter thus, mere weakness. If<br>The cause were not in being—part o‘th’ cause,<br>She, th’adultress; for the harlot King<br>Is quite beyond mine arm, out of the blank<br>And level of my brain, plot-proof; but she<br>I can hook to me. Say that she were gone,<br>Given to the fire, a moiety of my rest<br>Might come to me again. Who’s there? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servant |
| SERVANT | My lord. |
| LEONTES | How does the boy? |
| SERVANT | He took good rest tonight.<br>’Tis hoped his sickness is discharged. |
| LEONTES | To see his nobleness!<br>Conceiving the dishonour of his mother<br>He straight declined, drooped, took it deeply,<br>Fastened and fixed the shame on’t in himself;<br>Threw off his spirit, his appetite, his sleep,<br>And downright languished. Leave me solely. Go,<br>See how he fares. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Servant |
| LEONTES | Fie, fie, no thought of him.<br>The very thought of my revenges that way<br>Recoil upon me. In himself too mighty,<br>And in his parties, his alliance. Let him be<br>Until a time may serve. For present vengeance,<br>Take it on her. Camillo and Polixenes<br>Laugh at me, make their pastime at my sorrow.<br>They should not laugh if I could reach them, nor<br>Shall she, within my power. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Paulina, carrying a babe, with Antigonus, |
| LEONTES | Lords, and the Servant, trying to restrain her<br>A LORD<br>You must not enter. |
| PAULINA | Nay rather, good my lords, be second to me.<br>Fear you his tyrannous passion more, alas,<br>Than the Queen’s life?—a gracious, innocent soul,<br>More free than he is jealous. |
| ANTIGONUS | That’s enough. |
| SERVANT | Madam, he hath not slept tonight, commanded<br>None should come at him. |
| PAULINA | Not so hot, good sir.<br>I come to bring him sleep. ’Tis such as you,<br>That creep like shadows by him, and do sigh<br>At each his needless heavings, such as you<br>Nourish the cause of his awaking. I<br>Do come with words as medicinal as true,<br>Honest as either, to purge him of that humour<br>That presses him from sleep. |
| LEONTES | What noise there, ho? |
| PAULINA | No noise, my lord, but needful conference<br>About some gossips for your highness. |
| LEONTES | How?<br>Away with that audacious lady! Antigonus,<br>I charged thee that she should not come about me.<br>I knew she would. |
| ANTIGONUS | I told her so, my lord,<br>On your displeasure’s peril and on mine,<br>She should not visit you. |
| LEONTES | What, canst not rule her? |
| PAULINA | From all dishonesty he can. In this,<br>Unless he take the course that you have done—<br>Commit me for committing honour—trust it,<br>He shall not rule me. |
| ANTIGONUS | La you now, you hear.<br>When she will take the rein I let her run,<br>But she’ll not stumble. |
| PAULINA | (to Leontes) Good my liege, I come—<br>And I beseech you hear me, who professes<br>Myself your loyal servant, your physician,<br>Your most obedient counsellor; yet that dares<br>Less appear so in comforting your evils<br>Than such as most seem yours—I say, I come<br>From your good queen. |
| LEONTES | Good queen? |
| PAULINA | Good queen, my lord, good queen, I say good queen,<br>And would by combat make her good, so were I<br>A man, the worst about you. |
| LEONTES | (to Lords)<br>Force her hence. |
| PAULINA | Let him that makes but trifles of his eyes<br>First hand me. On mine own accord, I’ll off.<br>But first I’ll do my errand. The good Queen—<br>For she is good—hath brought you forth a daughter—<br>Here ’tis—commends it to your blessing.<br>She lays down the babe |
| LEONTES | Out!<br>A mankind witch! Hence with her, out o’door—<br>A most intelligencing bawd. |
| PAULINA | Not so.<br>I am as ignorant in that as you<br>In so entitling me, and no less honest<br>Than you are mad, which is enough, I’ll warrant,<br>As this world goes, to pass for honest. |
| LEONTES | (to Lords)<br>Traitors,<br>Will you not push her out?<br>(To Antigonus)<br>Give her the bastard.<br>Thou dotard, thou art woman-tired, unroosted<br>By thy Dame Partlet here. Take up the bastard,<br>Take’t up, I say. Give’t to thy crone. |
| PAULINA | (to Antigonus)<br>For ever<br>Unvenerable be thy hands if thou<br>Tak’st up the princess by that forced baseness<br>Which he has put upon’t. |
| LEONTES | He dreads his wife. |
| PAULINA | So I would you did. Then ’twere past all doubt<br>You’d call your children yours. |
| LEONTES | A nest of traitors. |
| ANTIGONUS | I am none, by this good light. |
| PAULINA | Nor I, nor any<br>But one that’s here, and that’s himself, for he<br>The sacred honour of himself, his queen’s,<br>His hopeful son’s, his babe‘s, betrays to slander,<br>Whose sting is sharper than the sword’s; and will<br>not—<br>For as the case now stands, it is a curse<br>He cannot be compelled to’t—once remove<br>The root of his opinion, which is rotten<br>As ever oak or stone was sound. |
| LEONTES | (to Lords)<br>A callat<br>Of boundless tongue, who late hath beat her husband,<br>And now baits me! This brat is none of mine.<br>It is the issue of Polixenes.<br>Hence with it, and together with the dam<br>Commit them to the fire. |
| PAULINA | It is yours,<br>And might we lay th‘old proverb to your charge,<br>So like you ’tis the worse. Behold, my lords,<br>Although the print be little, the whole matter<br>And copy of the father: eye, nose, lip,<br>The trick of’s frown, his forehead, nay, the valley,<br>The pretty dimples of his chin and cheek, his smiles,<br>The very mould and frame of hand, nail, finger.<br>And thou good goddess Nature, which hast made it<br>So like to him that got it, if thou hast<br>The ordering of the mind too, ‘mongst all colours<br>No yellow in’t, lest she suspect, as he does,<br>Her children not her husband’s. |
| LEONTES | (to Antigonus)<br>A gross hag!—<br>And lozel, thou art worthy to be hanged,<br>That wilt not stay her tongue. |
| ANTIGONUS | Hang all the husbands<br>That cannot do that feat, you’ll leave yourself<br>Hardly one subject. |
| LEONTES | Once more, take her hence. |
| PAULINA | A most unworthy and unnatural lord<br>Can do no more. |
| LEONTES | I’ll ha’ thee burnt. |
| PAULINA | I care not.<br>It is an heretic that makes the fire,<br>Not she which burns in’t. I’ll not call you tyrant;<br>But this most cruel usage of your queen—<br>Not able to produce more accusation<br>Than your own weak-hinged fancy—something<br>savours<br>Of tyranny, and will ignoble make you,<br>Yea, scandalous to the world. |
| LEONTES | (to Antigonus)<br>On your allegiance,<br>Out of the chamber with her! Were I a tyrant,<br>Where were her life? She durst not call me so<br>If she did know me one. Away with her! |
| PAULINA | I pray you do not push me, I’ll be gone.<br>Look to your babe, my lord; ‘tis yours. Jove send her<br>A better guiding spirit. What needs these hands?<br>You that are thus so tender o’er his follies<br>Will never do him good, not one of you.<br>So, so. Farewell, we are gone. Exit |
| LEONTES | (to Antigonus)<br>Thou, traitor, hast set on thy wife to this.<br>My child? Away with‘t! Even thou, that hast<br>A heart so tender o’er it, take it hence<br>And see it instantly consumed with fire.<br>Even thou, and none but thou. Take it up straight. |
| *(didascalia)* | Within this hour bring me word ‘tis done, |
| LEONTES | And by good testimony, or I’ll seize thy life,<br>With what thou else call’st thine. If thou refuse<br>And wilt encounter with my wrath, say so.<br>The bastard brains with these my proper hands<br>Shall I dash out. Go, take it to the fire;<br>For thou set’st on thy wife. |
| ANTIGONUS | I did not, sir.<br>These lords, my noble fellows, if they please<br>Can clear me in’t. |
| LORDS | We can. My royal liege,<br>He is not guilty of her coming hither. |
| LEONTES | You’re liars all.<br>A LORD<br>Beseech your highness, give us better credit.<br>We have always truly served you, and beseech<br>So to esteem of us. And on our knees we beg,<br>As recompense of our dear services<br>Past and to come, that you do change this purpose<br>Which, being so horrible, so bloody, must<br>Lead on to some foul issue. We all kneel. |
| LEONTES | I am a feather for each wind that blows.<br>Shall I live on, to see this bastard kneel<br>And call me father? Better burn it now<br>Than curse it then. But be it. Let it live.<br>It shall not neither.<br>(To Antigonus) You, sir, come you hither,<br>You that have been so tenderly officious<br>With Lady Margery your midwife there,<br>To save this bastard’s life—for ’tis a bastard,<br>So sure as this beard’s grey. What will you adventure<br>To save this brat’s life? |
| ANTIGONUS | Anything, my lord,<br>That my ability may undergo,<br>And nobleness impose. At least thus much,<br>I’ll pawn the little blood which I have left<br>To save the innocent; anything possible. |
| LEONTES | It shall be possible. Swear by this sword<br>Thou wilt perform my bidding. |
| ANTIGONUS | I will, my lord. |
| LEONTES | Mark, and perform it. Seest thou? For the fail<br>Of any point in’t shall not only be<br>Death to thyself but to thy lewd-tongued wife,<br>Whom for this time we pardon. We enjoin thee,<br>As thou art liegeman to us, that thou carry<br>This female bastard hence, and that thou bear it<br>To some remote and desert place, quite out<br>Of our dominions; and that there thou leave it,<br>Without more mercy, to it own protection<br>And favour of the climate. As by strange fortune<br>It came to us, I do in justice charge thee,<br>On thy soul’s peril and thy body’s torture,<br>That thou commend it strangely to some place<br>Where chance may nurse or end it. Take it up. |
| ANTIGONUS | I swear to do this, though a present death<br>Had been more merciful. Come on, poor babe,<br>Some powerful spirit instruct the kites and ravens<br>To be thy nurses. Wolves and bears, they say,<br>Casting their savageness aside, have done<br>Like offices of pity. Sir, be prosperous<br>In more than this deed does require; (to the babe) and<br>blessing<br>Against this cruelty, fight on thy side,<br>Poor thing, condemned to loss. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with the babe |
| LEONTES | No, I’ll not rear<br>Another’s issue. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servant |
| SERVANT | Please your highness, posts<br>From those you sent to th’oracle are come<br>An hour since. Cleomenes and Dion,<br>Being well arrived from Delphos, are both landed,<br>Hasting to th’ court.<br>A LORD (to Leontes)<br>So please you, sir, their speed Hath been. beyond account. |
| LEONTES | Twenty-three days<br>They have been absent. ’Tis good speed, foretells<br>The great Apollo suddenly will have<br>The truth of this appear. Prepare you, lords.<br>Summon a session, that we may arraign<br>Our most disloyal lady; for as she hath<br>Been publicly accused, so shall she have<br>A just and open trial. While she lives<br>My heart will be a burden to me. Leave me,<br>And think upon my bidding. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
