| *(didascalia)* | Enter Biron with a paper in his hand, [[Alone|alone]] |
| BIRON | The [[King]], he is hunting the deer. I am coursing myself. They have pitched a toil, I am toiling in a pitch—pitch that defiles. Denle—a foul word. [[Well]], set thee down, sorrow; for so they say the [[Fool|fool]] said, and so say I, and I the [[Fool|fool]]. [[Well]] proved, wit! By the Lord, this [[Love|love]] is as mad as [[Ajax]], it kills sheep, it kills me, I a sheep—[[Well|well]] proved again o’ my side. I will not [[Love|love]]. If I do, hang me; i’[[Faith|faith]], I will not. O, but her eye! By this light, but for her eye I would not [[Love|love]] her. Yes, for her two eyes. [[Well]], I do nothing in the world but lie, and lie in my throat. By heaven, I do [[Love|love]], and it hath taught me to rhyme and to be [[Melancholy|melancholy]], and here (showing a paper) is part of my rhyme, and here (touching his breast) my [[Melancholy|melancholy]]. [[Well]], she hath one o’ my sonnets already. The clown bore it, the [[Fool|fool]] sent it, and the lady hath it. Sweet clown, sweeter [[Fool|fool]], sweetest lady. By the world, I would not care a pin if the other three were in. Here comes one with a paper. God give him grace to groan.<br>He stands aside. The [[King]] entereth with a paper |
| [[King|KING]] | Ay me! |
| BIRON | (aside) Shot, by heaven! Proceed, sweet Cupid, thou hast thumped him with thy birdbolt under the left pap. In [[Faith|faith]], [[Secrets|secrets]]. |
| [[King|KING]] | (reads)<br>‘So sweet a kiss the golden sun gives not<br>To those fresh morning drops upon the [[Rose|rose]]<br>As thy eyebeams when their fresh rays have smote<br>The [[Night|night]] of dew that on my cheeks down flows.<br>Nor shines the silver [[Moon|moon]] one-half so bright<br>Through the transparent bosom of the deep<br>As doth thy face through [[Tears|tears]] of mine give light.<br>Thou shin’st in every tear that I do weep.<br>No drop but as a coach doth carry thee,<br>So ridest thou triumphing in my woe.<br>Do but behold the [[Tears|tears]] that swell in me<br>And they thy glory through my grief will show.<br>But do not [[Love|love]] thyself; then thou wilt keep<br>My [[Tears|tears]] for glasses, and still make me weep.<br>O [[Queen]] of queens, how far dost thou excel,<br>No thought can think nor tongue of mortal tell.’<br>How shall she know my griefs? I’ll drop the paper.<br>Sweet [[Leaves|leaves]], shade folly. Who is he comes here? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Longueville]] with papers. The [[King]] steps aside |
| [[King|KING]] | What, [[Longueville]], and reading—listen, ear! |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>Now in thy likeness one more [[Fool|fool]] appear! |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | Ay me! I am forsworn. |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>Why, he comes in like a perjure, wearing papers. |
| [[King|KING]] | (aside)<br>In [[Love|love]], I [[Hope|hope]]! Sweet fellowship in shame. |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>One drunkard loves another of the name. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | Am I the first that have been perjured so? |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>I could put thee in comfort, not by two that I know.<br>Thou makest the triumviry, the corner-cap of society,<br>The shape of [[Love|love]]’s Tyburn, that hangs up simplicity. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | I [[Fear|fear]] these stubborn lines lack power to move.<br>O sweet [[Maria]], empress of my [[Love|love]],<br>These numbers will I tear, and write in prose. |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>O, rhymes are guards on wanton Cupid’s hose,<br>Disfigure not his slop. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | This same shall go.<br>He reads the [[Sonnet|sonnet]]<br>‘Did not the heavenly rhetoric of thine eye,<br>’Gainst whom the world cannot hold argument,<br>Persuade my heart to this false perjury?<br>Vows for thee broke deserve not punishment.<br>A woman I forswore, but I will prove,<br>Thou being a goddess, I forswore not thee.<br>My vow was earthly, thou a heavenly [[Love|love]].<br>Thy grace being gained cures all disgrace in me.<br>Vows are but breath, and breath a vapour is.<br>Then thou, fair sun, which on my earth dost shine,<br>Exhal‘st this vapour-vow; in thee it is.<br>If broken then, it is no fault of mine.<br>If by me broke, what [[Fool|fool]] is not so wise<br>To lose an oath to win a paradise?’ |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>This is the liver vein, which makes flesh a deity,<br>A green goose a goddess, pure, pure idolatry.<br>God amend us, God amend: we are much out o’th’<br>way. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Dumaine]] with a paper |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | (aside)<br>By whom shall I send this? Company? Stay.<br>He steps aside |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>All hid, all hid—an old infant play.<br>Like a demigod here sit I in the sky,<br>And wretched fools’ [[Secrets|secrets]] heedfully o’er-eye.<br>More sacks to the mill! O heavens, I have my wish.<br>[[Dumaine]] transformed—four woodcocks in a dish! |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | O most divine Kate! |
| BIRON | (aside) O most profane coxcomb! |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | By heaven, the [[Wonder|wonder]] in a mortal eye! |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>By earth, she is not, corporal; there you lie. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Her amber hairs for foul hath amber quoted. |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>An amber-coloured raven was [[Well|well]] noted. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | As upright as the cedar. |
| BIRON | (aside) Stoop, I say.<br>Her shoulder is with [[Child|child]]. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | As fair as day. |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>Ay, as some days; but then no sun must shine. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | O that I had my wishl |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | (aside) And I had mine! |
| [[King|KING]] | (aside) And I mine too, good Lord! |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>Amen, so I had mine. Is not that a good word? |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | I would forget her, but a fever she<br>Reigns in my [[Blood|blood]] and will remembered be. |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>A fever in your [[Blood|blood]]—why then, incision<br>Would let her out in saucers—sweet misprision. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Once more I’ll read the ode that I have writ. |
| BIRON | (aside)<br>Once more I’ll mark how [[Love|love]] can vary wit.<br>[[Dumaine]] reads his [[Sonnet|sonnet]] |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | ‘On a day—atack the day—<br>[[Love]], whose month is ever May,<br>Spied a blossom passing fair<br>Playing in the wanton air.<br>Through the velvet [[Leaves|leaves]] the [[Wind|wind]]<br>All unseen can passage find,<br>That the [[Lover|lover]], sick to [[Death|death]],<br>Wished himself the heavens’ breath.<br>“Air”, quoth he, “thy cheeks may blow;<br>Air, would I might triumph so.<br>But, alack, my hand is sworn<br>Ne’er to pluck thee from thy thorn—<br>Vow, alack, for youth unmeet,<br>Youth so apt to pluck a sweet.<br>Do not call it sin in me<br>That I am forsworn for thee,<br>Thou for whom great Jove would swear<br>Juno but an Ethiop were,<br>And deny himself for Jove,<br>Turning mortal for thy [[Love|love]].”’<br>This will I send, and something else more plain,<br>That shall express my true [[Love|love]]’s fasting [[Pain|pain]].<br>O, would the [[King]], Biron, and [[Longueville]]<br>Were [[lovers]] too! Ill to example ill<br>Would from my forehead wipe a perjured note,<br>For none offend where all alike do dote. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | (coming forward)<br>[[Dumaine]], thy [[Love|love]] is far from [[Charity|charity]],<br>That in [[Love|love]]’s grief desir‘st society.<br>You may look pale, but I should blush, I know,<br>To be o’erheard and taken napping so. |
| [[King|KING]] | (coming forward)<br>Come, sir, you blush. As his, your case is such.<br>You chide at him, offending twice as much.<br>You do not [[Love|love]] [[Maria]]? [[Longueville]]<br>Did never [[Sonnet|sonnet]] for her sake compile,<br>Nor never lay his wreathed arms athwart<br>His loving bosom to keep down his heart?<br>I have been closely shrouded in this bush,<br>And marked you both, and for you both did blush.<br>I heard your guilty rhymes, observed your fashion,<br>Saw sighs reek from you, noted [[Well|well]] your passion.<br>‘Ay me!’ says one, ‘O jovel’ the other cries.<br>One, her hairs were gold; crystal the other’s eyes.<br>(To [[Longueville]]) You would for paradise break [[Faith|faith]] and troth,<br>(To [[Dumaine]]) And Jove for your [[Love|love]] would infringe an oath.<br>What will Biron say when that he shall hear<br>[[Faith]] so infringed, which such zeal did swear?<br>How will he scorn, how will he spend his wit!<br>How will he triumph, leap, and laugh at it!<br>For all the wealth that ever I did see<br>I would not have him know so much by me. |
| BIRON | (coming forward)<br>Now step I forth to whip hypocrisy.<br>Ah, good my liege, I pray thee pardon me.<br>Good heart, what grace hast thou thus to reprove<br>These worms for loving, that art most in [[Love|love]]?<br>Your eyes do make no coaches. In your [[Tears|tears]]<br>There is no certain princess that appears.<br>You’ll not be perjured, ‘tis a hateful thing;<br>Tush, none but minstrels like of sonneting!<br>But are you not ashamed, nay, are you not,<br>All three of you, to be thus much o’ershot?<br>(To [[Longueville]]) You found his mote, the [[King]] your mote did see,<br>But I a beam do find in each of three.<br>O, what a scene of [[Fool|fool]]’ry have I seen,<br>Of sighs, of groans, of sorrow, and of teen!<br>O me, with what strict patience have I sat,<br>To see a [[King|king]] transformed to a gnat!<br>To see great Hercules whipping a gig,<br>And profound Solomon to tune a jig,<br>And Nestor play at pushpin with the boys,<br>And critic [[Timon]] laugh at idle toys!<br>Where lies thy grief, O tell me, good [[Dumaine]]?<br>And, gentle [[Longueville]], where lies thy [[Pain|pain]]?<br>And where my liege’s? All about the breast.<br>A caudle, ho! |
| [[King|KING]] | Too bitter is thy jest.<br>Are we betrayed thus to thy over-view? |
| BIRON | Not you to me, but I betrayed by you.<br>I that am honest, I that hold it sin<br>To break the vow I am engaged in.<br>I am betrayed by keeping company<br>With men like you, men of inconstancy.<br>When shall you see me write a thing in rhyme,<br>Or groan for Joan, or spend a minute’s [[Time|time]]<br>In pruning me? When shall you hear that I<br>Will praise a hand, a foot, a face, an eye,<br>A gait, a state, a brow, a breast, a waist,<br>A leg, a limb? |
| [[King|KING]] | Soft, whither away so fast?<br>A true man or a thief, that gallops so? |
| BIRON | I post from [[Love|love]]; good [[Lover|lover]], let me go. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter aquenetta with a letter, and [[Costard]] the clown |
| JAQUENETTA | God bless the [[King]]! |
| [[King|KING]] | What present hast thou there? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Some certain treason. |
| [[King|KING]] | What makes treason here? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Nay, it makes nothing, sir. |
| [[King|KING]] | If it mar nothing neither,<br>The treason and you go in peace away together! |
| JAQUENETTA | I beseech your grace, let this letter be read.<br>Our parson misdoubts it; ’twas treason, he said. |
| [[King|KING]] | Biron, read it over.<br>Biron takes and reads the letter<br>(To Jaquenetta) Where hadst thou it? |
| JAQUENETTA | Of [[Costard]]. |
| [[King|KING]] | (to [[Costard]]) Where hadst thou it? |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Of Dun Adramadio, Dun Adramadio.<br>Biron [[Tears|tears]] the letter |
| [[King|KING]] | (to Biron)<br>How now, what is in you? Why dost thou tear it? |
| BIRON | A toy, my liege, a toy. Your grace needs not [[Fear|fear]] it. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | It did move him to passion, and therefore let’s hear it. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | (taking up a piece of the letter)<br>It is Biron’s writing, and here is his name. |
| BIRON | (to [[Costard]])<br>Ah, you whoreson loggerhead, you were born to do<br>me shamel<br>Guilty, my lord, guilty! I confess, I confess. |
| [[King|KING]] | What? |
| BIRON | That you three fools lacked me [[Fool|fool]] to make up the<br>mess.<br>He, he, and you-e’en you, my liege-and I<br>Are pickpurses in [[Love|love]], and we deserve to die.<br>O, dismiss this audience, and I shall tell you more. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Now the number is even. |
| BIRON | True, true; we are four.<br>Will these turtles be gone? |
| [[King|KING]] | Hence, sirs; away. |
| [[Costard|COSTARD]] | Walk aside the true folk, and let the traitors stay. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Costard]] and Jaquenetta |
| BIRON | Sweet lords, sweet tovers!—O, let us embrace.<br>As true we are as flesh and [[Blood|blood]] can be.<br>The sea will ebb and flow, heaven show his face.<br>Young [[Blood|blood]] doth not obey an old decree.<br>We cannot [[Cross|cross]] the cause why we were born,<br>Therefore of all hands must we be forsworn. |
| [[King|KING]] | What, did these rent lines show some [[Love|love]] of thine? |
| BIRON | ‘Did they’, quoth you? Who sees the heavenly [[Rosaline]]<br>That, like a rude and savage man of Ind<br>At the first op’ning of the gorgeous east,<br>Bows not his vassal head and, strucken blind,<br>Kisses the base ground with obedient breast?<br>What peremptory eagle-sighted eye<br>Dares look upon the heaven of her brow<br>That is not blinded by her majesty? |
| [[King|KING]] | What zeal, what fury hath inspired thee now?<br>My [[Love|love]], her mistress, is a gracious [[Moon|moon]],<br>She an attending star, scarce seen a light. |
| BIRON | My eyes are then no eyes, nor I Biron.<br>O, but for my [[Love|love]], day would turn to [[Night|night]].<br>Of all complexions the culled sovereignty<br>Do meet as at a fair in her fair cheek,<br>Where several worthies make one dignity,<br>Where nothing wants that want itself doth seek.<br>Lend me the flourish of all gentle tongues—<br>Fie, painted rhetoric! O, she needs it not.<br>To things of sale a seller’s praise belongs.<br>She passes praise—then praise too short doth blot.<br>A withered hermit fivescore winters worn<br>Might shake off fifty, looking in her eye.<br>[[Beauty]] doth varnish age as if new-born,<br>And gives the crutch the cradle’s infancy.<br>O, ’tis the sun that maketh all things shine. |
| [[King|KING]] | By heaven, thy [[Love|love]] is black as ebony. |
| BIRON | Is ebony like her? O word divine!<br>A wife of such wood were felicity.<br>O, who can give an oath? Where is a book,<br>That I may swear [[Beauty|beauty]] doth [[Beauty|beauty]] lack<br>If that she learn not of her eye to look?<br>No face is fair that is not full so black. |
| [[King|KING]] | O [[Paradox|paradox]] ! Black is the badge of hell,<br>The hue of dungeons and the style of [[Night|night]],<br>And [[Beauty|beauty]]’s crest becomes the heavens [[Well|well]]. |
| BIRON | Devils soonest tempt, resembling spirits of light.<br>O, if in black my lady’s brows be decked,<br>It mourns that painting and usurping hair<br>Should ravish doters with a false aspect,<br>And therefore is she born to make black fair.<br>Her favour turns the fashion of the days,<br>For native [[Blood|blood]] is counted painting now,<br>And therefore red that would avoid dispraise<br>Paints itself black to imitate her brow. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | To look like her are chimney-sweepers black. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | And since her [[Time|time]] are colliers counted bright. |
| [[King|KING]] | And Ethiops of their sweet complexion crack. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Dark needs no candles now, for dark is light. |
| BIRON | Your mistresses dare never come in rain,<br>For [[Fear|fear]] their colours should be washed away. |
| [[King|KING]] | ‘Twere good yours did; for, sir, to tell you plain,<br>I’ll find a fairer face not washed today. |
| BIRON | I’ll prove her fair, or talk till doomsday here. |
| [[King|KING]] | No devil will fright thee then so much as she. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | I never knew man hold vile stuff so dear. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | (showing his foot)<br>Look, here’s thy [[Love|love]]—my foot and her face see. |
| BIRON | O, if the streets were paved with thine eyes<br>Her feet were much too dainty for such tread. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | O vile! Then as she goes, what upward lies<br>The street should see as she walked overhead. |
| [[King|KING]] | But what of this? Are we not all in [[Love|love]]? |
| BIRON | Nothing so sure, and thereby all forsworn. |
| [[King|KING]] | Then leave this chat and, good Biron, now prove<br>Our loving lawful and our [[Faith|faith]] not torn. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Ay, marry there, some flattery for this evil. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | O, some authority how to proceed,<br>Some tricks, some quillets how to cheat the devil. |
| [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Some salve for perjury. |
| BIRON | O, ‘tis more than need.<br>Have at you, then, affection’s men-at-arms.<br>Consider what you first did swear unto:<br>To fast, to study, and to see no woman—<br>Flat treason ’gainst the kingly state of youth.<br>Say, can you fast? Your stomachs are too young,<br>And abstinence engenders maladies.<br>O, we have made a vow to study, lords,<br>And in that vow we have forsworn our books;<br>For when would you, my liege, or you, or you<br>In leaden contemplation have found out<br>Such fiery numbers as the prompting eyes<br>Of [[Beauty|beauty]]’s tutors have enriched you with?<br>Other slow arts entirely keep the brain,<br>And therefore, finding barren practisers,<br>Scarce show a harvest of their heavy toil.<br>But [[Love|love]], first learned in a lady’s eyes,<br>Lives not [[Alone|alone]] immured in the brain,<br>But with the motion of all elements<br>Courses as swift as thought in every power,<br>And gives to every power a [[Double|double]] power<br>Above their functions and their offices.<br>It adds a precious seeing to the eye—<br>A [[Lover|lover]]’s eyes will gaze an eagle blind.<br>A [[Lover|lover]]’s ear will hear the lowest sound<br>When the suspicious head of theft is stopped.<br>[[Love]]’s feeling is more soft and sensible<br>Than are the tender horns of cockled snails.<br>[[Love]]’s tongue proves dainty Bacchus gross in taste.<br>For valour, is not [[Love|love]] a Hercules,<br>Still climbing trees in the Hesperides?<br>Subtle as Sphinx, as sweet and musical<br>As bright [[Apollo]]’s lute strung with his hair;<br>And when [[Love|love]] speaks, the voice of all the gods<br>Make heaven drowsy with the harmony.<br>Never durst poet touch a pen to write<br>Until his ink were tempered with [[Love|love]]’s sighs.<br>O, then his lines would ravish savage ears,<br>And plant in tyrants mild [[Humility|humility]].<br>From women’s eyes this doctrine I derive.<br>They sparkle still the right Promethean [[Fire|fire]].<br>They are the books, the arts, the academes<br>That show, contain, and nourish all the world,<br>Else none at all in aught proves excellent.<br>Then fools you were these women to forswear,<br>Or keeping what is sworn, you will prove fools.<br>For wisdom’s sake—a word that all men [[Love|love]]—<br>Or for [[Love|love]]’s sake—a word that loves all men—<br>Or for men’s sake—the authors of these women—<br>Or women’s sake—by whom we men are men—<br>Let us once lose our oaths to find ourselves,<br>Or else we lose ourselves to keep our oaths.<br>It is religion to be thus forsworn,<br>For [[Charity|charity]] itself fulfils the law,<br>And who can sever [[Love|love]] from [[Charity|charity]]? |
| [[King|KING]] | Saint Cupid, then, and, soldiers, to the field! |
| BIRON | Advance your standards, and upon them, lords.<br>Pell-mell, down with them; but be first advised<br>In conflict that you get the sun of them. |
| [[Longueville|LONGUEVILLE]] | Now to plain dealing. Lay these glozes by.<br>Shall we resolve to woo these girls of France? |
| [[King|KING]] | And win them, too! Therefore let us devise<br>Some entertainment for them in their tents. |
| BIRON | First, from the park let us conduct them thither;<br>Then homeward every man attach the hand<br>Of his fair mistress. In the afternoon<br>We will with some strange pastime solace them,<br>Such as the shortness of the [[Time|time]] can shape,<br>For revels, dances, masques, and merry hours<br>Forerun fair [[Love|love]], strewing her way with [[Flowers|flowers]]. |
| [[King|KING]] | Away, away, no [[Time|time]] shall be omitted<br>That will be [[Time|time]], and may by us be fitted. |
| BIRON | Allons, allons! Sowed cockle reaped no corn,<br>And justice always whirls in equal measure.<br>Light wenches may prove plagues to men forsworn.<br>If so, our copper buys no better treasure. Exeunt |
