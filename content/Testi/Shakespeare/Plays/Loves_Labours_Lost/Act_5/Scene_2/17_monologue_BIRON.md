| BIRON | Honest plain words best pierce the ear of grief, And by these badges understand the [[King]]. For your fair sakes have we neglected [[Time|time]], Played foul play with our oaths. Your [[Beauty|beauty]], ladies, Hath much deformed us, fashioning our humours Even to the opposed end of our intents, And what in us hath seemed ridiculous— As [[Love|love]] is full of unbefitting strains, All wanton as a [[Child|child]], skipping and vain, Formed by the eye and therefore like the eye, Full of strange shapes, of habits and of forms, Varying in subjects as the eye doth roll To every varied object in his glance; Which parti-coated presence of loose [[Love|love]] Put on by us, if in your heavenly eyes Have misbecomed our oaths and gravities, Those heavenly eyes that look into these faults Suggested us to make them. Therefore, ladies, Our [[Love|love]] being yours, the error that [[Love|love]] makes Is likewise yours. We to ourselves prove false By being once false for ever to be true To those that make us both—fair ladies, you. And even that falsehood, in itself a sin, Thus purifies itself and turns to grace. |
