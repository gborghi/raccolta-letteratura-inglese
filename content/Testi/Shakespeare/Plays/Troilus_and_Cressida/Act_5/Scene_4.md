| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter [[Thersites]] in excursions |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Now they are clapper-clawing one another. I’ll go look on. That dissembling abominable varlet Diomed has got that same scurvy doting foolish young knave’s sleeve of Troy there in his helm. I would fain see them meet, that that same young Trojan ass that loves the whore there might send that Greekish whoremasterly [[Villain|villain]] with the sleeve back to the dissembling luxurious drab of a sleeveless errand. O‘th’ t’other side, the policy of those crafty swearing rascals—that stale old mouse-eaten dry [[cheese]] Nestor and that same dog-fox [[Ulysses]]—is proved not worth a blackberry. They set me up in policy that mongrel cur [[Ajax]] against that dog of as bad a kind [[Achilles]]. And now is the cur [[Ajax]] prouder than the cur [[Achilles]], and will not arm today—whereupon the Grecians began to proclaim barbarism, and policy grows into an ill opinion. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Diomedes, followed by [[Troilus]] |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Soft, here comes sleeve and t’other. |
| [[Troilus|TROILUS]] | (to Diomedes)<br>Fly not, for shouldst thou take the [[River|river]] Styx<br>I would swim after. |
| DIOMEDES | Thou dost miscall retire.<br>I do not fly, but advantageous care<br>Withdrew me from the odds of multitude. Have at<br>thee!<br>They fight |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | Hold thy whore, Grecian! Now for thy whore, Trojan! Now the sleeve, now the sleeve! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Diomedes driving in [[Troilus]] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Hector]] behind |
| [[Hector|HECTOR]] | What art thou, Greek? Art thou for [[Hector]]’s match?<br>Art thou of [[Blood|blood]] and [[Honour|honour]]? |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | No, no, I am a rascal, a scurvy railing knave, a very filthy rogue. |
| [[Hector|HECTOR]] | I do believe thee: live. |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | God-a-[[Mercy|mercy]], that thou wilt believe me— |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Hector]] |
| [[Thersites|THERSITES]] | but a plague break thy neck for frighting me. What’s become of the wenching rogues? I think they have swallowed one another. I would laugh at that miracle—yet in a sort lechery eats itself. I’ll seek them. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
