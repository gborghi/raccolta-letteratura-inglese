| *(didascalia)* | Enter at one door Aeneas with a torch; at another Paris, Deiphobus, Antenor, and Diomedes the Grecian, with torch-bearers |
| PARIS | See, hol Who is that there? |
| DEIPHOBUS | It is the Lord Aeneas. |
| AENEAS | Is the Prince there in person? Had I so good occasion to lie long<br>As you, Prince Paris, nothing but heavenly business<br>Should rob my bed-mate of my company. |
| DIOMEDES | That’s my mind too. Good morrow, Lord Aeneas. |
| PARIS | A valiant Greek, Aeneas, take his hand.<br>Witness the process of your speech, wherein<br>You told how Diomed e’en a whole week by days<br>Did haunt you in the field. |
| AENEAS | (to Diomedes) Health to you, valiant sir,<br>During all question of the gentle truce.<br>But when I meet you armed, as black defiance<br>As heart can think or courage execute. |
| DIOMEDES | The one and other Diomed embraces.<br>Our bloods are now in calm; and so long, health.<br>But when contention and occasion meet,<br>By Jove I’ll play the hunter for thy life<br>With all my force, pursuit, and policy. |
| AENEAS | And thou shalt hunt a lion that will fly<br>With his face backward. In humane gentleness,<br>Welcome to Troy. Now by Anchises’ life,<br>Welcome indeed) By Venus’ hand I swear<br>No man alive can love in such a sort<br>The thing he means to kill more excellently. |
| DIOMEDES | We sympathize. Jove, let Aeneas live—<br>If to my sword his fate be not the glory—<br>A thousand complete courses of the sun;<br>But, in mine emulous honour, let him die<br>With every joint a wound—and that, tomorrow. |
| AENEAS | We know each other well. |
| DIOMEDES | We do, and long to know each other worse. |
| PARIS | This is the most despitefull‘st gentle greeting,<br>The noblest hateful love, that e’er I heard of.<br>What business, lord, so early? |
| AENEAS | I was sent for to the King; but why, I know not. |
| PARIS | His purpose meets you: ’twas to bring this Greek<br>To Calchas’ house, and there to render him,<br>For the enfreed Antenor, the fair Cressid.<br>Let’s have your company, or if you please |
| *(didascalia)* | Haste there before us. Aside I constantly do think— |
| PARIS | Or rather, call my thought a certain knowledge—<br>My brother Troilus lodges there tonight.<br>Rouse him and give him note of our approach,<br>With the whole quality wherefore. I fear<br>We shall be much unwelcome. |
| *(didascalia)* | AENEAS aside That I assure you. |
| PARIS | Troilus had rather Troy were borne to Greece<br>Than Cressid borne from Troy. |
| *(didascalia)* | PARIS aside |
| PARIS | There is no help.<br>The bitter disposition of the time<br>Will have it so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Aloud On, lord, we’ll follow you. |
| AENEAS | Good morrow all. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| PARIS | And tell me, noble Diomed—faith, tell me true,<br>Even in the soul of sound good-fellowship-<br>Who in your thoughts merits fair Helen most,<br>Myself or Menelaus? |
| DIOMEDES | Both alike.<br>He merits well to have her that doth seek her,<br>Not making any scruple of her soilure,<br>With such a hell of pain and world of charge;<br>And you as well to keep her that defend her,<br>Not palating the taste of her dishonour,<br>With such a costly loss of wealth and friends.<br>He like a puling cuckold would drink up<br>The lees and dregs of a flat ’tamed piece;<br>You like a lecher out of whorish loins<br>Are pleased to breed out your inheritors.<br>Both merits poised, each weighs nor less nor more,<br>But he as he: which heavier for a whore? |
| PARIS | You are too bitter to your countrywoman. |
| DIOMEDES | She’s bitter to her country. Hear me, Paris.<br>For every false drop in her bawdy veins<br>A Grecian’s life hath sunk; for every scruple<br>Of her contaminated carrion weight<br>A Trojan hath been slain. Since she could speak<br>She hath not given so many good words breath<br>As, for her, Greeks and Trojans suffered death. |
| PARIS | Fair Diomed, you do as chapmen do:<br>Dispraise the thing that you desire to buy.<br>But we in silence hold this virtue well:<br>We’ll but commend what we intend to sell.—<br>Here lies our way. Exeunt |
