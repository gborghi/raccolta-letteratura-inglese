| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of [[Kent]], the [[Duke of Gloucester]], and Edmond |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I thought the [[King]] had more affected the Duke of Albany than Cornwall.<br>[[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]]) It did always seem so to us, but now in the division of the kingdom it appears not which of the Dukes he values most; for qualities are so weighed that curiosity in neither can make choice of either’s moiety. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Is not this your son, my lord? |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | His breeding, sir, hath been at my charge. I have so often blushed to acknowledge him that now I am brazed to’t. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I cannot conceive you. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | Sir, this young fellow’s mother could, whereupon she grew round-wombed and had indeed, sir, a son for her cradle ere she had a husband for her bed. Do you smell a fault? |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I cannot wish the fault undone, the issue of it being so proper. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | But I have a son, sir, by order of law, some year older than this, who yet is no dearer in my account. Though this knave came something saucily to the world before he was sent for, yet was his mother fair, there was good sport at his making, and the whoreson must be acknowledged. (To Edmond) Do you know this noble gentleman, Edmond? |
| EDMOND | No, my lord. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to Edmond) My lord of [[Kent]]. Remember him hereafter as my honourable friend. |
| EDMOND | (to [[Kent]]) My services to your lordship. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | I must [[Love|love]] you, and sue to know you better. |
| EDMOND | Sir, I shall study deserving. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | (to [[Kent]]) He hath been out nine years, and away he shall again. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sennet |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | The [[King]] is coming. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King Lear]], the Dukes of Cornwall and Albany, [[Goneril]], [[Regan]], [[Cordelia]], and attendants |
| LEAR | Attend the lords of France and Burgundy, [[Gloucester]]. |
| [[Gloucester|GLOUCESTER]] | I shall, my lord. Exit |
| LEAR | Meantime we shall express our darker purpose.<br>Give me the map there. Know that we have divided<br>In three our kingdom, and ’tis our fast intent<br>To shake all cares and business from our age,<br>Conferring them on younger strengths while we<br>Unburdened crawl toward [[Death|death]]. Our son of Cornwall,<br>And you, our no less loving son of Albany,<br>We have this hour a constant will to publish<br>Our daughters’ several dowers, that future strife<br>May be prevented now. The princes France and<br>Burgundy—<br>Great rivals in our youngest daughter’s [[Love|love]]—<br>Long in our [[Court|court]] have made their amorous sojourn,<br>And here are to be answered. Tell me, my daughters—<br>Since now we will divest us both of rule,<br>Interest of territory, cares of state—<br>Which of you shall we say doth [[Love|love]] us most,<br>That we our largest bounty may extend<br>Where [[Nature|nature]] doth with merit challenge? [[Goneril]],<br>Our eldest born, speak first. |
| [[Goneril|GONERIL]] | Sir, I [[Love|love]] you more than words can wield the matter;<br>Dearer than eyesight, space, and liberty;<br>Beyond what can be valued, rich or rare,<br>No less than life; with grace, health, [[Beauty|beauty]], [[Honour|honour]];<br>As much as [[Child|child]] e’er loved or father found;<br>A [[Love|love]] that makes breath poor and speech unable.<br>Beyond all manner of so much I [[Love|love]] you. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | (aside)<br>What shall [[Cordelia]] speak? [[Love]] and be silent. |
| LEAR | (to [[Goneril]])<br>Of all these bounds even from this line to this,<br>With shadowy forests and with champaigns riched,<br>With plenteous rivers and wide-skirted meads,<br>We make thee lady. To thine and Albany’s issues<br>Be this perpetual.—What says our second daughter?<br>Our dearest [[Regan]], wife of Cornwall? |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | I am made of that [[Self|self]] mettle as my sister,<br>And prize me at her worth. In my true heart<br>I find she names my very deed of [[Love|love]]—<br>Only she comes too short, that I profess<br>Myself an enemy to all other joys<br>Which the most precious square of sense possesses,<br>And find I am [[Alone|alone]] felicitate<br>In your dear highness’ [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | (aside) Then poor [[Cordelia]]—<br>And yet not so, since I am sure my [[Love|love]]’s<br>More ponderous than my tongue. |
| LEAR | (to [[Regan]])<br>To thee and thine hereditary ever<br>Remain this ample third of our fair kingdom,<br>No less in space, validity, and pleasure<br>Than that conferred on [[Goneril]]. (To [[Cordelia]]) Now our<br>joy,<br>Although our last and least, to whose young [[Love|love]]<br>The vines of France and milk of Burgundy<br>Strive to be interessed: what can you say to draw<br>A third more opulent than your sisters? Speak. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Nothing, my lord. |
| LEAR | Nothing? |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Nothing. |
| LEAR | Nothing will come of nothing. Speak again. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Unhappy that I am, I cannot heave<br>My heart into my mouth. I [[Love|love]] your majesty<br>According to my bond, no more nor less. |
| LEAR | How, how, [[Cordelia]]? Mend your speech a little<br>Lest you may mar your fortunes. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Good my lord,<br>You have begot me, bred me, loved me.<br>I return those duties back as are right fit-<br>Obey you, [[Love|love]] you, and most [[Honour|honour]] you.<br>Why have my sisters husbands if they say<br>They [[Love|love]] you all? Haply when I shall wed<br>That lord whose hand must take my plight shall carry<br>Half my [[Love|love]] with him, half my care and duty.<br>Sure, I shall never marry like my sisters. |
| LEAR | But goes thy heart with this? |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Ay, my good lord. |
| LEAR | So young and so untender? |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | So young, my lord, and true. |
| LEAR | Let it be so. Thy [[Truth|truth]] then be thy dower;<br>For by the sacred radiance of the sun,<br>The mysteries of Hecate and the [[Night|night]],<br>By all the operation of the orbs<br>From whom we do exist and cease to be,<br>Here I disclaim all my paternal care,<br>Propinquity, and property of [[Blood|blood]],<br>And as a stranger to my heart and me<br>Hold thee from this for ever. The barbarous Scythian,<br>Or he that makes his generation messes<br>To gorge his appetite, shall to my bosom<br>Be as [[Well|well]] neighboured, pitied, and relieved<br>As thou, my sometime daughter. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Good my liege— |
| LEAR | Peace, [[Kent]].<br>Come not between the dragon and his wrath.<br>I loved her most, and thought to set my rest |
| *(didascalia)* | On her kind nursery. To [[Cordelia]] Hence, and avoid |
| LEAR | my sight!—<br>So be my [[Grave|grave]] my peace as here I give<br>Her father’s heart from her. Call France. Who stirs?<br>Call Burgundy. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| LEAR | one or |
| *(didascalia)* | more |
| LEAR | Cornwall and Albany,<br>With my two daughters’ dowers digest the third.<br>Let [[Pride|pride]], which she calls plainness, marry her.<br>I do invest you jointly with my power,<br>Pre-eminence, and all the large effects<br>That troop with majesty. Ourself by monthly course,<br>With reservation of an hundred knights<br>By you to be sustained, shall our abode<br>Make with you by due turn. Only we shall retain<br>The name and all th’addition to a [[King|king]]. The sway,<br>Revenue, execution of the rest,<br>Beloved sons, be yours; which to confirm,<br>This crownet part between you. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Royal Lear,<br>Whom I have ever honoured as my [[King|king]],<br>Loved as my father, as my master followed,<br>As my great patron thought on in my prayers— |
| LEAR | The bow is bent and drawn; make from the shaft. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Let it fall rather, though the fork invade<br>The region of my heart. Be [[Kent]] unmannerly<br>When Lear is mad. What wouldst thou do, old man?<br>Think’st thou that duty shall have dread to speak<br>When power to flattery bows? To plainness [[Honour|honour]]’s<br>bound<br>When majesty falls to folly. Reserve thy state,<br>And in thy best consideration check<br>This hideous rashness. Answer my life my judgement,<br>Thy youngest daughter does not [[Love|love]] thee least,<br>Nor are those empty-hearted whose low sounds<br>Reverb no hollowness. |
| LEAR | [[Kent]], on thy life, no more! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | My life I never held but as a pawn<br>To wage against thine enemies, ne’er feared to lose it,<br>Thy safety being motive. |
| LEAR | Out of my sight! |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | See better, Lear, and let me still remain<br>The true blank of thine eye. |
| LEAR | Now, by [[Apollo]]— |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Now, by [[Apollo]], [[King]], thou swear’st thy gods in vain. |
| *(didascalia)* | LEAR making to strike him |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | O vassal! Miscreant! |
| *(didascalia)* | ALBANY and [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] Dear sir, forbear. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | (to Lear)<br>Kill thy physician, and thy fee bestow<br>Upon the foul disease. Revoke thy gift,<br>Or whilst I can vent clamour from my throat<br>I’ll tell thee thou dost evil. |
| LEAR | Hear me, recreant; on thine allegiance hear me!<br>That thou hast sought to make us break our vows,<br>Which we durst never yet, and with strained [[Pride|pride]]<br>To come betwixt our sentence and our power,<br>Which nor our [[Nature|nature]] nor our place can bear,<br>Our potency made good take thy reward:<br>Five days we do allot thee for provision<br>To shield thee from disasters of the world,<br>And on the sixth to turn thy hated back<br>Upon our kingdom. If on the seventh day following<br>Thy banished trunk be found in our dominions,<br>The moment is thy [[Death|death]]. Away! By Jupiter,<br>This shall not be revoked. |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Fare thee [[Well|well]], [[King]]; sith thus thou wilt appear,<br>[[Freedom]] lives hence, and banishment is here.<br>(To [[Cordelia]]) The gods to their dear shelter take thee,<br>maid,<br>That justly think’st, and hast most rightly said.<br>(To [[Goneril]] and [[Regan]]) And your large speeches may<br>your deeds approve,<br>That good effects may [[spring]] from words of [[Love|love]].<br>Thus [[Kent]], O princes, bids you all adieu;<br>He’ll shape his old course in a country new. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter the [[Duke of Gloucester]] with the |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | [[King of France]], the Duke of Burgundy, and attendants |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] |
| [[Kent|KENT]] | Here’s France and Burgundy, my noble lord. |
| LEAR | My lord of Burgundy,<br>We first address toward you, who with this [[King]]<br>Hath rivalled for our daughter: what in the least<br>Will you require in present dower with her<br>Or cease your [[Quest|quest]] of [[Love|love]]? |
| BURGUNDY | Most royal majesty,<br>I crave no more than hath your highness offered;<br>Nor will you tender less. |
| LEAR | Right noble Burgundy,<br>When she was dear to us we did hold her so;<br>But now her price is fallen. Sir, there she stands.<br>If aught within that little seeming substance,<br>Or all of it, with our displeasure pieced,<br>And nothing more, may fitly like your grace,<br>She’s there, and she is yours. |
| BURGUNDY | I know no answer. |
| LEAR | Will you with those infirmities she owes,<br>Unfriended, new adopted to our hate,<br>Dowered with our curse and strangered with our oath,<br>Take her or leave her? |
| BURGUNDY | Pardon me, royal sir.<br>Election makes not up in such conditions. |
| LEAR | Then leave her, sir; for by the power that made me,<br>I tell you all her wealth. (To France) For you, great [[King]],<br>I would not from your [[Love|love]] make such a stray<br>To match you where I hate, therefore beseech you<br>T‘avert your liking a more worthier way<br>Than on a wretch whom [[Nature|nature]] is ashamed<br>Almost t’acknowledge hers. |
| FRANCE | This is most strange,<br>That she whom even but now was your best object,<br>The argument of your praise, balm of your age,<br>The best, the dear’st, should in this trice of [[Time|time]]<br>Commit a thing so monstrous to dismantle<br>So many folds of favour. Sure, her offence<br>Must be of such unnatural degree<br>That monsters it, or your fore-vouched affection<br>Fall into taint; which to believe of her<br>Must be a [[Faith|faith]] that reason without miracle<br>Should never plant in me. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | (to Lear)<br>I yet beseech your majesty,<br>If for I want that glib and oily art<br>To speak and purpose not—since what I [[Well|well]] intend,<br>I’ll do’t before I speak—that you make known<br>It is no vicious blot, murder, or foulness,<br>No unchaste action or dishonoured step<br>That hath deprived me of your grace and favour,<br>But even the want of that for which I am richer—<br>A still-soliciting eye, and such a tongue<br>That I am glad I have not, though not to have it<br>Hath lost me in your liking. |
| LEAR | Better thou<br>Hadst not been born than not t’have pleased me better. |
| FRANCE | Is it but this—a tardiness in [[Nature|nature]],<br>Which often [[Leaves|leaves]] the [[history]] unspoke<br>That it intends to do?—My lord of Burgundy,<br>What say you to the lady? [[Love]]’s not [[Love|love]]<br>When it is mingled with regards that stands<br>Aloof from th’entire point. Will you have her?<br>She is herself a dowry. |
| BURGUNDY | (to Lear) Royal [[King]],<br>Give but that portion which yourself proposed,<br>And here I take [[Cordelia]] by the hand,<br>Duchess of Burgundy. |
| LEAR | Nothing. I have sworn. I am firm. |
| BURGUNDY | (to [[Cordelia]])<br>I am sorry, then, you have so lost a father<br>That you must lose a husband. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Peace be with Burgundy;<br>Since that respect and fortunes are his [[Love|love]],<br>I shall not be his wife. |
| FRANCE | Fairest [[Cordelia]], that art most rich, being poor;<br>Most choice, forsaken; and most loved, despised:<br>Thee and thy virtues here I seize upon.<br>Be it lawful, I take up what’s cast away.<br>Gods, gods! ‘Tis strange that from their cold’st neglect<br>My [[Love|love]] should kindle to inflamed respect.—<br>Thy dowerless daughter, [[King]], thrown to my chance,<br>Is [[Queen|queen]] of us, of ours, and our fair France.<br>Not all the dukes of wat’rish Burgundy<br>Can buy this unprized precious maid of me.—<br>Bid them farewell, [[Cordelia]], though unkind.<br>Thou losest here, a better where to find. |
| LEAR | Thou hast her, France. Let her be thine, for we<br>Have no such daughter, nor shall ever see<br>That face of hers again. Therefore be gone,<br>Without our grace, our [[Love|love]], our benison.—<br>Come, noble Burgundy. Flourish. Exeunt all but France<br><br>and the sisters |
| FRANCE | Bid farewell to your sisters. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | Ye jewels of our father, with washed eyes<br>[[Cordelia]] [[Leaves|leaves]] you. I know you what you are,<br>And like a sister am most loath to call<br>Your faults as they are named. [[Love]] [[Well|well]] our father.<br>To your professed bosoms I commit him.<br>But yet, alas, stood I within his grace<br>I would prefer him to a better place.<br>So farewell to you both. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Prescribe not us our duty. |
| [[Goneril|GONERIL]] | Let your study<br>Be to content your lord, who hath received you<br>At fortune’s alms. You have obedience scanted,<br>And [[Well|well]] are worth the want that you have wanted. |
| [[Cordelia|CORDELIA]] | [[Time]] shall unfold what pleated cunning hides,<br>Who covert faults at last with shame derides.<br>[[Well]] may you prosper. |
| FRANCE | Come, my fair [[Cordelia]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt France and [[Cordelia]] |
| [[Goneril|GONERIL]] | Sister, it is not little I have to say of what most nearly appertains to us both. I think our father will hence tonight. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | That’s most certain, and with you. Next month with us. |
| [[Goneril|GONERIL]] | You see how full of changes his age is. The observation we have made of it hath been little. He always loved our sister most, and with what poor judgement he hath now cast her off appears too grossly. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | ’Tis the infirmity of his age; yet he hath ever but slenderly known himself. |
| [[Goneril|GONERIL]] | The best and soundest of his [[Time|time]] hath been but rash; then must we look from his age to receive not [[Alone|alone]] the imperfections of long-engrafted condition, but therewithal the unruly waywardness that infirm and choleric years bring with them. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | Such unconstant starts are we like to have from him as this of [[Kent]]’s banishment. |
| [[Goneril|GONERIL]] | There is further compliment of leave-taking between France and him. Pray you, let us sit together. If our father carry authority with such disposition as he bears, this last surrender of his will but offend us. |
| [[Regan|REGAN]] | We shall further think of it. [[Goneril|GONERIL]] We must do something, and i’th’ heat. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
