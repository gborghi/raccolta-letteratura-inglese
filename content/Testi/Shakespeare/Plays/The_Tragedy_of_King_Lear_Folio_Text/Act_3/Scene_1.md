| *(didascalia)* | Storm still. Enter the Earl of Kent disguised and the First Gentleman, severally |
| KENT | Who’s there, besides foul weather? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| KENT | One minded like the weather,<br>Most unquietly. |
| KENT | I know you. Where’s the King? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| KENT | Contending with the fretful elements;<br>Bids the wind blow the earth into the sea<br>Or swell the curled waters ’bove the main,<br>That things might change or cease. |
| KENT | But who is with him? |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| KENT | None but the Fool, who labours to outjest<br>His heart-struck injuries. |
| KENT | Sir, I do know you,<br>And dare upon the warrant of my note<br>Commend a dear thing to you. There is division,<br>Although as yet the face of it is covered<br>With mutual cunning, ’twixt Albany and Cornwall,<br>Who have—as who have not that their great stars<br>Throned and set high—servants, who seem no less,<br>Which are to France the spies and speculations<br>Intelligent of our state. What hath been seen,<br>Either in snuffs and packings of the Dukes,<br>Or the hard rein which both of them hath borne<br>Against the old kind King; or something deeper,<br>Whereof perchance these are but furnishings— |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| KENT | I will talk further with you. |
| KENT | No, do not.<br>For confirmation that I am much more<br>Than my out-wall, open this purse, and take<br>What it contains. If you shall see Cordelia—<br>As fear not but you shall—show her this ring<br>And she will tell you who that fellow is<br>That yet you do not know. Fie on this storm!<br>I will go seek the King. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRST GENTLEMAN |
| KENT | Give me your hand. Have you no more to say? |
| KENT | Few words, but to effect more than all yet:<br>That when we have found the King—in which your<br>pain<br>That way, I’ll this—he that first lights on him<br>Holla the other. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt severally |
