| *(didascalia)* | Enter with a drummer and colours, Queen Cordelia, Gentlemen, and soldiers |
| CORDELIA | Alack, ’tis he! Why, he was met even now,<br>As mad as the vexed sea, singing aloud,<br>Crowned with rank fumitor and furrow-weeds,<br>With burdocks, hemlock, nettles, cuckoo-flowers,<br>Darnel, and all the idle weeds that grow<br>In our sustaining corn. A century send forth.<br>Search every acre in the high-grown field,<br>And bring him to our eye. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | one or more |
| CORDELIA | What can man’s wisdom<br>In the restoring his<br>He that helps him take all my outward worth. |
| *(didascalia)* | FIRSTGENTLEMAN There is means, madam. |
| CORDELIA | Our foster-nurse of nature is repose,<br>The which he lacks. That to provoke in him<br>Are many simples operative, whose power<br>Will close the eye of anguish. |
| CORDELIA | All blest secrets,<br>All you unpublished virtues of the earth,<br>Spring with my tears, be aidant and remediate<br>In the good man’s distress!—Seek, seek for him,<br>Lest his ungoverned rage dissolve the life<br>That wants the means to lead it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter |
| CORDELIA | a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | News, madam.<br>The British powers are marching hitherward. |
| CORDELIA | ’Tis known before; our preparation stands<br>In expectation of them.—O dear father,<br>It is thy business that I go about;<br>Therefore great France<br>My mourning and importuned tears hath pitied.<br>No blown ambition doth our arms incite,<br>But love, dear love, and our aged father’s right.<br>Soon may I hear and see him! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
