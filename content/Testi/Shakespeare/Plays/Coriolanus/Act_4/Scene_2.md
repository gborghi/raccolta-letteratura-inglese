| *(didascalia)* | Enter the two tribunes, Sicinius and Brutus, with the Aedile |
| SICINIUS | (to the Aedile)<br>Bid them all home. He’s gone, and we’ll no further.<br>The nobility are vexed, whom we see have sided<br>In his behalf. |
| BRUTUS | Now we have shown our power,<br>Let us seem humbler after it is done<br>Than when it was a-doing. |
| SICINIUS | (to the Aedile) Bid them home.<br>Say their great enemy is gone, and they<br>Stand in their ancient strength. |
| BRUTUS | Dismiss them home. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Aedile |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Volumnia, Virgilia, weeping, and Menenius |
| BRUTUS | Here comes his mother. |
| SICINIUS | Let’s not meet her. |
| BRUTUS | Why? |
| SICINIUS | They say she’s mad. |
| BRUTUS | They have ta’en note of us. Keep on your way. |
| VOLUMNIA | O, you’re well met! Th‘hoarded plague o’th’ gods<br>Requite your love! |
| MENENIUS | Peace, peace, be not so loud. |
| VOLUMNIA | (to the tribunes)<br>If that I could for weeping, you should hear—<br>Nay, and you shall hear some. Will you be gone? |
| VIRGILIA | (to the tribunes)<br>You shall stay, too. I would I had the power<br>To say so to my husband. |
| SICINIUS | (to Volumnia) Are you mankind? |
| VOLUMNIA | Ay, fool. Is that a shame? Note but this, fool:<br>Was not a man my father? Hadst thou foxship<br>To banish him that struck more blows for Rome<br>Than thou hast spoken words? |
| SICINIUS | O blessed heavens! |
| VOLUMNIA | More noble blows than ever thou wise words,<br>And for Rome’s good. I’ll tell thee what—yet go.<br>Nay, but thou shalt stay too. I would my son<br>Were in Arabia, and thy tribe before him,<br>His good sword in his hand. |
| SICINIUS | What then? |
| VIRGILIA | What then?<br>He’d make an end of thy posterity. |
| VOLUMNIA | Bastards and all.<br>Good man, the wounds that he does bear for Romeǃ MENENIUS Come, come, peace. |
| SICINIUS | I would he had continued to his country<br>As he began, and not unknit himself<br>The noble knot he made. |
| BRUTUS | I would he had. |
| VOLUMNIA | ‘I would he had’! ’Twas you incensed the rabble—<br>Cats that can judge as fitly of his worth<br>As I can of those mysteries which heaven<br>Will not have earth to know. |
| BRUTUS | (to Sicinius) Pray, let’s go. |
| VOLUMNIA | Now pray, sir, get you gone.<br>You have done a brave deed. Ere you go, hear this:<br>As far as doth the Capitol exceed<br>The meanest house in Rome, so far my son—<br>This lady’s husband here, this, do you see?—<br>Whom you have banished does exceed you all. |
| BRUTUS | Well, well, we’ll leave you. |
| SICINIUS | Why stay we to be baited<br>With one that wants her wits? |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt tribunes |
| VOLUMNIA | Take my prayers with you.<br>I would the gods had nothing else to do<br>But to confirm my curses. Could I meet ’em<br>But once a day, it would unclog my heart<br>Of what lies heavy to’t. |
| MENENIUS | You have told them home<br>And, by my troth, you have cause. You’ll sup with me? |
| VOLUMNIA | Anger’s my meat, I sup upon myself,<br>And so shall starve with feeding.<br>(To Virgilia) Come, let’s go.<br>Leave this faint puling and lament as I do,<br>In anger, Juno-like. Come, come, come. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Volumnia and Virgilia |
| MENENIUS | Fie, fie, fie. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
