| *(didascalia)* | Enter the two tribunes, Sicinius and Brutus |
| SICINIUS | We hear not of him, neither need we fear him.<br>His remedies are tame—the present peace<br>And quietness of the people, which before<br>Were in wild hurry. Here do we make his friends<br>Blush that the world goes well, who rather had,<br>Though they themselves did suffer by‘t, behold<br>Dissentious numbers pest’ring streets than see<br>Our tradesmen singing in their shops and going<br>About their functions friendly. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Menenius |
| BRUTUS | We stood to’t in good time. Is this Menenius? |
| SICINIUS | ‘Tis he, ’tis he. O, he is grown most kind of late.<br>Hail, sir. |
| MENENIUS | Hail to you both. |
| SICINIUS | Your Coriolanus is not much missed<br>But with his friends. The commonwealth doth stand,<br>And so would do were he more angry at it. |
| MENENIUS | All’s well, and might have been much better if<br>He could have temporized. |
| SICINIUS | Where is he, hear you? |
| MENENIUS | Nay, I hear nothing.<br>His mother and his wife hear nothing from him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter three or four Citizens |
| ALL THE CITIZENS | (to the tribunes)<br>The gods preserve you both. |
| SICINIUS | Good e’en, our neighbours. |
| BRUTUS | Good e‘en to you all, good e’en to you all. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | Ourselves, our wives and children, on our knees<br>Are bound to pray for you both. |
| SICINIUS | Live and thrive. |
| BRUTUS | Farewell, kind neighbours.<br>We wished Coriolanus had loved you as we did. |
| ALL THE CITIZENS | Now the gods keep you! |
| SICINIUS | and BRUTUS<br>Farewell, farewell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Citizens |
| SICINIUS | This is a happier and more comely time<br>Than when these fellows ran about the streets<br>Crying confusion. |
| BRUTUS | Caius Martius was<br>A worthy officer i‘th’ war, but insolent,<br>O’ercome with pride, ambitious past all thinking,<br>Self-loving— |
| SICINIUS | And affecting one sole throne<br>Without assistance. |
| MENENIUS | I think not so. |
| SICINIUS | We should by this, to all our lamentation,<br>If he had gone forth consul found it so. |
| BRUTUS | The gods have well prevented it, and Rome<br>Sits safe and still without him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter an Aedile |
| AEDILE | Worthy tribunes,<br>There is a slave whom we have put in prison<br>Reports the Volsces, with two several powers,<br>Are entered in the Roman territories,<br>And with the deepest malice of the war<br>Destroy what lies before ’em. |
| MENENIUS | ’Tis Aufidius,<br>Who, hearing of our Martius’ banishment,<br>Thrusts forth his horns again into the world,<br>Which were inshelled when Martius stood for Rome,<br>And durst not once peep out. |
| SICINIUS | Come, what talk you of Martius? BRUTUS (to the Aedile)<br>Go see this rumourer whipped. It cannot be<br>The Volsces dare break with us. |
| MENENIUS | Cannot be?<br>We have record that very well it can,<br>And three examples of the like hath been |
| *(didascalia)* | Within my age. But reason with the fellow, |
| MENENIUS | Before you punish him, where he heard this,<br>Lest you shall chance to whip your information<br>And beat the messenger who bids beware<br>Of what is to be dreaded. |
| SICINIUS | Tell not me.<br>I know this cannot be. |
| BRUTUS | Not possible. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | The nobles in great earnestness are going<br>All to the senate-house. Some news is come<br>That turns their countenances. |
| SICINIUS | ’Tis this slave.<br>(To the Aedile) Go whip him fore the people’s eyes.—<br>His raising,<br>Nothing but his report. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Aedile |
| MESSENGER | Yes, worthy sir,<br>The slave’s report is seconded, and more,<br>More fearful, is delivered. |
| SICINIUS | What more fearful? |
| MESSENGER | It is spoke freely out of many mouths—<br>How probable I do not know—that Martius,<br>Joined with Aufidius, leads a power ‘gainst Rome,<br>And vows revenge as spacious as between<br>The young’st and oldest thing. |
| SICINIUS | This is most likely! |
| BRUTUS | Raised only that the weaker sort may wish<br>Good Martius home again. |
| SICINIUS | The very trick on’t. |
| MENENIUS | This is unlikely.<br>He and Aufidius can no more atone<br>Than violent’st contrariety. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger |
| SECOND MESSENGER | You are sent for to the senate.<br>A fearful army, led by Caius Martius<br>Associated with Aufidius, rages<br>Upon our territories, and have already<br>O’erborne their way, consumed with fire and took<br>What lay before them. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Cominius |
| COMINIUS | O, you have made good work! |
| MENENIUS | What news? What news? |
| COMINIUS | You have holp to ravish your own daughters and<br>To melt the city leads upon your pates,<br>To see your wives dishonoured to your noses. |
| MENENIUS | What’s the news? What’s the news? |
| COMINIUS | Your temples burned in their cement, and<br>Your franchises, whereon you stood, confined<br>Into an auger’s bore. |
| MENENIUS | Pray now, your news?<br>(To the tribunes) You have made fair work, I fear me.<br>(To Cominius) Pray, your news.<br>If Martius should be joined wi’th’ Volscians— |
| COMINIUS | If? He is their god. He leads them like a thing<br>Made by some other deity than nature,<br>That shapes man better, and they follow him<br>Against us brats with no less confidence<br>Than boys pursuing summer butterflies,<br>Or butchers killing flies. |
| MENENIUS | (to the tribunes) You have made good work,<br>You and your apron-men, you that stood so much<br>Upon the voice of occupation and<br>The breath of garlic-eaters! |
| COMINIUS | (to the tribunes)<br>He’ll shake your Rome about your ears. |
| MENENIUS | As Hercules did shake down mellow fruit. (To the tribunes) You have made fair work. |
| BRUTUS | But is this true, sir? |
| COMINIUS | Ay, and you’ll look pale<br>Before you find it other. All the regions<br>Do smilingly revolt, and who resists<br>Are mocked for valiant ignorance,<br>And perish constant fools. Who is’t can blame him?<br>Your enemies and his find something in him. |
| MENENIVS | We are all undone unless<br>The noble man have mercy. |
| COMINIUS | Who shall ask it?<br>The tribunes cannot do‘t, for shame; the people<br>Deserve such pity of him as the wolf<br>Does of the shepherds. For his best friends, if they<br>Should say ‘Be good to Rome’, they charged him even<br>As those should do that had deserved his hate,<br>And therein showed like enemies. |
| MENENIUS | ’Tis true.<br>If he were putting to my house the brand<br>That should consume it, I have not the face<br>To say ‘Beseech you, cease.’<br>(To the tribunes) You have made fair hands,<br>You and your crafts! You have crafted fair! |
| COMINIUS | (to the tribunes)<br>You have brought<br>A trembling upon Rome such as was never<br>S’incapable of help. |
| SICINIUS | and BRUTUS Say not we brought it. |
| MENENIUS | How? Was’t we?<br>We loved him, but like beasts and cowardly nobles<br>Gave way unto your clusters, who did hoot<br>Him out o’th’ city. |
| COMINIUS | But I fear<br>They’ll roar him in again. Tullus Aufidius,<br>The second name of men, obeys his points<br>As if he were his officer. Desperation<br>Is all the policy, strength, and defence<br>That Rome can make against them. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a troop of Citizens |
| MENENIUS | Here come the clusters.<br>(To the Citizens) And is Aufidius with him? You are they<br>That made the air unwholesome when you cast<br>Your stinking greasy caps in hooting at<br>Coriolanus’ exile. Now he’s coming,<br>And not a hair upon a soldier’s head<br>Which will not prove a whip. As many coxcombs<br>As you threw caps up will he tumble down,<br>And pay you for your voices. ’Tis no matter.<br>If he could burn us all into one coal,<br>We have deserved it. |
| ALL THE CITIZENS | Faith, we hear fearful news. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | For mine own part,<br>When I said ‘banish him’ I said ’twas pity. |
| SECOND CITIZEN | And so did I. |
| THIRD CITIZEN | And so did I, and to say the truth so did very many of us. That we did, we did for the best, and though we willingly consented to his banishment, yet it was against our will. |
| COMINIUS | You’re goodly things, you voices. |
| MENENIUS | You have made good work,<br>You and your cry. Shall’s to the Capitol? |
| COMINIUS | O, ay, what else? |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Menenius and Cominius |
| SICINIUS | Go, masters, get you home. Be not dismayed.<br>These are a side that would be glad to have<br>This true which they so seem to fear. Go home,<br>And show no sign of fear. |
| FIRST CITIZEN | The gods be good to us! Come, masters, let’s home. I ever said we were i’th’ wrong when we banished him. |
| SECOND CITIZEN | So did we all. But come, let’s home. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Citizens |
| BRUTUS | I do not like this news. |
| SICINIUS | Nor I. |
| BRUTUS | Let’s to the Capitol. Would half my wealth<br>Would buy this for a lie. |
| SICINIUS | Pray let’s go. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
