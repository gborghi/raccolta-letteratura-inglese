| *(didascalia)* | Enter two Gamekeepers, with crossbows in their hands |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | Under this thick-grown brake we’ll shroud ourselves,<br>For through this laund anon the deer will come,<br>And in this covert will we make our stand,<br>Culling the principal of all the deer. |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | I’ll stay above the [[Hill|hill]], so both may shoot. |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | That cannot be—the noise of thy crossbow<br>Will scare the herd, and so my shoot is lost.<br>Here stand we both, and aim we at the best.<br>And, for the [[Time|time]] shall not seem tedious,<br>I’ll tell thee what befell me on a day<br>In this [[Self|self]] place where now we mean to stand. |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | Here comes a man—let’s stay till he be past.<br>They stand apart. Enter [[King]] Henry, disguised, carrying a [[prayer]]-book |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | From Scotland am I stolen, even of pure [[Love|love]],<br>To greet mine own land with my wishful sight.<br>No, Harry, Harry—’tis no land of thine.<br>Thy place is filled, thy sceptre wrung from thee,<br>Thy balm washed off wherewith thou wast anointed.<br>No bending knee will call thee Caesar now,<br>No humble suitors press to speak for right,<br>No, not a man comes for redress of thee—<br>For how can I help them and not myself? |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | (to the Second Gamekeeper)<br>Ay, here’s a deer whose skin’s a keeper’s fee:<br>This is the quondam [[King|king]]—let’s seize upon him. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Let me embrace thee, sour adversity,<br>For wise men say it is the wisest course. |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | (to the First Gamekeeper)<br>Why linger we? Let us lay hands upon him. |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | (to the Second Gamekeeper)<br>Forbear awhile—we’ll hear a little more. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | My [[Queen|queen]] and son are gone to France for aid,<br>And, as I hear, the great commanding Warwick<br>Is thither gone to crave the French [[King]]’s sister<br>To wife for Edward. If this news be true,<br>Poor [[Queen]] and son, your labour is but lost—<br>For Warwick is a subtle orator,<br>And Louis a prince soon won with moving words.<br>By this account, then, Margaret may win him—<br>For she’s a woman to be pitied much.<br>Her sighs will make a batt‘ry in his breast,<br>Her [[Tears|tears]] will pierce into a marble heart,<br>The tiger will be mild whiles she doth mourn,<br>And Nero will be tainted with remorse<br>To hear and see her plaints, her brinish [[Tears|tears]].<br>Ay, but she’s come to beg; Warwick to give.<br>She on his left side, craving aid for Henry;<br>He on his right, asking a wife for Edward.<br>She weeps and says her Henry is deposed,<br>He smiles and says his Edward is installed;<br>That she, poor wretch, for grief can speak no more,<br>Whiles Warwick tells his title, smooths the wrong,<br>Inferreth arguments of mighty strength,<br>And in conclusion wins the [[King]] from her<br>With promise of his sister and what else<br>To strengthen and support [[King]] Edward’s place.<br>O, Margaret, thus ’twill be; and thou, poor [[Soul|soul]],<br>Art then forsaken, as thou went’st forlorn. |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | (coming orward)<br>Say, what art thou that talk’st of kings and queens? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | More than I seem, and less than I was born to:<br>A man at least, for less I should not be;<br>And men may talk of kings, and why not I? |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | Ay, but thou talk’st as if thou wert a [[King|king]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why, so I am, in mind—and that’s enough. |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | But if thou be a [[King|king]], where is thy [[Crown|crown]]? |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | My [[Crown|crown]] is in my heart, not on my head;<br>Not decked with diamonds and Indian stones,<br>Nor to be seen. My [[Crown|crown]] is called content—<br>A [[Crown|crown]] it is that seldom kings enjoy. |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | [[Well]], if you be a [[King|king]] crowned with content,<br>Your [[Crown|crown]] content and you must be contented<br>To go along with us—for, as we think,<br>You are the [[King|king]] [[King]] Edward hath deposed,<br>And we his subjects sworn in all allegiance<br>Will apprehend you as his enemy. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | But did you never swear and break an oath? |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | No—never such an oath, nor will not now. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Where did you dwell when I was [[King]] of England? |
| SECOND GAMEKEEPER | Here in this country, where we now remain. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | I was anointed [[King|king]] at nine months old,<br>My father and my grandfather were kings,<br>And you were sworn true subjects unto me—<br>And tell me, then, have you not broke your oaths ? |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | No, for we were subjects but while you were [[King|king]]. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | Why, am I dead? Do I not breathe a man?<br>Ah, simple men, you know not what you swear.<br>Look as I blow this feather from my face,<br>And as the air blows it to me again,<br>Obeying with my [[Wind|wind]] when I do blow,<br>And yielding to another when it blows,<br>Commanded always by the greater gust—<br>Such is the lightness of you common men.<br>But do not break your oaths, for of that sin<br>My mild entreaty shall not make you guilty.<br>Go where you will, the [[King]] shall be commanded;<br>And be you kings, command, and I’ll obey. |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | We are true subjects to the [[King]], [[King]] Edward. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | So would you be again to Henry,<br>If he were seated as [[King]] Edward is. |
| FIRST GAMEKEEPER | We charge you, in God’s name and in the [[King]]’s,<br>To go with us unto the officers. |
| [[King|KING]] HENRY | In God’s name, lead; your [[King|king]]’s name be obeyed;<br>And what God will, that let your [[King|king]] perform;<br>And what he will I humbly yield unto. Exeunt |
