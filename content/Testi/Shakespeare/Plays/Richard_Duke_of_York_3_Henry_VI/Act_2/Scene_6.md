| *(didascalia)* | A loud alarum. Enter Lord [[Clifford]], wounded with an arrow in his neck |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | Here burns my candle out—ay, here it dies,<br>Which, whiles it lasted, gave [[King]] Henry light.<br>O Lancaster, I [[Fear|fear]] thy overthrow<br>More than my [[Body|body]]’s parting with my [[Soul|soul]]!<br>My [[Love|love]] and [[Fear|fear]] glued many friends to thee—<br>And, now I fall, thy tough commixture melts,<br>Impairing Henry, strength’ning misproud York.<br>The common people swarm like [[Summer|summer]] flies,<br>And whither fly the gnats but to the sun?<br>And who shines now but Henry’s enemies?<br>O Phoebus, hadst thou never given consent<br>That Phaeton should check thy fiery steeds,<br>Thy burning car never had scorched the earth!<br>And, Henry, hadst thou swayed as kings should do,<br>Or as thy father and his father did,<br>Giving no ground unto the [[house]] of York,<br>They never then had sprung like [[Summer|summer]] flies;<br>I and ten thousand in this luckless realm<br>Had left no mourning widows for our [[Death|death]];<br>And thou this day hadst kept thy chair in peace.<br>For what doth cherish weeds, but gentle air?<br>And what makes robbers bold, but too much lenity?<br>Bootless are plaints, and cureless are my wounds;<br>No way to fly, nor strength to hold out flight;<br>The foe is merciless and will not pity,<br>For at their hands I have deserved no pity.<br>The air hath got into my deadly wounds,<br>And much effuse of [[Blood|blood]] doth make me faint.<br>Come York and [[Richard]], Warwick and the rest—<br>I stabbed your fathers’ bosoms; split my breast. |
| *(didascalia)* | He faints. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum and retreat. Enter Edward [[Duke of York]], |
| [[Clifford|CLIFFORD]] | his brothers George and [[Richard]], the Earl of |
| *(didascalia)* | Warwick, the Marquis of Montague, and soldiers |
| EDWARD | Now breathe we, lords—good fortune bids us pause,<br>And smooth the frowns of war with peaceful looks.<br>Some troops pursue the bloody-minded [[Queen]],<br>That led calm Henry, though he were a [[King|king]],<br>As doth a sail filled with a fretting gust<br>Command an argosy to stem the waves.<br>But think you, lords, that [[Clifford]] fled with them? |
| WARWICK | No—‘tis impossible he should escape;<br>For, though before his face I speak the words,<br>Your brother [[Richard]] marked him for the [[Grave|grave]].<br>And whereso’er he is, he’s surely dead.<br>[[Clifford]] groans |
| *(didascalia)* | EDWARD |
| WARWICK | Whose [[Soul|soul]] is that which takes her heavy leave? |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Richard|RICHARD]] |
| WARWICK | A deadly groan, like life and [[Death|death]]’s departing. |
| *(didascalia)* | EDWARD to [[Richard]] |
| WARWICK | See who it is. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Richard]] goes to [[Clifford]] |
| WARWICK | And now the battle’s ended,<br>If friend or foe, let him be gently used. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Revoke that doom of [[Mercy|mercy]], for ‘tis [[Clifford]];<br>Who not contented that he lopped the branch<br>In hewing Rutland when his [[Leaves|leaves]] put forth,<br>But set his murd’ring knife unto the root<br>From whence that tender spray did sweetly [[spring]]—<br>I mean our princely father, [[Duke of York]]. |
| WARWICK | From off the gates of York fetch down the head,<br>Your father’s head, which [[Clifford]] placed there.<br>Instead whereof let this supply the room—<br>Measure for measure must be answerèd. |
| EDWARD | Bring forth that fatal screech-owl to our [[house]],<br>That nothing sung but [[Death|death]] to us and ours. |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Clifford]] is dragged forward |
| EDWARD | Now [[Death|death]] shall stop his dismal threat’ning sound<br>And his ill-boding tongue no more shall speak. |
| WARWICK | I think his understanding is bereft.<br>Speak, [[Clifford]], dost thou know who speaks to thee?<br>Dark cloudy [[Death|death]] o’ershades his beams of life,<br>And he nor sees nor hears us what we say. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | O, would he did—and so perhaps he doth.<br>’Tis but his policy to counterfeit,<br>Because he would avoid such bitter taunts<br>Which in the [[Time|time]] of [[Death|death]] he gave our father. |
| GEORGE | If so thou think’st, vex him with eager words. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | [[Clifford]], ask [[Mercy|mercy]] and obtain no grace. |
| EDWARD | [[Clifford]], repent in bootless penitence. |
| WARWICK | [[Clifford]], devise excuses for thy faults. |
| GEORGE | While we devise fell tortures for thy faults. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Thou didst [[Love|love]] York, and I am son to York. |
| EDWARD | Thou pitied’st Rutland—I will pity thee. |
| GEORGE | Where’s Captain Margaret to fence you now? |
| WARWICK | They mock thee, [[Clifford]]—swear as thou wast wont. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | What, not an oath? Nay, then, the world goes hard<br>When [[Clifford]] cannot spare his friends an oath.<br>I know by that he’s dead—and, by my [[Soul|soul]],<br>If this right hand would buy but two hours’ life<br>That I, in all despite, might rail at him,<br>This hand should chop it off, and with the issuing<br>[[Blood|blood]]<br>Stifle the [[Villain|villain]] whose unstanchèd thirst<br>York and young Rutland could not satisfy. |
| WARWICK | Ay, but he’s dead. Off with the traitor’s head,<br>And rear it in the place your father’s stands.<br>And now to [[London]] with triumphant march,<br>There to be crowned England’s royal [[King|king]];<br>From whence shall Warwick cut the sea to France,<br>And ask the Lady Bona for thy [[Queen|queen]].<br>So shalt thou sinew both these lands together.<br>And, having France thy friend, thou shalt not dread<br>The scattered foe that hopes to rise again,<br>For though they cannot greatly sting to hurt,<br>Yet look to have them buzz to offend thine ears.<br>First will I see the coronation,<br>And then to Brittany I’ll [[Cross|cross]] the sea<br>To effect this [[Marriage|marriage]], so it please my lord. |
| EDWARD | Even as thou wilt, sweet Warwick, let it be.<br>For in thy shoulder do I build my seat,<br>And never will I undertake the thing<br>Wherein thy counsel and consent is wanting.<br>[[Richard]], I will create thee [[Duke of Gloucester]],<br>And George, of [[Clarence]]; Warwick, as ourself,<br>Shall do and undo as him pleaseth best. |
| [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Let me be Duke of [[Clarence]], George of [[Gloucester]]—<br>For [[Gloucester]]’s dukedom is too ominous. |
| WARWICK | Tut, that’s a foolish observation—<br>[[Richard]], be [[Duke of Gloucester]]. Now to [[London]]<br>To see these honours in possession. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt. York’s head is removed |
