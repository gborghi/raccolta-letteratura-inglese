| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Warwick, the Mayar of Coventry, two Messengers, and others upon the walls |
| WARWICK | Where is the post that came from valiant Oxford? |
| *(didascalia)* | The First Messenger steps forward |
| WARWICK | How far hence is thy lord, mine honest fellow? |
| FIRST MESSENGER | By this at Dunsmore, marching hitherward. |
| WARWICK | How far off is our brother Montague?<br>Where is the post that came from Montague? |
| *(didascalia)* | the Second Messenger steps forward |
| SECOND MESSENGER | By this at Da’ntry, with a puissant troop. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Somerville to them, abovel |
| WARWICK | Say, Somerville-what says my loving son?<br>And, by thy guess, how nigh is Clarence now? |
| SOMERVILLE | At Southam I did leave him with his forces,<br>And do expect him here some two hours hence.<br>A march afar off |
| WARWICK | Then Clarence is at hand-I hear his drum. |
| SOMERVILLE | It is not his, my lord. Here Southam lies.<br>The drum your honour hears marcheth from Warwick. |
| WARWICK | Who should that be? Belike, unlooked-for friends. |
| SOMERVILLE | They are at hand, and you shall quickly know. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter below King Edward and Richard Duke of Gloucester, with soldiers |
| KING EDWARD | Go, trumpet, to the walls, and sound a parley. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound a parley |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | See how the surly Warwick mans the wall. |
| WARWICK | O, unbid spite—is sportful Edward come?<br>Where slept our scouts, or how are they seduced,<br>That we could hear no news of his repair? |
| KING EDWARD | Now, Warwick, wilt thou ope the city gates,<br>Speak gentle words, and humbly bend thy knee,<br>Call Edward king, and at his hands beg mercy?<br>And he shall pardon thee these outrages. |
| WARWICK | Nay, rather, wilt thou draw thy forces hence,<br>Confess who set thee up and plucked thee down,<br>Call Warwick patron, and be penitent?<br>And thou shalt still remain the Duke of York. |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | I thought at least he would have said ‘the King’.<br>Or did he make the jest against his will? |
| WARWICK | Is not a dukedom, sir, a goodly gift? |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | Ay, by my faith, for a poor earl to give.<br>I’ll do thee service for so good a gift. |
| WARWICK | ’Twas I that gave the kingdom to thy brother. |
| KING EDWARD | Why then, ’tis mine, if but by Warwick’s gift. |
| WARWICK | Thou art no Atlas for so great a weight;<br>And, weakling, Warwick takes his gift again;<br>And Henry is my king, Warwick his subject. |
| KING EDWARD | But Warwick’s king is Edward’s prisoner,<br>And, gallant Warwick, do but answer this:<br>What is the body when the head is off? |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | Alas, that Warwick had no more forecast,<br>But whiles he thought to steal the single ten,<br>The king was slyly fingered from the deck. |
| *(didascalia)* | To Warwick You left poor Henry at the Bishop’s palace, |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | And ten to one you’ll meet him in the Tower. |
| *(didascalia)* | ’Tis even so—to Warwick yet you are Warwick still. |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | Come, Warwick, take the time—kneel down, kneel<br>down.<br>Nay, when? Strike now, or else the iron cools. |
| WARWICK | I had rather chop this hand off at a blow,<br>And with the other fling it at thy face,<br>Than bear so low a sail to strike to thee. |
| KING EDWARD | Sail how thou canst, have wind and tide thy friend,<br>This hand, fast wound about thy coal-black hair,<br>Shall, whiles thy head is warm and new cut off,<br>Write in the dust this sentence with thy blood:<br>‘Wind-changing Warwick now can change no more’. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Earl of Oxford, with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| WARWICK | O cheerful colours! See where Oxford comes. |
| OXFORD | Oxford, Oxford, for Lancaster! |
| *(didascalia)* | Oxford and his men pass over the stage and exeunt into the city |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | (to King Edward)<br>The gates are open—let us enter too. |
| KING EDWARD | So other foes may set upon our backs?<br>Stand we in good array, for they no doubt<br>Will issue out again and bid us battle.<br>If not, the city being but of small defence,<br>We’ll quickly rouse the traitors in the same. |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK to Oxford, within |
| KING EDWARD | O, welcome, Oxford—for we want thy help. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Marquis of Montague with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| MONTAGUE | Montague, Montague, for Lancaster! |
| *(didascalia)* | Montague and his men pass over the stage and exeunt into the city |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | Thou and thy brother both shall bye this treason<br>Even with the dearest blood your bodies bear. |
| KING EDWARD | The harder matched, the greater victory.<br>My mind presageth happy gain and conquest. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Duke of Somerset with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| SOMERSET | Somerset, Somerset, for Lancaster! |
| *(didascalia)* | Somerset and his men pass over the stage and exeunt into the city |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | Two of thy name, both dukes of Somerset,<br>Have sold their lives unto the house of York—<br>And thou shalt be the third, an this sword hold. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter George Duke of Clarence with a drummer and soldiers bearing colours |
| WARWICK | And lo, where George of Clarence sweeps along,<br>Of force enough to bid his brother battle;<br>With whom an upright zeal to right prevails<br>More than the nature of a brother’s love. |
| GEORGE OF CLARENCE | Clarence, Clarence, for Lancaster! |
| KING EDWARD | Et tu, Brute—wilt thou stab Caesar too?<br>(To a trumpeter) A parley, sirra, to George of Clarence. |
| *(didascalia)* | Sound a parley. Richard of Gloucester and George of |
| KING EDWARD | Clarence whisper together |
| WARWICK | Come, Clarence, come—thou wilt if Warwick call. |
| GEORGE OF CLARENCE | Father of Warwick, know you what this means? |
| *(didascalia)* | He takes his red rose out of his hat and throws it at Warwick |
| GEORGE OF CLARENCE | Look—here I throw my infamy at thee!<br>I will not ruinate my father’s house,<br>Who gave his blood to lime the stones together,<br>And set up Lancaster. Why, trowest thou, Warwick,<br>That Clarence is so harsh, so blunt, unnatural,<br>To bend the fatal instruments of war<br>Against his brother and his lawful king?<br>Perhaps thou wilt object my holy oath.<br>To keep that oath were more impiety<br>Than Jephthah, when he sacrificed his daughter.<br>I am so sorry for my trespass made<br>That, to deserve well at my brothers’ hands,<br>I here proclaim myself thy mortal foe,<br>With resolution, wheresoe’er I meet thee—<br>As I will meet thee, if thou stir abroad—<br>To plague thee for thy foul misleading me.<br>And so, proud-hearted Warwick, I defy thee,<br>And to my brothers turn my blushing cheeks.<br>(To King Edward)<br>Pardon me, Edward—I will make amends.<br>(To Richard)<br>And, Richard, do not frown upon my faults,<br>For I will henceforth be no more unconstant. |
| KING EDWARD | Now welcome more, and ten times more beloved,<br>Than if thou never hadst deserved our hate. |
| RICHARD OF GLOUCESTER | (to George)<br>Welcome, good Clarence—this is brother-like. |
| WARWICK | (to George)<br>O, passing traitor—perjured and unjust! |
| KING EDWARD | What, Warwick, wilt thou leave the town and fight?<br>Or shall we beat the stones about thine ears? |
| *(didascalia)* | WARWICK aside |
| KING EDWARD | Alas, I am not cooped here for defence.<br><br>(To King Edward)<br>I will away towards Barnet presently,<br>And bid thee battle, Edward, if thou dar’st. |
| KING EDWARD | Yes, Warwick—Edward dares, and leads the way.<br>Lords, to the Held—Saint George and victory! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt below King Edward and his company. |
| KING EDWARD | March. The Earl of Warwick and his company<br>descend and follow |
