| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Touchstone]] the clown and Audrey, followed by [[Jaques]] |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Come apace, good Audrey. I will fetch up your goats, Audrey. And how, Audrey, am I the man yet? Doth my simple feature content you? |
| AUDREY | Your features, Lord warrant us—what features? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | I am here with thee and thy goats as the most capricious poet honest Ovid was among the Goths. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | (aside) O knowledge ill-inhabited; worse than Jove in a thatched [[house]]. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | When a man’s verses cannot be understood, nor a man’s good wit seconded with the forward [[Child|child]], understanding, it strikes a man more dead than a great reckoning in a little room. Truly, I would the gods had made thee poetical. |
| AUDREY | I do not know what ‘poetical’ is. Is it honest in deed and word? Is it a true thing? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | No, truly; for the truest poetry is the most feigning, and [[lovers]] are given to poetry; and what they swear in poetry it may be said, as [[lovers]], they do feign. |
| AUDREY | Do you wish, then, that the gods had made me poetical? |
| TOUCHTONE | I do, truly; for thou swearest to me thou art honest. Now if thou wert a poet, I might have some [[Hope|hope]] thou didst feign. |
| AUDREY | Would you not have me honest? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | No, truly, unless thou wert hard-favoured; for honesty coupled to [[Beauty|beauty]] is to have honey a sauce to sugar. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | (aside) A material [[Fool|fool]]. |
| AUDREY | [[Well]], I am not fair, and therefore I pray the gods make me honest. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Truly, and to cast away honesty upon a foul slut were to put good meat into an unclean dish. |
| AUDREY | I am not a slut, though I thank the gods I am foul. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | [[Well]], praised be the gods for thy foulness. Sluttishness may come hereafter. But be it as it may be, I will marry thee; and to that end I have been with Sir [[Oliver]] Martext, the vicar of the next village, who hath promised to meet me in this place of the forest, and to couple us. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | (aside) I would fain see this meeting. |
| AUDREY | [[Well]], the gods give us joy. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Amen.—A man may, if he were of a fearful heart, stagger in this attempt; for here we have no temple but the wood, no assembly but horn-beasts. But what though? [[Courage]]. As horns are odious, they are necessary. It is said many a man knows no end of his goods. Right: many a man has good horns, and knows no end of them. [[Well]], that is the dowry of his wife, ‘tis none of his own getting. Horns? Even so. Poor men [[Alone|alone]]? No, no; the noblest deer hath them as huge as the rascal. Is the single man therefore blessed? No. As a walled town is more worthier than a village, so is the forehead of a married man more honourable than the bare brow of a bachelor. And by how much defence is better than no skill, by so much is a horn more precious than to want. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir [[Oliver]] Martext |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Here comes Sir [[Oliver]].—Sir [[Oliver]] Martext, you are<br>[[Well|well]] met. Will you dispatch us here under this tree, or<br>shall we go with you to your chapel? |
| SIR [[Oliver|OLIVER]] MARTEXT | Is there none here to give the woman? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | I will not take her on gift of any man. |
| SIR [[Oliver|OLIVER]] MARTEXT | Truly she must be given, or the [[Marriage|marriage]] is not lawful. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | (coming forward) Proceed, proceed. I’ll give her. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Good even, good Monsieur What-ye-call’t. How do you, sir? You are very [[Well|well]] met. God’ield you for your last company. I am very glad to see you. Even a toy in hand here, sir.<br>[[Jaques]] removes his hat<br>Nay, pray be covered. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | Will you be married, motley? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | As the ox hath his bow, sir, the [[Horse|horse]] his curb, and the falcon her [[Bells|bells]], so man hath his desires; and as pigeons bill, so wedlock would be nibbling. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | And will you, being a man of your breeding, be married under a bush, like a beggar? Get you to church, and have a good priest that can tell you what [[Marriage|marriage]] is. This fellow will but join you together as they join wainscot; then one of you will prove a shrunk panel and, like green timber, warp, warp. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | I am not in the mind but I were better to be married of him than of another, for he is not like to marry me [[Well|well]], and not being [[Well|well]] married, it will be a good excuse for me hereafter to leave my wife. |
| [[Jaques|JAQUES]] | Go thou with me, and let me counsel thee. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Come, sweet Audrey.<br>We must be married, or we must live in bawdry.<br>Farewell, good Master [[Oliver]]. Not<br>O, sweet [[Oliver]],<br>O, brave [[Oliver]],<br>Leave me not behind thee<br>but |
| *(didascalia)* | [[Wind]] away, |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Begone, I say,<br>I will not to wedding with thee. |
| SIR [[Oliver|OLIVER]] MARTEXT | (aside) ‘Tis no matter. Ne’er a fantastical knave of them all shall flout me out of my calling. Exeunt |
