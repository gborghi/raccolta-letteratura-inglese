| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Rosalind]] in man’s clothes as Ganymede; [[Celia]] as Aliena, a shepherdess; and [[Touchstone]] the clown |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | O Jupiter, how weary are my spirits! |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | I care not for my spirits, if my legs were not weary. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I could find in my heart to disgrace my man’s apparel and to cry like a woman. But I must comfort the weaker vessel, as doublet and hose ought to show itself courageous to petticoat; therefore, [[Courage|courage]], good Aliena! |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | I pray you, bear with me. I cannot go no further. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | For my part, I had rather bear with you than bear you. Yet I should bear no [[Cross|cross]] if I did bear you, for I think you have no money in your purse. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | [[Well]], this is the forest of Ardenne. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Ay, now am I in Ardenne; the more [[Fool|fool]] I. When I was at [[Home|home]] I was in a better place; but travellers must be content. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Corin and [[Silvius]] |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Ay, be so, good [[Touchstone]]. Look you, who<br>comes here—a young man and an old in solemn talk. |
| CORIN | (to [[Silvius]])<br>That is the way to make her scorn you still. |
| [[Silvius|SILVIUS]] | O Corin, that thou knew’st how I do [[Love|love]] her! |
| CORIN | I partly guess; for I have loved ere now. |
| [[Silvius|SILVIUS]] | No, Corin, being old thou canst not guess,<br>Though in thy youth thou wast as true a [[Lover|lover]]<br>As ever sighed upon a midnight pillow.<br>But if thy [[Love|love]] were ever like to mine—<br>As sure I think did never man [[Love|love]] so—<br>How many actions most ridiculous<br>Hast thou been drawn to by thy fantasy? |
| CORIN | Into a thousand that I have forgotten. |
| [[Silvius|SILVIUS]] | O, thou didst then never [[Love|love]] so heartily.<br>If thou rememberest not the slightest folly<br>That ever [[Love|love]] did make thee run into,<br>Thou hast not loved.<br>Or if thou hast not sat as I do now,<br>Wearing thy hearer in thy mistress’ praise,<br>Thou hast not loved.<br>Or if thou hast not broke from company<br>Abruptly, as my passion now makes me,<br>Thou hast not loved.<br>O, [[Phoebe]], [[Phoebe]], [[Phoebe]]! Exit |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Alas, poor shepherd, searching of thy wound,<br>I have by hard adventure found mine own. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | And I mine. I remember when I was in [[Love|love]] I broke my [[Sword|sword]] upon a stone and bid him take that for coming a-[[Night|night]] to Jane Smile, and I remember the kissing of her batlet, and the cow’s dugs that her pretty chapped hands had milked; and I remember the wooing of a peascod instead of her, from whom I took two cods, and giving her them again, said with weeping [[Tears|tears]], ‘Wear these for my sake.’ We that are true [[lovers]] run into strange capers. But as all is mortal in [[Nature|nature]], so is all [[Nature|nature]] in [[Love|love]] mortal in folly. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Thou speak’st wiser than thou art ware of. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Nay, I shall ne’er be ware of mine own wit till I break my shins against it. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Jove, Jove, this shepherd’s passion<br>Is much upon my fashion. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | And mine, but it grows something stale with me. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | I pray you, one of you question yon man<br>If he for gold will give us any food.<br>I faint almost to [[Death|death]]. |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | (to Corin) Holla, you clown! |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | Peace, [[Fool|fool]], he’s not thy kinsman. |
| CORIN | Who calls? |
| [[Touchstone|TOUCHSTONE]] | Your betters, sir. |
| CORIN | Else are they very wretched. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | (to [[Touchstone]])<br>Peace, I say. (To Corin) Good even to you, friend. |
| CORIN | And to you, gentle sir, and to you all. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I prithee, shepherd, if that [[Love|love]] or gold<br>Can in this [[Desert|desert]] place buy entertainment,<br>Bring us where we may rest ourselves, and feed.<br>Here’s a young maid with travel much oppressed,<br>And faints for succour. |
| CORIN | Fair sir, I pity her,<br>And wish, for her sake more than for mine own,<br>My fortunes were more able to relieve her.<br>But I am shepherd to another man,<br>And do not shear the fleeces that I graze.<br>My master is of churlish disposition,<br>And little recks to find the way to heaven<br>By doing deeds of hospitality.<br>Besides, his cot, his flocks, and bounds of feed<br>Are now on sale, and at our sheepcote now<br>By reason of his absence there is nothing<br>That you will feed on. But what is, come see,<br>And in my voice most welcome shall you be. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | What is he that shall buy his flock and pasture? |
| CORIN | That young swain that you saw here but erewhile,<br>That little cares for buying anything. |
| [[Rosalind|ROSALIND]] | I pray thee, if it stand with honesty,<br>Buy thou the cottage, pasture, and the flock,<br>And thou shalt have to pay for it of us. |
| [[Celia|CELIA]] | And we will mend thy wages. I like this place,<br>And willingly could waste my [[Time|time]] in it. |
| CORIN | Assuredly the thing is to be sold.<br>Go with me. If you like upon report<br>The soil, the profit, and this kind of life,<br>I will your very faithful feeder be,<br>And buy it with your gold right suddenly. Exeunt |
