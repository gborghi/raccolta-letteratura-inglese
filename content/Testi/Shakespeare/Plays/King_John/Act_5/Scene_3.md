| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Enter [[King John]] Fat one door and [[Hubert]] at another door |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | How goes the day with us? O, tell me, [[Hubert]]. |
| [[Hubert|HUBERT]] | Badly, I [[Fear|fear]]. How fares your majesty? |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | This fever that hath troubled me so long<br>Lies heavy on me. O, my heart is sick! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | My lord, your valiant kinsman Falconbridge<br>Desires your majesty to leave the field,<br>And send him word by me which way you go. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | Tell him toward Swineshead, to the abbey there. |
| MESSENGER | Be of good comfort, for the great supply<br>That was expected by the [[Dauphin]] here<br>Are wrecked three nights ago on Goodwin Sands.<br>This news was brought to [[Richard]], but even now<br>The French fight coldly and retire themselves. |
| [[King John|KING JOHN]] | Ay me, this [[Tyrant|tyrant]] fever burns me up,<br>And will not let me welcome this good news.<br>Set on toward Swineshead. To my litter straight;<br>Weakness possesseth me, and I am faint. Exeunt |
| *(didascalia)* | 5.4 Alarum.Enter the Earls of Salisbury and Pembroke, and Lord Bigot |
| SALISBURY | I did not think the [[King]] so stored with friends. |
| PEMBROKE | Up once again; put spirit in the French.<br>If they miscarry, we miscarry too. |
| SALISBURY | That misbegotten devil Falconbridge,<br>In spite of spite, [[Alone|alone]] upholds the day. |
| PEMBROKE | They say [[King John]], sore sick, hath left the field. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Count Melun, wounded, led by a soldier |
| MELUN | Lead me to the revolts of England here. |
| SALISBURY | When we were happy, we had other names. |
| PEMBROKE | It is the Count Melun. |
| SALISBURY | Wounded to [[Death|death]]. |
| MELUN | Fly, noble English, you are bought and sold.<br>Unthread the rude eye of rebellion,<br>And welcome [[Home|home]] again discarded [[Faith|faith]];<br>Seek out [[King John]] and fall before his feet,<br>For if the French be lords of this loud day<br>He means to recompense the pains you take<br>By cutting off your heads. Thus hath he sworn,<br>And I with him, and many more with me,<br>Upon the altar at Saint Edmundsbury,<br>Even on that altar where we swore to you<br>Dear amity and everlasting [[Love|love]]. |
| SALISBURY | May this be possible? May this be true? |
| MELUN | Have I not hideous [[Death|death]] within my view,<br>Retaining but a quantity of life,<br>Which bleeds away, even as a form of wax<br>Resolveth from his figure ‘gainst the [[Fire|fire]]?<br>What in the world should make me now deceive,<br>Since I must lose the use of all deceit?<br>Why should I then be false, since it is true<br>That I must die here, and live hence by [[Truth|truth]]?<br>I say again, if Louis do win the day,<br>He is forsworn if e’er those eyes of yours<br>Behold another daybreak in the east;<br>But even this [[Night|night]], whose black contagious breath<br>Already smokes about the burning cresset<br>Of the old, feeble, and day-wearied sun,<br>Even this ill [[Night|night]] your breathing shall expire,<br>Paying the fine of rated treachery<br>Even with a treacherous fine of all your lives,<br>If Louis by your assistance win the day.<br>Commend me to one [[Hubert]] with your [[King|king]].<br>The [[Love|love]] of him, and this respect besides,<br>For that my grandsire was an Englishman,<br>Awakes my [[Conscience|conscience]] to confess all this;<br>In lieu whereof, I pray you bear me hence<br>From forth the noise and rumour of the field,<br>Where I may think the remnant of my thoughts<br>In peace, and part this [[Body|body]] and my [[Soul|soul]]<br>With contemplation and devout desires. |
| SALISBURY | We do believe thee; and beshrew my [[Soul|soul]]<br>But I do [[Love|love]] the favour and the form<br>Of this most fair occasion; by the which<br>We will untread the steps of damned flight,<br>And like a bated and retired flood,<br>Leaving our rankness and irregular course,<br>Stoop low within those bounds we have o’erlooked,<br>And calmly run on in obedience<br>Even to our ocean, to our great [[King John]].<br>My arm shall give thee help to bear thee hence,<br>For I do see the cruel pangs of [[Death|death]]<br>Right in thine eye.—Away,my friends! New flight,<br>And happy newness that intends old right. Exeunt |
| *(didascalia)* | 5.5 Alarum,. retreat. Enter Louis the [[Dauphin]], and his train |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | The sun of heaven, methought, was loath to set,<br>But stayed and made the western welkin blush,<br>When English measured backward their own ground<br>In faint retire. O, bravely came we off,<br>When with a volley of our needless shot,<br>After such bloody toil, we bid good [[Night|night]],<br>And wound our tatt’ring colours clearly up,<br>Last in the field and almost lords of it. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | Where is my prince the [[Dauphin]]? |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Here. What news? |
| MESSENGER | The Count Melun is slain; the English lords<br>By his [[Persuasion|persuasion]] are again fall’n off;<br>And your supply which you have wished so long<br>Are cast away and sunk on Goodwin Sands. |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | Ah, foul shrewd news! Beshrew thy very heart<br>I did not think to be so sad tonight<br>As this hath made me. Who was he that said<br>[[King John]] did fly an hour or two before<br>The stumbling [[Night|night]] did part our weary powers? |
| MESSENGER | Whoever spoke it, it is true, my lord. |
| LOUIS THE [[Dauphin|DAUPHIN]] | [[Well]], keep good quarter and good care tonight.<br>The day shall not be up so soon as I,<br>To try the fair adventure of tomorrow. Exeunt |
