| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Othello]], [[Desdemona]], [[Cassio]], and attendants |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Good [[Michael]], look you to the guard tonight.<br>Let’s teach ourselves that honourable stop<br>Not to outsport discretion. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | [[Iago]] hath direction what to do,<br>But notwithstanding, with my personal eye<br>Will I look to’t. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | [[Iago]] is most honest.<br>[[Michael]], good [[Night|night]]. Tomorrow with your earliest<br>Let me have speech with you. (To [[Desdemona]]) Come,<br>my dear [[Love|love]],<br>The purchase made, the fruits are to ensue.<br>That profit’s yet to come ’tween me and you.<br>(To [[Cassio]]) Good [[Night|night]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Othello]], [[Desdemona]], and attendants |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Iago]] |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Welcome, [[Iago]]. We must to the watch. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Not this hour, lieutenant; ‘tis not yet ten o’th’ [[Clock|clock]]. Our general cast us thus early for the [[Love|love]] of his [[Desdemona]], who let us not therefore blame. He hath not yet made wanton the [[Night|night]] with her, and she is sport for Jove. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | She’s a most exquisite lady. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | And I’ll warrant her full of game. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Indeed, she’s a most fresh and delicate creature. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | What an eye she has! Methinks it sounds a parley to provocation. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | An inviting eye, and yet, methinks, right modest. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | And when she speaks, is it not an alarum to [[Love|love]]? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | She is indeed perfection. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | [[Well]], happiness to their sheets. Come, lieutenant. I have a stoup of wine, and here without are a brace of Cyprus gallants that would fain have a measure to the health of black [[Othello]]. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Not tonight, good [[Iago]]. I have very poor and unhappy brains for drinking. I could [[Well|well]] wish courtesy would invent some other custom of entertainment. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | O, they are our friends! But one cup. I’ll drink for you. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I ha’ drunk but one cup tonight, and that was craftily qualified, too, and behold what innovation it makes here! I am infortunate in the infirmity, and dare not task my weakness with any more. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | What, man, ’tis a [[Night|night]] of revels, the gallants [[Desire|desire]] it! |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Where are they? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Here at the door. I pray you call them in. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I’ll do’t, but it dislikes me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | If I can fasten but one cup upon him,<br>With that which he hath drunk tonight already<br>He’ll be as full of quarrel and offence<br>As my young mistress’ dog. Now my sick [[Fool|fool]] [[Roderigo]],<br>Whom [[Love|love]] hath turned almost the wrong side out,<br>To [[Desdemona]] hath tonight caroused<br>Potations pottle-deep, and he’s to watch.<br>Three else of Cyprus—noble swelling spirits<br>That hold their honours in a wary distance,<br>The very elements of this warlike isle—<br>Have I tonight flustered with flowing cups,<br>And they watch too. Now ’mongst this flock of<br>drunkards<br>Am I to put our [[Cassio]] in some action<br>That may offend the isle. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Montano, [[Cassio]], Gentlemen, [and servants] with wine |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | But here they come.<br>If consequence do but approve my dream,<br>My boat sails freely both with [[Wind|wind]] and stream. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Fore God, they have given me a rouse already. |
| MONTANO | Good [[Faith|faith]], a little one; not past a pint,<br>As I am a soldier. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Some wine, ho!<br>(Sings)<br>And let me the cannikin clink, clink, And let me the cannikin clink.<br>A soldier’s a man,<br>O, man’s life’s but a span,<br>Why then, let a soldier drink.<br>Some wine, boys! |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Fore God, an excellent [[Song|song]]. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | I learned it in England, where indeed they are most potent in potting. Your Dane, your German, and your swag-bellied Hollander—drink, ho!—are nothing to your English. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Is your Englishman so exquisite in his drinking? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Why, he drinks you with facility your Dane dead drunk. He sweats not to overthrow your Almain. He gives your Hollander a vomit ere the next pottle can be filled. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | To the health of our general! |
| MONTANO | I am for it, lieutenant, and I’ll do you justice. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | O sweet England!<br>(Sings)<br>[[King]] Stephen was and a worthy peer, His breeches cost him but a [[Crown|crown]];<br>He held them sixpence all too dear,<br>With that he called the tailor lown.<br>He was a wight of high renown,<br>And thou art but of low degree.<br>’Tis [[Pride|pride]] that pulls the country down,<br>Then take thy auld cloak about thee.<br>Some wine, ho! |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Fore God, this is a more exquisite [[Song|song]] than the other. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Will you hear’t again? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | No, for I hold him to be unworthy of his place that does those things. [[Well]], God’s above all, and there be souls must be saved, and there be souls must not be saved. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | It’s true, good lieutenant. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | For mine own part—no offence to the general, nor any man of quality—I [[Hope|hope]] to be saved. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | And so do I too, lieutenant. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Ay, but, by your leave, not before me. The lieutenant is to be saved before the ensign. Let’s ha’ no more of this. Let’s to our affairs. God forgive us our sins. Gentlemen, let’s look to our business. Do not think, gentlemen, I am drunk. This is my ensign, this is my right hand, and this is my left. I am not drunk now. I can stand [[Well|well]] enough, and I speak [[Well|well]] enough. |
| GENTLEMEN | Excellent [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Why, very [[Well|well]] then. You must not think then that I am drunk. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| MONTANO | To th’ platform, masters. Come, let’s set the watch. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Gentlemen |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | You see this fellow that is gone before—<br>He’s a soldier fit to stand by Caesar<br>And give direction; and do but see his vice.<br>‘Tis to his virtue a just equinox,<br>The one as long as th’other. ’Tis pity of him.<br>I [[Fear|fear]] the trust [[Othello]] puts him in,<br>On some odd [[Time|time]] of his infirmity,<br>Will shake this island. |
| MONTANO | But is he often thus? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | ’Tis evermore his prologue to his [[Sleep|sleep]].<br>He’ll watch the horologe a [[Double|double]] set<br>If drink rock not his cradle. |
| MONTANO | It were [[Well|well]]<br>The general were put in mind of it.<br>Perhaps he sees it not, or his good [[Nature|nature]]<br>Prizes the virtue that appears in [[Cassio]],<br>And looks not on his evils. Is not this true? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Roderigo]] |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | [aside] How now, [[Roderigo]]!<br>I pray you after the lieutenant, go. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Roderigo]] |
| MONTANO | And ’tis great pity that the noble Moor<br>Should hazard such a place as his own second<br>With one of an engraffed infirmity.<br>It were an honest action to say so<br>To the Moor. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Not I, for this fair island!<br>I do [[Love|love]] [[Cassio]] [[Well|well]], and would do much<br>To cure him of this evil. |
| VOICES | (within) Help, help! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | But hark, what noise? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Cassio]], driving in [[Roderigo]] |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | ’Swounds, you rogue, you rascal! |
| MONTANO | What’s the matter, lieutenant? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | A knave teach me my duty?—I’ll beat the knave into a twiggen bottle. |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | Beat me? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Dost thou prate, rogue? |
| MONTANO | Nay, good lieutenant, I pray you, sir, hold your hand. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Let me go, sir, or I’ll knock you o’er the mazard. |
| MONTANO | Come, come, you’re drunk. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Drunk?<br>They fight |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | (to [[Roderigo]])<br>Away, I say. Go out and cry a mutiny. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Roderigo]] |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Nay, good lieutenant. God’s will, gentlemen!<br>Help, ho! Lieutenant! Sir! Montano! Sir!<br>Help, masters. Here’s a goodly watch indeed.<br>A bell rung<br><br>Who’s that which rings the bell? Diablo, ho!<br>The town will rise. God’s will, lieutenant, hold.<br>You’ll be ashamed for ever. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Othello]] and attendants, with weapons |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | What is the matter here? |
| MONTANO | ’Swounds, I bleed still. I am hurt to th’ [[Death|death]].<br>(Attacking [[Cassio]]) He dies. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Hold, for your lives! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Hold, ho, lieutenant, sir, Montano, gentlemen!<br>Have you forgot all place of sense and duty?<br>Hold, the general speaks to you. Hold, hold, for shame. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Why, how now, ho? From whence ariseth this?<br>Are we turned Turks, and to ourselves do that<br>Which heaven hath forbid the Ottomites?<br>For Christian shame, put by this barbarous brawl.<br>He that stirs next to carve for his own rage<br>Holds his [[Soul|soul]] light. He dies upon his motion.<br>[[Silence]] that dreadful belt—it frights the isle<br>From her propriety. |
| *(didascalia)* | Bell stops |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | What is the matter, masters?<br>Honest [[Iago]], that looks dead with grieving,<br>Speak. Who began this? On thy [[Love|love]] I charge thee. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | I do not know. Friends all but now, even now,<br>In quarter and in terms like bride and groom<br>Devesting them for bed; and then but now—<br>As if some planet had unwitted men—<br>Swords out, and tilting one at others’ breasts<br>In opposition bloody. I cannot speak<br>Any beginning to this peevish odds,<br>And would in action glorious I had lost<br>Those legs that brought me to a part of it. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | How comes it, [[Michael]], you are thus forgot? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I pray you pardon me. I cannot speak. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Worthy Montano, you were wont be civil.<br>The gravity and stillness of your youth<br>The world hath noted, and your name is great<br>In mouths of wisest censure. What’s the matter,<br>That you unlace your reputation thus,<br>And spend your rich opinion for the name<br>Of a [[Night|night]]-brawler? Give me answer to it. |
| MONTANO | Worthy [[Othello]], I am hurt to danger. 1<br>Your officer [[Iago]] can inform you,<br>While I spare speech—which something now offends<br>me—<br>Of all that I do know; nor know I aught<br>By me that’s said or done amiss this [[Night|night]],<br>Unless [[Self|self]]-[[Charity|charity]] be sometimes a vice,<br>And to defend ourselves it be a sin<br>When violence assails us. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Now, by heaven,<br>My [[Blood|blood]] begins my safer guides to rule,<br>And passion, having my best judgement collied,<br>Essays to lead the way. ‘Swounds, if I stir,<br>Or do but lift this arm, the best of you<br>Shall sink in my rebuke. Give me to know<br>How this foul rout began, who set it on,<br>And he that is approved in this offence,<br>Though he had twinned with me, both at a birth,<br>Shall lose me. What, in a town of war<br>Yet wild, the people’s hearts brimful of [[Fear|fear]],<br>To manage private and domestic quarrel<br>In [[Night|night]], and on the [[Court|court]] and guard of safety!<br>’Tis monstrous. [[Iago]], who began’t? |
| MONTANO | (to [[Iago]])<br>If partially affined or leagued in office<br>Thou dost deliver more or less than [[Truth|truth]],<br>Thou art no soldier. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Touch me not so near.<br>I had rather ha’ this tongue cut from my mouth<br>Than it should do offence to [[Michael]] [[Cassio]].<br>Yet I persuade myself to speak the [[Truth|truth]]<br>Shall nothing wrong him. This it is, general.<br>Montano and myself being in speech,<br>There comes a fellow crying out for help,<br>And [[Cassio]] following him with determined [[Sword|sword]]<br>To execute upon him. Sir, this gentleman<br>Steps in to [[Cassio]], and entreats his pause.<br>Myself the crying fellow did pursue,<br>Lest by his clamour, as it so fell out,<br>The town might fall in fright. He, swift of foot,<br>Outran my purpose, and I returned, the rather<br>For that I heard the clink and fall of swords<br>And [[Cassio]] high in oath, which till tonight<br>I ne’er might say before. When I came back—<br>For this was brief—I found them close together<br>At blow and thrust, even as again they were<br>When you yourself did part them.<br>More of this matter cannot I report,<br>But men are men. The best sometimes forget.<br>Though [[Cassio]] did some little wrong to him,<br>As men in rage strike those that wish them best,<br>Yet surely [[Cassio]], I believe, received<br>From him that fled some strange indignity<br>Which patience could not pass. |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | I know, [[Iago]],<br>Thy honesty and [[Love|love]] doth mince this matter,<br>Making it light to [[Cassio]]. [[Cassio]], I [[Love|love]] thee,<br>But never more be officer of mine. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Desdemona]], attended |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | Look if my gentle [[Love|love]] be not raised up.<br>I’ll make thee an example. |
| [[Desdemona|DESDEMONA]] | What is the matter, dear? |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | All’s [[Well|well]] now, sweeting.<br>Come away to bed. (To Montano) Sir, for your hurts<br>Myself will be your surgeon. (To attendants) Lead him<br>off. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt attendants with Montano |
| [[Othello|OTHELLO]] | [[Iago]], look with care about the town,<br>And [[Silence|silence]] those whom this vile brawl distracted.<br>Come, [[Desdemona]]. ’Tis the soldier’s life<br>To have their balmy slumbers waked with strife. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Iago]] and [[Cassio]] |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | What, are you hurt, lieutenant? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Ay, past all surgery. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Marry, God forbid. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Reputation, reputation, reputation—O, I ha’ lost my reputation, I ha’ lost the immortal part of myself, and what remains is bestial! My reputation, [[Iago]], my reputation. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | As I am an honest man, I thought you had received some bodily wound. There is more sense in that than in reputation. Reputation is an idle and most false imposition, oft got without merit and lost without deserving. You have lost no reputation at all unless you repute yourself such a loser. What, man, there are more ways to recover the general again. You are but now cast in his mood—a punishment more in policy than in malice, even so as one would beat his offenceless dog to affright an imperious [[Lion|lion]]. Sue to him again, and he’s yours. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I will rather sue to be despised than to deceive so good a commander with so slight, so drunken, and so indiscreet an officer. Drunk, and speak parrot, and squabble? Swagger, swear, and discourse fustian with one’s own [[Shadow|shadow]]? O thou invisible spirit of wine, if thou hast no name to be known by, let us call thee devil. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | What was he that you followed with your [[Sword|sword]]? What had he done to you? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I know not. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Is’t possible? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I remember a mass of things, but nothing distinctly; a quarrel, but nothing wherefore. O God, that men should put an enemy in their mouths to steal away their brains! That we should with joy, pleasance, revel, and applause transform ourselves into beasts! |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Why, but you are now [[Well|well]] enough. How came you thus recovered? |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | It hath pleased the devil drunkenness to give place to the devil wrath. One unperfectness shows me another, to make me frankly despise myself. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Come, you are too severe a moraller. As the [[Time|time]], the place, and the condition of this country stands, I could heartily wish this had not befallen; but since it is as it is, mend it for your own good. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I will ask him for my place again. He shall tell me I am a drunkard. Had I as many mouths as Hydra, such an answer would stop them all. To be now a sensible man, by and by a [[Fool|fool]], and presently a beast! O, strange! Every inordinate cup is unblessed, and the ingredient is a devil. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Come, come. Good wine is a good familiar creature, if it be [[Well|well]] used. Exclaim no more against it. And, good lieutenant, I think you think I [[Love|love]] you. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I have [[Well|well]] approved it, sir—I drunk? |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | You or any man living may be drunk at a [[Time|time]], man. I’ll tell you what you shall do. Our general’s wife is now the general. I may say so in this respect, for that he hath devoted and given up himself to the contemplation, mark, and denotement of her parts and graces. Confess yourself freely to her. Importune her help to put you in your place again. She is of so free, so kind, so apt, so blessed a disposition, she holds it a vice in her goodness not to do more than she is requested. This broken joint between you and her husband entreat her to splinter, and, my fortunes against any lay worth naming, this crack of your [[Love|love]] shall grow stronger than it was before. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | You advise me [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | I protest, in the sincerity of [[Love|love]] and honest kindness. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | I think it freely, and betimes in the morning I will beseech the virtuous [[Desdemona]] to undertake for me. I am desperate of my fortunes if they check me here. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | You are in the right. Good [[Night|night]], lieutenant. I must to the watch. |
| [[Cassio|CASSIO]] | Good [[Night|night]], honest [[Iago]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | And what’s he then that says I play the [[Villain|villain]],<br>When this advice is free I give, and honest,<br>Probal to thinking, and indeed the course<br>To win the Moor again? For ‘tis most easy<br>Th’inclining [[Desdemona]] to subdue<br>In any honest suit. She’s framed as fruitful<br>As the free elements; and then for her<br>To win the Moor, were’t to renounce his baptism,<br>All seals and symbols of redeemed sin,<br>His [[Soul|soul]] is so enfettered to her [[Love|love]]<br>That she may make, unmake, do what she list,<br>Even as her appetite shall play the god<br>With his weak function. How am I then a [[Villain|villain]],<br>To counsel [[Cassio]] to this parallel course<br>Directly to his good? Divinity of hell:<br>When devils will the blackest sins put on,<br>They do suggest at first with heavenly shows,<br>As I do now; for whiles this honest [[Fool|fool]]<br>Plies [[Desdemona]] to repair his fortune,<br>And she for him pleads strongly to the Moor,<br>I’ll pour this pestilence into his ear:<br>That she repeals him for her [[Body|body]]’s lust,<br>And by how much she strives to do him good<br>She shall undo her credit with the Moor.<br>So will I turn her virtue into pitch,<br>And out of her own goodness make the net<br>That shall enmesh them all. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Roderigo]] |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | How now, [[Roderigo]]? |
| [[Roderigo|RODERIGO]] | I do follow here in the chase, not like a hound that hunts, but one that fills up the cry. My money is almost spent, I ha’ been tonight exceedingly [[Well|well]] cudgelled, and I think the issue will be I shall have so much experience for my pains: and so, with no money at all and a little more wit, return again to [[Venice]]. |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | How poor are they that ha’ not patience!<br>What wound did ever heal but by degrees?<br>Thou know‘st we work by wit and not by witchcraft,<br>And wit depends on dilatory [[Time|time]].<br>Does’t not go [[Well|well]]? [[Cassio]] hath beaten thee,<br>And thou by that small hurt hast cashiered [[Cassio]].<br>Though other things grow fair against the sun,<br>Yet fruits that blossom first will first be ripe.<br>Content thyself a while. By the mass, ’tis morning.<br>Pleasure and action make the hours seem short.<br>Retire thee. Go where thou art billeted.<br>Away, I say. Thou shalt know more hereafter.<br>Nay, get thee gone. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Roderigo]] |
| [[Iago|IAGO]] | Two things are to be done.<br>My wife must move for [[Cassio]] to her mistress.<br>I’ll set her on.<br>Myself a while to draw the Moor apart,<br>And bring him jump when he may [[Cassio]] find<br>Soliciting his wife. Ay, that’s the way.<br>Dull not device by coldness and delay. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
