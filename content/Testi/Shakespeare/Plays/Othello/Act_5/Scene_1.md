| *(didascalia)* | Enter Iago and Roderigo |
| IAGO | Here, stand behind this bulk. Straight will he come.<br>Wear thy good rapier bare, and put it home.<br>Quick, quick, fear nothing. I’ll be at thy elbow.<br>It makes us or it mars us. Think on that,<br>And fix most firm thy resolution. |
| RODERIGO | Be near at hand. I may miscarry in’t. |
| IAGO | Here at thy hand. Be bold, and take thy stand. |
| RODERIGO | (aside)<br>I have no great devotion to the deed,<br>And yet he hath given me satisfying reasons.<br>’Tis but a man gone. Forth my sword—he dies! |
| IAGO | (aside)<br>I have rubbed this young quat almost to the sense,<br>And he grows angry. Now, whether he kill Cassio<br>Or Cassio him, or each do kill the other,<br>Every way makes my gain. live Roderigo,<br>He calls me to a restitution large<br>Of gold and jewels that I bobbed from him<br>As gifts to Desdemona.<br>It must not be. If Cassio do remain,<br>He hath a daily beauty in his life<br>That makes me ugly; and besides, the Moor<br>May unfold me to him-there stand I in much peril.<br>No, he must die. But so, I hear him coming. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Cassio |
| RODERIGO | I know his gait, ’tis he. (Attacking Cassio) Villain, thou<br>diest. |
| CASSIO | That thrust had been mine enemy indeed,<br>But that my coat is better than thou know’st.<br>I will make proof of thine.<br>He stabs Roderigo, who falls |
| RODERIGO | O, I am slain!<br>Iago wounds Cassio in the leg from behind. Exit Iago |
| CASSIO | (falling)<br>I am maimed for ever. Help, ho, murder, murder! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Othello [above] |
| OTHELLO | The voice of Cassio. Iago keeps his word. |
| RODERIGO | O, villain that I am! |
| OTHELLO | It is even so. |
| CASSIO | O, help, ho! Light, a surgeon! |
| OTHELLO | ’Tis he. O brave Iago, honest and just,<br>That hast such noble sense of thy friend’s wrong—<br>Thou teachest me. Minion, your dear lies dead,<br>And your unblessed fate hies. Strumpet, I come.<br>Forth of my heart those charms, thine eyes, are blotted.<br>Thy bed, lust-stained, shall with lust’s blood be spotted. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lodovico and Graziano |
| CASSIO | What ho, no watch, no passage? Murder, murder! |
| GRAZIANO | ’Tis some mischance. The voice is very direful. |
| CASSIO | O, help! |
| LODOVICO | Hark. |
| RODERIGO | O wretched villain! |
| LODOVICO | Two or three groan. ’Tis heavy night.<br>These may be counterfeits. Let’s think’t unsafe<br>To come into the cry without more help. |
| RODERIGO | Nobody come? Then shall I bleed to death. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Iago with a light |
| LODOVICO | Hark. |
| GRAZIANO | Here’s one comes in his shirt, with light and weapons. |
| IAGO | Who’s there? Whose noise is this that cries on murder? |
| LODOVICO | We do not know. |
| IAGO | Do not you hear a cry? |
| CASSIO | Here, here. For heaven’s sake, help me. |
| IAGO | What’s the matter? |
| GRAZIANO | (to Lodovico)<br>This is Othello’s ensign, as I take it. |
| LODOVICO | The same indeed, a very valiant fellow. |
| IAGO | (to Cassio)<br>What are you here that cry so grievously? |
| CASSIO | Iago—O, I am spoiled, undone by villains.<br>Give me some help. |
| IAGO | O me, lieutenant, what villains have done this? |
| CASSIO | I think that one of them is hereabout<br>And cannot make away. |
| IAGO | O treacherous villains!<br>(To Lodovico and Graziano)<br>What are you there? Come in and give some help. |
| RODERIGO | O, help me there! |
| CASSIO | That’s one of ’em. |
| IAGO | (stabbing Roderigo) O murderous slave! O villain! |
| RODERIGO | O damned Iago! O inhuman dog! |
| IAGO | Kill men i’th’ dark? Where be these bloody thieves?<br>How silent is this town! Ho, murder, murder!<br>(To Lodovico and Graziano)<br>What may you be? Are you of good or evil? |
| LODOVICO | As you shall prove us, praise us. |
| IAGO | Signor Lodovico. |
| LODOVICO | He, sir. |
| IAGO | I cry you mercy. Here’s Cassio hurt by villains. |
| GRAZIANO | Cassio? |
| IAGO | How is’t, brother? |
| CASSIO | My leg is cut in two. |
| IAGO | Marry, heaven forbid!<br>Light, gentlemen. I’ll bind it with my shirt. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Bianca |
| BIANCA | What is the matter, ho? Who is’t that cried? |
| IAGO | Who is’t that cried? |
| BIANCA | O my dear Cassio,<br>My sweet Cassio, O, Cassio, Cassio! |
| IAGO | O notable strumpet! Cassio, may you suspect<br>Who they should be that have thus mangled you? 80 |
| CASSIO | No. |
| GRAZIANO | I am sorry to find you thus. I have been to seek you. |
| IAGO | Lend me a garter. So. O for a chair,<br>To bear him easily hence! |
| BIANCA | Alas, he faints. O, Cassio, Cassio, Cassio! |
| IAGO | Gentlemen all, I do suspect this trash<br>To be a party in this injury.<br>Patience a while, good Cassio. Come, come,<br>Lend me a light. (Going to Roderigo) Know we this face<br>or no?<br>Alas, my friend, and my dear countryman.<br>Roderigo? No—yes, sure—O heaven, Roderigo! |
| GRAZIANO | What, of Venice? |
| IAGO | Even he, sir. Did you know him? |
| GRAZIANO | Know him? Ay. |
| IAGO | Signor Graziano, I cry your gentle pardon.<br>These bloody accidents must excuse my manners<br>That so neglected you. |
| GRAZIANO | I am glad to see you. |
| IAGO | How do you, Cassio? O, a chair, a chair! |
| GRAZIANO | Roderigo. |
| IAGO | He, he, ’tis he. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter attendants with a chair |
| IAGO | O, that’s well said, the chair!<br>Some good man bear him carefully from hence.<br>I’ll fetch the general’s surgeon. (To Bianca) For you,<br>mistress,<br>Save you your labour. He that lies slain here, Cassio,<br>Was my dear friend. What malice was between you? |
| CASSIO | None in the world, nor do I know the man. |
| IAGO | (to Bianca)<br>What, look you pale? (To attendants) O, bear him out<br>o’th’ air.<br>(To Lodovico and Graziano)<br>Stay you, good gentlemen. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt attendants with Cassio in the chair [and with Roderigo’s body] |
| IAGO | (To Bianca) Look you pale, mistress?<br>(To Lodovico and Graziano)<br>Do you perceive the ghastness of her eye?<br>(To Bianca) Nay, an you stare we shall hear more<br>anon.<br>(To Lodovico and Graziano)<br>Behold her well; I pray you look upon her.<br>Do you see, gentlemen? Nay, guiltiness<br>Will speak, though tongues were out of use. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Emilia |
| EMILIA | Alas, what is the matter? What is the matter,<br>husband? |
| IAGO | Cassio hath here been set on in the dark<br>By Roderigo and fellows that are scaped.<br>He’s almost slain, and Roderigo dead. |
| EMILIA | Alas, good gentleman! Alas, good Cassio! |
| IAGO | This is the fruits of whoring. Prithee, Emilia,<br>Go know of Cassio where he supped tonight.<br>(To Bianca) What, do you shake at that? |
| BIANCA | He supped at my house, but I therefore shake not. |
| IAGO | O, did he so? I charge you go with me. |
| EMILIA | (to Bianca) O, fie upon thee, strumpet! |
| BIANCA | I am no strumpet, but of life as honest<br>As you that thus abuse me. |
| EMILIA | As I? Fough, fie upon thee! |
| IAGO | Kind gentlemen, let’s go see poor Cassio dressed.<br>(To Bianca) Come, mistress, you must tell’s another tale.<br>Emilia, run you to the citadel<br>And tell my lord and lady what hath happed.<br>Will you go on afore? |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Emilia |
| IAGO | (Aside) This is the night<br>That either makes me or fordoes me quite. Exeunt |
