| *(didascalia)* | Enter Helen, the Widow, and Diana |
| HELEN | That you may well perceive I have not wronged you,<br>One of the greatest in the Christian world<br>Shall be my surety; fore whose throne ‘tis needful,<br>Ere I can perfect mine intents, to kneel.<br>Time was, I did him a desired office<br>Dear almost as his life; which gratitude<br>Through flinty Tartar’s bosom would peep forth<br>And answer ‘Thanks’. I duly am informed<br>His grace is at Marseilles, to which place<br>We have convenient convoy. You must know<br>I am supposed dead. The army breaking,<br>My husband hies him home, where, heaven aiding,<br>And by the leave of my good lord the King,<br>We’ll be before our welcome. |
| WIDOW | Gentle madam,<br>You never had a servant to whose trust<br>Your business was more welcome. |
| HELEN | Nor you, mistress,<br>Ever a friend whose thoughts more truly labour<br>To recompense your love. Doubt not but heaven<br>Hath brought me up to be your daughter’s dower,<br>As it hath fated her to be my motive<br>And helper to a husband. But O, strange men,<br>That can such sweet use make of what they hate,<br>When saucy trusting of the cozened thoughts<br>Defiles the pitchy night; so lust doth play<br>With what it loathes, for that which is away.<br>But more of this hereafter. You, Diana,<br>Under my poor instructions yet must suffer<br>Something in my behalf. |
| DIANA | Let death and honesty<br>Go with your impositions, I am yours,<br>Upon your will to suffer. |
| HELEN | Yet, I pray you.—<br>But with that word the time will bring on summer,<br>When briers shall have leaves as well as thorns<br>And be as sweet as sharp. We must away,<br>Our wagon is prepared, and time revives us.<br>All’s well that ends well; still the fine’s the crown.<br>Whate’er the course, the end is the renown. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
