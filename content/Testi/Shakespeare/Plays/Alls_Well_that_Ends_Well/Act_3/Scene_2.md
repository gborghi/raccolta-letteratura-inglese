| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Countess with a letter, and Lavatch COUNTESS It hath happened all as I would have had it, save that he comes not along with her. |
| LAVATCH | By my troth, I take my young lord to be a very [[Melancholy|melancholy]] man. |
| COUNTESS | By what observance, I pray you? |
| LAVATCH | Why, he will look upon his boot and sing, mend the ruff and sing, ask questions and sing, pick his teeth and sing. I know a man that had this trick of [[Melancholy|melancholy]] sold a goodly manor for a [[Song|song]]. |
| COUNTESS | Let me see what he writes, and when he means to come.<br>She opens the letter and reads |
| LAVATCH | (aside) I have no mind to Isbel since I was at [[Court|court]]. Our old lings and our Isbels o‘th’ country are nothing like your old ling and your Isbels o’th’ [[Court|court]]. The brains of my Cupid’s knocked out, and I begin to [[Love|love]] as an old man loves money: with no stomach. |
| COUNTESS | What have we here? |
| LAVATCH | E’en that you have there. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| COUNTESS | (reads the letter aloud) ’I have sent you a daughter-in-law. She hath recovered the [[King]] and undone me. I have wedded her, not bedded her, and sworn to make the “not” eternal. You shall hear I am run away; know it before the report come. If there be breadth enough in the world I will hold a long distance. My duty to you.<br>Your unfortunate son,<br>[[Bertram]].’<br>This is not [[Well|well]], rash and unbridled boy,<br>To fly the favours of so good a [[King]],<br>To pluck his indignation on thy head<br>By the misprizing of a maid too virtuous<br>For the contempt of empire. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lavatch |
| LAVATCH | O madam, yonder is heavy news within, between two soldiers and my young lady. |
| COUNTESS | What is the matter? |
| LAVATCH | Nay, there is some comfort in the news, some comfort. Your son will not be killed so soon as I thought he would. |
| COUNTESS | Why should he be killed? |
| LAVATCH | So say I, madam—if he run away, as I hear he does. The danger is in standing to’t; that’s the loss of men, though it be the getting of children. Here they come will tell you more. For my part, I only heard your son was run away. [Exit] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Helen]] with a letter, and the two Lords [[Dumaine]] |
| SECOND LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | (to the Countess)<br>Save you, good madam. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | Madam, my lord is gone, for ever gone. |
| FIRST LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Do not say so. |
| COUNTESS | ([[To Helen|to Helen]])<br>Think upon patience.—Pray you, gentlemen,<br>I have felt so many quirks of joy and grief<br>That the first face of neither on the start<br>Can woman me unto’t. Where is my son, I pray you? |
| FIRST LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Madam, he’s gone to serve the Duke of Florence.<br>We met him thitherward, for thence we came,<br>And, after some dispatch in hand at [[Court|court]],<br>Thither we bend again. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | Look on his letter, madam: here’s my passport. |
| *(didascalia)* | She reads aloud |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | ‘When thou canst get the ring upon my finger, which never shall come off, and show me a [[Child|child]] begotten of thy [[Body|body]] that I am father to, then call me husband; but in such a “then” I write a “never”.’ This is a dreadful sentence. |
| COUNTESS | Brought you this letter, gentlemen? |
| FIRST LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Ay, madam,<br>And for the contents’ sake are sorry for our pains. |
| COUNTESS | I prithee, lady, have a better cheer.<br>If thou engrossest all the griefs are thine<br>Thou robb’st me of a moiety. He was my son,<br>But I do wash his name out of my [[Blood|blood]],<br>And thou art all my [[Child|child]].—Towards Florence is he? |
| FIRST LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Ay, madam. |
| COUNTESS | And to be a soldier? |
| FIRST LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Such is his noble purpose, and—believe’t—<br>The Duke will lay upon him all the [[Honour|honour]]<br>That good convenience claims. |
| COUNTESS | Return you thither? |
| SECOND LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Ay, madam, with the swiftest wing of [[Speed|speed]]. |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | ‘Till I have no wife, I have nothing in France.’<br>’Tis bitter. 75 |
| COUNTESS | Find you that there? |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | Ay, madam. |
| SECOND LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | ’Tis but the boldness of his hand,<br>Haply, which his heart was not consenting to. |
| COUNTESS | Nothing in France until he have no wife?<br>There’s nothing here that is too good for him<br>But only she, and she deserves a lord<br>That twenty such rude boys might tend upon<br>And call her, hourly, mistress. Who was with him? |
| SECOND LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | A servant only, and a gentleman<br>Which I have sometime known. |
| COUNTESS | Paroles, was it not? |
| SECOND LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Ay, my good lady, he. |
| COUNTESS | A very tainted fellow, and full of wickedness.<br>My son corrupts a [[Well|well]]-derivèd [[Nature|nature]]<br>With his inducement. |
| SECOND LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | Indeed, good lady,<br>The fellow has a deal of that too much,<br>Which holds him much to have. |
| COUNTESS | You’re welcome, gentlemen.<br>I will entreat you when you see my son<br>To tell him that his [[Sword|sword]] can never win<br>The [[Honour|honour]] that he loses. More I’ll entreat you<br>Written to bear along. |
| FIRST LORD [[Dumaine|DUMAINE]] | We serve you, madam,<br>In that and all your worthiest affairs. |
| COUNTESS | Not so, but as we change our courtesies.<br>Will you draw near? |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but [[Helen]] |
| [[Helen|HELEN]] | ‘Till I have no wife I have nothing in France.’<br>Nothing in France until he has no wife.<br>Thou shalt have none, Roussillon, none in France;<br>Then hast thou all again. Poor lord, is’t I<br>That chase thee from thy country and expose<br>Those tender limbs of thine to the event<br>Of the none-sparing war? And is it I<br>That drive thee from the sportive [[Court|court]], where thou<br>Wast shot at with fair eyes, to be the mark<br>Of smoky muskets? O you leaden messengers<br>That ride upon the violent [[Speed|speed]] of [[Fire|fire]],<br>Fly with false aim, cleave the still-piecing air<br>That sings with piercing, do not touch my lord.<br>Whoever shoots at him, I set him there.<br>Whoever charges on his forward breast,<br>I am the caitiff that do hold him to’t,<br>And though I kill him not, I am the cause<br>His [[Death|death]] was so effected. Better ’twere<br>I met the ravin [[Lion|lion]] when he roared<br>With sharp constraint of hunger; better ’twere<br>That all the miseries which [[Nature|nature]] owes<br>Were mine at once. No, come thou [[Home|home]], Roussillon,<br>Whence [[Honour|honour]] but of danger wins a scar,<br>As oft it loses all. I will be gone;<br>My being here it is that holds thee hence.<br>Shall I stay here to do’t? No, no, although<br>The air of paradise did fan the [[house]]<br>And angels officed all. I will be gone,<br>That pitiful rumour may report my flight<br>To consolate thine ear. Come [[Night|night]], end day;<br>For with the dark, poor thief, I’ll steal away. Exit |
