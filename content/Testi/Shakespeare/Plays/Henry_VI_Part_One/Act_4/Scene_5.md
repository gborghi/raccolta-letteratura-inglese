| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Lord Talbot]] and his son John |
| TALBOT | O young John Talbot, I did send for thee<br>To tutor thee in stratagems of war,<br>That Talbot’s name might be in thee revived<br>When sapless age and weak unable limbs<br>Should bring thy father to his drooping chair.<br>But O—malignant and ill-boding [[Stars|stars]]!—<br>Now thou art come unto a feast of [[Death|death]],<br>A terrible and unavoided danger.<br>Therefore, dear boy, mount on my swiftest [[Horse|horse]],<br>And I’ll direct thee how thou shalt escape<br>By sudden flight. Come, dally not, be gone. |
| JOHN | Is my name Talbot, and am I your son,<br>And shall I fly? O, if you [[Love|love]] my mother,<br>Dishonour not her honourable name<br>To make a bastard and a slave of me.<br>The world will say he is not Talbot’s [[Blood|blood]]<br>That basely fled when noble Talbot stood. |
| TALBOT | Fly to [[Revenge|revenge]] my [[Death|death]] if I be slain. |
| JOHN | He that flies so will ne’er return again. |
| TALBOT | If we both stay, we both are sure to die. |
| JOHN | Then let me stay and, father, do you fly.<br>Your loss is great; so your regard should be.<br>My worth unknown, no loss is known in me.<br>Upon my [[Death|death]] the French can little boast;<br>In yours they will: in you all hopes are lost.<br>Flight cannot stain the [[Honour|honour]] you have won,<br>But mine it will, that no exploit have done.<br>You fled for vantage, everyone will swear,<br>But if I bow, they’ll say it was for [[Fear|fear]].<br>There is no [[Hope|hope]] that ever I will stay<br>If the first hour I shrink and run away.<br>Here on my knee I beg [[Mortality|mortality]]<br>Rather than life preserved with infamy. |
| TALBOT | Shall all thy mother’s hopes lie in one tomb? |
| JOHN | Ay, rather than I’ll shame my mother’s womb. |
| TALBOT | Upon my blessing I command thee go. |
| JOHN | To fight I will, but not to fly the foe. |
| TALBOT | Part of thy father may be saved in thee. |
| JOHN | No part of him but will be shamed in me. |
| TALBOT | Thou never hadst renown, nor canst not lose it. |
| JOHN | Yes, your renowned name—shall flight abuse it? |
| TALBOT | Thy father’s charge shall clear thee from that stain. |
| JOHN | You cannot witness for me, being slain.<br>If [[Death|death]] be so apparent, then both fly. |
| TALBOT | And leave my followers here to fight and die?<br>My age was never tainted with such shame. |
| JOHN | And shall my youth be guilty of such blame?<br>No more can I be severed from your side<br>Than can yourself your [[Self|self]] in twain divide.<br>Stay, go, do what you will: the like do I,<br>For live I will not if my father die. |
| TALBOT | Then here I take my leave of thee, fair son,<br>Born to eclipse thy life this afternoon.<br>Come, side by side together live and die,<br>And [[Soul|soul]] with [[Soul|soul]] from France to heaven fly. Exeunt |
