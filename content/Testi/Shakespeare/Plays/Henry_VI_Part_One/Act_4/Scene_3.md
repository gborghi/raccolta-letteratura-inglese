| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger that meets the Duke of York. Enter Richard Duke of York with a trumpeter and many soldiers |
| RICHARD DUKE OF YORK | Are not the speedy scouts returned again<br>That dogged the mighty army of the Dauphin? |
| MESSENGER | They are returned, my lord, and give it out<br>That he is marched to Bordeaux with his power<br>To fight with Talbot. As he marched along,<br>By your espials were discovered<br>Two mightier troops than that the Dauphin led,<br>Which joined with him and made their march for<br>Bordeaux. |
| RICHARD DUKE OF YORK | A plague upon that villain Somerset<br>That thus delays my promised supply<br>Of horsemen that were levied for this siege!<br>Renowned Talbot doth expect my aid,<br>And I am louted by a traitor villain<br>And cannot help the noble chevalier.<br>God comfort him in this necessity;<br>If he miscarry, farewell wars in France! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another messenger, Sir William Lucy |
| LUCY | Thou princely leader of our English strength,<br>Never so needful on the earth of France,<br>Spur to the rescue of the noble Talbot,<br>Who now is girdled with a waste of iron<br>And hemmed about with grim destruction.<br>To Bordeaux, warlike Duke; to Bordeaux, York,<br>Else farewell Talbot, France, and England’s honour. |
| RICHARD DUKE OF YORK | O God, that Somerset, who in proud heart<br>Doth stop my cornets, were in Talbot’s place!<br>So should we save a valiant gentleman<br>By forfeiting a traitor and a coward.<br>Mad ire and wrathful fury makes me weep,<br>That thus we die while remiss traitors sleep. |
| LUCY | O, send some succour to the distressed lord. |
| RICHARD DUKE OF YORK | He dies, we lose; I break my warlike word;<br>We mourn, France smiles; we lose, they daily get,<br>All ‘long of this vile traitor Somerset. |
| LUCY | Then God take mercy on brave Talbot’s soul,<br>And on his son young John, who two hours since<br>I met in travel toward his warlike father.<br>This seven years did not Talbot see his son,<br>And now they meet where both their lives are done. |
| RICHARD DUKE OF YORK | Alas, what joy shall noble Talbot have<br>To bid his young son welcome to his grave?<br>Away—vexation almost stops my breath<br>That sundered friends greet in the hour of death.<br>Lucy, farewell. No more my fortune can<br>But curse the cause I cannot aid the man.<br>Maine, Blois, Poitiers, and Tours are won away<br>’Long all of Somerset and his delay. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt all but Lucy |
| LUCY | Thus while the vulture of sedition<br>Feeds in the bosom of such great commanders,<br>Sleeping neglection doth betray to loss<br>The conquest of our scarce-cold conqueror,<br>That ever-living man of memory<br>Henry the Fifth. Whiles they each other cross, |
| *(didascalia)* | Lives, honours, lands, and all hurry to loss. Exit |
