| *(didascalia)* | Here an alarum again, and Lord Talbot pursueth the Dauphin and driveth him. Then enter Joan la Pucelle driving Englishmen before her and exeunt. Then enter Lord Talbot |
| TALBOT | Where is my strength, my valour, and my force?<br>Our English troops retire; I cannot stay them.<br>A woman clad in armour chaseth men. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Joan la Pucelle |
| TALBOT | Here, here she comes. (To Joan) I’ll have a bout with<br>thee.<br>Devil or devil’s dam, I’ll conjure thee.<br>Blood will I draw on thee—thou art a witch—<br>And straightway give thy soul to him thou serv’st. |
| JOAN | Come, come, ’tis only I that must disgrace thee.<br>Here they fight |
| TALBOT | Heavens, can you suffer hell so to prevail?<br>My breast I’ll burst with straining of my courage<br>And from my shoulders crack my arms asunder<br>But I will chastise this high-minded strumpet.<br>They fight again |
| JOAN | Talbot, farewell. Thy hour is not yet come.<br>I must go victual Orléans forthwith. |
| *(didascalia)* | A short alarum, then the French pass over the stage and enter the town with soldiers |
| JOAN | O’ertake me if thou canst. I scorn thy strength.<br>Go, go, cheer up thy hungry-starved men.<br>Help Salisbury to make his testament.<br>This day is ours, as many more shall be. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit into the town |
| TALBOT | My thoughts are whirled like a potter’s wheel.<br>I know not where I am nor what I do.<br>A witch by fear, not force, like Hannibal<br>Drives back our troops and conquers as she lists.<br>So bees with smoke and doves with noisome stench<br>Are from their hives and houses driven away.<br>They called us, for our fierceness, English dogs;<br>Now, like to whelps, we crying run away. |
| *(didascalia)* | A short alarum. Enter English soldiers |
| TALBOT | Hark, countrymen: either renew the fight<br>Or tear the lions out of England’s coat.<br>Renounce your style; give sheep in lions’ stead.<br>Sheep run not half so treacherous from the wolf,<br>Or horse or oxen from the leopard,<br>As you fly from your oft-subduèd slaves. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alarum. Here another skirmish |
| TALBOT | It will not be. Retire into your trenches.<br>You all consented unto Salisbury’s death,<br>For none would strike a stroke in his revenge.<br>Pucelle is entered into Orléans<br>In spite of us or aught that we could do. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Soldiers |
| TALBOT | O would I were to die with Salisbury!<br>The shame hereof will make me hide my head. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit. Alarum. Retreat |
