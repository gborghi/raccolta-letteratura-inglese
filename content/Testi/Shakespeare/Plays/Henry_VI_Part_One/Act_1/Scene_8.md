| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Enter on the walls Joan la Pucelle, Charles the Dauphin, René Duke of Anjou, the Duke of Alençon and French Soldiers with colours |
| JOAN | Advance our waving colours on the walls;<br>Rescued is Orléans from the English.<br>Thus Joan la Pucelle hath performed her word. |
| CHARLES | Divinest creature, Astraea’s daughter,<br>How shall I honour thee for this success?<br>Thy promises are like Adonis’ garden,<br>That one day bloomed and fruitful were the next.<br>France, triumph in thy glorious prophetess!<br>Recovered is the town of Orléans.<br>More blessed hap did ne’er befall our state.<br>RENÉ<br>Why ring not out the bells aloud throughout the<br>town?<br>Dauphin, command the citizens make bonfires<br>And feast and banquet in the open streets<br>To celebrate the joy that God hath given us.<br>ALENÇON<br>All France will be replete with mirth and joy<br>When they shall hear how we have played the men. |
| CHARLES | ’Tis Joan, not we, by whom the day is won—<br>For which I will divide my crown with her,<br>And all the priests and friars in my realm<br>Shall in procession sing her endless praise.<br>A statelier pyramid to her I’ll rear<br>Than Rhodope’s of Memphis ever was.<br>In memory of her, when she is dead<br>Her ashes, in an urn more precious<br>Than the rich-jewelled coffer of Darius,<br>Transported shall be at high festivals<br>Before the kings and queens of France.<br>No longer on Saint Denis will we cry,<br>But Joan la Pucelle shall be France’s saint.<br>Come in, and let us banquet royally<br>After this golden day of victory. Flourish. Exeunt |
