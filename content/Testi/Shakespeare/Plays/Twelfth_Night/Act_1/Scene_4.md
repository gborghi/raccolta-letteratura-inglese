| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Valentine]], and [[Viola]] (as Cesario) in man’s attire |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | If the Duke continue these favours towards you, Cesario, you are like to be much advanced. He hath known you but three days, and already you are no stranger. |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | You either [[Fear|fear]] his [[Humour|humour]] or my negligence, that you call in question the continuance of his [[Love|love]]. Is he inconstant, sir, in his favours? |
| [[Valentine|VALENTINE]] | No, believe me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the Duke, Curio, and attendants |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | I thank you. Here comes the Count. |
| [[Orsino|ORSINO]] | Who saw Cesario, ho? |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | On your attendance, my lord, here. |
| [[Orsino|ORSINO]] | (to Curio and attendants)<br>Stand you a while aloof. (To [[Viola]]) Cesario,<br>Thou know’st no less but all. I have unclasped<br>To thee the book even of my secret [[Soul|soul]].<br>Therefore, good youth, address thy gait unto her,<br>Be not denied access, stand at her doors,<br>And tell them there thy fixed foot shall grow<br>Till thou have audience. |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | Sure, my noble lord,<br>If she be so abandoned to her sorrow<br>As it is spoke, she never will admit me. |
| [[Orsino|ORSINO]] | Be clamorous, and leap all civil bounds,<br>Rather than make unprofited return. |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | Say I do speak with her, my lord, what then? |
| [[Orsino|ORSINO]] | O then unfold the passion of my [[Love|love]],<br>Surprise her with discourse of my dear [[Faith|faith]].<br>It shall become thee [[Well|well]] to act my woes—<br>She will attend it better in thy youth<br>Than in a nuncio’s of more [[Grave|grave]] aspect. |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | I think not so, my lord. |
| [[Orsino|ORSINO]] | Dear lad, believe it;<br>For they shall yet belie thy happy years<br>That say thou art a man. [[Diana]]’s lip<br>Is not more smooth and rubious; thy small pipe<br>Is as the maiden’s organ, shrill and sound,<br>And all is semblative a woman’s part.<br>I know thy constellation is right apt<br>For this affair. (To Curio and attendants) Some four or<br>five attend him.<br>All if you will, for I myself am best<br>When least in company. (To [[Viola]]) Prosper [[Well|well]] in this<br>And thou shalt live as freely as thy lord,<br>To call his fortunes thine. |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | I’ll do my best |
| *(didascalia)* | To woo your lady—aside yet a barful strife— |
| [[Viola|VIOLA]] | Whoe’er I woo, myself would be his wife. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt |
