| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Antipholus of Ephesus]], his man Dromio, [[Angelo]] the goldsmith, and Balthasar the merchant |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Good Signor [[Angelo]], you must excuse us all.<br>My wife is shrewish when I keep not hours.<br>Say that I lingered with you at your shop<br>To see the making of her carcanet,<br>And that tomorrow you will bring it [[Home|home]].—<br>But here’s a [[Villain|villain]] that would face me down<br>He met me on the mart, and that I beat him,<br>And charged him with a thousand marks in gold,<br>And that I did deny my wife and [[house]].<br>Thou drunkard, thou, what didst thou mean by this? |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Say what you will, sir, but I know what I know—II<br>That you beat me at the mart I have your hand to<br>show.<br>If the skin were parchment, and the blows you gave<br>were ink,<br>Your own handwriting would tell you what I think. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | I think thou art an ass. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Marry, so it doth appear<br>By the wrongs I suffer and the blows I bear.<br>I should kick being kicked, and, being at that pass,<br>You would keep from my heels, and beware of an ass. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | You’re sad, Signor Balthasar. Pray God our cheer<br>May answer my good will, and your good welcome<br>here. |
| BALTHASAR | I hold your dainties cheap, sir, and your welcome dear. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | O, Signor Balthasar, either at flesh or fish<br>A table full of welcome makes scarce one dainty dish. |
| BALTHASAR | Good meat, sir, is common; that every churl affords. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | And welcome more common, for that’s nothing but words. |
| BALTHASAR | Small cheer and great welcome makes a merry feast. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Ay, to a niggardly host and more sparing guest.<br>But though my cates be mean, take them in good part.<br>Better cheer may you have, but not with better heart.<br>But soft, my door is locked. (To Dromio) Go bid them let us in. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | (calling)<br>Maud, Bridget, Marian, Cicely, Gillian, Ginn! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Dromio of Syracuse]] within the Phoenix |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>Mome, malt-[[Horse|horse]], capon, coxcomb, idiot, patch!<br>Either get thee from the door or sit down at the hatch.<br>Dost thou conjure for wenches, that thou call’st for such store<br>When one is one too many? Go, get thee from the door. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | What patch is made our porter? My master stays in the street. |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within)<br>Let him walk from whence he came, lest he catch cold on’s feet. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Who talks within there ? Ho, open the door! |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>Right, sir, I’ll tell you when, an you’ll tell me wherefore. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Wherefore? For my dinner—I have not dined today. |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>Nor today here you must not. Come again when you may. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | What art thou that keep’st me out from the [[house]] I owe? |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>The porter for this [[Time|time]], sir, and my name is Dromio. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | O [[Villain|villain]], thou hast stol’n both mine office and my name.<br>The one ne’er got me credit, the other mickle blame.<br>If thou hadst been Dromio today in my place,<br>Thou wouldst have changed thy pate for an aim, or<br>thy name for an ass. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Nell within the Phoenix |
| NELL | (within the Phoenix)<br>What a coil is there, Dromio? Who are those at the gate? |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Let my master in, Nell. |
| NELL | (within the Phoenix) [[Faith]] no, he comes too late;<br>And so tell your master. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | O Lord, I must laugh.<br>Have at you with a proverb: ‘Shall I set in my staff?’ |
| NELL | (within the Phoenix)<br>Have at you with another—that’s ‘When ? Can you tell?’ |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix) |
| *(didascalia)* | If thy name be called Nell, Nell, thou hast answered him [[Well|well]]. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | (to Nell)<br>Do you hear, you minion ? You’ll let us in, I [[Hope|hope]] ? |
| NELL | (within the Phoenix)<br>I thought to have asked you. |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within) And you said no. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | So, come help. |
| *(didascalia)* | He and Antipholus beat the door |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | [[Well]] struck! There was blow for blow. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | (to Nell)<br>Thou baggage, let me in. |
| NELL | (within the Phoenix) Can you tell for whose sake? |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Master, knock the door hard. |
| NELL | (within the Phoenix)<br>Let him knock till it ache. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | You’ll cry for this, minion, if I beat the door down. |
| NELL | (within the Phoenix)<br>What needs all that, and a pair of stocks in the town? |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Adriana]] within the Phoenix |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>Who is that at the door that keeps all this noise? |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>By my troth, your town is troubled with unruly boys. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | (to [[Adriana]])<br>Are you there, wife ? You might have come before. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>Your wife, sir knave? Go, get you from the door. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit with Nell |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | (to Antipholus)<br>If you went in [[Pain|pain]], master, this knave would go sore. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | (to Antipholus)<br>Here is neither cheer, sir, nor welcome; we would fain have either. |
| BALTHASAR | In debating which was best, we shall part with neither. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | (to Antipholus)<br>They stand at the door, master. Bid them welcome hither. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | There is something in the [[Wind|wind]], that we cannot get in. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | You would say so, master, if your garments were thin.<br>Your cake here is warm within: you stand here in the cold.<br>It would make a man mad as a buck to be so bought and sold. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Go fetch me something. I’ll break ope the gate. |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>Break any breaking here, and I’ll break your knave’s pate. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | A man may break a word with you, sir, and words are but [[Wind|wind]];<br>Ay, and break it in your face, so he break it not behind. |
| [[Dromio of Syracuse|DROMIO OF SYRACUSE]] | (within the Phoenix)<br>It seems thou want’st breaking. Out upon thee, hind! |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Here’s too much ‘Out upon thee!’ I pray thee, let me in. |
| DROMIO | or SYRACUSE (within the Phoenix)<br>Ay, when fowls have no feathers, and fish have no fin. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | [[Well]], I’ll break in.—Go borrow me a crow. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | A crow without feather? Master, mean you so?<br>For a fish without a fin, there’s a fowl without a feather.<br>(To [[Dromio of Syracuse]])<br>If a crow help us in, sirrah, we’ll pluck a crow together. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Go, get thee gone. Fetch me an iron crow. |
| BALTHASAR | Have patience, sir. O, let it not be so!<br>Herein you war against your reputation,<br>And draw within the compass of suspect<br>Th’unviolated [[Honour|honour]] of your wife.<br>Once this: your long experience of her wisdom,<br>Her sober virtue, years, and modesty,<br>Plead on her part some cause to you unknown;<br>And [[Doubt|doubt]] not, sir, but she will [[Well|well]] excuse<br>Why at this [[Time|time]] the doors are made against you.<br>Be ruled by me. Depart in patience,<br>And let us to the Tiger all to dinner,<br>And about evening come yourself [[Alone|alone]]<br>To know the reason of this strange restraint.<br>If by strong hand you offer to break in<br>Now in the stirring passage of the day,<br>A vulgar comment will be made of it,<br>And that supposed by the common rout<br>Against your yet ungallèd estimation,<br>That may with foul intrusion enter in<br>And dwell upon your [[Grave|grave]] when you are dead.<br>For slander lives upon succession,<br>For ever housed where once it gets possession. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | You have prevailed. I will depart in quiet,<br>And in despite of mirth mean to be merry.<br>I know a wench of excellent discourse,<br>Pretty and witty; wild, and yet, too, gentle.<br>There will we dine. This woman that I mean,<br>My wife—but, I protest, without [[Desert|desert]]—<br>Hath oftentimes upbraided me withal.<br>To her will we to dinner. (To [[Angelo]]) Get you [[Home|home]]<br>And fetch the chain. By this, I know, ’tis made.<br>Bring it, I pray you, to the Porcupine,<br>For there’s the [[house]]. That chain will I bestow—<br>Be it for nothing but to spite my wife—<br>Upon mine hostess there. Good sir, make haste:<br>Since mine own doors refuse to entertain me,<br>I’ll knock elsewhere, to see if they’ll disdain me. |
| [[Angelo|ANGELO]] | I’ll meet you at that place some hour hence. |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | Do so. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit [[Angelo]] |
| [[Antipholus of Ephesus|ANTIPHOLUS OF EPHESUS]] | This jest shall cost me some expense. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt [[Dromio of Syracuse]] within the |
| *(didascalia)* | Phoenix, and the others into the Porcupine |
