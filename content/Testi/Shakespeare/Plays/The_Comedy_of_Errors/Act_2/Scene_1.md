| *(didascalia)* | Enter from the Phoenix [[Adriana]], wife of [[Antipholus of Ephesus]], with [[Luciana]], her sister |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Neither my husband nor the slave returned<br>That in such haste I sent to seek his master?<br>Sure, [[Luciana]], it is two o’[[Clock|clock]]. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Perhaps some merchant hath invited him,<br>And from the mart he’s somewhere gone to dinner.<br>Good sister, let us dine, and never fret.<br>A man is master of his liberty.<br>[[Time]] is their mistress, and when they see [[Time|time]]<br>They’ll go or come. If so, be patient, sister. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Why should their liberty than ours be more? |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Because their business still lies out o’ door. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Look when I serve him so, he takes it ill. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | O, know he is the bridle of your will. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | There’s none but asses will be bridled so. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Why, headstrong liberty is lashed with woe.<br>There’s nothing situate under heaven’s eye<br>But hath his bound in earth, in sea, in sky.<br>The beasts, the fishes, and the winged fowls<br>Are their males’ subjects and at their controls.<br>Man, more divine, the master of all these,<br>Lord of the wide world and wild wat’ry seas,<br>Indued with intellectual sense and souls,<br>Of more pre-eminence than fish and fowls,<br>Are masters to their females, and their lords.<br>Then let your will attend on their accords. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | This servitude makes you to keep unwed. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Not this, but troubles of the [[Marriage|marriage]] bed. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | But were you wedded, you would bear some sway. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Ere I learn [[Love|love]], I’ll practise to obey. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | How if your husband start some otherwhere? |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Till he come [[Home|home]] again, I would forbear. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Patience unmoved! No marvel though she pause:<br>They can be meek that have no other cause.<br>A wretched [[Soul|soul]], bruised with adversity,<br>We bid be quiet when we hear it cry.<br>But were we burdened with like weight of [[Pain|pain]],<br>As much or more we should ourselves complain.<br>So thou, that hast no unkind mate to grieve thee,<br>With urging helpless patience would relieve me.<br>But if thou live to see like right bereft,<br>This [[Fool|fool]]-begged patience in thee will be left. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | [[Well]], I will marry one day, but to try. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Dromio of Ephesus]] |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Here comes your man. Now is your husband nigh. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Say, is your tardy master now at hand? |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Nay, he’s at two hands with me, and that my two ears can witness. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Say, didst thou speak with him? Know’st thou his mind? |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | I? Ay, he told his mind upon mine ear.<br>Beshrew his hand, I scarce could understand it. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Spake he so doubtfully thou couldst not feel his meaning? |
| DROMIO OF RPHESUS | Nay, he struck so plainly I could too [[Well|well]] feel his blows, and withal so doubtfully that I could scarce under-stand them. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | But say, I prithee, is he coming [[Home|home]]?<br>It seems he hath great care to please his wife. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Why, mistress, sure my master is horn-mad. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Horn-mad, thou [[Villain|villain]]? |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | I mean not cuckold-mad, but sure he is stark mad.<br>When I desired him to come [[Home|home]] to dinner,<br>He asked me for a thousand marks in gold.<br>‘’Tis dinner-[[Time|time]],’ quoth I. ‘My gold,’ quoth he.<br>‘Your meat doth burn,’ quoth I. ‘My gold,’ quoth he.<br>‘Will you come [[Home|home]]?’ quoth I. ‘My gold,’ quoth he;<br>‘Where is the thousand marks I gave thee, [[Villain|villain]]?’<br>‘The pig’, quoth I, ‘is burned.’ ‘My gold!’ quoth he.<br>‘My mistress, sir—’ quoth I. ‘Hang up thy mistress!<br>I know thy mistress not. Out on thy mistress!’ |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | Quoth who? |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Quoth my master.<br>‘I know’, quoth he, ‘no [[house]], no wife, no mistress.’<br>So that my errand, due unto my tongue,<br>I thank him, I bare [[Home|home]] upon my shoulders;<br>For, in conclusion, he did beat me there. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Go back again, thou slave, and fetch him [[Home|home]]. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Go back again and be new beaten [[Home|home]]?<br>For God’s sake, send some other messenger. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | A<br>Back, slave, or I will break thy pate across. |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | An he will bless that [[Cross|cross]] with other beating,<br>Between you I shall have a holy head. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Hence, prating peasant. Fetch thy master [[Home|home]].<br>She beats Dromio |
| [[Dromio of Ephesus|DROMIO OF EPHESUS]] | Am I so round with you as you with me,<br>That like a football you do spurn me thus?<br>You spurn me hence, and he will spurn me hither.<br>If I last in this service, you must case me in leather. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | (to [[Adriana]])<br>Fie, how impatience loureth in your face! |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | His company must do his minions grace,<br>Whilst I at [[Home|home]] starve for a merry look.<br>Hath homely age th’alluring [[Beauty|beauty]] took<br>From my poor cheek? Then he hath wasted it.<br>Are my discourses dull? Barren my wit?<br>If voluble and sharp discourse be marred,<br>Unkindness blunts it more than marble hard.<br>Do their gay vestments his affections bait?<br>That’s not my fault: he’s master of my state.<br>What ruins are in me that can be found<br>By him not ruined? Then is he the ground<br>Of my defeatures. My decayed fair<br>A sunny look of his would soon repair.<br>But, too unruly deer, he breaks the pale,<br>And feeds from [[Home|home]]. Poor I am but his stale. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | [[Self]]-harming [[Jealousy|jealousy]]! Fie, beat it hence. |
| [[Adriana|ADRIANA]] | Unfeeling fools can with such wrongs dispense.<br>I know his eye doth homage otherwhere,<br>Or else what lets it but he would be here?<br>Sister, you know he promised me a chain.<br>Would that [[Alone|alone]] o’ [[Love|love]] he would detain,<br>So he would keep fair quarter with his bed.<br>I see the [[Jewel|jewel]] best enamelled<br>Will lose her [[Beauty|beauty]]. Yet the gold bides still<br>That others touch; and often touching will<br>Wear gold, and yet no man that hath a name<br>By falsehood and corruption doth it shame.<br>Since that my [[Beauty|beauty]] cannot please his eye,<br>I’ll weep what’s left away, and weeping die. |
| [[Luciana|LUCIANA]] | How many fond fools serve mad [[Jealousy|jealousy]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt into the Phoenix |
