| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[Queen Elizabeth]], the old Duchess of York, and Marquis Dorset at one door; [[Lady Anne]] (Duchess of [[Gloucester]]) with [[Clarence]]’s daughter at another door |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Who meets us here? My niece Plantagenet,<br>Led in the hand of her kind aunt of [[Gloucester]]?<br>Now for my life, she’s wand’ring to the Tower,<br>On pure heart’s [[Love|love]], to greet the tender Prince.—<br>Daughter, [[Well|well]] met. |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | God give your graces both 5<br>A happy and a joyful [[Time|time]] of day. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | As much to you, good sister. Whither away? |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | No farther than the Tower, and—as I guess—<br>Upon the like devotion as yourselves:<br>To gratulate the gentle princes there. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Kind sister, thanks. We’ll enter all together— |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter from the Tower Brackenbury the Lieutenant |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | And in good [[Time|time]], here the Lieutenant comes.<br>Master Lieutenant, pray you by your leave,<br>How doth the Prince, and my young son of York? |
| BRACKENBURY | Right [[Well|well]], dear madam. By your patience,<br>I may not suffer you to visit them.<br>The [[King]] hath strictly charged the contrary. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | The [[King]]? Who’s that? |
| BRACKENBURY | I mean, the Lord Protector. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | The Lord protect him from that kingly title.<br>Hath he set bounds between their [[Love|love]] and me?<br>I am their mother; who shall bar me from them? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | I am their father’s mother; I will see them. |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | Their aunt I am in law, in [[Love|love]] their mother;<br>Then bring me to their sights. I’ll bear thy blame,<br>And take thy office from thee on my peril. |
| BRACKENBURY | No, madam, no; I may not leave it so.<br>I am bound by oath, and therefore pardon me. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord Stanley Earl of Derby |
| STANLEY | Let me but meet you ladies one hour hence,<br>And I’ll salute your grace of York as mother<br>And reverend looker-on of two fair queens.<br>(To Anne) Come, madam, you must straight to<br>Westminster,<br>There to be crowned [[Richard]]’s royal [[Queen|queen]]. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Ah, cut my lace asunder, that my pent heart<br>May have some scope to beat, or else I swoon<br>With this dead-killing news. |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | Despiteful tidings! O unpleasing news! |
| DORSET | (to Anne)<br>Be of good cheer.—Mother, how fares your grace? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | O Dorset, speak not to me. Get thee gone.<br>[[Death]] and destruction [[dogs]] thee at thy heels.<br>Thy mother’s name is ominous to children.<br>If thou wilt outstrip [[Death|death]], go [[Cross|cross]] the seas,<br>And live with Richmond from the reach of hell.<br>Go, hie thee! Hie thee from this slaughterhouse,<br>Lest thou increase the number of the dead,<br>And make me die the thrall of Margaret’s curses:<br>‘Nor mother, wife, nor counted England’s [[Queen]]’. |
| STANLEY | Full of wise care is this your counsel, madam.<br>(To Dorset) Take all the swift advantage of the hours.<br>You shall have letters from me to my son<br>In your behalf, to meet you on the way.<br>Be not ta’en tardy by unwise delay. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | O ill-dispersing [[Wind|wind]] of misery!<br>O my accursed womb, the bed of [[Death|death]]!<br>A cockatrice hast thou hatched to the world,<br>Whose unavoided eye is murderous. |
| STANLEY | (to Anne)<br>Come, madam, come. I in all haste was sent. |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | And I in all unwillingness will go.<br>O would to God that the inclusive verge<br>Of golden metal that must round my brow<br>Were red-hot steel, to sear me to the brains.<br>Anointed let me be with deadly venom,<br>And die ere men can say ‘God save the [[Queen]]’. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Go, go, poor [[Soul|soul]]. I envy not thy glory.<br>To feed my [[Humour|humour]], wish thyself no harm. |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | No? Why? When he that is my husband now<br>Came to me as I followed Henry’s corpse,<br>When scarce the [[Blood|blood]] was [[Well|well]] washed from his<br>hands,<br>Which issued from my other angel husband<br>And that dear saint which then I weeping followed—<br>O when, I say, I looked on [[Richard]]’s face,<br>This was my wish: ‘Be thou’, quoth I, ‘accursed<br>For making me, so young, so old a widow,<br>And when thou wedd’st, let sorrow haunt thy bed;<br>And be thy wife—if any be so mad—<br>More miserable made by the life of thee<br>Than thou hast made me by my dear lord’s [[Death|death]].’<br>Lo, ere I can repeat this curse again, |
| *(didascalia)* | Within so small a [[Time|time]], my woman’s heart |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | Grossly grew captive to his honey words<br>And proved the subject of mine own [[Soul|soul]]’s curse,<br>Which hitherto hath held mine eyes from rest—<br>For never yet one hour in his bed<br>Did I enjoy the golden dew of [[Sleep|sleep]],<br>But with his timorous [[Dreams|dreams]] was still awaked.<br>Besides, he hates me for my father Warwick,<br>And will, no [[Doubt|doubt]], shortly be rid of me. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Poor heart, adieu. I pity thy complaining. |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | No more than with my [[Soul|soul]] I mourn for yours. |
| DORSET | Farewell, thou woeful welcomer of glory. |
| [[Lady Anne|LADY ANNE]] | Adieu, poor [[Soul|soul]], that tak’st thy leave of it. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Go thou to Richmond, and good fortune guide thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Dorset |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Go thou to [[Richard]], and good angels tend thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exeunt Anne, Stanley, and [[Clarence]]’s daughter |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Go thou to sanctuary, and good thoughts possess thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Elizabeth |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | I to my [[Grave|grave]], where peace and rest lie with me.<br>Eighty odd years of sorrow have I seen,<br>And each hour’s joy racked with a week of teen. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
