| *(didascalia)* | Enter old [[Queen Margaret]] |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | So now prosperity begins to mellow<br>And drop into the rotten mouth of [[Death|death]].<br>Here in these confines slyly have I lurked<br>To watch the waning of mine enemies.<br>A dire induction am I witness to,<br>And will to France, hoping the consequence<br>Will prove as bitter, black, and tragical. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter the old Duchess of York and [[Queen Elizabeth]] |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Withdraw thee, wretched Margaret. Who comes here? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Ah, my poor princes! Ah, my tender babes!<br>My unblown [[Flowers|flowers]], new-appearing sweets!<br>If yet your gentle souls fly in the air,<br>And be not fixed in doom perpetual,<br>Hover about me with your airy wings<br>And hear your mother’s lamentation. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (aside)<br>Hover about her, say that right for right<br>Hath dimmed your infant morn to aged [[Night|night]]. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | So many miseries have crazed my voice<br>That my woe-wearied tongue is still and mute.<br>Edward Plantagenet, why art thou dead? |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (aside)<br>Plantagenet doth quit Plantagenet;<br>Edward for Edward pays a dying debt. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Wilt thou, O God, fly from such gentle lambs<br>And throw them in the entrails of the wolf?<br>When didst thou [[Sleep|sleep]], when such a deed was done? |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (aside)<br>When holy Harry died, and my sweet son. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Dead life, blind sight, poor mortal living [[Ghost|ghost]],<br>Woe’s scene, world’s shame, [[Grave|grave]]’s due by life<br>usurped,<br>Brief abstract and record of tedious days,<br>Rest thy unrest on England’s lawful earth,<br>Unlawfully made drunk with innocents’ [[Blood|blood]].<br>They sit |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Ah that thou wouldst as soon afford a [[Grave|grave]]<br>As thou canst yield a [[Melancholy|melancholy]] seat.<br>Then would I hide my bones, not rest them here.<br>Ah, who hath any cause to mourn but we? |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | (coming forward)<br>If ancient sorrow be most reverend,<br>Give mine the benefit of seniory,<br>And let my griefs frown on the upper hand.<br>If sorrow can admit society,<br>Tell o’er your woes again by viewing mine.<br>I had an Edward, till a [[Richard]] killed him;<br>I had a husband, till a [[Richard]] killed him.<br>(To Elizabeth) Thou hadst an Edward, till a [[Richard]] killed him;<br>Thou hadst a [[Richard]], till a [[Richard]] killed him. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | rising<br>I had a [[Richard]] too, and thou didst kill him;<br>I had a Rutland too, thou holpst to kill him. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Thou hadst a [[Clarence]] too, and [[Richard]] killed him.<br>From forth the kennel of thy womb hath crept<br>A hell-hound that doth hunt us all to [[Death|death]]:<br>That dog that had his teeth before his eyes,<br>To worry lambs and lap their gentle [[Blood|blood]];<br>That foul defacer of God’s handiwork,<br>That reigns in galled eyes of weeping souls;<br>That excellent grand [[Tyrant|tyrant]] of the earth<br>Thy womb let loose to chase us to our graves.<br>O upright, just, and true-disposing God,<br>How do I thank thee that this charnel cur<br>Preys on the issue of his mother’s [[Body|body]],<br>And makes her pewfellow with others’ moan. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | O Harry’s wife, triumph not in my woes.<br>God witness with me, I have wept for thine. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Bear with me. I am hungry for [[Revenge|revenge]],<br>And now I cloy me with beholding it.<br>Thy Edward, he is dead, that killed my Edward;<br>Thy other Edward dead, to quite my Edward;<br>Young York, he is but boot, because both they<br>Matched not the high perfection of my loss;<br>Thy [[Clarence]], he is dead, that stabbed my Edward,<br>And the beholders of this frantic plays—<br>Th’adulterate [[Hastings]], Rivers, Vaughan, Gray—<br>Untimely smothered in their dusky graves.<br>[[Richard]] yet lives, hell’s black intelligencer,<br>Only reserved their factor to buy souls<br>And send them thither; but at hand, at hand<br>Ensues his piteous and unpitied end.<br>Earth gapes, hell burns, fiends roar, saints pray,<br>To have him suddenly conveyed from hence.<br>Cancel his bond of life, dear God, I plead,<br>That I may live and say, ‘The dog is dead’. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | O thou didst prophesy the [[Time|time]] would come<br>That I should wish for thee to help me curse<br>That bottled spider, that foul bunch-backed toad. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | I called thee then ‘vain flourish of my fortune’;<br>I called thee then, poor [[Shadow|shadow]], ‘painted [[Queen|queen]]’—<br>The presentation of but what I was,<br>The flattering index of a direful pageant,<br>One heaved a-high to be hurled down below,<br>A mother only mocked with two fair babes,<br>[[A Dream|A dream]] of what thou wast, a garish [[Flag|flag]]<br>To be the aim of every dangerous shot,<br>A sign of dignity, a breath, a bubble,<br>A [[Queen|queen]] in jest, only to fill the scene.<br>Where is thy husband now? Where be thy brothers?<br>Where are thy two sons? Wherein dost thou joy?<br>Who sues, and kneels, and says ‘God save the [[Queen]]’?<br>Where be the bending peers that flattered thee?<br>Where be the thronging troops that followed thee?<br>Decline all this, and see what now thou art:<br>For happy wife, a most distressed widow;<br>For joyful mother, one that wails the name;<br>For [[Queen|queen]], a very caitiff, crowned with care;<br>For one being sued to, one that humbly sues;<br>For she that scorned at me, now scorned of me;<br>For she being feared of all, now fearing one;<br>For she commanding all, obeyed of none.<br>Thus hath the course of justice whirled about,<br>And left thee but a very prey to [[Time|time]],<br>Having no more but thought of what thou wert<br>To torture thee the more, being what thou art.<br>Thou didst usurp my place, and dost thou not<br>Usurp the just proportion of my sorrow?<br>Now thy proud neck bears half my burdened yoke—<br>From which, even here, I slip my weary head,<br>And leave the burden of it all on thee.<br>Farewell, York’s wife, and [[Queen|queen]] of sad mischance.<br>These English woes shall make me smile in France. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | (rising)<br>O thou, [[Well|well]] skilled in curses, stay a while,<br>And teach me how to curse mine enemies. |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Forbear to [[Sleep|sleep]] the nights, and fast the days;<br>Compare dead happiness with living woe;<br>Think that thy babes were sweeter than they were,<br>And he that slew them fouler than he is.<br>Bett’ring thy loss makes the bad causer worse.<br>Revolving this will teach thee how to curse. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | My words are dull. O quicken them with thine! |
| [[Queen Margaret|QUEEN MARGARET]] | Thy woes will make them sharp and pierce like mine. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Why should calamity be full of words? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Windy attorneys to their client woes,<br>Airy recorders of intestate joys,<br>Poor breathing orators of miseries.<br>Let them have scope. Though what they will impart<br>Help nothing else, yet do they ease the heart. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | If so, then be not tongue-tied; go with me,<br>And in the breath of bitter words let’s smother<br>My damned son, that thy two sweet sons smothered.<br>A march within<br>The trumpet sounds. Be copious in exclaims. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter [[King]] [[Richard]] and his train marching with drummers and trumpeters |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Who intercepts me in my expedition? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | O, she that might have intercepted thee,<br>By strangling thee in her accursed womb,<br>From all the slaughters, wretch, that thou hast done. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Hid’st thou that forehead with a golden [[Crown|crown]],<br>Where should be branded—if that right were right—<br>The slaughter of the prince that owed that [[Crown|crown]],<br>And the dire [[Death|death]] of my poor sons and brothers?<br>Tell me, thou [[Villain|villain]]-slave, where are my children? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Thou toad, thou toad, where is thy brother [[Clarence]]?<br>And little Ned Plantagenet his son? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Where is the gentle Rivers, Vaughan, Gray? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Where is kind [[Hastings]]? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (to his train) |
| *(didascalia)* | A flourish, trumpets! Strike alarum, drums! |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Let not the heavens hear these tell-tale women<br>Rail on the Lord’s anointed. Strike, I say! |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Alarums |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | (To the women) Either be patient and entreat me fair,<br>Or with the clamorous report of war<br>Thus will I drown your exclamations. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Art thou my son? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ay, I thank God, my father, and yourself. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Then patiently hear my impatience. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Madam, I have a touch of your condition,<br>That cannot brook the accent of reproof. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | O let me speak! |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Do, then; but I’ll not hear. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | I will be mild and gentle in my words. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | And brief, good mother, for I am in haste. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Art thou so hasty? I have stayed for thee,<br>God knows, in torment and in agony— |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | And came I not at last to comfort you? |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | No, by the Holy Rood, thou know‘st it [[Well|well]].<br>Thou cam’st on earth to make the earth my hell.<br>A grievous burden was thy birth to me;<br>Tetchy and wayward was thy infancy;<br>Thy schooldays frightful, desp’rate, wild, and furious;<br>Thy prime of manhood daring, bold, and venturous;<br>Thy age confirmed, proud, subtle, sly, and bloody;<br>More mild, but yet more harmful; kind in hatred.<br>What comfortable hour canst thou name<br>That ever graced me in thy company? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | [[Faith]], none but Humphrey Hewer, that called your grace<br>To breakfast once, forth of my company.<br>If I be so disgracious in your eye,<br>Let me march on, and not offend you, madam.—<br>Strike up the drum. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | I pray thee, hear me speak. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | You speak too bitterly. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Hear me a word,<br>For I shall never speak to thee again. |
| DUCHESS OF YORK | Either thou wilt die by God’s just ordinance<br>Ere from this war thou turn a conqueror,<br>Or I with grief and extreme age shall perish,<br>And never more behold thy face again.<br>Therefore take with thee my most heavy curse,<br>Which in the day of battle tire thee more<br>Than all the complete armour that thou wear’st.<br>My prayers on the adverse party fight,<br>And there the little souls of Edward’s children<br>Whisper the spirits of thine enemies,<br>And promise them [[success]] and victory.<br>Bloody thou art, bloody will be thy end;<br>Shame serves thy life, and doth thy [[Death|death]] attend. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Though far more cause, yet much less spirit to curse<br>Abides in me; I say ‘Amen’ to all. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Stay, madam. I must talk a word with you. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | I have no more sons of the royal [[Blood|blood]]<br>For thee to slaughter. For my daughters, [[Richard]],<br>They shall be praying nuns, not weeping queens,<br>And therefore level not to hit their lives. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | You have a daughter called Elizabeth,<br>Virtuous and fair, royal and gracious. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | And must she die for this? O let her live,<br>And I’ll corrupt her manners, stain her [[Beauty|beauty]],<br>Slander myself as false to Edward’s bed,<br>Throw over her the veil of infamy.<br>So she may live unscarred of bleeding slaughter,<br>I will confess she was not Edward’s daughter. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Wrong not her birth. She is a royal princess. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | To save her life I’ll say she is not so. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Her life is safest only in her birth. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | And only in that safety died her brothers. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Lo, at their births good [[Stars|stars]] were opposite. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | No, to their lives ill friends were contrary. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | All unavoided is the doom of destiny— |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | True, when avoided grace makes destiny.<br>My babes were destined to a fairer [[Death|death]],<br>If grace had blessed thee with a fairer life. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Madam, so thrive I in my enterprise<br>And dangerous [[success]] of bloody wars,<br>As I intend more good to you and yours<br>Than ever you or yours by me were harmed. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | What good is covered with the face of heaven,<br>To be discovered, that can do me good? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Th’advancement of your children, gentle lady. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Up to some scaffold, there to lose their heads. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Unto the dignity and height of fortune,<br>The high imperial type of this earth’s glory. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Flatter my sorrow with report of it.<br>Tell me what state, what dignity, what [[Honour|honour]],<br>Canst thou demise to any [[Child|child]] of mine? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Even all I have—ay, and myself and all,<br>Will I withal endow a [[Child|child]] of thine,<br>So in the Lethe of thy angry [[Soul|soul]]<br>Thou drown the sad remembrance of those wrongs,<br>Which thou supposest I have done to thee. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Be brief, lest that the process of thy kindness<br>Last longer telling than thy kindness’ date. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Then know that, from my [[Soul|soul]], I [[Love|love]] thy daughter. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | My daughter’s mother thinks that with her [[Soul|soul]]. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | What do you think? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | That thou dost [[Love|love]] my daughter from thy [[Soul|soul]];<br>So from thy [[Soul|soul]]’s [[Love|love]] didst thou [[Love|love]] her brothers,<br>And from my heart’s [[Love|love]] I do thank thee for it. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Be not so hasty to confound my meaning.<br>I mean, that with my [[Soul|soul]] I [[Love|love]] thy daughter,<br>And do intend to make her [[Queen|queen]] of England. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | [[Well]] then, who dost thou mean shall be her [[King|king]]? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Even he that makes her [[Queen|queen]]. Who else should be? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | What, thou? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Even so. How think you of it? |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | How canst thou woo her? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | That would I learn of you,<br>As one being best acquainted with her [[Humour|humour]]. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | And wilt thou learn of me? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Madam, with all my heart. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Send to her, by the man that slew her brothers,<br>A pair of bleeding hearts; thereon engrave<br>‘Edward’ and ‘York’; then haply will she weep.<br>Therefore present to her—as sometimes Margaret<br>Did to thy father, steeped in Rutland’s [[Blood|blood]]—<br>A handkerchief which, say to her, did drain<br>The purple sap from her sweet brother’s [[Body|body]],<br>And bid her wipe her weeping eyes withal.<br>If this inducement move her not to [[Love|love]],<br>Send her a letter of thy noble deeds.<br>Tell her thou mad’st away her uncle [[Clarence]],<br>Her uncle Rivers—ay, and for her sake<br>Mad’st quick conveyance with her good aunt Anne. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | You mock me, madam. This is not the way<br>To win your daughter. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | There is no other way,<br>Unless thou couldst put on some other shape,<br>And not be [[Richard]], that hath done all this. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Infer fair England’s peace by this alliance. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Which she shall purchase with still-lasting war. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Tell her the [[King]], that may command, entreats. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | That at her hands which the [[King]]’s [[King]] forbids. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Say she shall be a high and mighty [[Queen|queen]]. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | To vail the title, as her mother doth. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Say I will [[Love|love]] her everlastingly. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | But how long shall that title ‘ever’ last? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Sweetly in force unto her fair life’s end. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | But how long fairly shall her sweet life last? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | As long as heaven and [[Nature|nature]] lengthens it. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | As long as hell and [[Richard]] likes of it. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Say I, her sovereign, am her subject [[Love|love]]. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | But she, your subject, loathes such sovereignty. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Be eloquent in my behalf to her. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | An honest tale speeds best being plainly told. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Then plainly to her tell my loving tale. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Plain and not honest is too harsh a style. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Your reasons are too shallow and too quick. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | O no, my reasons are too deep and dead—<br>Too deep and dead, poor infants, in their graves. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Harp not on that string, madam. That is past. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Harp on it still shall I, till heart-strings break. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Now by my George, my garter, and my [[Crown|crown]]— |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Profaned, dishonoured, and the third usurped. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | I swear— |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | By nothing, for this is no oath.<br>Thy George, profaned, hath lost his holy [[Honour|honour]];<br>Thy garter, blemished, pawned his lordly virtue;<br>Thy [[Crown|crown]], usurped, disgraced his kingly glory.<br>If something thou wouldst swear to be believed,<br>Swear then by something that thou hast not wronged. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Then by mysetf— |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Thy [[Self|self]] is [[Self|self]]-misused. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Now by the world— |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | ‘Tis full of thy foul wrongs. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | My father’s [[Death|death]]— |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Thy life hath that dishonoured. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Why then, by God— |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | God’s wrong is most of all.<br>If thou didst [[Fear|fear]] to break an oath with him,<br>The [[unity]] the [[King]] my husband made<br>Thou hadst not broken, nor my brothers died.<br>If thou hadst feared to break an oath by him,<br>Th’imperial metal circling now thy head<br>Had graced the tender temples of my [[Child|child]],<br>And both the princes had been breathing here,<br>Which now—two tender bedfellows for [[Dust|dust]]—<br>Thy broken [[Faith|faith]] hath made the prey for worms.<br>What canst thou swear by now? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | The [[Time|time]] to come. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | That thou hast wronged in the [[Time|time]] o‘erpast,<br>For I myself have many [[Tears|tears]] to wash<br>Hereafter [[Time|time]], for [[Time|time]] past wronged by thee.<br>The children live, whose fathers thou hast slaughtered—<br>Ungoverned youth, to wail it in their age.<br>The parents live, whose children thou hast butchered—<br>Old barren plants, to wail it with their age.<br>Swear not by [[Time|time]] to come, for that thou hast<br>Misused ere used, by times ill-used o’erpast. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | As I intend to prosper and repent,<br>So thrive I in my dangerous affairs<br>Of hostile arms—myself myself confound,<br>Heaven and fortune bar me happy hours,<br>Day yield me not thy light nor [[Night|night]] thy rest;<br>Be opposite, all planets of good luck,<br>To my proceeding—if, with dear heart’s [[Love|love]],<br>Immaculate devotion, holy thoughts,<br>I tender not thy beauteous, princely daughter.<br>In her consists my happiness and thine.<br>Without her follows—to myself and thee,<br>Herself, the land, and many a Christian [[Soul|soul]]—<br>[[Death]], desolation, ruin, and [[Decay|decay]].<br>It cannot be avoided but by this;<br>It will not be avoided but by this.<br>Therefore, good-mother—I must call you so—<br>Be the attorney of my [[Love|love]] to her.<br>Plead what I will be, not what I have been;<br>Not my deserts, but what I will deserve.<br>Urge the necessity and state of times,<br>And be not peevish-fond in great designs. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Shall I be tempted of the devil thus? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ay, if the devil tempt you to do good. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Shall I forget myself to be myself? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ay, if yourself’s remembrance wrong yourself. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Yet thou didst kill my children. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | But in your daughter’s womb I bury them,<br>Where, in that nest of spicery, they will breed<br>Selves of themselves, to your recomfiture. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | Shall I go win my daughter to thy will? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | And be a happy mother by the deed. |
| [[Queen Elizabeth|QUEEN ELIZABETH]] | I go. Write to me very shortly,<br>And you shall understand from me her mind. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Bear her my true [[Love|love]]’s kiss,<br>He kisses her<br>and so farewell— |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit Elizabeth |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Relenting [[Fool|fool]], and shallow, changing woman. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Sir [[Richard]] Ratcliffe |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | How now, what news? |
| RATCLIFFE | Most mighty sovereign, on the western coast<br>Rideth a puissant navy. To our shores<br>Throng many doubtful, hollow-hearted friends,<br>Unarmed and unresolved, to beat them back.<br>‘Tis thought that Richmond is their admiral,<br>And there they hull, expecting but the aid<br>Of [[Buckingham]] to welcome them ashore. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Some light-foot friend post to the Duke of Norfolk.<br>Ratcliffe thyself, or Catesby—where is he? |
| CATESBY | Here, my good lord. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Catesby, fly to the Duke. |
| CATESBY | I will, my lord, with all convenient haste. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ratcliffe, come hither. Post to Salisbury;<br>When thou com‘st thither—(to Catesby) dull, unmindful [[Villain|villain]],<br>Why stay’st thou here, and goest not to the Duke? |
| CATESBY | First, mighty liege, tell me your highness’ pleasure:<br>What from your grace I shall deliver to him? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | O true, good Catesby. Bid him levy straight<br>The greatest strength and power that he can make,<br>And meet me suddenly at Salisbury. |
| CATESBY | I go. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit |
| RATCLIFFE | What, may it please you, shall I do at Salisbury? |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Why, what wouldst thou do there before I go? |
| RATCLIFFE | Your highness told me I should post before. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | My mind is changed. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Lord Stanley |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Stanley, what news with you? |
| STANLEY | None, good my liege, to please you with the hearing,<br>Nor none so bad but [[Well|well]] may be reported. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Hoyday, a [[Riddle|riddle]]! Neither good nor bad.<br>Why need’st thou run so many mile about<br>When thou mayst tell thy tale the nearest way?<br>Once more, what news? |
| STANLEY | Richmond is on the seas. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | There let him sink, and be the seas on him.<br>White-livered renegade, what doth he there? |
| STANLEY | I know not, mighty sovereign, but by guess. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | [[Well]], as you guess? |
| STANLEY | Stirred up by Dorset, [[Buckingham]], and Ely,<br>He makes for England, here to claim the [[Crown|crown]]. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Is the chair empty? Is the [[Sword|sword]] unswayed?<br>Is the [[King]] dead? The empire unpossessed?<br>What heir of York is there alive but we?<br>And who is England’s [[King|king]] but great York’s heir?<br>Then tell me, what makes he upon the seas? |
| STANLEY | Unless for that, my liege, I cannot guess. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Unless for that he comes to be your liege,<br>You cannot guess wherefore the Welshman comes.<br>Thou wilt revolt and fly to him, I [[Fear|fear]]. |
| STANLEY | No, my good lord, therefore mistrust me not. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Where is thy power then? To beat him back,<br>Where be thy tenants and thy followers?<br>Are they not now upon the western shore,<br>Safe-conducting the rebels from their ships? |
| STANLEY | No, my good lord, my friends are in the north. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Cold friends to me. What do they in the north,<br>When they should serve their sovereign in the west? |
| STANLEY | They have not been commanded, mighty [[King]].<br>Pleaseth your majesty to give me leave,<br>I’ll muster up my friends and meet your grace<br>Where and what [[Time|time]] your majesty shall please. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Ay, ay, thou wouldst be gone to join with Richmond.<br>But I’ll not trust thee. |
| STANLEY | Most mighty sovereign,<br>You have no cause to hold my [[Friendship|friendship]] doubtful.<br>I never was, nor never will be, false. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Go then and muster men—but leave behind<br>Your son George Stanley. Look your heart be firm,<br>Or else his head’s assurance is but frail. |
| STANLEY | So deal with him as I prove true to you. Exit |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Messenger |
| MESSENGER | My gracious sovereign, now in Devonshire,<br>As I by friends am [[Well|well]] advertised,<br>Sir Edward Courtenay and the haughty prelate,<br>Bishop of Exeter, his elder brother,<br>With many more confederates are in arms. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger |
| SECOND MESSENGER | In [[Kent]], my liege, the Guildfords are in arms,<br>And every hour more competitors<br>Flock to the rebels, and their power grows strong. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger |
| THIRD MESSENGER | My lord, the army of great [[Buckingham]]— |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Out on ye, owls! Nothing but [[songs]] of [[Death|death]]?<br>He striketh him<br>There, take thou that, till thou bring better news. |
| THIRD MESSENGER | The news I have to tell your majesty<br>Is that, by sudden flood and fall of [[Water|water]],<br>[[Buckingham]]’s army is dispersed and scattered,<br>And he himself wandered away [[Alone|alone]],<br>No man knows whither. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | I cry thee [[Mercy|mercy]].—<br>Ratcliffe, reward him for the blow I gave him.—<br>Hath any [[Well|well]]-advisèd friend proclaimed<br>Reward to him that brings the traitor in? |
| THIRD MESSENGER | Such proclamation hath been made, my lord. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter another Messenger |
| FOURTH MESSENGER | Sir Thomas Lovell and Lord Marquis Dorset—<br>‘Tis said, my liege—in Yorkshire are in arms.<br>But this good comfort bring I to your highness:<br>The Breton navy is dispersed by tempest.<br>Richmond in Dorsetshire sent out a boat<br>Unto the shore, to ask those on the banks<br>If they were his assistants, yea or no?<br>Who answered him they came from [[Buckingham]]<br>Upon his party. He, mistrusting them,<br>Hoist sail and made his course again for Bretagne. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | March on, march on, since we are up in arms,<br>If not to fight with foreign enemies,<br>Yet to beat down these rebels here at [[Home|home]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Catesby |
| CATESBY | My liege, the [[Duke of Buckingham]] is taken.<br>That is the best news. That the [[Earl of Richmond]]<br>Is with a mighty power landed at Milford<br>Is colder tidings, yet they must be told. |
| [[King|KING]] [[Richard|RICHARD]] | Away, towards Salisbury! While we reason here,<br>A royal battle might be won and lost.<br>Someone take order [[Buckingham]] be brought<br>To Salisbury. The rest march on with me. |
| *(didascalia)* | Flourish. Exeunt |
