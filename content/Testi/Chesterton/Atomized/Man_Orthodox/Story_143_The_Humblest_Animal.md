THE HUMBLEST ANIMAL

It is in the nature of man to find symbols outside himself
of the qualities within himself.  Thus, for instance,
he has divided his virtues among the beasts of the field.
He makes the lion represent bravery; yet men are braver than lions.
No lions are ever burnt for their opinions.  He makes the fox
the type of cunning; yet men are more cunning than foxes.
No fox has ever thought of getting on a horse's back to travel faster.
He makes the ant a symbol of industry; but he is himself
a stronger symbol of industry.  He speaks of dog-like fidelity;
it would be more emphatic to speak of man-like fidelity.
Men are merrier than larks and much more hypocritical than crocodiles.
But we men cannot see ourselves as the great things that we are,
and perhaps it is our greatness that we cannot.  Perhaps this
fantastic modesty is the highest of the attributes of man.
Perhaps, amid the arrogance of oysters and the self-sufficiency
of snails, man is the only humble animal.

                                              Daily News, March 21st, 1906
