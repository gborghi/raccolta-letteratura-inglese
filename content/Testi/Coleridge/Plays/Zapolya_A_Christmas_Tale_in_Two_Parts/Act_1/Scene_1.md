| *(didascalia)* | Front of the Palace with a magnificent Colonnade. On one side a military Guard-[[house]]. Sentries pacing backward and forward before the Palace. CHEF RAGOZZI, at the door of the Guard-[[house]], as looking forwards at some object in the distance. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | My eyes deceive me not, it must be he.<br>Who but our chief, my more than father, who<br>But Raab Kiuprili moves with such a gait?<br>Lo! e’en this eager and unwonted haste<br>But agitates, not quells, its majesty.<br>My patron! my commander! yes, ‘tis he!<br>Call out the guards. The Lord Kiuprili comes. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Drums beat, &c., the Guard turns out. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter RAAB KIUPRILI. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | (making a signal to stop the drums, &c.). [[Silence]]! enough! This is no [[Time|time]], young friend,<br>For ceremonious dues. The summoning drum,<br>Th’ air-shattering trumpet, and the horseman’s clatter,<br>Are insults to a dying sovereign’s ear.<br>Soldiers, ‘tis [[Well|well]]! Retire! your General greets you,<br>His loyal fellow-warriors.      [Guards retire. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | Pardon my surprise.<br>Thus sudden from the camp, and unattended!<br>What may these wonders prophesy? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Tell me first,<br>How fares the [[King|king]]? His majesty still lives? |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | We know no otherwise; but Emerick’s friends<br>(And none but they approach him) scoff at [[Hope|hope]]. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Ragozzi! I have reared thee from a [[Child|child]],<br>And as a [[Child|child]] I have reared thee. Whence this air<br>Of mystery? That face was wont to open<br>Clear as the morning to me, shewing all things.<br>Hide nothing from me. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | O most loved, most honoured,<br>The mystery that struggles in my looks<br>Betrayed my whole tale to thee, if it told thee<br>That I am ignorant; but [[Fear|fear]] the worst.<br>And mystery is contagious. All things here<br>Are full of motion: and yet all is silent:<br>And bad men’s hopes infect the good with fears. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | I have trembling proof within how true thou speakest. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | That the prince Emerick feasts the soldiery,<br>Gives splendid arms, pays the commanders’ debts,<br>And (it is whispered) by sworn promises<br>Makes himself debtor — hearing this, thou hast heard<br>All ——<br>But what my lord will learn too soon himself. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Ha! — [[Well]] then, let it come! Worse scarce can come.<br>This letter written by the trembling hand<br>Of royal Andreas calls me from the camp<br>To his immediate presence. It appoints me,<br>The [[Queen]], and Emerick, guardians of the realm,<br>And of the royal infant. Day by day,<br>Robbed of Zapolya’s soothing cares, the [[King|king]]<br>Yearns only to behold one precious boon,<br>And with his life breathe forth a father’s blessing. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | Remember you, my lord! that Hebrew leech<br>Whose face so much distempered you? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Barzoni?<br>I held him for a spy; but the proof failing<br>(More courteously, I own, than pleased myself),<br>I sent him from the camp. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | To him, in chief,<br>Prince Emerick trusts his royal brother’s health. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Hide nothing, I conjure you! What of him? |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | With pomp of words beyond a soldier’s cunning,<br>And shrugs and wrinkled brow, he smiles and whispers!<br>Talks in dark words of women’s fancies; hints<br>That ‘twere a useless and a cruel zeal<br>To rob a dying man of any [[Hope|hope]],<br>However vain, that soothes him: and, in fine,<br>Denies all chance of offspring from the [[Queen]]. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | The venomous snake! My heel was on its head,<br>And ([[Fool|fool]]!) I did not crush it! |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | Nay, he fears<br>Zapolya will not long survive her husband. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Manifest treason! Even this brief delay<br>Half makes me an accomplice —— (If he live,)<br>[Is moving toward the palace.<br>If he but live and know me, all may —— |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | Halt!     [Stops him.<br>On [[Pain|pain]] of [[Death|death]], my Lord! am I commanded<br>To stop all ingress to the palace. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Thou! |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | No place, no name, no rank excepted — |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Thou! |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | This life of mine, O take it, Lord Kiuprili!<br>I give it as a weapon to thy hands,<br>Mine own no longer. Guardian of Illyria,<br>Useless to thee, ‘tis worthless to myself.<br>Thou art the framer of my nobler being;<br>Nor does there live one virtue in my [[Soul|soul]],<br>One honourable [[Hope|hope]], but calls thee father.<br>Yet ere thou dost resolve, know that yon palace<br>Is guarded from within, that each access<br>Is thronged by armed conspirators, watched by ruffians<br>Pampered with gifts, and hot upon the spoil<br>Which that false promiser still trails before them.<br>I ask but this one boon — reserve my life<br>Till I can lose it for the realm and thee! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | My heart is rent asunder. O my country,<br>O fallen Illyria, stand I here spell-bound?<br>Did my [[King]] [[Love|love]] me? Did I earn his [[Love|love]]?<br>Have we embraced as brothers would embrace?<br>Was I his arm, his thunder-bolt? And now<br>Must I, hag-ridden, pant as in [[A Dream|a dream]]?<br>Or, like an eagle, whose strong wings press up<br>Against a coiling serpent’s folds, can I<br>Strike but for mockery, and with restless beak<br>Gore my own breast? — Ragozzi, thou art faithful? |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | Here before Heaven I dedicate my [[Faith|faith]]<br>To the royal line of Andreas. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Hark, Ragozzi!<br>[[Guilt]] is a timorous thing ere perpetration:<br>[[Despair]] [[Alone|alone]] makes wicked men be bold.<br>Come thou with me! They have heard my voice in flight,<br>Have faced round, terror-struck, and feared no longer<br>The whistling javelins of their fell pursuers.<br>Ha! what is this?<br>[Black [[Flag|flag]] displayed from the Tower of the Palace: a [[Death|death]]-bell tolls, &c.<br>Vengeance of Heaven! He is dead. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | At length then ‘tis announced. Alas! I [[Fear|fear]],<br>That these black [[Death|death]]-flags are but treason’s signals. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | A prophecy too soon fulfilled! See yonder!<br>O rank and ravenous wolves! the [[Death|death]]-bell echoes<br>Still in the doleful air — and see! they come. |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | Precise and faithful in their villainy<br>Even to the moment, that the master traitor<br>Had pre-ordained them. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Was it over-haste,<br>Or is it scorn, that in this race of treason<br>Their [[Guilt|guilt]] thus drops its mask, and blazons forth<br>Their infamous plot even to an idiot’s sense? |
| CHEF RAGOZZI | Doubtless they deem Heaven too usurp’d! Heaven’s justice<br>Bought like themselves!<br>Being equal all in crime,<br>Do you press on, ye spotted parricides!<br>For the one sole pre-eminence yet doubtful,<br>The prize of foremost impudence in [[Guilt|guilt]]? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | The bad man’s cunning still prepares the way<br>For its own outwitting. I applaud, Ragozzi!<br>Ragozzi! I applaud,<br>In thee, the virtuous [[Hope|hope]] that dares look onward<br>And keeps the life-spark warm of future action<br>Beneath the cloak of patient sufferance.<br>Act and appear, as [[Time|time]] and prudence prompt thee:<br>I shall not misconceive the part thou playest.<br>Mine is an easier part — to brave the [[Usurper|usurper]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Enter a procession of EMERICK’S Adherents, Nobles, Chieftains, and Soldiers, with Music. They advance toward the front of the stage. KIUPRILI makes the signal for them to stop. — The Music ceases. |
| LEADER OF THE PROCESSION | The Lord Kiuprili! — Welcome from the camp. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | [[Grave]] magistrates and chieftains of Illyria,<br>In good [[Time|time]] come ye hither, if ye come<br>As loyal men with honourable purpose<br>To mourn what can [[Alone|alone]] be mourned; but chiefly<br>To enforce the last commands of royal Andreas<br>And shield the [[Queen]], Zapolya: haply making<br>The mother’s joy light up the widow’s [[Tears|tears]]. |
| LEADER | Our purpose demands [[Speed|speed]]. Grace our procession;<br>A warrior best will greet a warlike [[King|king]]. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | This patent written by your lawful [[King|king]],<br>(Lo! his own seal and signature attesting)<br>Appoints as guardians of his realm and offspring,<br>The [[Queen]], and the Prince Emerick, and myself.<br>[Voices of Live [[King|KING]] EMERICK! an EMERICK! an EMERICK!<br>What means this clamour? Are these madmen’s voices?<br>Or is some knot of riotous slanderers leagued<br>To infamize the name of the [[King|king]]’s brother<br>With a lie black as Hell? unmanly cruelty,<br>Ingratitude, and most unnatural treason?   [Murmurs.<br>What mean these murmurs? Dare then any here<br>Proclaim Prince Emerick a spotted traitor?<br>One that has taken from you your sworn [[Faith|faith]],<br>And given you in return a Judas’ bribe,<br>Infamy now, oppression in reversion,<br>And Heaven’s inevitable curse hereafter?<br>[Loud murmurs, followed by cries — EMERICK! No Baby Prince! No Changelings!<br>Yet bear with me awhile! Have I for this<br>Bled for your safety, conquered for your [[Honour|honour]]?<br>Was it for this, Illyrians! that I forded<br>Your thaw-swoln torrents, when the shouldering ice<br>Fought with the foe, and stained its jagged points<br>With gore from wounds I felt not? Did the blast<br>Beat on this [[Body|body]], [[Frost|frost]]-and-famine-numbed,<br>Till my hard flesh distinguished not itself<br>From the insensate mail, its fellow warrior?<br>And have I brought [[Home|home]] with me Victory,<br>And with her, hand in hand, firm-footed Peace,<br>Her countenance twice lighted up with glory,<br>As if I had charmed a goddess down from Heaven?<br>But these will flee abhorrent from the throne<br>Of usurpation!<br>[Murmurs increase — and cries of Onward! Onward!<br>Have you then thrown off shame,<br>And shall not a dear friend, a loyal subject,<br>Throw off all [[Fear|fear]]? I tell ye, the fair trophies<br>Valiantly wrested from a valiant foe,<br>[[Love]]’s natural offerings to a rightful [[King|king]],<br>Will hang as ill on this usurping traitor,<br>This brother-blight, this Emerick, as robes<br>Of gold plucked from the images of gods<br>Upon a sacrilegious robber’s back.       [Enter LORD CASIMIR. |
| CASIMIR | Who is this factious insolent, that dares brand<br>The elected [[King]], our chosen Emerick?<br>My father! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Casimir! He, he a traitor!<br>Too soon indeed, Ragozzi! have I learnt it.      [Aside. |
| CASIMIR | My father and my lord! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | I know thee not! |
| LEADER | Yet the remembrancing did sound right filial. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | A holy name and words of natural duty<br>Are blasted by a thankless traitor’s utterance. |
| CASIMIR | O hear me, Sire! not lightly have I sworn<br>Homage to Emerick. Illyria’s sceptre<br>Demands a manly hand, a warrior’s grasp.<br>The [[Queen|queen]] Zapolya’s [[Self|self]]-expected offspring<br>At least is doubtful: and of all our nobles,<br>The [[King|king]], inheriting his brother’s heart,<br>Hath honoured us the most. Your rank, my lord!<br>Already eminent, is — all it can be —<br>Confirmed: and me the [[King|king]]’s grace hath appointed<br>Chief of his council and the lord high steward. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | (Bought by a bribe!) I know thee now still less. |
| CASIMIR | So much of Raab Kiuprili’s [[Blood|blood]] flows here,<br>That no power, save that holy name of father,<br>Could shield the man who so dishonoured me. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | The son of Raab Kiuprili a bought bond-slave,<br>[[Guilt]]’s pander, treason’s mouth-piece, a gay parrot,<br>School’d to shrill forth his feeder’s usurp’d titles.<br>And scream, Long live [[King]] Emerick! |
| LEADERS | Aye, [[King]] Emerick!<br>Stand back, my lord! Lead us, or let us pass. |
| SOLDIER | Nay, let the general speak! |
| SOLDIERS | Hear him! hear him! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Hear me,<br>Assembled lords and warriors of Illyria,<br>Hear, and avenge me! Twice ten years have I<br>Stood in your presence, honoured by the [[King|king]]:<br>Beloved and trusted. Is there one among you<br>Accuses Raab Kiuprili of a bribe?<br>Or one false whisper in his sovereign’s ear?<br>Who here dares charge me with an [[Orphan|orphan]]’s rights<br>Outfaced, or widow’s plea left undefended?<br>And shall I now be branded by a traitor,<br>A bought, bribed wretch, who, being called my son,<br>Doth libel a chaste matron’s name, and plant<br>Hensbane and aconite on a mother’s [[Grave|grave]]?<br>The underling accomplice of a robber,<br>That from a widow and a widow’s offspring<br>Would steal their heritage? To God a [[Rebel|rebel]],<br>And to the common father of his country<br>A recreant ingrate! |
| CASIMIR | Sire! your words grow dangerous.<br>High-flown romantic fancies ill-beseem<br>Your age and wisdom. ‘Tis a statesman’s virtue,<br>To guard his country’s safety by what means<br>It best may be protected — come what will<br>Of these monk’s morals! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | (aside). Ha! the elder [[Brutus]]<br>Made his [[Soul|soul]] iron, though his sons repented.<br>They boasted not their baseness.   [Draws his [[Sword|sword]].<br>Infamous changeling!<br>Recant this instant, and swear loyalty,<br>And strict obedience to thy sovereign’s will;<br>Or, by the spirit of departed Andreas,<br>Thou diest ——<br>[Chiefs, &c., rush to interpose; during the tumult enter EMERICK, alarmed. |
| EMERICK | Call out the guard! Ragozzi! seize the assassin. ——<br>Kiuprili? Ha! ——     [Making signs to the guard to retire.<br>Pass on, friends! to the palace.<br>[Music recommences. — The Procession passes into the Palace. |
| EMERICK | What? Raab Kiuprili? What? a father’s [[Sword|sword]]<br>Against his own son’s breast? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | ‘Twould best excuse him,<br>Were he thy son, Prince Emerick. I abjure him. |
| EMERICK | This is my thanks, then, that I have commenced<br>A reign to which the free voice of the nobles<br>Hath called me, and the people, by regards<br>Of [[Love|love]] and grace to Raab Kiuprili’s [[house]]? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | What right hadst thou, Prince Emerick, to bestow them? |
| EMERICK | By what right dares Kiuprili question me? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | By a right common to all loyal subjects —<br>To me a duty! As the realm’s co-regent,<br>Appointed by our sovereign’s last free act,<br>Writ by himself. —      [Grasping the Patent. |
| EMERICK | Aye! — Writ in a delirium! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | I likewise ask, by whose authority<br>The access to the sovereign was refused me? |
| EMERICK | By whose authority dared the general leave<br>His camp and army, like a fugitive? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | A fugitive, who, with victory for his comrade,<br>Ran, open-eyed, upon the face of [[Death|death]]!<br>A fugitive, with no other [[Fear|fear]], than bodements<br>To be belated in a loyal purpose —<br>At the command, Prince! of my [[King|king]] and thine,<br>Hither I came; and now again require<br>Audience of [[Queen]] Zapolya; and (the States<br>Forthwith convened) that thou dost shew at large,<br>On what ground of defect thou’st dared annul<br>This thy [[King]]’s last and solemn act — hast dared<br>Ascend the throne, of which the law had named,<br>And [[Conscience|conscience]] should have made thee, a protector. |
| EMERICK | A sovereign’s ear ill brooks a subject’s questioning!<br>Yet for thy past [[Well|well]]-doing — and because<br>‘Tis hard to erase at once the fond belief<br>Long cherished, that Illyria had in thee<br>No dreaming priest’s slave, but a Roman [[Lover|lover]]<br>Of her true weal and [[Freedom|freedom]] — and for this, too,<br>That, hoping to call forth to the broad day-light<br>And fostering breeze of glory all deservings,<br>I still had placed thee foremost. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Prince! I listen. |
| EMERICK | Unwillingly I tell thee, that Zapolya,<br>Maddened with grief, her erring hopes proved idle — |
| CASIMIR | Sire! speak the whole [[Truth|truth]]! Say, her fraud detected! |
| EMERICK | According to the sworn attests in council<br>Of her physician —— |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | (aside). Yes! the Jew, Barzoni! |
| EMERICK | Under the imminent risk of [[Death|death]] she lies,<br>Or irrecoverable loss of reason,<br>If known friend’s face or voice renew the frenzy. |
| CASIMIR | (to Kiuprili). Trust me, my lord! a woman’s trick has duped you —<br>Us too — but most of all, the sainted Andreas.<br>Even for his own fair fame, his grace prays hourly<br>For her recovery, that (the States convened)<br>She may take counsel of her friends. |
| EMERICK | Right, Casimir!<br>Receive my pledge, lord general. It shall stand<br>In her own will to appear and voice her claims;<br>Or (which in [[Truth|truth]] I hold the wiser course)<br>With all the past passed by, as family quarrels,<br>Let the [[Queen]] Dowager, with unblenched honours,<br>Resume her state, our first Illyrian matron. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Prince Emerick! you speak fairly, and your pledge too<br>Is such, as [[Well|well]] would suit an honest meaning. |
| CASIMIR | My lord! you scarce know half his grace’s goodness.<br>The wealthy heiress, high-born fair Sarolta,<br>Bred in the convent of our noble ladies,<br>Her relative, the venerable abbess,<br>Hath, at his grace’s urgence, wooed and won for me. |
| EMERICK | Long may the race, and long may that name flourish,<br>Which your heroic deeds, brave chief, have rendered<br>Dear and illustrious to all true Illyrians. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | The longest line that ever tracing herald<br>Or found or feigned, placed by a beggar’s [[Soul|soul]]<br>Hath but a mushroom’s date in the comparison:<br>And with the [[Soul|soul]], the [[Conscience|conscience]] is coeval,<br>Yea, the [[Soul|soul]]’s essence. |
| EMERICK | [[Conscience]], good my lord,<br>Is but the pulse of reason. Is it [[Conscience|conscience]],<br>That a free nation should be handed down,<br>Like the dull clods beneath our feet, by chance<br>And the blind law of lineage? That whether infant,<br>Or man matured, a wise man or an idiot,<br>[[Hero]] or natural coward, shall have guidance<br>Of a free people’s destiny, should fall out<br>In the mere lottery of a reckless [[Nature|nature]],<br>Where few the prizes and the blanks are countless?<br>Or haply that a nation’s fate should hang<br>On the bald accident of a midwife’s handling<br>The unclosed sutures of an infant’s skull? |
| CASIMIR | What better claim can sovereign wish or need<br>Than the free voice of men who [[Love|love]] their country?<br>Those chiefly who have fought for’t? Who by right,<br>Claim for their monarch one, who having obeyed,<br>So hath best learnt to govern; who, having suffered,<br>Can feel for each brave sufferer and reward him?<br>Whence sprang the name of Emperor? Was it not<br>By [[Nature]]’s fiat? In the [[Storm|storm]] of triumph,<br>‘Mid warriors’ shouts, did her oracular voice<br>Make itself heard: Let the commanding spirit<br>Possess the station of command! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Prince Emerick,<br>Your cause will prosper best in your own pleading. |
| EMERICK | (aside to Casimir). Ragozzi was thy school-mate — a bold spirit!<br>Bind him to us! — Thy father thaws apace!    [Then aloud.<br>Leave us awhile, my lord! — Your friend, Ragozzi,<br>Whom you have not yet seen since his return,<br>Commands the guard to-day.<br>[CASIMIR retires to the Guard-[[house]]; and after a [[Time|time]] appears before it with CHEF RAGOZZI.<br>We are [[Alone|alone]].<br>What further pledge or proof desires Kiuprili?<br>Then, with your assent —— |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Mistake not for assent<br>The unquiet [[Silence|silence]] of a stern resolve<br>Throttling the impatient voice. I have heard thee, Prince!<br>And I have watched thee, too; but have small [[Faith|faith]] in<br>A plausible tale told with a flitting eye.<br>[EMERICK turns as about to call for the Guard.<br>In the next moment I am in thy power,<br>In this thou art in mine. Stir but a step,<br>Or make one sign — I swear by this good [[Sword|sword]],<br>Thou diest that instant. |
| EMERICK | Ha, ha! — [[Well]], Sir! — Conclude your homily. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | A tale which, whether true or false, comes guarded<br>Against all means of proof, detects itself.<br>The [[Queen]] mew’d up — this too from anxious care<br>And [[Love|love]] brought forth of a sudden, a twin birth<br>With thy discovery of her plot to rob thee<br>Of a rightful throne! — Mark how the scorpion, falsehood,<br>Coils round in its own perplexity, and fixes<br>Its sting in its own head! |
| EMERICK | Aye! to the mark! |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Had’st thou believed thine own tale, had’st thou fancied<br>Thyself the rightful successor of Andreas,<br>Would’st thou have pilfered from our school-boys’ themes<br>These shallow sophisms of a popular choice?<br>What people? How convened? or, if convened,<br>Must not the [[Magic|magic]] power that charms together<br>Millions of men in council, needs have power<br>To win or wield them? Better, O far better<br>Shout forth thy titles to yon circling [[Mountains|mountains]],<br>And with a thousand-fold reverberation<br>Make the rocks flatter thee, and the volleying air,<br>Unbribed, shout back to thee, [[King]] Emerick!<br>By wholesome laws to embank the sovereign power,<br>To deepen by restraint, and by prevention<br>Of lawless will to amass and guide the flood<br>In its majestic channel, is man’s task<br>And the true patriot’s glory! In all else<br>Men safelier trust to Heaven, than to themselves<br>When least themselves in the mad whirl of crowds<br>Where folly is contagious, and too oft<br>Even wise men leave their better sense at [[Home|home]]<br>To chide and [[Wonder|wonder]] at them when returned. |
| EMERICK | (aloud). Is’t thus thou scoff’st the people? most of all,<br>The soldiers, the defenders of the people? |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | O most of all, most miserable nation,<br>For whom the imperial power, enormous bubble!<br>Is blown and kept aloft, or burst and shattered<br>By the bribed breath of a lewd soldiery!<br>Chiefly of such, as from the frontiers far,<br>(Which is the noblest station of true warriors)<br>In rank licentious idleness beleaguer<br>[[City]] and [[Court]], a venomed thorn i’the side<br>Of virtuous kings, the [[Tyrant|tyrant]]’s slave and [[Tyrant|tyrant]],<br>Still ravening for fresh largess! But with such<br>What title claim’st thou, save thy birth? What merits<br>Which many a liegeman may not plead as [[Well|well]],<br>Brave though I grant thee? If a life outlaboured<br>Head, heart, and fortunate arm, in watch and war,<br>For the land’s fame and weal; if large acquests,<br>Made honest by the aggression of the foe,<br>And whose best praise is, that they bring us safety;<br>If victory, doubly-wreathed, whose under-garland<br>Of laurel-[[Leaves|leaves]] looks greener and more sparkling<br>Thro’ the grey olive-branch; if these, Prince Emerick!<br>Give the true title to the throne, not thou —<br>No! (let Illyria, let the infidel enemy<br>Be judge and arbiter between us!) I,<br>I were the rightful sovereign! |
| EMERICK | I have [[Faith|faith]]<br>That thou both think’st and hop’st it. Fair Zapolya,<br>A provident lady — |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | Wretch beneath all answer! |
| EMERICK | Offers at once the royal bed and throne! |
