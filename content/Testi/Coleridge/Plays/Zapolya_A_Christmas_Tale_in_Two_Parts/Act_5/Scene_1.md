| *(didascalia)* | A glade in a wood. Enter CASIMIR looking anxiously around. |
| CASIMIR | This needs must be the spot! O, here he comes! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter LORD RUDOLPH. |
| CASIMIR | [[Well]] met, Lord Rudolph! ——<br>Your whisper was not lost upon my ear,<br>And I dare trust — |
| LORD RUDOLPH | Enough! the [[Time|time]] is precious!<br>You left Temeswar late on yester-eve?<br>And sojourned there some hours? |
| CASIMIR | I did so! |
| LORD RUDOLPH | Heard you<br>Aught of a hunt preparing? |
| CASIMIR | Yes; and met<br>The assembled huntsmen! |
| LORD RUDOLPH | Was there no word given? |
| CASIMIR | The word for me was this: — The royal Leopard<br>Chases thy milk-white dedicated Hind. |
| LORD RUDOLPH | Your answer? |
| CASIMIR | As the word proves false or true<br>Will Casimir [[Cross|cross]] the hunt, or join the huntsmen! |
| LORD RUDOLPH | The event redeemed their pledge? |
| CASIMIR | It did, and therefore<br>Have I sent back both pledge and invitation.<br>The spotless Hind hath fled to them for shelter,<br>And bears with her my seal of fellowship!   [They take hands. |
| LORD RUDOLPH | But Emerick! how when you reported to him<br>Sarolta’s disappearance, and the flight<br>Of Bethlen with his guards? |
| CASIMIR | O he received it<br>As evidence of their mutual [[Guilt|guilt]]. In fine,<br>With cozening warmth condoled with, and dismissed me. |
| LORD RUDOLPH | I entered as the door was closing on you:<br>His eye was fixed, yet seemed to follow you, —<br>With such a look of hate, and scorn and triumph,<br>As if he had you in the toils already,<br>And were then choosing where to stab you first.<br>But hush! draw back! |
| CASIMIR | This nook is at the furthest<br>From any beaten track. |
| LORD RUDOLPH | There! mark them!<br>[Points to where LASKA and PESTALUTZ [[Cross|cross]] the Stage. |
| CASIMIR | Laska! |
| LORD RUDOLPH | One of the two I recognized this morning;<br>His name is Pestalutz: a trusty ruffian<br>Whose face is prologue still to some dark murder.<br>Beware no stratagem, no trick of message,<br>Dispart you from your servants. |
| CASIMIR | (aside).     I deserve it.<br>The comrade of that ruffian is my servant:<br>The one I trusted most and most preferred.<br>But we must part. What makes the [[King|king]] so late?<br>It was his wont to be an early stirrer. |
| LORD RUDOLPH | And his main policy.<br>To enthral the sluggard [[Nature|nature]] in ourselves<br>Is, in good [[Truth|truth]], the better half of the secret<br>To enthral the world: for the will governs all.<br>See, the sky lowers! the [[Cross|cross]]-winds waywardly<br>Chase the fantastic masses of the clouds<br>With a wild mockery of the coming hunt! |
| CASIMIR | Mark yonder mass! I make it wear the shape<br>Of a huge ram that butts with head depressed. |
| LORD RUDOLPH | (smiling). Belike, some stray sheep of the oozy flock,<br>Which, if bards lie not, the Sea-shepherds tend,<br>Glaucus or [[Proteus]]. But my fancy shapes it<br>A [[Monster|monster]] couchant on a rocky shelf. |
| CASIMIR | Mark too the edges of the lurid mass —<br>Restless, as if some idly-vexing Sprite,<br>On swift wing coasting by, with tetchy hand<br>Pluck’d at the ringlets of the vaporous Fleece.<br>These are sure signs of conflict nigh at hand,<br>And elemental war!<br>[A single trumpet heard at some distance. |
| LORD RUDOLPH | That single blast<br>Announces that the [[Tyrant|tyrant]]’s pawing courser<br>Neighs at the gate.         [Trumpets.<br>Hark! now the [[King|king]] comes forth!<br>For ever ‘midst this crash of horns and clarions<br>He mounts his steed, which proudly rears an-end<br>While he looks round at ease, and scans the crowd,<br>Vain of his stately form and horsemanship!<br>I must away! my absence may be noticed. |
| CASIMIR | Oft as thou canst, [[Essay|essay]] to lead the hunt<br>Hard by the forest-skirts; and ere high [[Noon|noon]]<br>Expect our sworn confederates from Temeswar.<br>I trust, ere yet this clouded sun slopes westward,<br>That Emerick’s [[Death|death]], or Casimir’s, will appease<br>The manes of Zapolya and Kiuprili!    [Exit RUDOLPH.<br>The traitor, Laska! ——<br>And yet Sarolta, simple, inexperienced,<br>Could see him as he was, and often warned me.<br>Whence learned she this? — O she was [[Innocent|innocent]]!<br>And to be [[Innocent|innocent]] is [[Nature]]’s wisdom!<br>The fledge-dove knows the prowlers of the air,<br>Feared soon as seen, and flutters back to shelter.<br>And the young steed recoils upon his haunches,<br>The never-yet-seen adder’s hiss first heard.<br>O surer than Suspicion’s hundred eyes<br>Is that fine sense, which to the pure in heart,<br>By mere oppugnancy of their own goodness,<br>Reveals the approach of evil. Casimir!<br>O [[Fool|fool]]! O parricide! through yon wood did’st thou,<br>With [[Fire|fire]] and [[Sword|sword]], pursue a patriot father,<br>A widow and an [[Orphan|orphan]]. Dar’st thou then<br>(Curse-laden wretch) put forth these hands to raise<br>The ark, all sacred, of thy country’s cause?<br>Look down in pity on thy son, Kiuprili!<br>And let this deep abhorrence of his crime,<br>Unstained with selfish fears, be his atonement!<br>O strengthen him to nobler compensation<br>In the deliverance of his bleeding country!    [Exit CASIMIR. |
| *(didascalia)* | Scene changes to the mouth of a Cavern, as in Act II. ZAPOLYA and GLYCINE discovered. |
| ZAPOLYA | Our friend is gone to seek some safer [[Cave|cave]]:<br>Do not then leave me long [[Alone|alone]], Glycine!<br>Having enjoyed thy commune, [[Loneliness|loneliness]],<br>That but oppressed me hitherto, now scares. |
| GLYCINE | I shall know Bethlen at the furthest distance,<br>And the same moment I descry him, lady,<br>I will return to you.   [Exit GLYCINE.<br>[Enter OLD BATHORY, speaking as he enters. |
| OLD BATHORY | Who hears? A friend!<br>A messenger from him who bears the signet! |
| ZAPOLYA | He hath the watch-word! — Art thou not Bathory? |
| OLD BATHORY | O noble lady! greetings from your son!<br>[BATHORY kneels. |
| ZAPOLYA | Rise! rise! Or shall I rather kneel beside thee,<br>And call down blessings from the wealth of Heaven<br>Upon thy honoured head? When thou last saw’st me<br>I would full fain have knelt to thee, and could not,<br>Thou dear old man! How oft since then in [[Dreams|dreams]]<br>Have I done worship to thee, as an angel<br>Bearing my helpless babe upon thy wings! |
| OLD BATHORY | O he was born to [[Honour|honour]]! Gallant deeds<br>And perilous hath he wrought since yester-eve.<br>Now from Temeswar (for to him was trusted<br>A life, save thine, the dearest) he hastes hither — |
| ZAPOLYA | Lady Sarolta mean’st thou? |
| OLD BATHORY | She is safe.<br>The royal brute hath overleapt his prey,<br>And when he turned, a sworded Virtue faced him.<br>My own brave boy — O pardon, noble lady!<br>Your son —— |
| ZAPOLYA | Hark! Is it he? |
| OLD BATHORY | I hear a voice<br>Too hoarse for Bethlen’s! ‘Twas his scheme and [[Hope|hope]],<br>Long ere the hunters could approach the forest,<br>To have led you hence. — Retire. |
| ZAPOLYA | O life of terrors! |
| OLD BATHORY | In the [[Cave|cave]]’s mouth we have such ‘vantage ground<br>That even this old arm —<br>[Exeunt ZAPOLYA and BATHORY into the [[Cave|cave]].<br>Enter LASKA and PESTALUTZ. |
| LASKA | Not a step further! |
| PESTALUTZ | Dastard! was this your promise to the [[King|king]]? |
| LASKA | I have fulfilled his orders. Have walked with you<br>As with a friend: have pointed out Lord Casimir:<br>And now I leave you to take care of him.<br>For the [[King|king]]’s purposes are doubtless friendly. |
| PESTALUTZ | Be on your guard, man! |
| LASKA | Ha! what now? |
| PESTALUTZ | Behind you!<br>‘Twas one of [[Satan]]’s imps, that grinned and threatened you<br>For your most impudent [[Hope|hope]] to cheat his master! |
| LASKA | Pshaw! What! you think ‘tis [[Fear|fear]] that makes me leave you? |
| PESTALUTZ | Is’t not enough to play the knave to others,<br>But thou must lie to thine own heart? |
| LASKA | Friend! Laska will be found at his own post,<br>Watching elsewhere for the [[King|king]]’s interest.<br>There’s a rank plot that Laska must hunt down,<br>‘Twixt Bethlen and Glycine! |
| PESTALUTZ | What! the girl<br>Whom Laska saw the war-wolf tear in pieces? |
| LASKA | [[Well]]! Take my arms! Hark! should your javelin fail you,<br>These points are tipt with venom.    [Seeing GLYCINE without.<br>By Heaven! Glycine!<br>Now as you [[Love|love]] the [[King|king]], help me to seize her!<br>[They run out after GLYCINE. Enter BATHORY from the cavern. |
| OLD BATHORY | Rest, lady, rest! I feel in every sinew<br>A young man’s strength returning! Which way went they?<br>The shriek came thence.         [Enter GLYCINE. |
| GLYCINE | Ha! weapons here? Then, Bethlen, thy Glycine<br>Will die with thee or save thee!<br>[She seizes them and rushes out. BATHORY following. Music, and Peasants with hunting spears [[Cross|cross]] the stage, singing chorally. |
| *(didascalia)* | CHORAL [[Song|SONG]]<br>Up, up! ye dames, ye lasses gay!<br>To the meadows trip away.<br>‘Tis you must tend the flocks this morn,<br>And scare the small [[Birds|birds]] from the corn.<br>Not a [[Soul|soul]] at [[Home|home]] may stay:<br>For the shepherds must go<br>With lance and bow<br>To hunt the wolf in the woods to-day.<br>Leave the hearth and leave the [[house]]<br>To the cricket and the mouse:<br>Find grannam out a sunny seat,<br>With babe and lambkin at her feet.<br>Not a [[Soul|soul]] at [[Home|home]] may stay:<br>For the shepherds must go<br>With lance and bow<br>To hunt the wolf in the woods to-day.<br>[Exeunt Huntsmen. |
| *(didascalia)* | Re-enter BATHORY, BETHLEN, and GLYCINE. |
| GLYCINE | And now once more a woman —— |
| BETHLEN | Was it then<br>That timid eye, was it those maiden hands<br>That sped the shaft, which saved me and avenged me? |
| OLD BATHORY | ‘Twas as a vision blazoned on a cloud<br>By [[Lightning|lightning]], shaped into a passionate scheme<br>Of life and [[Death|death]]! I saw the traitor, Laska,<br>Stoop and snatch up the javelin of his comrade;<br>The point was at your back, when her shaft reached him.<br>The coward turned, and at the [[Self|self]]-same instant<br>The braver [[Villain|villain]] fell beneath your [[Sword|sword]].<br>[Enter ZAPOLYA. |
| ZAPOLYA | Bethlen! my [[Child|child]]! and safe too! |
| BETHLEN | Mother! [[Queen]].<br>Royal Zapolya! name me Andreas!<br>Nor blame thy son, if being a [[King|king]], he yet<br>Hath made his own arm minister of his justice.<br>So do the gods who launch the thunderbolt! |
| ZAPOLYA | O Raab Kiuprili! Friend! Protector! Guide!<br>In vain we trenched the altar round with waters,<br>A flash from Heaven hath touched the hidden incense — |
| BETHLEN | And that majestic form that stood beside thee<br>Was Raab Kiuprili! |
| ZAPOLYA | It was Raab Kiuprili;<br>As sure as thou art Andreas, and the [[King|king]]. |
| OLD BATHORY | Hail Andreas! hail my [[King|king]]! |
| ANDREAS | Stop, thou revered one,<br>Lest we offend the jealous destinies<br>By shouts ere victory. Deem it then thy duty<br>To pay this homage, when ‘tis mine to claim it. |
| GLYCINE | Accept thine hand-maid’s service!       [Kneeling. |
| ZAPOLYA | Raise her, son!<br>O raise her to thine arms! she saved thy life,<br>And through her [[Love|love]] for thee, she saved thy mother’s!<br>Hereafter thou shalt know, that this dear maid<br>Hath other and hereditary claims<br>Upon thy heart, and with Heaven guarded instinct<br>But carried on the work her sire began! |
| ANDREAS | Dear maid! more dear thou canst not be! the rest<br>Shall make my [[Love|love]] religion. Haste we hence:<br>For as I reached the skirts of this high forest,<br>I heard the noise and uproar of the chase,<br>Doubling its echoes from the mountain foot. |
| GLYCINE | Hark! sure the hunt approaches.<br>[Horn without, and afterwards distant thunder. |
| ZAPOLYA | O Kiuprili! |
| OLD BATHORY | The demon-hunters of the middle air<br>Are in full cry, and scare with arrowy [[Fire|fire]]<br>The guilty! Hark! now here, now there, a horn<br>Swells singly with irregular blast! the tempest<br>Has scattered them!       [Horns at a distance. |
| ZAPOLYA | O Heavens! where stays Kiuprili? |
| OLD BATHORY | The wood will be surrounded! leave me here. |
| ANDREAS | My mother! let me see thee once in safety.<br>I too will hasten back, with [[Lightning|lightning]]’s [[Speed|speed]],<br>To seek the [[Hero|hero]]! |
| OLD BATHORY | Haste! my life upon it<br>I’ll guide him safe. |
| ANDREAS | (thunder). Ha! what a crash was there!<br>Heaven seems to claim a mightier criminal<br>Than yon vile subaltern. |
| ZAPOLYA | Your behest, High powers,<br>Lo, I obey! To the appointed spirit,<br>That hath so long kept watch round this drear cavern,<br>In fervent [[Faith|faith]], Kiuprili, I entrust thee!<br>[Exeunt ZAPOLYA, ANDREAS, and GLYCINE. |
| OLD BATHORY | Yon bleeding corse may work us mischief still:<br>Once seen, ‘twill rouse alarm and crowd the hunt<br>From all parts towards this spot. Stript of its armour,<br>I’ll drag it hither.<br>[Exit BATHORY. Several Hunters [[Cross|cross]] the Stage. Enter KIUPRILI. |
| RAAB KIUPRILI | (throwing off his disguise). Since Heaven [[Alone|alone]] can save me, Heaven [[Alone|alone]]<br>Shall be my trust.<br>Haste! haste! Zapolya, flee!<br>Gone! Seized perhaps? Oh no, let me not perish<br>Despairing of Heaven’s justice! Faint, disarmed,<br>Each sinew powerless; senseless rock, sustain me!<br>Thou art parcel of my native land!<br>A [[Sword|sword]]!<br>Ha! and my [[Sword|sword]]! Zapolya hath escaped,<br>The murderers are baffled, and there lives<br>An Andreas to avenge Kiuprili’s fall! —<br>There was a [[Time|time]], when this dear [[Sword|sword]] did flash<br>As dreadful as the [[Storm|storm]]-[[Fire|fire]] from mine arm —<br>I can scarce raise it now — yet come, fell [[Tyrant|tyrant]]!<br>And bring with thee my shame and bitter anguish,<br>To end his work and thine! Kiuprili now<br>Can take the [[Death|death]]-blow as a soldier should.<br>[Re-enter BATHORY, with the dead [[Body|body]] of PESTALUTZ. |
| OLD BATHORY | Poor tool and victim of another’s [[Guilt|guilt]]!<br>Thou follow’st heavily: a reluctant weight!<br>Good [[Truth|truth]], it is an undeservéd [[Honour|honour]]<br>That in Zapolya and Kiuprili’s [[Cave|cave]]<br>A wretch like thee should find a burial-place.<br>‘Tis he! — In Andreas’ and Zapolya’s name<br>Follow me, reverend form! Thou need’st not speak,<br>For thou canst be no other than Kiuprili. |
| KIUPRILI | And are they safe?       [Noise without. |
| OLD BATHORY | Conceal yourself, my lord!<br>I will mislead them! |
| KIUPRILI | Is Zapolya safe? |
| OLD BATHORY | I [[Doubt|doubt]] it not; but haste, haste, I conjure you!      [Enter CASIMIR. |
| CASIMIR | [[Monster]]!<br>Thou shalt not now escape me! |
| OLD BATHORY | Stop, lord Casimir!<br>It is no [[Monster|monster]]. |
| CASIMIR | Art thou too a traitor?<br>Is this the place where Emerick’s murderers lurk?<br>Say where is he that, tricked in this disguise,<br>First lured me on, then scared my dastard followers?<br>Thou must have seen him. Say where is th’ assassin? |
| OLD BATHORY | There lies the assassin! slain by that same [[Sword|sword]]<br>That was descending on his curst employer,<br>When entering thou beheld’st Sarolta rescued! |
| CASIMIR | Strange [[Providence|providence]]! what then was he who fled me?<br>Thy looks speak fearful things! Whither, old man!<br>Would thy hand point me? |
| OLD BATHORY | Casimir, to thy father. |
| CASIMIR | The curse! the curse! Open and [[Swallow|swallow]] me,<br>Unsteady earth! Fall, dizzy rocks! and hide me! |
| OLD BATHORY | Speak, speak, my lord! |
| KIUPRILI | Bid him fulfil his work! |
| CASIMIR | Thou art Heaven’s immediate minister, dread spirit!<br>O for sweet [[Mercy|mercy]], take some other form,<br>And save me from perdition and [[Despair|despair]]! |
| OLD BATHORY | He lives! |
| CASIMIR | Lives! A father’s curse can never die! |
| KIUPRILI | O Casimir! Casimir! |
| OLD BATHORY | Look! he doth forgive you!<br>Hark! ‘tis the [[Tyrant|tyrant]]’s voice.      [EMERICK’S voice without. |
| CASIMIR | I kneel, I kneel!<br>Retract thy curse! O, by my mother’s ashes,<br>Have pity on thy [[Self|self]]-abhorring [[Child|child]]!<br>If not for me, yet for my [[Innocent|innocent]] wife,<br>Yet for my country’s sake, give my arm strength,<br>Permitting me again to call thee father! |
| KIUPRILI | Son, I forgive thee! Take thy father’s [[Sword|sword]];<br>When thou shalt lift it in thy country’s cause,<br>In that same instant doth thy father bless thee!<br>[Enter EMERICK. |
| EMERICK | Fools! Cowards! follow — or by Hell I’ll make you<br>Find reason to [[Fear|fear]] Emerick, more than all<br>The mummer-fiends that ever masqueraded<br>As gods or wood-nymphs! —<br>Ha! ‘tis done then!<br>Our necessary [[Villain|villain]] hath proved faithful,<br>And there lies Casimir, and our last fears!<br>[[Well]]! — Aye, [[Well|well]]! ——<br>And is it not [[Well|well]]? For though grafted on us,<br>And filled too with our sap, the deadly power<br>Of the parent [[Poison|poison]]-tree lurked in its fibres:<br>There was too much of Raab Kiuprili in him:<br>The old enemy looked at me in his face,<br>E’en when his words did flatter me with duty. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter CASIMIR and BATHORY. |
| OLD BATHORY | (aside). This way they come! |
| CASIMIR | (aside).       Hold them in check awhile,<br>The path is narrow! Rudolph will assist thee. |
| EMERICK | (aside). And ere I ring the alarum of my sorrow,<br>I’ll scan that face once more, and murmur — Here<br>Lies Casimir, the last of the Kiuprilis!<br>Hell! ‘tis Pestalutz! |
| CASIMIR | (coming forward). Yes, thou ingrate Emerick!<br>‘Tis Pestalutz! ‘tis thy trusty murderer!<br>To quell thee more, see Raab Kiuprili’s [[Sword|sword]]! |
| EMERICK | Curses on it and thee! Think’st thou that petty omen<br>Dare whisper [[Fear|fear]] to Emerick’s destiny?<br>Ho! Treason! Treason! |
| CASIMIR | Then have at thee, [[Tyrant|tyrant]]!<br>[They fight. EMERICK falls. |
| EMERICK | Betrayed and baffled<br>By mine own tool! —— Oh!        [Dies. |
| CASIMIR | Hear, hear, my Father!<br>Thou should’st have witnessed thine own deed. O Father,<br>Wake from that envious swoon! The [[Tyrant|tyrant]]’s fallen!<br>Thy [[Sword|sword]] hath conquered! As I lifted it<br>Thy blessing did indeed descend upon me;<br>Dislodging the dread curse. It flew forth from me<br>And lighted on the [[Tyrant|tyrant]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter RUDOLPH, BATHORY, and Attendants. |
| RUDOLPH and BATHORY | Friends! friends to Casimir! |
| CASIMIR | Rejoice, Illyrians! the [[Usurper|usurper]]’s fallen. |
| RUDOLPH | So perish tyrants! so end usurpation! |
| CASIMIR | Bear hence the [[Body|body]], and move slowly on!<br>One moment ——<br>Devoted to a joy, that bears no witness,<br>I follow you, and we will greet our countrymen<br>With the two best and fullest gifts of heaven —<br>A [[Tyrant|tyrant]] fallen, a patriot chief restored!<br>[CASIMIR enters the Cavern. |
