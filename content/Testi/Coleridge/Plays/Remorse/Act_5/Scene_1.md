| *(didascalia)* | A Dungeon. |
| *(didascalia)* | ALVAR ([[Alone\|alone]]) rises slowly from a bed of reeds. |
| ALVAR | And this place my forefathers made for man!<br>This is the process of our [[Love\|love]] and wisdom<br>To each poor brother who offends against us —<br>Most [[Innocent\|innocent]], perhaps — and what if guilty?<br>Is this the only cure? Merciful God!<br>Each pore and natural outlet shrivelled up<br>By ignorance and parching [[Poverty\|poverty]],<br>His energies roll back upon his heart,<br>And stagnate and corrupt, till, chang'd to [[Poison\|poison]],<br>They break out on him, like a loathsome plague-spot!<br>Then we call in our pampered mountebanks:<br>And this is their best cure! uncomforted<br>And friendless [[Solitude\|solitude]], groaning and [[Tears\|tears]],<br>And savage faces, at the clanking hour,<br>Seen through the steam and vapours of his dungeon<br>By the lamp's dismal twilight! So he lies<br>Circled with evil, till his very [[Soul\|soul]]<br>Unmoulds its essence, hopelessly deformed<br>By sights of evermore deformity!<br>With other ministrations thou, O [[Nature]]!<br>Healest thy wandering and distempered [[Child\|child]]:<br>Thou pourest on him thy soft influences,<br>Thy sunny hues, fair forms, and breathing sweets;<br>Thy melodies of woods, and winds, and waters!<br>Till he relent, and can no more endure<br>To be a jarring and a dissonant thing<br>Amid this general [[Dance\|dance]] and minstrelsy;<br>But, bursting into [[Tears\|tears]], wins back his way,<br>His angry spirit healed and harmonized<br>By the benignant touch of [[Love\|love]] and [[Beauty\|beauty]].<br>I am chill and weary! Yon rude bench of stone,<br>In that dark angle, the sole resting-place!<br>But the [[Self\|self]]-approving mind is its own light<br>And life's best warmth still radiates from the heart<br>Where [[Love\|love]] sits brooding, and an honest purpose. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Retires out of sight. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter TERESA with a taper. |
| TERESA | It has chilled my very life —— my own voice scares me;<br>Yet when I hear it not I seem to lose<br>The substance of my being — my strongest grasp<br>Sends inwards but weak witness that I am.<br>I seek to cheat the echo. — How the half sounds<br>Blend with this strangled light! Is he not here — |
| *(didascalia)* | [Looking round. |
| TERESA | O for one human face here — but to see<br>One human face here to sustain me. — [[Courage]]!<br>It is but my own [[Fear\|fear]]! The life within me,<br>It sinks and wavers like this cone of flame,<br>Beyond which I scarce dare look onward! Oh!<br>If I faint? If this inhuman den should be<br>At once my [[Death\|death]]-bed and my burial vault? |
| *(didascalia)* | [Faintly screams as ALVAR emerges from the recess. |
| *(didascalia)* | Alvar (rushes towards her, and catches her as she is falling). |
| ALVAR | O gracious heaven! it is, it is Teresa!<br>Shall I reveal myself? The sudden shock<br>Of rapture will blow out this spark of life,<br>And joy complete what terror has begun.<br>O ye impetuous beatings here, be still!<br>Teresa, best beloved! pale, pale, and cold!<br>Her pulse doth flutter! Teresa! my Teresa! |
| TERESA | *(recovering)* I heard a voice; but often in my [[Dreams\|dreams]]<br>I hear that voice! and wake and try — and try —<br>To hear it waking! but I never could —<br>And 'tis so now — even so! [[Well]]! he is dead —<br>Murdered perhaps! and I am faint, and feel<br>As if it were no painful thing to die! |
| ALVAR | Believe it not, sweet maid! Believe it not,<br>Belovéd woman! 'Twas a low imposture<br>Framed by a guilty wretch. |
| TERESA | Ha! Who art thou? |
| ALVAR | Suborned by his brother — |
| TERESA | Didst thou murder him?<br>And dost thou now repent? Poor troubled man,<br>I do forgive thee, and may Heaven forgive thee! |
| ALVAR | Ordonio — he — |
| TERESA | If thou didst murder him —<br>His spirit ever at the throne of God<br>Asks [[Mercy\|mercy]] for thee: prays for [[Mercy\|mercy]] for thee,<br>With [[Tears\|tears]] in Heaven! |
| ALVAR | Alvar was not murdered.<br>Be calm! Be calm, sweet maid! |
| TERESA | Nay, nay, but tell me!      [A pause.<br>O 'tis lost again!<br>This dull confuséd [[Pain\|pain]] —  [A pause.<br>Mysterious man!<br>Methinks I can not [[Fear\|fear]] thee: for thine eye<br>Doth swim with [[Love\|love]] and pity — [[Well]]! Ordonio —<br>Oh my foreboding heart! And he suborned thee,<br>And thou didst spare his life? Blessings shower on thee,<br>As many as the drops twice counted o'er<br>In the fond faithful heart of his Teresa! |
| ALVAR | I can endure no more. The Moorish sorcerer<br>Exists but in the stain upon his face.<br>That picture — |
| TERESA | Ha! speak on! |
| ALVAR | Beloved Teresa!<br>It told but half the [[Truth\|truth]]. O let this portrait<br>Tell all — that Alvar lives — that he is here!<br>Thy much deceived but ever faithful Alvar. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Takes her portrait from his neck, and gives it her. |
| TERESA | *(receiving the portrait)* The same — it is the same! Ah!<br>Who art thou?<br>Nay, I will call thee, Alvar!    [She falls on his neck. |
| ALVAR | O joy unutterable!<br>But hark! a sound as of removing bars<br>At the dungeon's outer door. A brief, brief while<br>Conceal thyself, my [[Love\|love]]! It is Ordonio.<br>For the [[Honour\|honour]] of our race, for our dear father;<br>O for himself too (he is still my brother)<br>Let me recall him to his nobler [[Nature\|nature]],<br>That he may wake as from [[A Dream\|a dream]] of murder!<br>O let me reconcile him to himself,<br>Open the sacred source of penitent [[Tears\|tears]],<br>And be once more his own beloved Alvar. |
| TERESA | O my all virtuous [[Love\|love]]! I [[Fear\|fear]] to leave thee<br>With that obdurate man. |
| ALVAR | Thou dost not leave me!<br>But a brief while retire into the darkness:<br>O that my joy could spread its sunshine round thee! |
| TERESA | The sound of thy voice shall be my music!<br>Alvar! my Alvar! am I sure I hold thee?<br>Is it no dream? thee in my arms, my Alvar!    [Exit. |
| *(didascalia)* | [A noise at the Dungeon door. It opens, and ORDONIO enters, with a goblet in his hand. |
| ORDONIO | Hail, potent wizard! in my gayer mood<br>I poured forth a libation to old Pluto,<br>And as I brimmed the bowl, I thought on thee.<br>Thou hast conspired against my life and [[Honour\|honour]],<br>Hast tricked me foully; yet I hate thee not.<br>Why should I hate thee? this same world of ours,<br>'Tis but a pool amid a [[Storm\|storm]] of rain,<br>And we the air-bladders that course up and down,<br>And joust and tilt in merry tournament;<br>And when one bubble runs foul of another,<br>The weaker needs must break. |
| ALVAR | I see thy heart!<br>There is a frightful glitter in thine eye<br>Which doth betray thee. Inly-tortured man,<br>This is the revelry of a drunken anguish,<br>Which fain would scoff away the pang of [[Guilt\|guilt]],<br>And quell each human feeling. |
| ORDONIO | Feeling! feeling!<br>The [[Death\|death]] of a man — the breaking of a bubble —<br>'Tis true I cannot sob for such misfortunes;<br>But faintness, cold and hunger — curses on me<br>If willingly I e'er inflicted them!<br>Come, take the beverage; this chill place demands it. |
| *(didascalia)* | [ORDONIO proffers the goblet. |
| ALVAR | Yon insect on the wall,<br>Which moves this way and that its hundred limbs,<br>Were it a toy of mere mechanic craft,<br>It were an infinitely curious thing!<br>But it has life, Ordonio! life, enjoyment!<br>And by the power of its miraculous will<br>Wields all the complex movements of its frame<br>Unerringly to pleasurable ends!<br>Saw I that insect on this goblet's brim<br>I would remove it with an anxious pity! |
| ORDONIO | What meanest thou? |
| ALVAR | There's [[Poison\|poison]] in the wine. |
| ORDONIO | Thou hast guessed right; there's [[Poison\|poison]] in the wine.<br>There's [[Poison\|poison]] in't — which of us two shall drink it?<br>For one of us must die! |
| ALVAR | Whom dost thou think me? |
| ORDONIO | The accomplice and sworn friend of Isidore. |
| ALVAR | I know him not.<br>And yet methinks, I have heard the name but lately.<br>Means he the husband of the Moorish woman?<br>Isidore? Isidore? |
| ORDONIO | Good! good! that lie! by heaven it has restored me.<br>Now I am thy master! — [[Villain]]! thou shalt drink it,<br>Or die a bitterer [[Death\|death]]. |
| ALVAR | What strange solution<br>Hast thou found out to satisfy thy fears,<br>And drug them to unnatural [[Sleep\|sleep]]? |
| *(didascalia)* | [ALVAR takes the goblet, and throws it to the ground. |
| ALVAR | My master! |
| ORDONIO | Thou mountebank! |
| ALVAR | Mountebank and [[Villain\|villain]]!<br>What then art thou? For shame, put up thy [[Sword\|sword]]!<br>What boots a weapon in a withered arm?<br>I fix mine eye upon thee, and thou tremblest!<br>I speak, and [[Fear\|fear]] and [[Wonder\|wonder]] crush thy rage,<br>And turn it to a motionless distraction!<br>Thou blind [[Self\|self]]-worshipper! thy [[Pride\|pride]], thy cunning,<br>Thy [[Faith\|faith]] in universal villainy,<br>Thy shallow sophisms, thy pretended scorn<br>For all thy human brethren — out upon them!<br>What have they done for thee? have they given thee peace?<br>Cured thee of starting in thy [[Sleep\|sleep]]? or made<br>The darkness pleasant when thou wak'st at midnight?<br>Art happy when [[Alone\|alone]]? Can'st walk by thyself<br>With even step and quiet cheerfulness?<br>Yet, yet thou may'st be saved —— |
| ORDONIO | Saved? saved? |
| ALVAR | One pang!<br>Could I call up one pang of true remorse! |
| ORDONIO | He told me of the babes that prattled to him.<br>His fatherless little ones! remorse! remorse!<br>Where got'st thou that [[Fool\|fool]]'s word? Curse on remorse!<br>Can it give up the dead, or recompact<br>A mangled [[Body\|body]]? mangled — dashed to atoms!<br>Not all the blessings of a host of angels<br>Can blow away a desolate widow's curse!<br>And though thou spill thy heart's [[Blood\|blood]] for atonement,<br>It will not weigh against an [[Orphan\|orphan]]'s tear! |
| ALVAR | But Alvar —— |
| ORDONIO | Ha! it chokes thee in the throat,<br>Even thee; and yet I pray thee speak it out.<br>Still Alvar! — Alvar! — howl it in mine ear!<br>Heap it like coals of [[Fire\|fire]] upon my heart,<br>And shoot it hissing through my brain! |
| ALVAR | Alas!<br>That day when thou didst leap from off the rock<br>Into the waves, and grasped thy sinking brother,<br>And bore him to the strand; then, son of Valdez,<br>How sweet and musical the name of Alvar!<br>Then, then, Ordonio, he was dear to thee,<br>And thou wert dear to him: heaven only knows<br>How very dear thou wert! Why did'st thou hate him!<br>O heaven! how he would fall upon thy neck,<br>And weep [[Forgiveness\|forgiveness]]! |
| ORDONIO | Spirit of the dead!<br>Methinks I know thee! ha! my brain turns wild<br>At its own [[Dreams\|dreams]]! — off — off, fantastic [[Shadow\|shadow]]! |
| ALVAR | I fain would tell thee what I am, but dare not! |
| ORDONIO | Cheat! [[Villain\|villain]]! traitor! whatsoever thou be —<br>I [[Fear\|fear]] thee, man! |
| TERESA | *(rushing out and falling on Alvar's neck)* Ordonio! 'tis<br>thy brother! |
| *(didascalia)* | [ORDONIO runs upon ALVAR with his [[Sword\|sword]]. TERESA flings herself on ORDONIO and arrests his arm. |
| TERESA | Stop, madman, stop! |
| ALVAR | Does then this thin disguise impenetrably<br>Hide Alvar from thee? Toil and painful wounds<br>And long [[Imprisonment\|imprisonment]] in unwholesome dungeons,<br>Have marred perhaps all trait and lineament<br>Of what I was! But chiefly, chiefly, brother,<br>My anguish for thy [[Guilt\|guilt]]!<br>Ordonio — Brother!<br>Nay, nay, thou shalt embrace me. |
| ORDONIO | *(drawing back, and gazing at Alvar)* Touch me not!<br>Touch not pollution, Alvar! I will die. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He attempts to fall on his [[Sword\|sword]], ALVAR and TERESA prevent him. |
| ALVAR | We will find means to save your [[Honour\|honour]]. Live,<br>Oh live, Ordonio! for our father's sake!<br>Spare his grey hairs! |
| TERESA | And you may yet be happy. |
| ORDONIO | O horror! not a thousand years in heaven<br>Could recompose this miserable heart,<br>Or make it capable of one brief joy!<br>Live! live! Why yes! 'Twere [[Well\|well]] to live with you:<br>For is it fit a [[Villain\|villain]] should be proud?<br>My brother! I will kneel to you, my brother!       [Kneeling.<br>Forgive me, Alvar! —— Curse me with [[Forgiveness\|forgiveness]]! |
| ALVAR | Call back thy [[Soul\|soul]], Ordonio, and look round thee!<br>Now is the [[Time\|time]] for [[Greatness\|greatness]]! Think that heaven — |
| TERESA | O mark his eye! he hears not what you say. |
| ORDONIO | Yes, mark his eye! there's fascination in it!<br>Thou said'st thou did'st not know him — That is he!<br>He comes upon me! |
| ALVAR | Heal, O heal him, heaven! |
| ORDONIO | Nearer and nearer! and I can not stir!<br>Will no one hear these stifled groans, and wake me?<br>He would have died to save me, and I killed him —<br>A husband and a father! — |
| TERESA | Some secret [[Poison\|poison]]<br>Drinks up his spirits! |
| ORDONIO | Let the eternal justice<br>Prepare my punishment in the obscure world —<br>I will not bear to live — to live — O agony!<br>And be myself [[Alone\|alone]] my own sore torment! |
| *(didascalia)* | [The doors of the dungeon are broken open, and in rush ALHADRA, and the band of Morescoes. |
| ALHADRA | Seize first that man! |
| *(didascalia)* | [ALVAR presses onward to defend ORDONIO. |
| ORDONIO | Off, ruffians! I have flung away my [[Sword\|sword]].<br>Woman, my life is thine! to thee I give it!<br>Off! he that touches me with his hand of flesh,<br>I'll rend his limbs asunder! I have strength<br>With this bare arm to scatter you like ashes. |
| ALHADRA | My husband — |
| ORDONIO | Yes, I murdered him most foully. |
| ALVAR AND TERESA | O horrible! |
| ALHADRA | Why did'st thou leave his children?<br>Demon, thou should'st have sent thy [[dogs]] of hell<br>To lap their [[Blood\|blood]]. Then, then I might have hardened<br>My [[Soul\|soul]] in misery, and have had comfort.<br>I would have stood far off, quiet though dark,<br>And bade the race of men raise up a mourning<br>For a deep horror of desolation,<br>Too great to be one [[Soul\|soul]]'s particular lot!<br>Brother of Zagri! let me lean upon thee.<br>The [[Time\|time]] is not yet come for woman's anguish,<br>I have not seen his [[Blood\|blood]] — Within an hour<br>Those little ones will crowd around and ask me,<br>Where is our father? I shall curse thee then!<br>Wert thou in heaven, my curse would pluck thee thence! |
| TERESA | He doth repent! See, see, I kneel to thee!<br>O let him live! That agéd man, his father —— |
| ALHADRA | Why had he such a son? |
| *(didascalia)* | [Shouts from the distance of Rescue! Rescue! Alvar! Alvar! and the voice of VALDEZ heard. |
| ALHADRA | Rescue? — and Isidore's spirit unavenged? —<br>The deed be mine!      [Suddenly stabs ORDONIO.<br>Now take my life! |
| ORDONIO | *(staggering from the wound)* Atonement! |
| ALVAR | *(while with Teresa supporting Ordonio)* Arm of avenging<br>Heaven<br>Thou hast snatched from me my most cherished [[Hope\|hope]] —<br>But go! my word was pledged to thee. |
| ORDONIO | Away!<br>Brave not my Father's rage! I thank thee! Thou — |
| *(didascalia)* | [Then turning his eyes languidly to ALVAR. |
| ORDONIO | She hath avenged the [[Blood\|blood]] of Isidore!<br>I stood in [[Silence\|silence]] like a slave before her<br>That I might taste the wormwood and the gall,<br>And satiate this [[Self\|self]]-accusing heart<br>With bitterer agonies than [[Death\|death]] can give.<br>Forgive me, Alvar!<br>Oh! — could'st thou forget me!       [Dies. |
| *(didascalia)* | [ALVAR and TERESA bend over the [[Body\|body]] of ORDONIO. |
| ALHADRA | *(to the Moors)* I thank thee, Heaven! thou hast ordained it wisely,<br>That still extremes bring their own cure. That point<br>In misery, which makes the oppressed Man<br>Regardless of his own life, makes him too<br>Lord of the Oppressor's — Knew I a hundred men<br>Despairing, but not palsied by [[Despair\|despair]],<br>This arm should shake the kingdoms of the world;<br>The deep foundations of iniquity<br>Should sink away, earth groaning from beneath them;<br>The strongholds of the cruel men should fall,<br>Their temples and their mountainous towers should fall;<br>Till desolation seemed a beautiful thing,<br>And all that were and had the spirit of life,<br>Sang a new [[Song\|song]] to her who had gone forth,<br>Conquering and still to conquer! |
| *(didascalia)* | [ALHADRA hurries off with the Moors; the stage fills with armed Peasants, and Servants, ZULIMEZ and VALDEZ at their head. VALDEZ rushes into ALVAR'S arms. |
| ALVAR | Turn not thy face that way, my father! hide,<br>Oh hide it from his eye! Oh let thy joy<br>Flow in unmingled stream through thy first blessing. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Both kneel to VALDEZ. |
| VALDEZ | My Son! My Alvar! bless, Oh bless him, heaven! |
| TERESA | Me too, my Father? |
| VALDEZ | Bless, Oh bless my children! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Both rise. |
| ALVAR | Delights so full, if unalloyed with grief,<br>Were ominous. In these strange dread events<br>Just Heaven instructs us with an awful voice,<br>That [[Conscience]] rules us e'en against our choice.<br>Our inward Monitress to guide or warn,<br>If listened to; but if repelled with scorn,<br>At length as dire Remorse, she reappears,<br>Works in our guilty hopes, and selfish fears!<br>Still bids, Remember! and still cries, Too late!<br>And while she scares us, goads us to our fate. |

| *(didascalia)* | APPENDIX<br>The following Scene, as unfit for the stage, was taken from the [[Tragedy\|tragedy]], in the year 1797, and published in the Lyrical Ballads. [1798, pp. 28-31: vide ante, pp. 182-4.] |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter Teresa and Selma. |
| TERESA | 'Tis said, he spake of you familiarly,<br>As mine and Alvar's common foster-mother. |
| SELMA | Now blessings on the man, whoe'er he be<br>That joined your names with mine! O my sweet Lady,<br>As often as I think of those dear times,<br>When you two little ones would stand, at eve,<br>On each side of my chair, and make me learn<br>All you had learnt in the day; and how to talk<br>In gentle phrase; then bid me sing to you ——<br>'Tis more like heaven to come, than what has been! |
| TERESA | But that entrance, Selma? |
| SELMA | Can no one hear? It is a perilous tale! |
| TERESA | No one. |
| SELMA | My husband's father told it me,<br>Poor old Sesina — angels rest his [[Soul\|soul]];<br>He was a woodman, and could fell and saw<br>With lusty arm. You know that huge round beam<br>Which props the hanging wall of the old chapel?<br>Beneath that tree, while yet it was a tree,<br>He found a baby wrapt in mosses, lined<br>With thistle-beards, and such small locks of wool<br>As hang on brambles. [[Well]], he brought him [[Home\|home]],<br>And reared him at the then Lord Valdez' cost.<br>And so the babe grew up a pretty boy,<br>A pretty boy, but most unteachable ——<br>And never learn'd a [[prayer]], nor told a bead,<br>But knew the names of [[Birds\|birds]], and mocked their notes,<br>And whistled, as he were a bird himself.<br>And all the [[Autumn\|autumn]] 'twas his only play<br>To gather seeds of wild [[Flowers\|flowers]], and to plant them<br>With earth and [[Water\|water]] on the stumps of trees.<br>A Friar, who gathered simples in the wood,<br>A grey-haired man, he loved this little boy:<br>The boy loved him, and, when the friar taught him,<br>He soon could write with the pen; and from that [[Time\|time]]<br>Lived chiefly at the convent or the [[Castle\|castle]].<br>So he became a rare and learned youth:<br>But O! poor wretch! he read, and read, and read,<br>Till his brain turned; and ere his twentieth year<br>He had unlawful thoughts of many things:<br>And though he prayed, he never loved to pray<br>With holy men, nor in a holy place.<br>But yet his speech, it was so soft and sweet,<br>The late Lord Valdez ne'er was wearied with him.<br>And once, as by the north side of the chapel<br>They stood together chained in deep discourse,<br>The earth heaved under them with such a groan,<br>That the wall tottered, and had [[Well\|well]] nigh fallen<br>Right on their heads. My Lord was sorely frightened;<br>A fever seized him, and he made confession<br>Of all the heretical and lawless talk<br>Which brought this judgment: so the youth was seized,<br>And cast into that hole. My husband's father<br>Sobbed like a [[Child\|child]] — it almost broke his heart:<br>And once he was working near this dungeon,<br>He heard a voice distinctly; 'twas the youth's,<br>Who sung a doleful [[Song\|song]] about green fields,<br>How sweet it were on lake or wide savanna<br>To hunt for food, and be a naked man,<br>And wander up and down at liberty.<br>He always doted on the youth, and now<br>His [[Love\|love]] grew desperate; and defying [[Death\|death]],<br>He made that cunning entrance I described,<br>And the young man escaped. |
| TERESA | 'Tis a sweet tale:<br>Such as would lull a listening [[Child\|child]] to [[Sleep\|sleep]],<br>His rosy face besoiled with unwiped [[Tears\|tears]].<br>And what became of him? |
| SELMA | He went on shipboard<br>With those bold voyagers who made discovery<br>Of golden lands. Sesina's younger brother<br>Went likewise, and when he returned to Spain,<br>He told Sesina, that the poor mad youth,<br>Soon after they arrived in that new world,<br>In spite of his dissuasion, seized a boat,<br>And all [[Alone\|alone]] set sail by silent moonlight<br>Up a great [[River\|river]], great as any sea,<br>And ne'er was heard of more: but 'tis supposed,<br>He lived and died among the savage men. |
