|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | The inside of a Cottage, around which [[Flowers\|flowers]] and plants of various kinds are seen. Discovers ALVAR, ZULIMEZ and ALHADRA, as on the point of leaving. |
| ALHADRA | Farewell then! and though many thoughts perplex me,<br>Aught evil or ignoble never can I<br>Suspect of thee! If what thou seem'st thou art,<br>The oppressed brethren of thy [[Blood\|blood]] have need<br>Of such a leader. |
| ALVAR | Nobly-minded woman!<br>Long [[Time\|time]] against oppression have I fought,<br>And for the native liberty of [[Faith\|faith]]<br>Have bled and suffered bonds. Of this be certain:<br>[[Time]], as he courses onward, still unrolls<br>The volume of concealment. In the future,<br>As in the optician's glassy cylinder,<br>The indistinguishable blots and colours<br>Of the dim past collect and shape themselves,<br>Upstarting in their own completed image<br>To scare or to reward.<br>I sought the guilty,<br>And what I sought I found: but ere the spear<br>Flew from my hand, there [[Rose\|rose]] an angel form<br>Betwixt me and my aim. With baffled purpose<br>To the [[Avenger]] I leave vengeance, and depart!<br>Whate'er betide, if aught my arm may aid,<br>Or power protect, my word is pledged to thee:<br>For many are thy wrongs, and thy [[Soul\|soul]] noble.<br>Once more, farewell. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit ALHADRA. |
| ALVAR | Yes, to the Belgic states<br>We will return. These robes, this stained complexion,<br>Akin to falsehood, weigh upon my spirit.<br>Whate'er befall us, the heroic Maurice<br>Will grant us an asylum, in remembrance<br>Of our past services. |
| ZULIMEZ | And all the wealth, power, influence which is yours,<br>You let a murderer hold? |
| ALVAR | O faithful Zulimez!<br>That my return involved Ordonio's [[Death\|death]],<br>I trust, would give me an unmingled pang,<br>Yet bearable: but when I see my father<br>Strewing his scant grey hairs, e'en on the ground,<br>Which soon must be his [[Grave\|grave]], and my Teresa —<br>Her husband proved a murderer, and her infants<br>His infants — poor Teresa! — all would perish,<br>All perish — all! and I (nay bear with me)<br>Could not survive the complicated ruin! |
| ZULIMEZ | Nay now! I have distress'd you — you [[Well\|well]] know,<br>I ne'er will quit your fortunes. True,'tis tiresome!<br>You are a painter, one of many fancies!<br>You can call up past deeds, and make them live<br>On the blank canvas! and each little herb,<br>That grows on mountain bleak, or tangled forest,<br>You have learnt to name ——<br>Hark! heard you not some footsteps? |
| ALVAR | What if it were my brother coming onwards?<br>I sent a most mysterious message to him. |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter ORDONIO |
| ALVAR | It is he! |
| ORDONIO | (to himself as he enters) If I distinguish'd right her gait and stature,<br>It was the Moorish woman, Isidore's wife,<br>That passed me as I entered. A lit taper,<br>In the [[Night\|night]] air, doth not more naturally<br>Attract the [[Night\|night]]-flies round it, than a conjuror<br>Draws round him the whole female neighbourhood. |
| *(didascalia)* | Addressing ALVAR. |
| ORDONIO | You know my name, I guess, if not my person.<br>I am Ordonio, son of the Lord Valdez. |
| ALVAR | The Son of Valdez! |
| *(didascalia)* | ORDONIO walks leisurely round the room, and looks attentively at the plants. |
| ZULIMEZ | Why, what ails you now?<br>How your hand trembles! Alvar, speak! what wish you? |
| ALVAR | To fall upon his neck and weep [[Forgiveness\|forgiveness]]! |
| ORDONIO | (returning, and aloud) Plucked in the moonlight from a ruined abbey —<br>Those only, which the pale rays visited!<br>O the unintelligible power of weeds,<br>When a few odd prayers have been muttered o'er them:<br>Then they work miracles! I warrant you,<br>There's not a leaf, but underneath it lurks<br>Some serviceable imp.<br>There's one of you<br>Hath sent me a strange message. |
| ALVAR | I am he. |
| ORDONIO | With you, then, I am to speak: |
| *(didascalia)* | Haughtily waving his hand to ZULIMEZ. |
| ORDONIO | And mark you, [[Alone\|alone]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit ZULIMEZ. |
| ORDONIO | 'He that can bring the dead to life again!' —<br>Such was your message, Sir! You are no dullard,<br>But one that strips the outward rind of things! |
| ALVAR | 'Tis fabled there are fruits with tempting rinds,<br>That are all [[Dust\|dust]] and rottenness within.<br>Would'st thou I should strip such? |
| ORDONIO | Thou quibbling [[Fool\|fool]],<br>What dost thou mean? Think'st thou I journeyed hither<br>To sport with thee? |
| ALVAR | O no, my lord! to sport<br>Best suits the gaiety of [[innocence]]. |
| ORDONIO | (aside) O what a thing is man! the wisest heart<br>A [[Fool\|fool]]! a [[Fool\|fool]] that laughs at its own folly,<br>Yet still a [[Fool\|fool]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Looks round the cottage. |
| ORDONIO | You are poor! |
| ALVAR | What follows thence? |
| ORDONIO | That you would fain be richer.<br>The [[Inquisition\|inquisition]], too — You comprehend me?<br>You are poor, in peril. I have wealth and power,<br>Can quench the flames, and cure your [[Poverty\|poverty]]:<br>And for the boon I ask of you but this,<br>That you should serve me — once — for a few hours. |
| ALVAR | Thou art the son of Valdez! would to Heaven<br>That I could truly and for ever serve thee. |
| ORDONIO | (aside) The slave begins to soften.<br>You are my friend,<br>'He that can bring the dead to life again,'<br>Nay, no defence to me! The holy brethren<br>Believe these calumnies — I know thee better.<br>Thou art a man, and as a man I'll trust thee! |
| ALVAR | (aside) Alas! this hollow mirth — Declare your business. |
| ORDONIO | I [[Love\|love]] a lady, and she would [[Love\|love]] me<br>But for an idle and fantastic scruple.<br>Have you no servants here, no listeners? |
| *(didascalia)* | ORDONIO steps to the door. |
| ALVAR | What, faithless too? False to his angel wife?<br>To such a wife? [[Well]] might'st thou look so wan,<br>Ill-starr'd Teresa! —— Wretch! my softer [[Soul\|soul]]<br>Is pass'd away, and I will probe his [[Conscience\|conscience]]! |
| ORDONIO | In [[Truth\|truth]] this lady lov'd another man,<br>But he has perish'd. |
| ALVAR | What! you kill'd him? hey? |
| ORDONIO | I'll dash thee to the earth, if thou but think'st it!<br>Insolent slave! how dar'dst thou — |
| *(didascalia)* | Turns abruptly from ALVAR, and then to himself. |
| ORDONIO | Why! what's this?<br>'Twas idiotcy! I'll tie myself to an aspen,<br>And wear a [[Fool\|fool]]'s cap — |
| ALVAR | Fare thee [[Well\|well]] —<br>I pity thee, Ordonio, even to anguish. |
| *(didascalia)* | ALVAR is retiring. |
| ORDONIO | Ho! |
| *(didascalia)* | Calling to ALVAR. |
| ALVAR | Be brief, what wish you? |
| ORDONIO | You are deep at bartering — You charge yourself<br>At a round sum. Come, come, I spake unwisely. |
| ALVAR | I listen to you. |
| ORDONIO | In a sudden tempest<br>Did Alvar perish — he, I mean — the [[Lover\|lover]] —<br>The fellow —— |
| ALVAR | Nay, speak out! 'twill ease your heart<br>To call him [[Villain\|villain]]! — Why stand'st thou aghast?<br>Men think it natural to hate their rivals. |
| ORDONIO | Now, till she knows him dead, she will not wed me. |
| ALVAR | Are you not wedded, then? Merciful Heaven!<br>Not wedded to Teresa? |
| ORDONIO | Why, what ails thee?<br>What, art thou mad? why look'st thou upward so?<br>Dost pray to [[Lucifer]], Prince of the Air? |
| ALVAR | Proceed. I shall be silent. |
| ORDONIO | To Teresa?<br>Politic wizard! ere you sent that message,<br>You had conn'd your lesson, made yourself proficient<br>In all my fortunes. Hah! you prophesied<br>A golden crop! [[Well]], you have not mistaken —<br>Be faithful to me and I'll pay thee nobly. |
| ALVAR | [[Well]]! and this lady! |
| ORDONIO | If we could make her certain of his [[Death\|death]],<br>She needs must wed me. Ere her [[Lover\|lover]] left her,<br>She tied a little portrait round his neck,<br>Entreating him to wear it. |
| ALVAR | Yes! he did so! |
| ORDONIO | Why no: he was afraid of accidents,<br>Of robberies, and shipwrecks, and the like.<br>In secrecy he gave it me to keep,<br>Till his return. |
| ALVAR | What! he was your friend then? |
| ORDONIO | I was his friend. —<br>Now that he gave it me,<br>This lady knows not. You are a mighty wizard —<br>Can call the dead man up — he will not come. —<br>He is in heaven then — there you have no influence.<br>Still there are tokens — and your imps may bring you<br>Something he wore about him when he died.<br>And when the smoke of the incense on the altar<br>Is pass'd, your spirits will have left this picture.<br>What say you now? |
| ALVAR | Ordonio, I will do it. |
| ORDONIO | We'll hazard no delay. Be it to-[[Night\|night]],<br>In the early evening. Ask for the Lord Valdez.<br>I will prepare him. Music too, and incense,<br>(For I have arranged it — music, altar, incense)<br>All shall be ready. Here is this same picture,<br>And here, what you will value more, a purse.<br>Come early for your [[Magic\|magic]] ceremonies. |
| ALVAR | I will not fail to meet you. |
| ORDONIO | Till next we meet, farewell! |
| *(didascalia)* | Exit ORDONIO. |
| ALVAR | (alone, indignantly flings the purse away and gazes passionately at the portrait) And I did curse thee!<br>At midnight! on my knees! and I believed<br>Thee perjur'd, thee a traitress! thee dishonour'd!<br>O blind and credulous [[Fool\|fool]]! O [[Guilt\|guilt]] of folly!<br>Should not thy inarticulate fondnesses,<br>Thy infant loves — should not thy maiden vows<br>Have come upon my heart? And this sweet Image<br>Tied round my neck with many a chaste endearment,<br>And thrilling hands, that made me weep and tremble —<br>Ah, coward dupe! to yield it to the miscreant,<br>Who spake pollution of thee! barter for life<br>This farewell pledge, which with impassioned vow<br>I had sworn that I would grasp — ev'n in my [[Death]]-pang!<br>I am unworthy of thy [[Love\|love]], Teresa,<br>Of that unearthly smile upon those lips,<br>Which ever smiled on me! Yet do not scorn me —<br>I lisp'd thy name, ere I had learnt my mother's.<br>Dear portrait! rescued from a traitor's keeping,<br>I will not now profane thee, holy image,<br>To a dark trick. That worst bad man shall find<br>A picture, which will wake the hell within him,<br>And rouse a fiery whirlwind in his [[Conscience\|conscience]]. |
