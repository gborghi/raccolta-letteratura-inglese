| *(didascalia)* | A wild and mountainous country. ORDONIO and ISIDORE are discovered, supposed at a little distance from ISIDORE'S [[house]]. |
| ORDONIO | Here we may stop: your [[house]] distinct in view,<br>Yet we secured from listeners. |
| ISIDORE | Now indeed<br>My [[house]]! and it looks cheerful as the clusters<br>Basking in sunshine on yon vine-clad rock,<br>That over-brows it! Patron! Friend! Preserver!<br>Thrice have you saved my life. Once in the battle<br>You gave it me: next rescued me from [[suicide]]<br>When for my follies I was made to wander,<br>With mouths to feed, and not a morsel for them:<br>Now but for you, a dungeon's slimy stones<br>Had been my bed and pillow. |
| ORDONIO | Good Isidore!<br>Why this to me? It is enough, you know it. |
| ISIDORE | A common trick of gratitude, my lord,<br>Seeking to ease her own full heart —— |
| ORDONIO | Enough!<br>A debt repaid ceases to be a debt.<br>You have it in your power to serve me greatly. |
| ISIDORE | And how, my lord? I pray you to name the thing.<br>I would climb up an ice-glazed precipice<br>To pluck a weed you fancied! |
| ORDONIO | Why — that — Lady — |
| ISIDORE | 'Tis now three years, my lord, since last I saw you:<br>Have you a son, my lord? |
| ORDONIO | O miserable —     [Aside.<br>Isidore! you are a man, and know mankind.<br>I told you what I wished — now for the [[Truth\|truth]] —<br>She loved the man you kill'd. |
| ISIDORE | You jest, my lord? |
| ORDONIO | And till his [[Death\|death]] is proved she will not wed me. |
| ISIDORE | You sport with me, my lord? |
| ORDONIO | Come, come! this foolery<br>Lives only in thy looks, thy heart disowns it! |
| ISIDORE | I can bear this, and any thing more grievous<br>From you, my lord — but how can I serve you here? |
| ORDONIO | Why, you can utter with a solemn gesture<br>Oracular sentences of deep no-meaning,<br>Wear a quaint garment, make mysterious antics — |
| ISIDORE | I am dull, my lord! I do not comprehend you. |
| ORDONIO | In blunt terms, you can play the sorcerer.<br>She hath no [[Faith\|faith]] in Holy Church, 'tis true:<br>Her [[Lover\|lover]] schooled her in some newer nonsense!<br>Yet still a tale of spirits works upon her.<br>She is a lone enthusiast, sensitive,<br>Shivers, and can not keep the [[Tears\|tears]] in her eye:<br>And such do [[Love\|love]] the marvellous too [[Well\|well]]<br>Not to believe it. We will [[Wind\|wind]] up her fancy<br>With a strange music, that she knows not of —<br>With fumes of frankincense, and mummery,<br>Then leave, as one sure token of his [[Death\|death]],<br>That portrait, which from off the dead man's neck<br>I bade thee take, the trophy of thy conquest. |
| ISIDORE | Will that be a sure sign? |
| ORDONIO | Beyond suspicion.<br>Fondly caressing him, her favour'd [[Lover\|lover]],<br>(By some base spell he had bewitched her senses)<br>She whispered such dark fears of me forsooth,<br>As made this heart pour gall into my veins.<br>And as she coyly bound it round his neck<br>She made him promise [[Silence\|silence]]; and now holds<br>The secret of the existence of this portrait<br>Known only to her [[Lover\|lover]] and herself.<br>But I had traced her, stolen unnotic'd on them,<br>And unsuspected saw and heard the whole. |
| ISIDORE | But now I should have cursed the man who told me<br>You could ask aught, my lord, and I refuse —<br>But this I can not do. |
| ORDONIO | Where lies your scruple? |
| ISIDORE | Why — why, my lord!<br>You know you told me that the lady lov'd you,<br>Had loved you with incautious tenderness;<br>That if the young man, her betrothéd husband,<br>Returned, yourself, and she, and the [[Honour\|honour]] of both<br>Must perish. Now though with no tenderer scruples<br>Than those which being native to the heart,<br>Than those, my lord, which merely being a man — |
| ORDONIO | This fellow is a Man — he killed for hire<br>One whom he knew not, yet has tender scruples! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Then turning to ISIDORE. |
| ORDONIO | These doubts, these fears, thy whine, thy stammering —<br>Pish, [[Fool\|fool]]! thou blunder'st through the book of [[Guilt\|guilt]],<br>Spelling thy villainy. |
| ISIDORE | My lord — my lord,<br>I can bear much — yes, very much from you!<br>But there's a point where sufferance is meanness:<br>I am no [[Villain\|villain]] — never kill'd for hire —<br>My gratitude —— |
| ORDONIO | O aye — your gratitude!<br>'Twas a [[Well\|well]]-sounding word — what have you done with it? |
| ISIDORE | Who proffers his past favours for my virtue — |
| ORDONIO | Virtue —— |
| ISIDORE | Tries to o'erreach me — is a very sharper,<br>And should not speak of gratitude, my lord.<br>I knew not 'twas your brother! |
| ORDONIO | And who told you? |
| ISIDORE | He himself told me. |
| ORDONIO | Ha! you talk'd with him!<br>And those, the two Morescoes who were with you? |
| ISIDORE | Both fell in a [[Night\|night]] brawl at Malaga. |
| ORDONIO | *(in a low voice)* My brother — |
| ISIDORE | Yes, my lord, I could not tell you!<br>I thrust away the thought — it drove me wild.<br>But listen to me now — I pray you listen —— |
| ORDONIO | [[Villain]]! no more. I'll hear no more of it. |
| ISIDORE | My lord, it much imports your future safety<br>That you should hear it. |
| ORDONIO | *(turning off from Isidore)* Am not I a man!<br>'Tis as it should be! tut — the deed itself<br>Was idle, and these after-pangs still idler! |
| ISIDORE | We met him in the very place you mentioned.<br>Hard by a grove of firs — |
| ORDONIO | Enough — enough — |
| ISIDORE | He fought us valiantly, and wounded all;<br>In fine, compelled a parley. |
| ORDONIO | Alvar! brother! |
| ISIDORE | He offered me his purse — |
| ORDONIO | Yes? |
| ISIDORE | Yes — I spurned it. —<br>He promised us I know not what — in vain!<br>Then with a look and voice that overawed me,<br>He said, What mean you, friends? My life is dear:<br>I have a brother and a promised wife,<br>Who make life dear to me — and if I fall,<br>That brother will roam earth and hell for vengeance.<br>There was a likeness in his face to yours;<br>I asked his brother's name: he said — Ordonio,<br>Son of Lord Valdez! I had [[Well\|well]] nigh fainted.<br>At length I said (if that indeed I said it,<br>And that no Spirit made my tongue its organ,)<br>That woman is dishonoured by that brother,<br>And he the man who sent us to destroy you.<br>He drove a thrust at me in rage. I told him<br>He wore her portrait round his neck. He look'd<br>As he had been made of the rock that propt his back —<br>Aye, just as you look now — only less ghastly!<br>At length recovering from his trance, he threw<br>His [[Sword\|sword]] away, and bade us take his life,<br>It was not worth his keeping. |
| ORDONIO | And you kill'd him?<br>Oh [[Blood\|blood]] hounds! may eternal wrath flame round you!<br>He was his Maker's Image undefac'd!<br>It seizes me — by Hell I will go on!<br>What — would'st thou stop, man? thy pale looks won't save thee!<br>Oh cold — cold — cold! shot through with icy cold! |
| ISIDORE | *(aside)* Were he alive he had returned ere now.<br>The consequence the same — dead through his plotting! |
| ORDONIO | O this unutterable dying away — here —<br>This sickness of the heart!<br>What if I went<br>And liv'd in a hollow tomb, and fed on weeds?<br>Aye! that's the [[Road\|road]] to heaven! O [[Fool\|fool]]! [[Fool\|fool]]! [[Fool\|fool]]!<br>What have I done but that which [[Nature\|nature]] destined,<br>Or the blind elements stirred up within me?<br>If good were meant, why were we made these beings?<br>And if not meant — |
| ISIDORE | You are disturbed, my lord! |
| ORDONIO | *(starts)* A gust of the [[Soul\|soul]]! i'[[Faith\|faith]] it overset me.<br>O 'twas all folly — all! idle as laughter!<br>Now, Isidore! I swear that thou shalt aid me. |
| ISIDORE | *(in a low voice)* I'll perish first! |
| ORDONIO | What dost thou mutter of? |
| ISIDORE | Some of your servants know me, I am certain. |
| ORDONIO | There's some sense in that scruple; but we'll mask you. |
| ISIDORE | They'll know my gait: but stay! last [[Night\|night]] I watched<br>A stranger near the ruin in the wood,<br>Who as it seemed was gathering herbs and wild [[Flowers\|flowers]].<br>I had followed him at distance, seen him scale<br>Its western wall, and by an easier entrance<br>Stole after him unnoticed. There I marked,<br>That mid the chequer work of light and shade<br>With curious choice he plucked no other [[Flowers\|flowers]],<br>But those on which the moonlight fell: and once<br>I heard him muttering o'er the plant. A wizard —<br>Some gaunt slave prowling here for dark employment. |
| ORDONIO | Doubtless you question'd him? |
| ISIDORE | 'Twas my intention,<br>Having first traced him homeward to his haunt.<br>But lo! the stern Dominican, whose spies<br>Lurk every where, already (as it seemed)<br>Had given commission to his apt familiar<br>To seek and sound the Moor; who now returning,<br>Was by this trusty agent stopped midway.<br>I, dreading fresh suspicion if found near him<br>In that lone place, again concealed myself:<br>Yet within hearing. So the Moor was question'd,<br>And in your name, as lord of this domain,<br>Proudly he answered, 'Say to the Lord Ordonio,<br>He that can bring the dead to life again!' |
| ORDONIO | A strange reply! |
| ISIDORE | Aye, all of him is strange.<br>He called himself a Christian, yet he wears<br>The Moorish robes, as if he courted [[Death\|death]]. |
| ORDONIO | Where does this wizard live? |
| ISIDORE | *(pointing to the distance)* You see that brooklet?<br>Trace its course backward: through a narrow opening<br>It leads you to the place. |
| ORDONIO | How shall I know it? |
| ISIDORE | You cannot err. It is a small green dell<br>Built all around with high off-sloping hills,<br>And from its shape our peasants aptly call it<br>The Giant's Cradle. There's a lake in the midst,<br>And round its banks tall wood that branches over,<br>And makes a kind of faery forest grow<br>Down in the [[Water\|water]]. At the further end<br>A puny cataract falls on the lake;<br>And there, a curious sight! you see its [[Shadow\|shadow]]<br>For ever curling, like a wreath of smoke,<br>Up through the foliage of those faery trees.<br>His cot stands opposite. You cannot miss it. |
| ORDONIO | *(in retiring stops suddenly at the edge of the scene, then turning round to Isidore)* Ha! — Who lurks there! Have we been overheard?<br>There where the smooth high wall of slate-rock glitters —— |
| ISIDORE | 'Neath those tall stones, which propping each the other,<br>Form a mock portal with their pointed arch?<br>Pardon my smiles! 'Tis a poor idiot boy,<br>Who sits in the sun, and twirls a bough about,<br>His weak eyes seeth'd in most unmeaning [[Tears\|tears]].<br>And so he sits, swaying his cone-like head,<br>And staring at his bough from morn to sun-set,<br>See-saws his voice in inarticulate noises. |
| ORDONIO | 'Tis [[Well\|well]], and now for this same wizard's lair. |
| ISIDORE | Some three strides up the [[Hill\|hill]], a mountain ash<br>Stretches its lower boughs and scarlet clusters<br>O'er the old thatch. |
| ORDONIO | I shall not fail to find it. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exeunt ORDONIO and ISIDORE. |
