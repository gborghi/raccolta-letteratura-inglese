| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN and WRANGEL. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Your name is Wrangel? |
| WRANGEL | Gustave Wrangel, General<br>Of the Sudermanian Blues. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It was a Wrangel<br>Who injured me materially at Stralsund,<br>And by his brave resistance was the cause<br>Of the opposition which that sea-port made. |
| WRANGEL | It was the doing of the element<br>With which you fought, my Lord! and not my merit.<br>The Baltic Neptune did assert his [[Freedom\|freedom]],<br>The sea and land, it seemed, were not to serve<br>One and the same. |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (makes a motion for him to take a seat, and seats himself). |
| WALLENSTEIN | And where are your credentials?<br>Come you provided with full powers, Sir General? |
| WRANGEL | There are so many scruples yet to solve —— |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (having read the credentials). |
| WALLENSTEIN | An able letter! — Ay — he is a prudent,<br>Intelligent master, whom you serve, Sir General!<br>The Chancellor writes me, that he but fulfils<br>His late departed Sovereign's own idea<br>In helping me to the Bohemian [[Crown\|crown]]. |
| WRANGEL | He says the [[Truth\|truth]]. Our great [[King]], now in heaven,<br>Did ever deem most highly of your Grace's<br>Pre-eminent sense and military genius;<br>And always the commanding Intellect,<br>He said, should have command, and be the [[King]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Taking his hand. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Yes, he might say it safely. — General Wrangel,<br>Come, fair and open — Trust me, I was always<br>A Swede at heart. Ey! that did you experience<br>Both in Silesia and at Nuremburg;<br>I had you often in my power, and let you<br>Always slip out by some back door or other.<br>'Tis this for which the [[Court]] can ne'er forgive me,<br>Which drives me to this present step: and since<br>Our interests so run in one direction,<br>E'en let us have a thorough confidence<br>Each in the other. |
| WRANGEL | Confidence will come<br>Has each but only first security. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The Chancellor still, I see, does not quite trust me;<br>And, I confess — the gain does not wholly lie<br>To my advantage — Without [[Doubt\|doubt]] he thinks<br>If I can play false with the Emperor,<br>Who is my Sov'reign, I can do the like<br>With the enemy, and that the one too were<br>Sooner to be forgiven me than the other.<br>Is not this your opinion too, Sir General? |
| WRANGEL | I have here an office merely, no opinion. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The Emperor hath urged me to the uttermost.<br>I can no longer honourably serve him.<br>For my security, in [[Self\|self]]-defence,<br>I take this hard step, which my [[Conscience\|conscience]] blames. |
| WRANGEL | That I believe. So far would no one go<br>Who was not forced to it. |
| *(didascalia)* | [After a pause. |
| WRANGEL | What may have impelled<br>Your princely Highness in this wise to act<br>Toward your Sovereign Lord and Emperor,<br>Beseems not us to expound or criticize.<br>The Swede is fighting for his good old cause.<br>With his good [[Sword\|sword]] and [[Conscience\|conscience]]. This concurrence,<br>This opportunity, is in our favour,<br>And all advantages in war are lawful.<br>We take what offers without questioning;<br>And if all have its due and just proportions —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | Of what then are ye doubting? Of my will?<br>Or of my power? I pledged me to the Chancellor,<br>Would he trust me with sixteen thousand men,<br>That I would instantly go over to them<br>With eighteen thousand of the Emperor's troops. |
| WRANGEL | Your Grace is known to be a mighty war-chief,<br>To be a second Attila and Pyrrhus.<br>'Tis talked of still with fresh astonishment,<br>How some years past, beyond all human [[Faith\|faith]],<br>You called an army forth, like a creation:<br>But yet —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | But yet? |
| WRANGEL | But still the Chancellor thinks,<br>It might yet be an easier thing from nothing<br>To call forth sixty thousand men of battle,<br>Than to persuade one sixtieth part of them — |
| WALLENSTEIN | What now? Out with it, friend! |
| WRANGEL | To break their oaths. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And he thinks so? — He judges like a Swede,<br>And like a Protestant. You Lutherans<br>Fight for your [[Bible]]. You are interested<br>About the cause; and with your hearts you follow<br>Your banners. — Among you, whoe'er deserts<br>To the enemy, hath broken covenant<br>With two Lords at one [[Time\|time]]. — We've no such fancies. |
| WRANGEL | Great God in Heaven! Have then the people here<br>No [[house]] and [[Home\|home]], no [[Fire\|fire]]-side, no altar? |
| WALLENSTEIN | I will explain that to you, how it stands —<br>The Austrian has a country, ay, and loves it,<br>And has good cause to [[Love\|love]] it — but this army,<br>That calls itself the Imperial, this that houses<br>Here in Bohemia, this has none — no country;<br>This is an outcast of all foreign lands,<br>Unclaimed by town or tribe, to whom belongs<br>Nothing, except the universal sun. |
| WRANGEL | But then the Nobles and the Officers?<br>Such a desertion, such a felony,<br>It is without example, my Lord Duke,<br>In the world's [[history]]. |
| WALLENSTEIN | They are all mine —<br>Mine unconditionally — mine on all terms.<br>Not me, your own eyes you must trust. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He gives him the paper containing the written oath. WRANGEL reads it through, and, having read it, lays it on the table, remaining silent. |
| WALLENSTEIN | So then?<br>Now comprehend you? |
| WRANGEL | Comprehend who can!<br>My Lord Duke; I will let the mask drop — yes!<br>I've full powers for a final settlement.<br>The Rhinegrave stands but four days' march from here<br>With fifteen thousand men, and only waits<br>For orders to proceed and join your army.<br>Those orders I give out, immediately<br>We're compromised. |
| WALLENSTEIN | What asks the Chancellor? |
| WRANGEL | Twelve Regiments, every man a Swede — my head<br>The warranty — and all might prove at last<br>Only false play —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | Sir Swede! |
| WRANGEL | Am therefore forced<br>T' insist thereon, that he do formally,<br>Irrevocably break with the Emperor,<br>Else not a Swede is trusted to Duke Friedland. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Come, brief and open! What is the demand? |
| WRANGEL | That he forthwith disarm the Spanish regiments<br>Attached to the Emperor, that he seize Prague,<br>And to the Swedes give up that [[City\|city]], with<br>The strong pass Egra. |
| WALLENSTEIN | That is much indeed!<br>Prague! — Egra's granted — But — but Prague!—'Twon't do.<br>I give you every security<br>Which you may ask of me in common reason —<br>But Prague — Bohemia — these, Sir General,<br>I can myself protect. |
| WRANGEL | We [[Doubt\|doubt]] it not.<br>But 'tis not the protection that is now<br>Our sole concern. We want security,<br>That we shall not expend our men and money<br>All to no purpose. |
| WALLENSTEIN | 'Tis but reasonable. |
| WRANGEL | And till we are indemnified, so long<br>Stays Prague in pledge. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Then trust you us so little? |
| *(didascalia)* | (rising) |
| WRANGEL | The Swede, if he would treat [[Well\|well]] with the German,<br>Must keep a sharp look-out. We have been called<br>Over the Baltic, we have saved the empire<br>From ruin — with our best [[Blood\|blood]] have we seal'd<br>The liberty of [[Faith\|faith]], and gospel [[Truth\|truth]].<br>But now already is the benefaction<br>No longer felt, the load [[Alone\|alone]] is felt. ——<br>Ye look askance with evil eye upon us,<br>As foreigners, intruders in the empire,<br>And would fain send us, with some paltry sum<br>Of money, [[Home\|home]] again to our old forests.<br>No, no! my Lord Duke! no! — it never was<br>For Judas' pay, for chinking gold and silver,<br>That we did leave our [[King]] by the Great Stone.<br>No, not for gold and silver have there bled<br>So many of our Swedish Nobles — neither<br>Will we, with empty laurels for our payment,<br>Hoist sail for our own country. Citizens<br>Will we remain upon the soil, the which<br>Our Monarch conquered for himself, and died. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Help to keep down the common enemy,<br>And the fair border land must needs be yours. |
| WRANGEL | But when the common enemy lies vanquished,<br>Who knits together our new [[Friendship\|friendship]] then?<br>We know, Duke Friedland! though perhaps the Swede<br>Ought not t' have known it, that you carry on<br>Secret negotiations with the Saxons.<br>Who is our warranty, that we are not<br>The sacrifices in those articles<br>Which 'tis thought needful to conceal from us? |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (rises). |
| WALLENSTEIN | Think you of something better, Gustave Wrangel!<br>Of Prague no more. |
| WRANGEL | Here my commission ends. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Surrender up to you my capital!<br>Far liever would I face about, and step<br>Back to my Emperor. |
| WRANGEL | If [[Time\|time]] yet permits —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | That lies with me, even now, at any hour. |
| WRANGEL | Some days ago, perhaps. To-day, no longer,<br>No longer since Sesina is a prisoner.<br>My Lord Duke, hear me — We believe that you<br>At present do mean honourably by us.<br>Since yesterday we're sure of that — and now<br>This paper warrants for the troops, there's nothing<br>Stands in the way of our full confidence.<br>Prague shall not part us. Hear! The Chancellor<br>Contents himself with Albstadt, to your Grace<br>He gives up Ratschin and the narrow side,<br>But Egra above all must open to us,<br>Ere we can think of any junction. |
| WALLENSTEIN | You,<br>You therefore must I trust, and you not me?<br>I will consider of your proposition. |
| WRANGEL | I must entreat, that your consideration<br>Occupy not too long a [[Time\|time]]. Already<br>Has this negotiation, my Lord Duke!<br>Crept on into the second year. If nothing<br>Is settled this [[Time\|time]], will the Chancellor<br>Consider it as broken off for ever. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Ye press me hard. A measure, such as this,<br>Ought to be thought of. |
| WRANGEL | Ay! but think of this too,<br>That sudden action only can procure it<br>[[success\|Success]] — think first of this, your Highness. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit WRANGEL. |
