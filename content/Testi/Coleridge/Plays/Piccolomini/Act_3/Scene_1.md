| *(didascalia)* | SCENE. — A Chamber in PICCOLOMINI'S Mansion. — [[Night]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | OCTAVIO PICCOLOMINI. A Valet de Chambre, with Lights. |
| OCTAVIO | And when my son comes in, conduct him hither.<br>What is the hour? |
| VALET | 'Tis on the point of morning. |
| OCTAVIO | Set down the light. We mean not to undress.<br>You may retire to [[Sleep\|sleep]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit Valet. OCTAVIO paces, musing, across the chamber;<br>MAX PICCOLOMINI enters unobserved, and looks at his<br>father for some moments in [[Silence\|silence]]. |
| MAX | Art thou offended with me? Heaven knows<br>That odious business was no fault of mine.<br>'Tis true, indeed, I saw thy signature.<br>What thou hadst sanctioned, should not, it might seem,<br>Have come amiss to me. But—'tis my [[Nature\|nature]] —<br>Thou know'st that in such matters I must follow<br>My own light, not another's. |
| OCTAVIO | (embraces him). Follow it,<br>O follow it still further, my best son!<br>To-[[Night\|night]], dear boy! it hath more faithfully<br>Guided thee than the example of thy father. |
| MAX | Declare thyself less darkly. |
| OCTAVIO | I will do so.<br>For after what has taken place this [[Night\|night]],<br>There must remain no [[Secrets\|secrets]] 'twixt us two. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Both seat themselves. |
| MAX | Max Piccolomini! what thinkest thou of<br>The oath that was sent round for signatures? |
| MAX | I hold it for a thing of harmless import,<br>Although I [[Love\|love]] not these set declarations. |
| OCTAVIO | And on no other ground hast thou refused<br>The signature they fain had wrested from thee? |
| MAX | It was a serious business —— I was absent —<br>The affair itself seemed not so urgent to me. |
| OCTAVIO | Be open, Max. Thou hadst then no suspicion? |
| MAX | Suspicion! what suspicion? Not the least. |
| OCTAVIO | Thank thy good angel, Piccolomini:<br>He drew thee back unconscious from the abyss. |
| MAX | I know not what thou meanest. |
| OCTAVIO | I will tell thee.<br>Fain would they have extorted from thee, son,<br>The sanction of thy name to villainy;<br>Yea, with a single flourish of thy pen,<br>Made thee renounce thy duty and thy [[Honour\|honour]]! |
| MAX | Octavio! |
| OCTAVIO | Patience! Seat yourself. Much yet<br>Hast thou to hear from me, friend! — hast for years<br>Lived in incomprehensible illusion.<br>Before thine eyes is Treason drawing out<br>As black a web as e'er was spun for venom:<br>A power of hell o'erclouds thy understanding.<br>I dare no longer stand in [[Silence\|silence]] — dare<br>No longer see thee wandering on in darkness,<br>Nor pluck the bandage from thine eyes. |
| MAX | My father!<br>Yet, ere thou speak'st, a moment's pause of thought!<br>If your disclosures should appear to be<br>Conjectures only — and almost I [[Fear\|fear]]<br>They will be nothing further — spare them! I<br>Am not in that collected mood at present,<br>That I could listen to them quietly. |
| OCTAVIO | The deeper cause thou hast to hate this light,<br>The more impatient cause have I, my son,<br>To force it on thee. To the [[innocence]]<br>And wisdom of thy heart I could have trusted thee<br>With calm assurance — but I see the net<br>Preparing — and it is thy heart itself<br>Alarms me for thine [[innocence]] — that secret,<br>Which thou concealest, forces mine from me.<br>Know, then, they are duping thee! — a most foul game<br>With thee and with us all — nay, hear me calmly —<br>The Duke even now is playing. He assumes<br>The mask, as if he would forsake the army;<br>And in this moment makes he preparations<br>That army from the Emperor to steal,<br>And carry it over to the enemy! |
| MAX | That low Priest's legend I know [[Well\|well]], but did not<br>Expect to hear it from thy mouth. |
| OCTAVIO | That mouth,<br>From which thou hearest it at this present moment,<br>Doth warrant thee that it is no Priest's legend. |
| MAX | How mere a maniac they supposed the Duke!<br>What, he can meditate? — the Duke? — can dream<br>That he can lure away full thirty thousand<br>Tried troops and true, all honourable soldiers,<br>More than a thousand noblemen among them,<br>From oaths, from duty, from their [[Honour\|honour]] lure them,<br>And make them all unanimous to do<br>A deed that brands them scoundrels? |
| OCTAVIO | Such a deed,<br>With such a front of infamy, the Duke<br>No wise desires — what he requires of us<br>Bears a far gentler appellation. Nothing<br>He wishes, but to give the Empire peace.<br>And so, because the Emperor hates this peace,<br>Therefore the Duke — the Duke will force him to it.<br>All parts of the Empire will he pacify,<br>And for his trouble will retain in payment<br>(What he has already in his gripe) — Bohemia! |
| MAX | Has he, Octavio, merited of us,<br>That we — that we should think so vilely of him? |
| OCTAVIO | What we would think is not the question here.<br>The affair speaks for itself — and clearest proofs!<br>Hear me, my son—'tis not unknown to thee,<br>In what ill credit with the [[Court]] we stand.<br>But little dost thou know, or guess, what tricks,<br>What base intrigues, what lying artifices,<br>Have been employed — for this sole end — to sow<br>Mutiny in the camp! All bands are loosed —<br>Loosed all the bands, that link the officer<br>To his liege Emperor, all that bind the soldier<br>Affectionately to the citizen.<br>Lawless he stands, and threateningly beleaguers<br>The state he's bound to guard. To such a height<br>'Tis swoln, that at this hour the Emperor<br>Before his armies — his own armies — trembles;<br>Yea, in his capital, his palace, fears<br>The traitor's poniards, and is meditating<br>To hurry off and hide his tender offspring ——<br>Not from the Swedes, not from the Lutherans —<br>No! from his own troops hide and hurry them! |
| MAX | Cease, cease! thou tortur'st, shatter'st me. I know<br>That oft we tremble at an empty terror;<br>But the false phantasm brings a real misery. |
| OCTAVIO | It is no phantasm. An intestine war,<br>Of all the most unnatural and cruel,<br>Will burst out into flames, if instantly<br>We do not fly and stifle it. The Generals<br>Are many of them long ago won over;<br>The subalterns are vacillating — whole<br>Regiments and garrisons are vacillating.<br>To foreigners our strong holds are entrusted;<br>To that suspected Schafgotch is the whole<br>Force of Silesia given up: to Tertsky<br>Five regiments, foot and [[Horse\|horse]] — to Isolani,<br>To Illo, Kinsky, Butler, the best troops. |
| MAX | Likewise to both of us. |
| OCTAVIO | Because the Duke<br>Believes he has secured us — means to lure us<br>Still further on by splendid promises.<br>To me he portions forth the princedoms, Glatz<br>And Sagan; and too plain I see the angle<br>With which he doubts not to catch thee. |
| MAX | No! no!<br>I tell thee — no! |
| OCTAVIO | O open yet thine eyes!<br>And to what purpose think'st thou he has called us<br>Hither to Pilsen? — to avail himself<br>Of our advice? — O when did Friedland ever<br>Need our advice? — Be calm, and listen to me.<br>To sell ourselves are we called hither, and,<br>Decline we that — to be his hostages.<br>Therefore doth noble Galas stand aloof;<br>Thy father, too, thou would'st not have seen here,<br>If higher duties had not held him fettered. |
| MAX | He makes no secret of it — needs make none —<br>That we're called hither for his sake — he owns it.<br>He needs our aidance to maintain himself —<br>He did so much for us; and 'tis but fair<br>That we too should do somewhat now for him. |
| OCTAVIO | And know'st thou what it is which we must do?<br>That Illo's drunken mood betrayed it to thee.<br>Bethink thyself — what hast thou heard, what seen?<br>The counterfeited paper — the omission<br>Of that particular clause, so full of meaning,<br>Does it not prove, that they would bind us down<br>To nothing good? |
| MAX | That counterfeited paper<br>Appears to me no other than a trick<br>Of Illo's own device. These underhand<br>Traders in great men's interests ever use<br>To urge and hurry all things to the extreme.<br>They see the Duke at variance with the [[Court\|court]],<br>And fondly think to serve him, when they widen<br>The breach irreparably. Trust me, father,<br>The Duke knows nothing of all this. |
| OCTAVIO | It grieves me<br>That I must dash to earth, that I must shatter<br>A [[Faith\|faith]] so specious; but I may not spare thee!<br>For this is not a [[Time\|time]] for tenderness.<br>Thou must take measures, speedy ones — must act.<br>I therefore will confess to thee, that all<br>Which I've entrusted to thee now — that all<br>Which seems to thee so unbelievable,<br>That — yes, I will tell thee — Max! I had it all<br>From his own mouth — from the Duke's mouth I had it. |
| MAX | No! — no! — never! |
| OCTAVIO | Himself confided to me<br>What I, 'tis true, had long before discovered<br>By other means — himself confided to me,<br>That 'twas his settled plan to join the Swedes;<br>And, at the head of the united armies,<br>Compel the Emperor — |
| MAX | He is passionate.<br>The [[Court]] has stung him — he is sore all over<br>With injuries and affronts; and in a moment<br>Of irritation, what if he, for once,<br>Forgot himself? He's an impetuous man. |
| OCTAVIO | Nay, in cold [[Blood\|blood]] he did confess this to me:<br>And having construed my astonishment<br>Into a scruple of his power, he shewed me<br>His written evidences — shewed me letters,<br>Both from the Saxon and the Swede, that gave<br>Promise of aidance, and defin'd the amount. |
| MAX | It cannot be! — can not be! can not be!<br>Dost thou not see, it cannot!<br>Thou wouldest of necessity have shewn him<br>Such horror, such deep loathing — that or he<br>Had taken thee for his better genius, or<br>Thou stood'st not now a living man before me — |
| OCTAVIO | I have laid open my objections to him,<br>Dissuaded him with pressing earnestness;<br>But my abhorrence, the full sentiment<br>Of my whole heart — that I have still kept sacred<br>To my own consciousness. |
| MAX | And thou hast been<br>So treacherous? That looks not like my father!<br>I trusted not thy words, when thou didst tell me<br>Evil of him; much less can I now do it,<br>That thou calumniatest thy own [[Self\|self]]. |
| OCTAVIO | I did not thrust myself into his secrecy. |
| MAX | Uprightness merited his confidence. |
| OCTAVIO | He was no longer worthy of sincerity. |
| MAX | Dissimulation, sure, was still less worthy<br>Of thee, Octavio! |
| OCTAVIO | Gave I him a cause<br>To entertain a scruple of my [[Honour\|honour]]? |
| MAX | That he did not, evinced his confidence. |
| OCTAVIO | Dear son, it is not always possible<br>Still to preserve that infant purity<br>Which the voice teaches in our inmost heart.<br>Still in alarm, for ever on the watch<br>Against the wiles of wicked men, e'en Virtue<br>Will sometimes bear away her outward robes<br>Soiled in the wrestle with Iniquity.<br>This is the curse of every evil deed,<br>That, propagating still, it brings forth evil.<br>I do not cheat my better [[Soul\|soul]] with sophisms:<br>I but perform my orders; the Emperor<br>Prescribes my conduct to me. Dearest boy,<br>Far better were it, doubtless, if we all<br>Obeyed the heart at all times; but so doing,<br>In this our present sojourn with bad men,<br>We must abandon many an honest object.<br>'Tis now our call to serve the Emperor,<br>By what means he can best be served — the heart<br>May whisper what it will — this is our call! |
| MAX | It seems a thing appointed, that to-day<br>I should not comprehend, not understand thee.<br>The Duke thou say'st did honestly pour out<br>His heart to thee, but for an evil purpose;<br>And thou dishonestly hast cheated him<br>For a good purpose! [[Silence]], I entreat thee —<br>My friend thou stealest not from me —<br>Let me not lose my father! |
| OCTAVIO | As yet thou know'st not all, my son. I have<br>Yet somewhat to disclose to thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | [After a pause. |
| OCTAVIO | Duke Friedland<br>Hath made his preparations. He relies<br>Upon his [[Stars\|stars]]. He deems us unprovided,<br>And thinks to fall upon us by surprise.<br>Yea, in his dream of [[Hope\|hope]], he grasps already<br>The golden circle in his hand. He errs.<br>We too have been in action — he but grasps<br>His evil fate, most evil, most mysterious! |
| MAX | O nothing rash, my sire! By all that's good<br>Let me invoke thee — no precipitation! |
| OCTAVIO | With light tread stole he on his evil way,<br>With light tread hath Vengeance stole on after him.<br>Unseen she stands already, dark behind him —<br>But one step more — he shudders in her grasp!<br>Thou hast seen Questenberg with me. As yet<br>Thou know'st but his ostensible commission;<br>He brought with him a private one, my son!<br>And that was for me only. |
| MAX | May I know it? |
| OCTAVIO | (seizes the patent). Max! |
| *(didascalia)* | [A pause. |
| OCTAVIO | —— In this disclosure place I in thy hands<br>The Empire's welfare and thy father's life.<br>Dear to thy inmost heart is Wallenstein:<br>A powerful tie of [[Love\|love]], of veneration,<br>Hath knit thee to him from thy earliest youth.<br>Thou nourishest the wish. — O let me still<br>Anticipate thy loitering confidence!<br>The [[Hope\|hope]] thou nourishest to knit thyself<br>Yet closer to him —— |
| MAX | Father —— |
| OCTAVIO | O my son!<br>I trust thy heart undoubtingly. But am I<br>Equally sure of thy collectedness?<br>Wilt thou be able, with calm countenance,<br>To enter this man's presence, when that I<br>Have trusted to thee his whole fate? |
| MAX | According<br>As thou dost trust me, father, with his crime. |
| *(didascalia)* | [OCTAVIO takes a paper out of his escrutoire, and gives it to him. |
| MAX | What? how? a full Imperial patent! |
| OCTAVIO | Read it. |
| MAX | Duke Friedland sentenced and condemned! |
| OCTAVIO | Even so. |
| MAX | O this is too much! O unhappy error! |
| OCTAVIO | Read on. Collect thyself. |
| MAX | (after he has read further, with a look of affright and astonishment on his father). How! what! Thou! thou! |
| OCTAVIO | But for the present moment, till the [[King]]<br>Of Hungary may safely join the army,<br>Is the command assigned to me. |
| MAX | And think'st thou,<br>Dost thou believe, that thou wilt tear it from him?<br>O never [[Hope\|hope]] it! — Father! father! father!<br>An inauspicious office is enjoined thee.<br>This paper here — this! and wilt thou enforce it?<br>The mighty in the middle of his host,<br>Surrounded by his thousands, him would'st thou<br>Disarm — degrade! Thou art lost, both thou and all of us. |
| OCTAVIO | What hazard I incur thereby, I know.<br>In the great hand of God I stand. The Almighty<br>Will cover with his shield the Imperial [[house]],<br>And shatter, in his wrath, the work of darkness.<br>The Emperor hath true servants still; and even<br>Here in the camp, there are enough brave men,<br>Who for the good cause will fight gallantly.<br>The faithful have been warned — the dangerous<br>Are closely watched. I wait but the first step,<br>And then immediately —— |
| MAX | What! on suspicion?<br>Immediately? |
| OCTAVIO | The Emperor is no [[Tyrant\|tyrant]].<br>The deed [[Alone\|alone]] he'll punish, not the wish.<br>The Duke hath yet his destiny in his power.<br>Let him but leave the treason uncompleted,<br>He will be silently displaced from office,<br>And make way to his Emperor's royal son.<br>An honourable [[Exile\|exile]] to his castles<br>Will be a benefaction to him rather<br>Than punishment. But the first open step —— |
| MAX | What callest thou such a step? A wicked step<br>Ne'er will he take; but thou mightest easily,<br>Yea, thou hast done it, misinterpret him. |
| OCTAVIO | Nay, howsoever punishable were<br>Duke Friedland's purposes, yet still the steps<br>Which he hath taken openly, permit<br>A mild construction. It is my intention<br>To leave this paper wholly uninforced<br>Till some act is committed which convicts him<br>Of a high-treason, without [[Doubt\|doubt]] or plea,<br>And that shall sentence him. |
| MAX | But who the judge? |
| OCTAVIO | Thyself. |
| MAX | For ever, then, this paper will lie idle. |
| OCTAVIO | Too soon, I [[Fear\|fear]], its powers must all be proved.<br>After the counter-promise of this evening,<br>It cannot be but he must deem himself<br>Secure of the majority with us;<br>And of the army's general sentiment<br>He hath a pleasing proof in that petition<br>Which thou delivered'st to him from the regiments.<br>Add this too — I have letters that the Rhinegrave<br>Hath changed his route, and travels by forced marches<br>To the Bohemian Forest. What this purports,<br>Remains unknown; and, to confirm suspicion,<br>This [[Night\|night]] a Swedish nobleman arrived here. |
| MAX | I have thy word. Thou'lt not proceed to action<br>Before thou hast convinced me — me myself. |
| OCTAVIO | Is it possible? Still, after all thou know'st,<br>Canst thou believe still in his [[innocence]]? |
| MAX | Thy judgment may mistake; my heart can not.<br>These reasons might expound thy spirit or mine;<br>But they expound not Friedland — I have [[Faith\|faith]]:<br>For as he knits his fortunes to the [[Stars\|stars]],<br>Even so doth he resemble them in secret,<br>Wonderful, still inexplicable courses!<br>Trust me, they do him wrong. All will be solved.<br>These smokes, at once, will kindle into flame —<br>The edges of this black and stormy cloud<br>Will brighten suddenly, and we shall view<br>The Unapproachable glide out in splendour. |
| OCTAVIO | I will await it. |
