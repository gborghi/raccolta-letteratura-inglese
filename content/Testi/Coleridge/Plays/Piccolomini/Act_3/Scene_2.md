| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | OCTAVIO and MAX as before. To them the Valet of the Chamber. |
| OCTAVIO | How now, then? |
| VALET | A dispatch is at the door. |
| OCTAVIO | So early? From whom comes he then? Who is it? |
| VALET | That he refused to tell me. |
| OCTAVIO | Lead him in:<br>And, hark you — let it not transpire. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit Valet — the Cornet steps in. |
| OCTAVIO | Ha! Cornet — is it you? and from Count Galas?<br>Give me your letters. |
| CORNET | The Lieutenant-General<br>Trusted it not to letters. |
| OCTAVIO | And what is it? |
| CORNET | He bade me tell you — Dare I speak openly here? |
| OCTAVIO | My son knows all. |
| CORNET | We have him. |
| OCTAVIO | Whom? |
| CORNET | Sesina,<br>The old negotiator. |
| OCTAVIO | And you have him? |
| CORNET | In the Bohemian Forest Captain Mohrbrand<br>Found and secured him yester morning early:<br>He was proceeding then to Regenspurg,<br>And on him were dispatches for the Swede. |
| OCTAVIO | And the dispatches —— |
| CORNET | The Lieutenant-General<br>Sent them that instant to Vienna, and<br>The prisoner with them. |
| OCTAVIO | This is, indeed, a tiding!<br>That fellow is a precious casket to us,<br>Enclosing weighty things. — Was much found on him? |
| CORNET | I think, six packets, with Count Tertsky's arms. |
| OCTAVIO | None in the Duke's own hand? |
| CORNET | Not that I know. |
| OCTAVIO | And old Sesina? |
| CORNET | He was sorely frightened,<br>When it was told him he must to Vienna.<br>But the Count Altringer bade him take heart,<br>Would he but make a full and free confession. |
| OCTAVIO | Is Altringer then with your Lord? I heard<br>That he lay sick at Linz. |
| CORNET | These three days past<br>He's with my master, the Lieutenant-General,<br>At Frauenberg. Already have they sixty<br>Small companies together, chosen men;<br>Respectfully they greet you with assurances,<br>That they are only waiting your commands. |
| OCTAVIO | In a few days may great events take place.<br>And when must you return? |
| CORNET | I wait your orders. |
| OCTAVIO | Remain till evening. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Cornet signifies his assent and obeisance, and is going. |
| OCTAVIO | No one saw you — ha? |
| CORNET | No living creature. Through the cloister wicket<br>The Capuchins, as usual, let me in. |
| OCTAVIO | Go, rest your limbs, and keep yourself concealed.<br>I hold it probable, that yet ere evening<br>I shall dispatch you. The development<br>Of this affair approaches: ere the day,<br>That even now is dawning in the heaven,<br>Ere this eventful day hath set, the lot<br>That must decide our fortunes will be drawn. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit Cornet. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Before 10] Octavio (eagerly). 1800, 1828, 1829. |
