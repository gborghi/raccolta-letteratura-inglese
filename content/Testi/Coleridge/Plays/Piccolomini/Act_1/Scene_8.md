| *(didascalia)* | Enter the COUNTESS TERTSKY, leading in her hand the PRINCESS THEKLA,<br>richly adorned with brilliants.<br>COUNTESS, THEKLA, WALLENSTEIN, DUCHESS. |
| COUNTESS | How, sister? What already upon business,<br>And business of no pleasing kind I see,<br>Ere he has gladdened at his [[Child\|child]]. The first<br>Moment belongs to joy. Here, Friedland! father!<br>This is thy daughter. |
| *(didascalia)* | (THEKLA approaches with a shy and timid air, and bends<br>herself as about to kiss his hand. He receives her<br>in his arms, and remains standing for some [[Time\|time]]<br>lost in the feeling of her presence.) |
| WALLENSTEIN | Yes! pure and lovely hath [[Hope\|hope]] risen on me:<br>I take her as the pledge of greater fortune. |
| DUCHESS | 'Twas but a little [[Child\|child]] when you departed<br>To raise up that great army for the Emperor:<br>And after, at the close of the campaign,<br>When you returned [[Home\|home]] out of Pomerania,<br>Your daughter was already in the convent,<br>Wherein she has remain'd till now. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The while<br>We in the field here gave our cares and toils<br>To make her great, and fight her a free way<br>To the loftiest earthly good, lo! mother [[Nature]]<br>Within the peaceful silent convent walls<br>Has done her part, and out of her free grace<br>Hath she bestowed on the beloved [[Child\|child]]<br>The godlike; and now leads her thus adorned<br>To meet her splendid fortune, and my [[Hope\|hope]]. |
| DUCHESS | Thou wouldst not have recognized thy father,<br>Wouldst thou, my [[Child\|child]]? She counted scarce eight years,<br>When last she saw your face. |
| THEKLA | O yes, yes, mother!<br>At the first glance! — My father is not altered.<br>The form, that stands before me, falsifies<br>No feature of the image that hath lived<br>So long within me! |
| WALLENSTEIN | The voice of my [[Child\|child]]!<br>[Then after a pause.<br>I was indignant at my destiny<br>That it denied me a man-[[Child\|child]] to be<br>Heir of my name and of my prosperous fortune,<br>And re-illume my soon extinguished being<br>In a proud line of princes.<br>I wronged my destiny. Here upon this head<br>So lovely in its maiden bloom will I<br>Let fall the garland of a life of war,<br>Nor deem it lost, if only I can wreath it<br>Transmitted to a regal ornament,<br>Around these beauteous brows. |
