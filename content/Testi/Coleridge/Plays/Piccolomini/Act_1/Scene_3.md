| *(didascalia)* | QUESTENBERG and OCTAVIO. |
| QUESTENBERG | What have I not been forced to hear, Octavio!<br>What sentiments! what fierce, uncurbed defiance!<br>And were this spirit universal — |
| OCTAVIO | Hm!<br>You are now acquainted with three-fourths of the army. |
| QUESTENBERG | Where must we seek then for a second host<br>To have the custody of this? That Illo<br>Thinks worse, I [[Fear\|fear]] me, than he speaks. And then<br>This Butler too — he cannot even conceal<br>The passionate workings of his ill intentions. |
| OCTAVIO | Quickness of temper — irritated [[Pride\|pride]];<br>'Twas nothing more. I cannot give up Butler.<br>I know a spell that will soon dispossess<br>The evil spirit in him. |
| QUESTENBERG | Friend, friend!<br>O! this is worse, far worse, than we had suffered<br>Ourselves to dream of at Vienna. There<br>We saw it only with a courtier's eyes,<br>Eyes dazzled by the splendour of the throne.<br>We had not seen the War-Chief, the Commander,<br>The man all-powerful in his camp. Here, here,<br>'Tis quite another thing.<br>Here is no Emperor more — the Duke is Emperor.<br>Alas, my friend! alas, my noble friend!<br>This walk which you have ta'en me through the camp<br>Strikes my hopes prostrate. |
| OCTAVIO | Now you see yourself<br>Of what a perilous kind the office is,<br>Which you deliver to me from the [[Court]].<br>The least suspicion of the General<br>Costs me my [[Freedom\|freedom]] and my life, and would<br>But hasten his most desperate enterprise. |
| QUESTENBERG | Where was our reason sleeping when we trusted<br>This madman with the [[Sword\|sword]], and placed such power<br>In such a hand? I tell you, he'll refuse,<br>Flatly refuse, to obey the Imperial orders.<br>Friend, he can do 't, and what he can, he will.<br>And then the impunity of his defiance —<br>O! what a proclamation of our weakness! |
| OCTAVIO | D'ye think too, he has brought his wife and daughter<br>Without a purpose hither? Here in camp!<br>And at the very point of [[Time\|time]], in which<br>We're arming for the war? That he has taken<br>These, the last pledges of his loyalty,<br>Away from out the Emperor's domains —<br>This is no doubtful token of the nearness<br>Of some eruption! |
| QUESTENBERG | How shall we hold footing<br>Beneath this tempest, which collects itself<br>And threats us from all quarters? The enemy<br>Of the empire on our borders, now already<br>The master of the Danube, and still farther,<br>And farther still, extending every hour!<br>In our interior the alarum-[[Bells\|bells]]<br>Of insurrection — peasantry in arms ——<br>All orders discontented — and the army,<br>Just in the moment of our expectation<br>Of aidance from it — lo! this very army<br>Seduced, run wild, lost to all discipline,<br>Loosened, and rent asunder from the state<br>And from their sovereign, the blind instrument<br>Of the most daring of mankind, a weapon<br>Of fearful power, which at his will he wields! |
| OCTAVIO | Nay, nay, friend! let us not [[Despair\|despair]] too soon,<br>Men's words are ever bolder than their deeds:<br>And many a resolute, who now appears<br>Made up to all extremes, will, on a sudden<br>Find in his breast a heart he knew not of,<br>Let but a single honest man speak out<br>The true name of his crime! Remember, too,<br>We stand not yet so wholly unprotected.<br>Counts Altringer and Galas have maintained<br>Their little army faithful to its duty,<br>And daily it becomes more numerous.<br>Nor can he take us by surprise: you know,<br>I hold him all-encompassed by my listeners.<br>Whate'er he does, is mine, even while 'tis doing —<br>No step so small, but instantly I hear it;<br>Yea, his own mouth discloses it. |
| QUESTENBERG | 'Tis quite<br>Incomprehensible, that he detects not<br>The foe so near! |
| OCTAVIO | Beware, you do not think,<br>That I by lying arts, and complaisant<br>Hypocrisy, have skulked into his graces:<br>Or with the sustenance of smooth professions<br>Nourish his all-confiding [[Friendship\|friendship]]! No —<br>Compelled alike by prudence, and that duty<br>Which we all owe our country, and our sovereign,<br>To hide my genuine feelings from him, yet<br>Ne'er have I duped him with base counterfeits! |
| QUESTENBERG | It is the visible ordinance of heaven. |
| OCTAVIO | I know not what it is that so attracts<br>And links him both to me and to my son.<br>Comrades and friends we always were — long habit,<br>Adventurous deeds performed in company,<br>And all those many and various incidents<br>Which store a soldier's [[Memory\|memory]] with affections,<br>Had bound us long and early to each other —<br>Yet I can name the day, when all at once<br>His heart [[Rose\|rose]] on me, and his confidence<br>Shot out in sudden growth. It was the morning<br>Before the memorable fight at Lützner.<br>Urged by an ugly dream, I sought him out,<br>To press him to accept another charger.<br>At distance from the tents, beneath a tree,<br>I found him in a [[Sleep\|sleep]]. When I had waked him,<br>And had related all my bodings to him,<br>Long [[Time\|time]] he stared upon me, like a man<br>Astounded; thereon fell upon my neck,<br>And manifested to me an emotion<br>That far outstripped the worth of that small service.<br>Since then his confidence has followed me<br>With the same pace that mine has fled from him. |
| QUESTENBERG | You lead your son into the secret? |
| OCTAVIO | No! |
| QUESTENBERG | What? and not warn him either what bad hands<br>His lot has placed him in? |
| OCTAVIO | I must perforce<br>Leave him in wardship to his [[innocence]].<br>His young and open [[Soul\|soul]] — dissimulation<br>Is foreign to its habits! Ignorance<br>[[Alone]] can keep alive the cheerful air,<br>The unembarrassed sense and light free spirit,<br>That make the Duke secure. |
| QUESTENBERG | My honoured friend! most highly do I deem<br>Of Colonel Piccolomini — yet — if ——<br>Reflect a little —— |
| OCTAVIO | I must venture it.<br>Hush! — There he comes! |
