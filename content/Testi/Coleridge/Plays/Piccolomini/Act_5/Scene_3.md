| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, MAX PICCOLOMINI. |
| MAX | My General! |
| WALLENSTEIN | That am I no longer, if<br>Thou styl'st thyself the Emperor's officer. |
| MAX | Then thou wilt leave the army, General? |
| WALLENSTEIN | I have renounced the service of the Emperor. |
| MAX | And thou wilt leave the army? |
| WALLENSTEIN | Rather [[Hope\|hope]] I<br>To bind it nearer still and faster to me. |
| *(didascalia)* | He seats himself. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Yes, Max, I have delayed to open it to thee,<br>Even till the hour of acting 'gins to strike.<br>Youth's fortunate feeling doth seize easily<br>The absolute right, yea, and a joy it is<br>To exercise the single apprehension<br>Where the sums square in proof;<br>But where it happens, that of two sure evils<br>One must be taken, where the heart not wholly<br>Brings itself back from out the strife of duties,<br>There 'tis a blessing to have no election,<br>And blank necessity is grace and favour.<br>— This is now present: do not look behind thee. —<br>It can no more avail thee. Look thou forwards!<br>Think not! judge not! prepare thyself to act!<br>The [[Court]] — it hath determined on my ruin,<br>Therefore I will to be beforehand with them.<br>We'll join the Swedes — right gallant fellows are they,<br>And our good friends. |
| *(didascalia)* | He stops himself, expecting PICCOLOMINI'S answer. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I have ta'en thee by surprise. Answer me not.<br>I grant thee [[Time\|time]] to recollect thyself. |
| *(didascalia)* | He rises, and retires at the back of the stage. MAX remains for a long [[Time\|time]] motionless, in a trance of excessive anguish. At his first motion WALLENSTEIN returns, and places himself before him. |
| MAX | My General, this day thou makest me<br>Of age to speak in my own right and person,<br>For till this day I have been spared the trouble<br>To find out my own [[Road\|road]]. Thee have I followed<br>With most implicit unconditional [[Faith\|faith]],<br>Sure of the right path if I followed thee.<br>To-day, for the first [[Time\|time]], dost thou refer<br>Me to myself, and forcest me to make<br>Election between thee and my own heart. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Soft cradled thee thy Fortune till to-day;<br>Thy duties thou couldst exercise in sport,<br>Indulge all lovely instincts, act for ever<br>With undivided heart. It can remain<br>No longer thus. Like enemies, the roads<br>Start from each other. Duties strive with duties.<br>Thou must needs choose thy party in the war<br>Which is now kindling 'twixt thy friend and him<br>Who is thy Emperor. |
| MAX | War! is that the name?<br>War is as frightful as heaven's pestilence.<br>Yet it is good, is it heaven's will as that is.<br>Is that a good war, which against the Emperor<br>Thou wagest with the Emperor's own army?<br>O God of heaven! what a change is this.<br>Beseems it me to offer such [[Persuasion\|persuasion]]<br>To thee, who like the fixed star of the pole<br>Wert all I gazed at on life's trackless ocean?<br>O! what a rent thou makest in my heart!<br>The ingrained instinct of old reverence.<br>The holy habit of obediency,<br>Must I pluck live asunder from thy name?<br>Nay, do not turn thy countenance upon me —<br>It always was as a god looking at me!<br>Duke Wallenstein, its power is not departed:<br>The senses still are in thy bonds, although,<br>Bleeding, the [[Soul\|soul]] hath freed itself. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Max, hear me. |
| MAX | O! do it not, I pray thee, do it not!<br>There is a pure and noble [[Soul\|soul]] within thee,<br>Knows not of this unblest, unlucky doing.<br>Thy will is chaste, it is thy fancy only<br>Which hath polluted thee — and [[innocence]],<br>It will not let itself be driven away<br>From that world-awing aspect. Thou wilt not,<br>Thou canst not, end in this. It would reduce<br>All human creatures to disloyalty<br>Against the nobleness of their own [[Nature\|nature]].<br>'Twill justify the vulgar misbelief,<br>Which holdeth nothing noble in free will,<br>And trusts itself to impotence [[Alone\|alone]]<br>Made powerful only in an unknown power. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The world will judge me sternly, I expect it.<br>Already have I said to my own [[Self\|self]]<br>All thou canst say to me. Who but avoids<br>The extreme, — can he by going round avoid it?<br>But here there is no choice. Yes — I must use<br>Or suffer violence — so stands the case,<br>There remains nothing possible but that. |
| MAX | O that is never possible for thee!<br>'Tis the last desperate resource of those<br>Cheap souls, to whom their [[Honour\|honour]], their good name<br>Is their poor saving, their last worthless keep,<br>Which having staked and lost, they stake themselves<br>In the mad rage of gaming. Thou art rich,<br>And glorious; with an unpolluted heart<br>Thou canst make conquest of whate'er seems highest!<br>But he, who once hath acted infamy,<br>Does nothing more in this world. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Calmly, Max!<br>Much that is great and excellent will we<br>Perform together yet. And if we only<br>Stand on the height with dignity, 'tis soon<br>Forgotten, Max, by what [[Road\|road]] we ascended.<br>Believe me, many a [[Crown\|crown]] shines spotless now,<br>That yet was deeply sullied in the winning.<br>To the evil spirit doth the earth belong,<br>Not to the good. All, that the powers divine<br>Send from above, are universal blessings:<br>Their light rejoices us, their air refreshes,<br>But never yet was man enriched by them:<br>In their eternal realm no property<br>Is to be struggled for — all there is general.<br>The [[Jewel\|jewel]], the all-valued gold we win<br>From the deceiving Powers, depraved in [[Nature\|nature]],<br>That dwell beneath the day and blessed sun-light.<br>Not without sacrifices are they rendered<br>Propitious, and there lives no [[Soul\|soul]] on earth<br>That e'er retired unsullied from their service. |
| *(didascalia)* | grasps his hand |
| MAX | Whate'er is human, to the human being<br>Do I allow — and to the vehement<br>And striving spirit readily I pardon<br>The excess of action; but to thee, my General!<br>Above all others make I large concession.<br>For thou must move a world, and be the master —<br>He kills thee, who condemns thee to inaction.<br>So be it then! maintain thee in thy post<br>By violence. Resist the Emperor,<br>And if it must be, force with force repel:<br>I will not praise it, yet I can forgive it.<br>But not — not to the traitor — yes! — the word<br>Is spoken out ——<br>Not to the traitor can I yield a pardon.<br>That is no mere excess! that is no error<br>Of human [[Nature\|nature]] — that is wholly different,<br>O that is black, black as the pit of hell!<br>Thou canst not hear it nam'd, and wilt thou do it?<br>O turn back to thy duty. That thou canst,<br>I hold it certain. Send me to Vienna.<br>I'll make thy peace for thee with the Emperor.<br>He knows thee not. But I do know thee. He<br>Shall see thee, Duke! with my unclouded eye,<br>And I bring back his confidence to thee. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It is too late. Thou knowest not what has happened. |
| MAX | Were it too late, and were things gone so far,<br>That a crime only could prevent thy fall,<br>Then — fall! fall honourably, even as thou stood'st.<br>Lose the command. Go from the stage of war.<br>Thou canst with splendour do it — do it too<br>With [[innocence]]. Thou hast liv'd much for others,<br>At length live thou for thy own [[Self\|self]]. I follow thee.<br>My destiny I never part from thine. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It is too late! Even now, while thou art losing<br>Thy words, one after the other are the mile-stones<br>Left fast behind by my post couriers,<br>Who bear the order on to Prague and Egra.<br>Yield thyself to it. We act as we are forced.<br>I cannot give assent to my own shame<br>And ruin. Thou — no — thou canst not forsake me!<br>So let us do, what must be done, with dignity,<br>With a firm step. What am I doing worse<br>Than did famed Cæsar at the Rubicon,<br>When he the legions led against his country,<br>The which his country had delivered to him?<br>Had he thrown down the [[Sword\|sword]], he had been lost,<br>As I were, if I but disarmed myself.<br>I trace out something in me of his spirit.<br>Give me his luck, that other thing I'll bear. |
| *(didascalia)* | MAX quits him abruptly. WALLENSTEIN, startled and overpowered, continues looking after him, and is still in this posture when TERTSKY enters. |
