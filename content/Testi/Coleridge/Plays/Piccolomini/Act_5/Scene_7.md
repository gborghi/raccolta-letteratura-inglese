| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | OCTAVIO and MAX PICCOLOMINI. |
| OCTAVIO | I am going off, my son. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Receiving no answer he takes his hand. |
| OCTAVIO | My son, farewell. |
| MAX | Farewell. |
| OCTAVIO | Thou wilt soon follow me? |
| MAX | I follow thee?<br>Thy way is crooked — it is not my way. |
| *(didascalia)* | [OCTAVIO drops his hand, and starts back. |
| MAX | O, hadst thou been but simple and sincere,<br>Ne'er had it come to this — all had stood otherwise.<br>He had not done that foul and horrible deed,<br>The virtuous had retained their influence o'er him:<br>He had not fallen into the snares of villains.<br>Wherefore so like a thief, and thief's accomplice<br>Did'st creep behind him — lurking for thy prey?<br>O, unblest falsehood! Mother of all evil!<br>Thou misery-making demon, it is thou<br>That sink'st us in perdition. Simple [[Truth\|truth]],<br>Sustainer of the world, had saved us all!<br>Father, I will not, I cannot excuse thee!<br>Wallenstein has deceived me — O, most foully!<br>But thou hast acted not much better. |
| OCTAVIO | Son!<br>My son, ah! I forgive thy agony! |
| MAX | Was't possible? had'st thou the heart, my father,<br>Had'st thou the heart to drive it to such lengths,<br>With cold premeditated purpose? Thou —<br>Had'st thou the heart, to wish to see him guilty,<br>Rather than saved? Thou risest by his fall.<br>Octavio, 'twill not please me. |
| OCTAVIO | God in Heaven! |
| MAX | O, woe is me! sure I have changed my [[Nature\|nature]].<br>How comes suspicion here — in the free [[Soul\|soul]]?<br>[[Hope]], confidence, belief, are gone; for all<br>Lied to me, all what I e'er loved or honoured.<br>No! No! Not all! She — she yet lives for me,<br>And she is true, and open as the Heavens!<br>Deceit is every where, hypocrisy,<br>Murder, and poisoning, treason, perjury:<br>The single holy spot is now our [[Love\|love]],<br>The only unprofaned in human [[Nature\|nature]]. |
| OCTAVIO | Max! — we will go together. 'Twill be better. |
| MAX | What? ere I've taken a last parting leave,<br>The very last — no never! |
| OCTAVIO | Spare thyself<br>The pang of necessary separation.<br>Come with me! Come, my son! |
| *(didascalia)* | [Attempts to take him with him. |
| MAX | No! as sure as God lives, no! |
| OCTAVIO | Come with me, I command thee! I, thy father. |
| MAX | Command me what is human. I stay here. |
| OCTAVIO | Max! in the Emperor's name I bid thee come. |
| MAX | No Emperor has power to prescribe<br>Laws to the heart; and would'st thou wish to rob me<br>Of the sole blessing which my fate has left me,<br>Her sympathy? Must then a cruel deed<br>Be done with cruelty? The unalterable<br>Shall I perform ignobly — steal away,<br>With stealthy coward flight forsake her? No!<br>She shall behold my [[Suffering\|suffering]], my sore anguish,<br>Hear the complaints of the disparted [[Soul\|soul]],<br>And weep [[Tears\|tears]] o'er me. Oh! the human race<br>Have steely souls — but she is as an angel.<br>From the black deadly [[Madness\|madness]] of [[Despair\|despair]]<br>Will she redeem my [[Soul\|soul]], and in soft words<br>Of comfort, plaining, loose this pang of [[Death\|death]]! |
| OCTAVIO | Thou wilt not tear thyself away; thou canst not.<br>O, come, my son! I bid thee save thy virtue. |
| MAX | Squander not thou thy words in vain.<br>The heart I follow, for I dare trust to it. |
| OCTAVIO | Max! Max! if that most damnéd thing could be,<br>If thou — my son — my own [[Blood\|blood]] — (dare I think it?)<br>Do sell thyself to him, the infamous,<br>Do stamp this brand upon our noble [[house]],<br>Then shall the world behold the horrible deed,<br>And in unnatural combat shall the steel<br>Of the son trickle with the father's [[Blood\|blood]]. |
| MAX | O hadst thou always better thought of men,<br>Thou hadst then acted better. Curst suspicion!<br>Unholy miserable [[Doubt\|doubt]]! To him<br>Nothing on earth remains unwrenched and firm,<br>Who has no [[Faith\|faith]]. |
| OCTAVIO | And if I trust thy heart,<br>Will it be always in thy power to follow it? |
| MAX | The heart's voice thou hast not o'erpower'd — as little<br>Will Wallenstein be able to o'erpower it. |
| OCTAVIO | O, Max! I see thee never more again! |
| MAX | Unworthy of thee wilt thou never see me. |
| OCTAVIO | I go to Frauenberg — the Pappenheimers<br>I leave thee here, the Lothrings too; Toskana<br>And Tiefenbach remain here to protect thee.<br>They [[Love\|love]] thee, and are faithful to their oath,<br>And will far rather fall in gallant contest<br>Than leave their rightful leader, and their [[Honour\|honour]]. |
| MAX | Rely on this, I either leave my life<br>In the struggle, or conduct them out of Pilsen. |
| OCTAVIO | Farewell, my son! |
| MAX | Farewell! |
| OCTAVIO | How? not one look<br>Of filial [[Love\|love]]? No grasp of the hand at parting?<br>It is a bloody war, to which we are going,<br>And the event uncertain and in darkness.<br>So used we not to part — it was not so!<br>Is it then true? I have a son no longer? |
| *(didascalia)* | [MAX falls into his arms, they hold each other for a long [[Time\|time]] in a speechless embrace, then go away at different sides. |
| *(didascalia)* | The Curtain drops. |

He paces with long strides through the chamber, then stands still again, and at last throws himself into a chair, staring vacantly at the object directly before him). 1800, 1828, 1829. |
