| *(didascalia)* | OCTAVIO PICCOLOMINI, BUTLER. |
| BUTLER | At your command, Lieutenant-General. |
| OCTAVIO | Welcome, as honoured friend and visitor. |
| BUTLER | You do me too much [[Honour\|honour]]. |
| OCTAVIO | You have not<br>Returned the advances which I made you yesterday —<br>Misunderstood them, as mere empty forms.<br>That wish proceeded from my heart — I was<br>In earnest with you — for 'tis now a [[Time\|time]]<br>In which the honest should unite most closely. |
| BUTLER | 'Tis only the like-minded can unite. |
| OCTAVIO | True! and I name all honest men like-minded.<br>I never charge a man but with those acts<br>To which his character deliberately<br>Impels him; for alas! the violence<br>Of blind misunderstandings often thrusts<br>The very best of us from the right track.<br>You came through Frauenberg. Did the Count Galas<br>Say nothing to you? Tell me. He's my friend. |
| BUTLER | His words were lost on me. |
| OCTAVIO | It grieves me sorely<br>To hear it: for his counsel was most wise.<br>I had myself the like to offer. |
| BUTLER | Spare<br>Yourself the trouble — me th' embarrassment,<br>To have deserved so ill your good opinion. |
| OCTAVIO | The [[Time\|time]] is precious — let us talk openly.<br>You know how matters stand here. Wallenstein<br>Meditates treason — I can tell you further —<br>He has committed treason; but few hours<br>Have past, since he a covenant concluded<br>With the enemy. The messengers are now<br>Full on their way to Egra and to Prague.<br>To-morrow he intends to lead us over<br>To the enemy. But he deceives himself;<br>For prudence wakes — the Emperor has still<br>Many and faithful friends here, and they stand<br>In closest [[Union\|union]], mighty though unseen.<br>This manifesto sentences the Duke —<br>Recalls the obedience of the army from him,<br>And summons all the loyal, all the honest,<br>To join and recognize in me their leader.<br>Choose — will you share with us an honest cause?<br>Or with the evil share an evil lot? |
| BUTLER | His lot is mine. |
| OCTAVIO | Is that your last resolve? |
| BUTLER | It is. |
| OCTAVIO | Nay, but bethink you, Colonel Butler!<br>As yet you have [[Time\|time]]. Within my faithful breast<br>That rashly uttered word remains interred.<br>Recall it, Butler! choose a better party:<br>You have not chosen the right one. |
| BUTLER | Any other<br>Commands for me, Lieutenant-General? |
| OCTAVIO | See your white hairs! Recall that word! |
| BUTLER | Farewell! |
| OCTAVIO | What, would you draw this good and gallant [[Sword\|sword]]<br>In such a cause? Into a curse would you<br>Transform the gratitude which you have earned<br>By forty years' fidelity from Austria? |
| BUTLER | Gratitude from the [[house\|House]] of Austria. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He is going. |
| *(didascalia)* | Octavio permits him to go as far as the door, then calls after him. |
| OCTAVIO | Butler! |
| BUTLER | What wish you? |
| OCTAVIO | How was't with the Count? |
| BUTLER | Count? what? |
| OCTAVIO | The title that you wished, I mean. |
| BUTLER | Hell and damnation! |
| OCTAVIO | You<br>petitioned for it —<br>And your petition was repelled — Was it so? |
| BUTLER | Your insolent scoff shall not go by unpunished.<br>Draw! |
| OCTAVIO | Nay! your [[Sword\|sword]] to 'ts sheath! and tell me calmly,<br>How all that happened. I will not refuse you<br>Your satisfaction afterwards. — Calmly, Butler! |
| BUTLER | Be the whole world acquainted with the weakness<br>For which I never can forgive myself.<br>Lieutenant-General! Yes — I have [[Ambition\|ambition]].<br>Ne'er was I able to endure contempt.<br>It stung me to the quick, that birth and title<br>Should have more weight than merit has in the army.<br>I would fain not be meaner than my equal,<br>So in an evil hour I let myself<br>Be tempted to that measure — It was folly!<br>But yet so hard a penance it deserved not.<br>It might have been refused; but wherefore barb<br>And venom the refusal with contempt?<br>Why dash to earth and crush with heaviest scorn<br>The grey-haired man, the faithful veteran?<br>Why to the baseness of his parentage<br>Refer him with such cruel roughness, only<br>Because he had a weak hour and forgot himself?<br>But [[Nature\|nature]] gives a sting e'en to the worm<br>Which wanton power treads on in sport and insult. |
| OCTAVIO | You must have been calumniated. Guess you<br>The enemy, who did you this ill service? |
| BUTLER | Be't who it will — a most low-hearted scoundrel,<br>Some vile [[Court\|court]]-minion must it be, some Spaniard,<br>Some young squire of some ancient family,<br>In whose light I may stand, some envious knave,<br>Stung to his [[Soul\|soul]] by my fair [[Self\|self]]-earned honours! |
| OCTAVIO | But tell me! Did the Duke approve that measure? |
| BUTLER | Himself impelled me to it, used his interest<br>In my behalf with all the warmth of [[Friendship\|friendship]]. |
| OCTAVIO | Ay? Are you sure of that? |
| BUTLER | I read the letter. |
| OCTAVIO | And so did I — but the contents were different.<br>By chance I'm in possession of that letter —<br>Can leave it to your own eyes to convince you. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He gives him the letter. |
| BUTLER | Ha! what is this? |
| OCTAVIO | I [[Fear\|fear]] me, Colonel Butler,<br>An infamous game have they been playing with you.<br>The Duke, you say, impelled you to this measure?<br>Now, in this letter talks he in contempt<br>Concerning you, counsels the Minister<br>To give sound chastisement to your conceit,<br>For so he calls it. |
| *(didascalia)* | [BUTLER reads through the letter, his knees tremble, he seizes a chair, and sinks down in it. |
| OCTAVIO | You have no enemy, no persecutor;<br>There's no one wishes ill to you. Ascribe<br>The insult you received to the Duke only.<br>His aim is clear and palpable. He wished<br>To tear you from your Emperor — he hoped<br>To gain from your [[Revenge\|revenge]] what he [[Well\|well]] knew<br>(What your long-tried fidelity convinced him)<br>He ne'er could dare expect from your calm reason.<br>A blind tool would he make you, in contempt<br>Use you, as means of most abandoned ends.<br>He has gained his point. Too [[Well\|well]] has he succeeded<br>In luring you away from that good path<br>On which you had been journeying forty years! |
| BUTLER | Can e'er the Emperor's Majesty forgive me? |
| OCTAVIO | More than forgive you. He would fain compensate<br>For that affront, and most unmerited grievance<br>Sustained by a deserving, gallant veteran.<br>From his free impulse he confirms the present,<br>Which the Duke made you for a wicked purpose.<br>The regiment, which you now command, is yours. |
| *(didascalia)* | [BUTLER attempts to rise, sinks down again. He labours inwardly with violent emotions; tries to speak, and cannot. At length he takes his [[Sword\|sword]] from the belt, and offers it to PICCOLOMINI. |
| OCTAVIO | What wish you? Recollect yourself, friend. |
| BUTLER | Take it. |
| OCTAVIO | But to what purpose? Calm yourself. |
| BUTLER | O take it!<br>I am no longer worthy of this [[Sword\|sword]]. |
| OCTAVIO | Receive it then anew from my hands — and<br>Wear it with [[Honour\|honour]] for the right cause ever. |
| BUTLER | —— Perjure myself to such a gracious Sovereign! |
| OCTAVIO | You'll make amends. Quick! break off from the Duke! |
| BUTLER | Break off from him! |
| OCTAVIO | What now? Bethink thyself. |
| BUTLER | Only break off from<br>him? — He dies! — he dies! |
| OCTAVIO | Come after me to Frauenberg, where now<br>All who are loyal are assembling under<br>Counts Altringer and Galas. Many others<br>I've brought to a remembrance of their duty.<br>This [[Night\|night]] be sure that you escape from Pilsen. |
| BUTLER | Count Piccolomini! Dare that man speak<br>Of [[Honour\|honour]] to you, who once broke his troth? |
| OCTAVIO | He, who repents so deeply of it, dares. |
| BUTLER | Then leave me here, upon my word of [[Honour\|honour]]! |
| OCTAVIO | What's your design? |
| BUTLER | Leave me and my regiment. |
| OCTAVIO | I have full confidence in you. But tell me<br>What are you brooding? |
| BUTLER | That the deed will tell you.<br>Ask me no more at present. Trust to me.<br>Ye may trust safely. By the living God<br>Ye give him over, not to his good angel!<br>Farewell. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit BUTLER. |
| SERVANT | A stranger left it, and is gone.<br>The Prince-Duke's horses wait for you below. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit Servant. |
| OCTAVIO | 'Be sure, make haste! Your faithful Isolan.'<br>— O that I had but left this town behind me.<br>To split upon a rock so near the haven! —<br>Away! This is no longer a safe place for me!<br>Where can my son be tarrying? |
