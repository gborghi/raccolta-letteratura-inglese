|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | THEKLA (hurries forward), COUNTESS, MAX PICCOLOMINI. |
| THEKLA | Spare yourself the trouble:<br>That hears he better from myself. |
| MAX | My Princess!<br>What have you let her hear me say, aunt Tertsky? |
| THEKLA | Has he been here long? |
| COUNTESS | Yes; and soon<br>must go.<br>Where have you stayed so long? |
| THEKLA | Alas! my mother<br>Wept so again! and I — I see her suffer,<br>Yet cannot keep myself from being happy. |
| MAX | Now once again I have [[Courage\|courage]] to look on you.<br>To-day at [[Noon\|noon]] I could not.<br>The dazzle of the jewels that play'd round you<br>Hid the beloved from me. |
| THEKLA | Then you saw me<br>With your eye only — and not with your heart? |
| MAX | This morning, when I found you in the circle<br>Of all your kindred, in your father's arms,<br>Beheld myself an alien in this circle,<br>O! what an impulse felt I in that moment<br>To fall upon his neck, to call him father!<br>But his stern eye o'erpowered the swelling passion —<br>It dared not but be silent. And those brilliants,<br>That like a [[Crown\|crown]] of [[Stars\|stars]] enwreathed your brows,<br>They scared me too! O wherefore, wherefore should he<br>At the first meeting spread as 'twere the ban<br>Of excommunication round you, wherefore<br>Dress up the angel as for sacrifice,<br>And cast upon the light and joyous heart<br>The mournful burthen of his station? Fitly<br>May [[Love\|love]] dare woo for [[Love\|love]]; but such a splendour<br>Might none but monarchs venture to approach. |
| THEKLA | Hush! not a word more of this mummery.<br>You see how soon the burthen is thrown off. |
| *(didascalia)* | [To the COUNTESS. |
| THEKLA | He is not in spirits. Wherefore is he not?<br>'Tis you, aunt, that have made him all so gloomy!<br>He had quite another [[Nature\|nature]] on the [[Journey\|journey]] —<br>So calm, so bright, so joyous eloquent. |
| *(didascalia)* | [To MAX. |
| THEKLA | It was my wish to see you always so,<br>And never otherwise! |
| MAX | You find yourself<br>In your great father's arms, belovéd lady!<br>All in a new world, which does homage to you,<br>And which, wer't only by its novelty,<br>Delights your eye. |
| THEKLA | Yes; I confess to you<br>That many things delight me here: this camp,<br>This motley stage of warriors, which renews<br>So manifold the image of my fancy,<br>And binds to life, binds to reality,<br>What hitherto had but been present to me<br>As a sweet dream! |
| MAX | Alas! not so to me.<br>It makes [[A Dream\|a dream]] of my reality.<br>Upon some island in the ethereal heights<br>I've lived for these last days. This mass of men<br>Forces me down to earth. It is a bridge<br>That, reconducting to my former life,<br>Divides me and my heaven. |
| THEKLA | The game of life<br>Looks cheerful, when one carries in one's heart<br>The inalienable treasure. 'Tis a game,<br>Which having once reviewed, I turn more joyous<br>Back to my deeper and appropriate bliss.<br>In this short [[Time\|time]] that I've been present here,<br>What new unheard-of things have I not seen!<br>And yet they all must give place to the [[Wonder\|wonder]]<br>Which this mysterious [[Castle\|castle]] guards. |
| COUNTESS | And what<br>Can this be then? Methought I was acquainted<br>With all the dusky corners of this [[house]]. |
| THEKLA | Ay, but the [[Road\|road]] thereto is watched by spirits,<br>Two griffins still stand sentry at the door. |
| *(didascalia)* | Countess (laughs). |
| COUNTESS | The astrological tower! — How happens it<br>That this same sanctuary, whose access<br>Is to all others so impracticable,<br>Opens before you even at your approach? |
| THEKLA | A dwarfish old man with a friendly face<br>And [[Snow\|snow]]-white hairs, whose gracious services<br>Were mine at first sight, opened me the doors. |
| MAX | That is the Duke's astrologer, old Seni. |
| THEKLA | He questioned me on many points; for instance,<br>When I was born, what month, and on what day,<br>Whether by day or in the [[Night\|night]]. |
| COUNTESS | He wished<br>To erect a figure for your horoscope. |
| THEKLA | My hand too he examined, shook his head<br>With much sad meaning, and the lines methought,<br>Did not square over truly with his wishes. |
| COUNTESS | [[Well]], Princess, and what found you in this tower?<br>My highest privilege has been to snatch<br>A side-glance, and away! |
| THEKLA | It was a strange<br>Sensation that came o'er me, when at first<br>From the broad sunshine I stepped in; and now<br>The narrowing line of day-light, that ran after<br>The closing door, was gone; and all about me<br>'Twas pale and dusky [[Night\|night]], with many shadows<br>Fantastically cast. Here six or seven<br>Colossal statues, and all kings, stood round me<br>In a half-circle. Each one in his hand<br>A sceptre bore, and on his head a star;<br>And in the tower no other light was there<br>But from these [[Stars\|stars]]: all seemed to come from them.<br>'These are the planets,' said that low old man,<br>'They govern worldly fates, and for that cause<br>Are imaged here as kings. He farthest from you,<br>Spiteful, and cold, an old man [[Melancholy\|melancholy]],<br>With bent and yellow forehead, he is [[Saturn]].<br>He opposite, the [[King\|king]] with the red light,<br>An arm'd man for the battle, that is Mars:<br>And both these bring but little luck to man.'<br>But at his side a lovely lady stood,<br>The star upon her head was soft and bright,<br>And that was [[Venus]], the bright star of joy.<br>On the left hand, lo! Mercury, with wings.<br>Quite in the middle glittered silver-bright<br>A cheerful man, and with a monarch's mien;<br>And this was Jupiter, my father's star:<br>And at his side I saw the Sun and [[Moon]]. |
| MAX | O never rudely will I blame his [[Faith\|faith]]<br>In the might of [[Stars\|stars]] and angels! 'Tis not merely<br>The human being's [[Pride]] that peoples space<br>With life and mystical predominance;<br>Since likewise for the stricken heart of [[Love]]<br>This visible [[Nature\|nature]], and this common world,<br>Is all too narrow: yea, a deeper import<br>Lurks in the legend told my infant years<br>Than lies upon that [[Truth\|truth]], we live to learn.<br>For [[Fable\|fable]] is [[Love]]'s world, his [[Home\|home]], his birth-place;<br>Delightedly dwells he 'mong fays and talismans,<br>And spirits; and delightedly believes<br>Divinities, being himself divine.<br>The intelligible forms of ancient poets,<br>The fair humanities of old religion,<br>The Power, the [[Beauty]], and the Majesty,<br>That had their haunts in dale, or piny mountain,<br>Or forest by slow stream, or pebbly [[spring]],<br>Or chasms and wat'ry depths; all these have vanished.<br>They live no longer in the [[Faith\|faith]] of reason!<br>But still the heart doth need a language, still<br>Doth the old instinct bring back the old names,<br>And to yon starry world they now are gone,<br>Spirits or gods, that used to share this earth<br>With man as with their friend; and to the [[Lover\|lover]]<br>Yonder they move, from yonder visible sky<br>Shoot influence down: and even at this day<br>'Tis Jupiter who brings whate'er is great,<br>And [[Venus]] who brings every thing that's fair! |
| THEKLA | And if this be the science of the [[Stars\|stars]],<br>I too, with glad and zealous industry,<br>Will learn acquaintance with this cheerful [[Faith\|faith]].<br>It is a gentle and affectionate thought,<br>That in immeasurable heights above us,<br>At our first birth, the wreath of [[Love\|love]] was woven,<br>With sparkling [[Stars\|stars]] for [[Flowers\|flowers]]. |
| COUNTESS | Not only roses,<br>But thorns too hath the heaven; and [[Well\|well]] for you<br>Leave they your wreath of [[Love\|love]] inviolate;<br>What [[Venus]] twined, the bearer of glad fortune,<br>The sullen orb of Mars soon [[Tears\|tears]] to pieces. |
| MAX | Soon will his gloomy empire reach its close.<br>Blest be the General's zeal: into the laurel<br>Will he inweave the olive-branch, presenting<br>Peace to the shouting nations. Then no wish<br>Will have remained for his great heart! Enough<br>Has he performed for glory, and can now<br>Live for himself and his. To his domains<br>Will he retire; he has a stately seat<br>Of fairest view at Gitschin; Reichenberg,<br>And Friedland [[Castle]], both lie pleasantly —<br>Even to the foot of the huge [[Mountains\|mountains]] here<br>Stretches the chase and covers of his forests:<br>His ruling passion, to create the splendid,<br>He can indulge without restraint; can give<br>A princely patronage to every art,<br>And to all worth a Sovereign's protection.<br>Can build, can plant, can watch the starry courses — |
| COUNTESS | Yet I would have you look, and look again,<br>Before you lay aside your arms, young friend!<br>A gentle bride, as she is, is [[Well\|well]] worth it,<br>That you should woo and win her with the [[Sword\|sword]]. |
| MAX | O, that the [[Sword\|sword]] could win her! |
| COUNTESS | What was that?<br>Did you hear nothing? Seem'd, as if I heard<br>Tumult and larum in the banquet-room. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Exit COUNTESS. |
