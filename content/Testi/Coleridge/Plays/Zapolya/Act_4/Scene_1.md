| *(didascalia)* | A stately room in LORD CASIMIR’S [[Castle|castle]]. Enter EMERICK and LASKA. |
| EMERICK | I do perceive thou hast a tender [[Conscience|conscience]],<br>Laska, in all things that concern thine own<br>Interest or safety. |
| LASKA | In this sovereign presence<br>I can [[Fear|fear]] nothing, but your dread displeasure. |
| EMERICK | Perchance, thou think’st it strange, that I of all men<br>Should covet thus the [[Love|love]] of fair Sarolta,<br>Dishonouring Casimir? |
| LASKA | Far be it from me!<br>Your Majesty’s [[Love|love]] and choice bring [[Honour|honour]] with them. |
| EMERICK | Perchance, thou hast heard that Casimir is my friend,<br>Fought for me, yea, for my sake, set at nought<br>A parent’s blessing; braved a father’s curse? |
| LASKA | (aside). Would I but knew now, what his Majesty meant!<br>Oh yes, Sire! ‘tis our common talk, how Lord<br>Kiuprili, my Lord’s father — |
| EMERICK | ‘Tis your talk,<br>Is it, good statesman Laska? |
| LASKA | No, not mine,<br>Not mine, an please your Majesty! There are<br>Some insolent malcontents indeed that talk thus —<br>Nay worse, mere treason. As Bathory’s son,<br>The [[Fool|fool]] that ran into the [[Monster|monster]]’s jaws. |
| EMERICK | [[Well]], ‘tis a loyal [[Monster|monster]] if he rids us<br>Of traitors! But art sure the youth’s devoured? |
| LASKA | Not a limb left, an please your Majesty!<br>And that unhappy girl — |
| EMERICK | Thou followed’st her<br>Into the wood?     [LASKA bows assent.<br>Henceforth then I’ll believe<br>That [[Jealousy|jealousy]] can make a hare a [[Lion|lion]]. |
| LASKA | Scarce had I got the first glimpse of her veil,<br>When, with a horrid roar that made the [[Leaves|leaves]]<br>Of the wood shake — |
| EMERICK | Made thee shake like a leaf! |
| LASKA | The war-wolf leapt; at the first plunge he seized her;<br>Forward I rushed! |
| EMERICK | Most marvellous! |
| LASKA | Hurled my javelin;<br>Which from his dragon-scales recoiling — |
| EMERICK | Enough!<br>And take, friend, this advice. When next thou tonguest it,<br>Hold constant to thy exploit with this [[Monster|monster]],<br>And leave untouched your common talk aforesaid,<br>What your Lord did, or should have done. |
| LASKA | My talk?<br>The saints forbid! I always said, for my part,<br>‘Was not the [[King|king]] Lord Casimir’s dearest friend?<br>Was not that friend a [[King|king]]? Whate’er he did<br>‘Twas all from pure [[Love|love]] to his Majesty.’ |
| EMERICK | And this then was thy talk? While knave and coward,<br>Both strong within thee, wrestle for the uppermost,<br>In slips the [[Fool|fool]] and takes the place of both.<br>Babbler! Lord Casimir did, as thou and all men.<br>He loved himself, loved honours, wealth, dominion.<br>All these were set upon a father’s head:<br>Good [[Truth|truth]]! a most unlucky accident!<br>For he but wished to hit the prize; not graze<br>The head that bore it: so with steady eye<br>Off flew the parricidal arrow. — Even<br>As Casimir loved Emerick, Emerick<br>Loves Casimir, intends him no dishonour.<br>He winked not then, for [[Love|love]] of me forsooth!<br>For [[Love|love]] of me now let him wink! Or if<br>The dame prove half as wise as she is fair,<br>He may still pass his hand, and find all smooth.<br>[Passing his hand across his brow. |
| LASKA | Your Majesty’s reasoning has convinced me. |
| EMERICK | Thee!<br>‘Tis [[Well|well]]! and more than meant. For by my [[Faith|faith]]<br>I had half forgotten thee. — Thou hast the key?   [LASKA bows.<br>And in your lady’s chamber there’s full space? |
| LASKA | Between the wall and arras to conceal you. |
| EMERICK | Here! This purse is but an earnest of thy fortune,<br>If thou prov’st faithful. But if thou betrayest me,<br>Hark you! — the wolf that shall drag thee to his den<br>Shall be no fiction.<br>[Exit EMERICK. LASKA manet with a key in one hand, and a purse in the other. |
| LASKA | [[Well]] then! here I stand,<br>Like Hercules, on either side a goddess.<br>Call this (looking at the purse)<br>Preferment; this (holding up the key) Fidelity!<br>And first my golden goddess: what bids she?<br>Only:—’This way, your Majesty! hush! The household<br>Are all safe lodged.’ — Then, put Fidelity<br>Within her proper wards, just turn her round —<br>So — the door opens — and for all the rest,<br>‘Tis the [[King|king]]’s deed, not Laska’s. Do but this<br>And—’I’m the mere earnest of your future fortunes.’<br>But what says the other? — Whisper on! I hear you!<br>[Putting the key to his ear.<br>All very true! — but, good Fidelity!<br>If I refuse [[King]] Emerick, will you promise,<br>And swear now, to unlock the dungeon door,<br>And save me from the hangman? Aye! you’re silent!<br>What, not a word in answer? A clear nonsuit!<br>Now for one look to see that all are lodged<br>At the due distance — then — yonder lies the [[Road|road]]<br>For Laska and his royal friend, [[King]] Emerick!<br>[Exit LASKA. Then enter BATHORY and BETHLEN. |
| BETHLEN | He looked as if he were some God disguised<br>In an old warrior’s venerable shape<br>To guard and guide my mother. Is there not<br>Chapel or oratory in this mansion? |
| OLD BATHORY | Even so. |
| BETHLEN | From that place then am I to take<br>A helm and breast-plate, both inlaid with gold,<br>And the good [[Sword|sword]] that once was Raab Kiuprili’s. |
| OLD BATHORY | Those very arms this day Sarolta show’d me —<br>With wistful look. I’m lost in wild conjectures! |
| BETHLEN | O tempt me not, e’en with a wandering guess,<br>To break the first command a mother’s will<br>Imposed, a mother’s voice made known to me!<br>‘Ask not, my son,’ said she, ‘our names or thine.<br>The [[Shadow|shadow]] of the eclipse is passing off<br>The full orb of thy destiny! Already<br>The victor Crescent glitters forth and sheds<br>O’er the yet lingering haze a [[Phantom|phantom]] light.<br>Thou canst not hasten it! Leave then to Heaven<br>The work of Heaven: and with a silent spirit<br>Sympathize with the powers that work in [[Silence|silence]]!’<br>Thus spake she, and she looked as she were then<br>Fresh from some heavenly vision!<br>[Re-enter LASKA, not perceiving them. |
| LASKA | All asleep!<br>[Then observing BETHLEN, stands in idiot-affright.<br>I must speak to it first — Put — put the question!<br>I’ll confess all!        [Stammering with [[Fear|fear]]. |
| OLD BATHORY | Laska! what ails thee, man? |
| LASKA | (pointing to Bethlen). There! |
| OLD BATHORY | I see nothing! where? |
| LASKA | He does not see it!<br>Bethlen, torment me not! |
| BETHLEN | Soft! Rouse him gently!<br>He hath outwatched his hour, and half asleep,<br>With eyes half open, mingles sight with [[Dreams|dreams]]. |
| OLD BATHORY | Ho! Laska! Don’t you know us! ‘tis Bathory<br>And Bethlen! |
| LASKA | Good now! Ha! ha! An excellent trick.<br>Afraid? Nay, no offence! But I must laugh.<br>But are you sure now, that ‘tis you, yourself? |
| BETHLEN | Would’st be convinced? |
| LASKA | No nearer, pray! consider!<br>If it should prove his [[Ghost|ghost]], the touch would freeze me<br>To a tombstone. No nearer! |
| BETHLEN | The [[Fool|fool]] is drunk! |
| LASKA | [[Well]] now! I [[Love|love]] a brave man to my heart.<br>I myself braved the [[Monster|monster]], and would fain<br>Have saved the false one from the fate she tempted. |
| OLD BATHORY | You, Laska? |
| BETHLEN | (to Bathory).    Mark! Heaven grant it may be so!<br>Glycine? |
| LASKA | She! I traced her by the voice.<br>You’ll scarce believe me, when I say I heard<br>The close of a [[Song|song]]: the poor wretch had been singing:<br>As if she wished to compliment the war-wolf<br>At once with music and a meal! |
| BETHLEN | (to Bathory).   Mark that! |
| LASKA | At the next moment I beheld her running,<br>Wringing her hands with, ‘Bethlen! O poor Bethlen!’<br>I almost [[Fear|fear]], the sudden noise I made,<br>Rushing impetuous through the brake, alarmed her.<br>She stopt, then mad with [[Fear|fear]], turned round and ran<br>Into the [[Monster|monster]]’s gripe. One piteous scream<br>I heard. There was no second — I — |
| BETHLEN | Stop there!<br>We’ll spare your modesty! Who dares not [[Honour|honour]]<br>Laska’s brave tongue, and high heroic fancy? |
| LASKA | You too, Sir Knight, have come back safe and sound!<br>You played the [[Hero|hero]] at a cautious distance!<br>Or was it that you sent the poor girl forward<br>To stay the [[Monster|monster]]’s stomach? Dainties quickly<br>Pall on the taste and cloy the appetite! |
| OLD BATHORY | Laska, beware! Forget not what thou art!<br>Should’st thou but dream thou’rt valiant, [[Cross|cross]] thyself!<br>And ache all over at the dangerous fancy! |
| LASKA | What then! you swell upon my lady’s favour,<br>High Lords and perilous of one day’s growth!<br>But other judges now sit on the bench!<br>And haply, Laska hath found audience there,<br>Where to defend the treason of a son<br>Might end in lifting up both son and father<br>Still higher; to a height from which indeed<br>You both may drop, but, spite of fate and fortune,<br>Will be secured from falling to the ground.<br>‘Tis possible too, young man! that royal Emerick,<br>At Laska’s rightful suit, may make inquiry<br>By whom seduced, the maid so strangely missing — |
| BETHLEN | Soft! my good Laska! might it not suffice,<br>If to yourself, being Lord Casimir’s steward,<br>I should make record of Glycine’s fate? |
| LASKA | ‘Tis [[Well|well]]! it shall content me! though your [[Fear|fear]]<br>Has all the credit of these lowered tones.<br>First we demand the manner of her [[Death|death]]? |
| BETHLEN | Nay! that’s superfluous! Have you not just told us,<br>That you yourself, led by impetuous valour,<br>Witnessed the whole? My tale’s of later date.<br>After the fate, from which your valour strove<br>In vain to rescue the rash maid, I saw her! |
| LASKA | Glycine? |
| BETHLEN | Nay! Dare I accuse wise Laska,<br>Whose words find access to a monarch’s ear,<br>Of a base, braggart lie? It must have been<br>Her spirit that appeared to me. But haply<br>I come too late? It has itself delivered<br>Its own commission to you? |
| OLD BATHORY | ‘Tis most likely!<br>And the [[Ghost|ghost]] doubtless vanished, when we entered<br>And found brave Laska staring wide — at nothing! |
| LASKA | ‘Tis [[Well|well]]! You’ve ready wits! I shall report them,<br>With all due [[Honour|honour]], to his Majesty!<br>Treasure them up, I pray! A certain person,<br>Whom the [[King|king]] flatters with his confidence,<br>Tells you, his royal friend asks startling questions!<br>‘Tis but a hint! And now what says the [[Ghost|ghost]]! |
| BETHLEN | Listen! for thus it spake: ‘Say thou to Laska,<br>Glycine, knowing all thy thoughts engrossed<br>In thy new office of [[King|king]]’s [[Fool|fool]] and knave,<br>Foreseeing thou’lt forget with thine own hand<br>To make due penance for the wrongs thou’st caused her,<br>For thy [[Soul|soul]]’s safety, doth consent to take it<br>From Bethlen’s cudgel’ — thus.   [Beats him off.<br>Off! scoundrel! off!<br>[LASKA runs away. |
| OLD BATHORY | The sudden swelling of this shallow dastard<br>Tells of a recent [[Storm|storm]]: the first disruption<br>Of the black cloud that hangs and threatens o’er us. |
| BETHLEN | E’en this reproves my loitering. Say where lies<br>The oratory? |
| OLD BATHORY | Ascend yon flight of stairs!<br>Midway the corridor a silver lamp<br>Hangs o’er the entrance of Sarolta’s chamber,<br>And facing it, the low arched oratory!<br>Me thou’lt find watching at the outward gate:<br>For a petard might burst the bars, unheard<br>By the drenched porter, and Sarolta hourly<br>Expects Lord Casimir, spite of Emerick’s message! |
| BETHLEN | There I will meet you! And till then good-[[Night|night]]!<br>Dear good old man, good-[[Night|night]]! |
| OLD BATHORY | O yet one moment!<br>What I repelled, when it did seem my own,<br>I cling to, now ‘tis parting — call me father!<br>It can not now mislead thee. O my son,<br>Ere yet our tongues have learnt another name,<br>Bethlen! — say ‘Father’ to me! |
| BETHLEN | Now, and for ever<br>My father! other sire than thou, on earth<br>I never had, a dearer could not have!<br>From the base earth you raised me to your arms,<br>And I would leap from off a throne, and kneeling,<br>Ask Heaven’s blessing from thy lips. My father! |
| BATHORY | Go! Go!      [Exit BETHLEN.<br>May every star now shining over us,<br>Be as an angel’s eye, to watch and guard him!  [Exit BATHORY. |
| *(didascalia)* | Scene changes to a splendid Bed-chamber, hung with tapestry. |
| *(didascalia)* | SAROLTA and an Attendant. |
| ATTENDANT | We all did [[Love|love]] her, madam! |
| SAROLTA | She deserved it!<br>Luckless Glycine! rash, unhappy girl!<br>‘Twas the first [[Time|time]] she e’er deceived me. |
| ATTENDANT | She was in [[Love|love]], and had she not died thus,<br>With grief for Bethlen’s loss, and [[Fear|fear]] of Laska,<br>She would have pined herself to [[Death|death]] at [[Home|home]]. |
| SAROLTA | Has the youth’s father come back from his search? |
| ATTENDANT | He never will, I [[Fear|fear]] me. O dear lady!<br>That Laska did so triumph o’er the old man —<br>It was quite cruel—’You’ll be sure,’ said he,<br>‘To meet with part at least of your son Bethlen,<br>Or the war-wolf must have a quick digestion!<br>Go! Search the wood by all means! Go! I pray you!’ |
| SAROLTA | Inhuman wretch! |
| ATTENDANT | And old Bathory answered<br>With a sad smile, ‘It is a [[Witch|witch]]’s [[prayer]],<br>And may Heaven read it backwards.’ Though she was rash,<br>‘Twas a small fault for such a punishment! |
| SAROLTA | Nay! ‘twas my grief, and not my anger spoke.<br>Small fault indeed! but leave me, my poor girl!<br>I feel a weight that only [[prayer]] can lighten.<br>[Exit Attendant.<br>O they were [[Innocent|innocent]], and yet have perished<br>In their May of life; and Vice grows old in triumph.<br>Is it [[Mercy]]’s hand, that for the bad man holds<br>Life’s closing gate? ——<br>Still passing thence petitionary Hours<br>To woo the obdurate spirit to repentance?<br>Or would this dullness tell me, that there is<br>[[Guilt]] too enormous to be duly punished,<br>Save by increase of [[Guilt|guilt]]? The Powers of Evil<br>Are jealous claimants. [[Guilt]] too hath its ordeal,<br>And Hell its own probation! — Merciful Heaven,<br>Rather than this, pour down upon thy suppliant<br>Disease, and agony, and comfortless want!<br>O send us forth to wander on, unsheltered!<br>Make our food bitter with despiséd [[Tears|tears]]!<br>Let viperous scorn hiss at us as we pass!<br>Yea, let us sink down at our enemy’s gate,<br>And beg [[Forgiveness|forgiveness]] and a morsel of bread!<br>With all the heaviest worldly visitations<br>Let the dire father’s curse that hovers o’er us<br>Work out its dread fulfilment, and the spirit<br>Of wronged Kiuprili be appeased. But only,<br>Only, O merciful in vengeance! let not<br>That plague turn inward on my Casimir’s [[Soul|soul]]!<br>Scare thence the fiend [[Ambition]], and restore him<br>To his own heart! O save him! Save my husband!<br>[During the latter part of this speech EMERICK comes forward from his hiding-place. SAROLTA seeing him, without recognising him.<br>In such a shape a father’s curse should come. |
| EMERICK | (advancing). [[Fear]] not. |
| SAROLTA | Who art thou? Robber? Traitor? |
| EMERICK | Friend!<br>Who in good hour hath startled these dark fancies,<br>Rapacious traitors, that would fain depose<br>Joy, [[Love|love]], and [[Beauty|beauty]], from their natural thrones:<br>Those lips, those angel eyes, that regal forehead. |
| SAROLTA | Strengthen me, Heaven! I must not seem afraid!<br>[Aside.<br>The [[King|king]] to-[[Night|night]] then deigns to play the masker.<br>What seeks your Majesty? |
| EMERICK | Sarolta’s [[Love|love]];<br>And Emerick’s power lies prostrate at her feet. |
| SAROLTA | Heaven guard the sovereign’s power from such debasement!<br>Far rather, Sire, let it descend in vengeance<br>On the base [[Villain|villain]], on the faithless slave<br>Who dared unbar the doors of these retirements!<br>For whom? Has Casimir deserved this insult?<br>O my misgiving heart! If — if — from Heaven<br>Yet not from you, Lord Emerick! |
| EMERICK | Chiefly from me.<br>Has he not like an ingrate robbed my [[Court|court]]<br>Of [[Beauty]]’s star, and kept my heart in darkness?<br>First then on him I will administer justice —<br>If not in [[Mercy|mercy]], yet in [[Love|love]] and rapture.<br>[Seizes her. |
| SAROLTA | Help! Treason! Help! |
| EMERICK | Call louder! Scream again!<br>Here’s none can hear you! |
| SAROLTA | Hear me, hear me, Heaven! |
| EMERICK | Nay, why this rage? Who best deserves you? Casimir,<br>Emerick’s bought implement, the jealous slave<br>That mews you up with bolts and bars? or Emerick<br>Who proffers you a throne? Nay, mine you shall be.<br>Hence with this fond resistance! Yield; then live<br>This month a widow, and the next a [[Queen|queen]]! |
| SAROLTA | Yet, yet for one brief moment    [Struggling.<br>Unhand me, I conjure you.<br>[She throws him off, and rushes towards a toilet. EMERICK follows, and as she takes a dagger, he grasps it in her hand. |
| EMERICK | Ha! Ha! a dagger;<br>A seemly ornament for a lady’s casket!<br>‘Tis held, devotion is akin to [[Love|love]],<br>But yours is tragic! [[Love]] in war! It charms me,<br>And makes your [[Beauty|beauty]] worth a [[King|king]]’s embraces!<br>[During this speech BETHLEN enters armed. |
| BETHLEN | Ruffian, forbear! Turn, turn and front my [[Sword|sword]]! |
| EMERICK | Pish! who is this? |
| SAROLTA | O sleepless eye of Heaven!<br>A blest, a blessed spirit! Whence camest thou?<br>May I still call thee Bethlen? |
| BETHLEN | Ever, lady,<br>Your faithful soldier! |
| EMERICK | Insolent slave! Depart<br>Know’st thou not me? |
| BETHLEN | I know thou art a [[Villain|villain]]<br>And coward! That thy devilish purpose marks thee!<br>What else, this lady must instruct my [[Sword|sword]]! |
| SAROLTA | [[Monster]], retire! O touch him not, thou blest one!<br>This is the hour that fiends and damnéd spirits<br>Do walk the earth, and take what form they list!<br>Yon devil hath assumed a [[King|king]]’s! |
| BETHLEN | Usurped it! |
| EMERICK | The [[King|king]] will play the devil with thee indeed!<br>But that I mean to hear thee howl on the rack,<br>I would debase this [[Sword|sword]], and lay thee prostrate<br>At this thy paramour’s feet; then drag her forth<br>Stained with adulterous [[Blood|blood]], and —<br>— mark you, traitress!<br>Strumpeted first, then turned adrift to beggary!<br>Thou prayed’st for’t too. |
| SAROLTA | Thou art so fiendish wicked,<br>That in thy blasphemies I scarce hear thy threats! |
| BETHLEN | Lady, be calm! [[Fear|fear]] not this [[King|king]] of the buskin!<br>A [[King|king]]? Oh laughter! A [[King|king]] Bajazet!<br>That from some vagrant actor’s tiring-room,<br>Hath stolen at once his speech and [[Crown|crown]]! |
| EMERICK | Ah! treason!<br>Thou hast been lessoned and tricked up for this!<br>As surely as the wax on thy [[Death|death]]-warrant<br>Shall take the impression of this royal signet,<br>So plain thy face hath ta’en the mask of [[Rebel|rebel]]!<br>[BETHLEN seizes EMERICK’S hand and eagerly observes the signet. |
| BETHLEN | It must be so! ‘Tis e’en the counterpart!<br>But with a foul usurping cypher on it!<br>The light hath flashed from Heaven, and I must follow it!<br>O curst [[Usurper|usurper]]! O thou brother-murderer!<br>That mad’st a star-bright [[Queen|queen]] a fugitive widow!<br>Who fill’st the land with curses, being thyself<br>All curses in one [[Tyrant|tyrant]]! see and tremble!<br>This is Kiuprili’s [[Sword|sword]] that now hangs o’er thee!<br>Kiuprili’s blasting curse, that from its point<br>Shoots lightnings at thee. Hark! in Andreas’ name,<br>Heir of his vengeance, hell-hound! I defy thee.<br>[They fight, and just as EMERICK is disarmed, in rush CASIMIR, OLD BATHORY, and Attendants. CASIMIR runs in between the combatants, and parts them; in the struggle BETHLEN’S [[Sword|sword]] is thrown down. |
| CASIMIR | The [[King|king]]! disarmed too by a stranger! Speak!<br>What may this mean? |
| EMERICK | Deceived, dishonored lord!<br>Ask thou yon fair adultress! She will tell thee<br>A tale, which would’st thou be both dupe and traitor,<br>Thou wilt believe against thy friend and sovereign!<br>Thou art present now, and a friend’s duty ceases:<br>To thine own justice leave I thine own wrongs.<br>Of half thy vengeance I perforce must rob thee,<br>For that the sovereign claims. To thy allegiance<br>I now commit this traitor and assassin.<br>[Then to the Attendants.<br>Hence with him to the dungeon! and to-morrow,<br>Ere the sun rises, — Hark! your heads or his! |
| BETHLEN | Can Hell work miracles to mock Heaven’s justice? |
| EMERICK | Who speaks to him dies! The traitor that has menaced<br>His [[King|king]], must not pollute the breathing air,<br>Even with a word! |
| CASIMIR | (to Bathory). Hence with him to the dungeon!<br>[Exit BETHLEN, hurried off by BATHORY and Attendants. |
| EMERICK | We hunt to-morrow in your upland forest:<br>Thou (to Casimir) wilt attend us: and wilt then explain<br>This sudden and most fortunate arrival.<br>[Exit EMERICK; Manent CASIMIR and SAROLTA. |
| SAROLTA | My lord! my husband! look whose [[Sword|sword]] lies yonder!<br>It is Kiuprili’s, Casimir; ‘tis thy father’s!<br>And wielded by a stripling’s arm, it baffled,<br>Yea, fell like Heaven’s own lightnings on that [[Tarquin]]. |
| CASIMIR | Hush! hush!<br>I had detected ere I left the [[City|city]]<br>The [[Tyrant|tyrant]]’s curst intent. Lewd, damnéd ingrate!<br>For him did I bring down a father’s curse!<br>Swift, swift must be our means! To-morrow’s sun<br>Sets on his fate or mine! O blest Sarolta!<br>No other [[prayer]], late penitent, dare I offer,<br>But that thy spotless virtues may prevail<br>O’er Casimir’s crimes, and dread Kiuprili’s curse!<br>[Exeunt. |
