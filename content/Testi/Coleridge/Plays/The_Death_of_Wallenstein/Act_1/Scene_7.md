| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, TERTSKY. |
| WALLENSTEIN *(stepping to the [[Window\|window]])* | What now, then? |
| TERTSKY | There are strange movements among all the troops,<br>And no one knows the cause. Mysteriously,<br>With gloomy silentness, the several corps<br>Marshal themselves, each under its own banners.<br>Tiefenbach's corps makes threatening movements; only<br>The Pappenheimers still remain aloof<br>In their own quarters, and let no one enter. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Does Piccolomini appear among them? |
| TERTSKY | We are seeking him: he is no where to be met with. |
| WALLENSTEIN | What did the Aid-de-Camp deliver to you? |
| TERTSKY | My regiments had dispatched him; yet once more<br>They swear fidelity to thee, and wait<br>The shout for onset, all prepared, and eager. |
| WALLENSTEIN | But whence arose this larum in the camp?<br>It should have been kept secret from the army,<br>Till fortune had decided for us at Prague. |
| TERTSKY | O that thou hadst believed me! Yester evening<br>Did we conjure thee not to let that skulker,<br>That fox, Octavio, pass the gates of Pilsen.<br>Thou gav'st him thy own horses to flee from thee. |
| WALLENSTEIN | The old tune still! Now, once for all, no more<br>Of this suspicion — it is doting folly. |
| TERTSKY | Thou did'st confide in Isolani too;<br>And lo! he was the first that did [[Desert\|desert]] thee. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It was but yesterday I rescued him<br>From abject wretchedness. Let that go by.<br>I never reckon'd yet on gratitude.<br>And wherein doth he wrong in going from me?<br>He follows still the god whom all his life<br>He has worshipped at the gaming table. With<br>My Fortune, and my seeming destiny,<br>He made the bond, and broke it not with me.<br>I am but the [[Ship\|ship]] in which his hopes were stowed,<br>And with the which [[Well\|well]]-pleased and confident<br>He traversed the open sea; now he beholds it<br>In imminent jeopardy among the coast-rocks,<br>And hurries to preserve his wares. As light<br>As the free bird from the hospitable twig<br>Where it had nested, he flies off from me:<br>No human tie is snapped betwixt us two.<br>Yea, he deserves to find himself deceived,<br>Who seeks a heart in the unthinking man.<br>Like shadows on a stream, the forms of life<br>Impress their characters on the smooth forehead,<br>Nought sinks into the bosom's silent depth:<br>Quick sensibility of [[Pain\|pain]] and pleasure<br>Moves the light fluids lightly; but no [[Soul\|soul]]<br>Warmeth the inner frame. |
| TERTSKY | Yet, would I rather<br>Trust the smooth brow than that deep furrowed one. |
