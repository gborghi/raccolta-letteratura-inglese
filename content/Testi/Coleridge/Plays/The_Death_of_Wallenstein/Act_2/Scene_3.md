| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, TERTSKY, ILLO, Ten Cuirassiers (led by an Anspessade, march up and arrange themselves, after the word of command, in one front before the DUKE, and make their obeisance. He takes his hat off, and immediately covers himself again). |
| ANSPESSADE | Halt! Front! Present! |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (after he has run through them with his eye, to the Anspessade). |
| WALLENSTEIN | I know thee [[Well\|well]]. Thou art out of Brüggin in Flanders:<br>Thy name is [[Mercy]]. |
| ANSPESSADE | Henry [[Mercy]]. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Thou wert cut off on the march, surrounded<br>by the Hessians, and didst fight thy way with a hundred and<br>eighty men through their thousand. |
| ANSPESSADE | 'Twas even so, General! |
| WALLENSTEIN | What reward hadst thou for this gallant exploit? |
| ANSPESSADE | That which I asked for: the [[Honour\|honour]] to serve<br>in this corps. |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (turning to a second). |
| WALLENSTEIN | Thou wert among the<br>volunteers that seized and made booty of the Swedish battery<br>at Altenburg. |
| SECOND CUIRASSIER | Yes, General! |
| WALLENSTEIN | I forget no one with whom I have exchanged<br>words. (A pause). Who sends you? |
| ANSPESSADE | Your noble regiment, the Cuirassiers of<br>Piccolomini. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Why does not your colonel deliver in your<br>request, according to the custom of service? |
| ANSPESSADE | Because we would first know whom we serve. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Begin your address. |
| ANSPESSADE | *(giving the word of command)* Shoulder your arms! |
| *(didascalia)* | Wallenstein (turning to a third). |
| WALLENSTEIN | Thy name is Risbeck, Cologne<br>is thy birthplace. |
| THIRD CUIRASSIER | Risbeck of Cologne. |
| WALLENSTEIN | It was thou that broughtest in the Swedish<br>colonel, Diebald, prisoner, in the camp at Nuremberg. |
| THIRD CUIRASSIER | It was not I, General! |
| WALLENSTEIN | Perfectly right! It was thy elder brother:<br>thou hadst a younger brother too: Where did he stay? |
| THIRD CUIRASSIER | He is stationed at Olmutz with the<br>Imperial army. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Now then — begin. |
| ANSPESSADE | There came to hand a letter from the Emperor<br>Commanding us —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | Who chose you? |
| ANSPESSADE | Every company<br>Drew its own man by lot. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Now! to the business. |
| ANSPESSADE | There came to hand a letter from the Emperor<br>Commanding us collectively, from thee<br>All duties of obedience to withdraw,<br>Because thou wert an enemy and traitor. |
| WALLENSTEIN | And what did you determine? |
| ANSPESSADE | All our comrades<br>At Brannau, Budweiss, Prague and Olmutz, have<br>Obeyed already, and the regiments here,<br>Tiefenbach and Toscana, instantly<br>Did follow their example. But — but we<br>Do not believe that thou art an enemy<br>And traitor to thy country, hold it merely<br>For lie and trick, and a trumped-up Spanish story!<br>Thyself shalt tell us what thy purpose is,<br>For we have found thee still sincere and true:<br>No mouth shall interpose itself betwixt<br>The gallant General and the gallant troops. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Therein I recognize my Pappenheimers. |
| ANSPESSADE | And this proposal makes thy regiment to thee:<br>Is it thy purpose merely to preserve<br>In thy own hands this military sceptre,<br>Which so becomes thee, which the Emperor<br>Made over to thee by a covenant?<br>Is it thy purpose merely to remain<br>Supreme commander of the Austrian armies? —<br>We will stand by thee, General! and guarantee<br>Thy honest rights against all opposition.<br>And should it chance, that all the other regiments<br>Turn from thee, by ourselves will we stand forth<br>Thy faithful soldiers, and, as is our duty,<br>Far rather let ourselves be cut to pieces,<br>Than suffer thee to fall. But if it be<br>As the Emperor's letter says, if it be true,<br>That thou in traitorous wise wilt lead us over<br>To the enemy, which God in heaven forbid!<br>Then we too will forsake thee, and obey<br>That letter —— |
| WALLENSTEIN | Hear me, children! |
| ANSPESSADE | Yes, or no!<br>There needs no other answer. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Yield attention.<br>You're men of sense, examine for yourselves;<br>Ye think, and do not follow with the herd:<br>And therefore have I always shewn you [[Honour\|honour]]<br>Above all others, suffered you to reason;<br>Have treated you as free men, and my orders<br>Were but the echoes of your prior suffrage. — |
| ANSPESSADE | Most fair and noble has thy conduct been<br>To us, my General! With thy confidence<br>Thou hast honoured us, and shewn us grace and favour<br>Beyond all other regiments; and thou seest<br>We follow not the common herd. We will<br>Stand by thee faithfully. Speak but one word —<br>Thy word shall satisfy us, that it is not<br>A treason which thou meditatest — that<br>Thou meanest not to lead the army over<br>To the enemy; nor e'er betray thy country. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Me, me are they betraying. The Emperor<br>Hath sacrificed me to my enemies,<br>And I must fall, unless my gallant troops<br>Will rescue me. See! I confide in you.<br>And be your hearts my strong hold! At this breast<br>The aim is taken, at this hoary head.<br>This is your Spanish gratitude, this is our<br>Requital for that murderous fight at Lutzen!<br>For this we threw the naked breast against<br>The halbert, made for this the frozen earth<br>Our bed, and the hard stone our pillow! never stream<br>Too rapid for us, nor wood too impervious:<br>With cheerful spirit we pursued that Mansfield<br>Through all the turns and windings of his flight;<br>Yea, our whole life was but one restless march;<br>And homeless, as the stirring [[Wind\|wind]], we travelled<br>O'er the war-wasted earth. And now, even now,<br>That we have [[Well\|well]]-nigh finished the hard toil,<br>The unthankful, the curse-laden toil of weapons,<br>With faithful indefatigable arm<br>Have rolled the heavy war-load up the [[Hill\|hill]],<br>Behold! this boy of the Emperor's bears away<br>The honours of the peace, an easy prize!<br>He'll weave, forsooth, into his flaxen locks<br>The olive branch, the hard-earn'd ornament<br>Of this grey head, grown grey beneath the helmet. |
| ANSPESSADE | That shall he not, while we can hinder it!<br>No one, but thou, who hast conducted it<br>With fame, shall end this war, this frightful war.<br>Thou led'st us out into the bloody field<br>Of [[Death\|death]], thou and no other shalt conduct us [[Home\|home]],<br>Rejoicing, to the lovely plains of peace —<br>Shalt share with us the fruits of the long toil — |
| WALLENSTEIN | What? Think you then at length in late old age<br>To enjoy the fruits of toil? Believe it not.<br>Never, no never, will you see the end<br>Of the contest! you and me, and all of us,<br>This war will [[Swallow\|swallow]] up! War, war, not peace,<br>Is Austria's wish; and therefore, because I<br>Endeavoured after peace, therefore I fall.<br>For what cares Austria, how long the war<br>Wears out the armies and lays waste the world?<br>She will but wax and grow amid the ruin,<br>And still win new domains. |
| *(didascalia)* | [The Cuirassiers express agitation by their gestures. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Ye're moved — I see<br>A noble rage flash from your eyes, ye warriors!<br>Oh that my spirit might possess you now<br>Daring as once it led you to the battle!<br>Ye would stand by me with your veteran arms,<br>Protect me in my rights; and this is noble!<br>But think not that you can accomplish it,<br>Your scanty number! to no purpose will you<br>Have sacrificed you for your General.<br>No! let us tread securely, seek for friends;<br>The Swedes have proffered us assistance, let us<br>Wear for a while the appearance of good will,<br>And use them for your profit, till we both<br>Carry the fate of Europe in our hands,<br>And from our camp to the glad jubilant world<br>Lead Peace forth with the garland on her head! |
| ANSPESSADE | 'Tis then but mere appearances which thou<br>Dost put on with the Swede? Thou'lt not betray<br>The Emperor? Wilt not turn us into Swedes?<br>This is the only thing which we [[Desire\|desire]]<br>To learn from thee. |
| WALLENSTEIN | What care I for the Swedes?<br>I hate them as I hate the pit of hell,<br>And under [[Providence]] I trust right soon<br>To chase them to their homes across their Baltic.<br>My cares are only for the whole: I have<br>A heart — it bleeds within me for the miseries<br>And piteous groaning of my fellow-Germans.<br>Ye are but common men, but yet ye think<br>With minds not common; ye appear to me<br>Worthy before all others, that I whisper ye<br>A little word or two in confidence!<br>See now! already for full fifteen years<br>The war-torch has continued burning, yet<br>No rest, no pause of conflict. Swede and German,<br>Papist and Lutheran! neither will give way<br>To the other, every hand's against the other.<br>Each one is party and no one a judge.<br>Where shall this end? Where's he that will unravel<br>This tangle, ever tangling more and more.<br>It must be cut asunder.<br>I feel that I am the man of destiny,<br>And trust, with your assistance, to accomplish it. |
