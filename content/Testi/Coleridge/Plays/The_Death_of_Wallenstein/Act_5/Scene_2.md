| Chi parla | Battuta |
|---|---|
| *(didascalia)* | WALLENSTEIN, GORDON. |
| WALLENSTEIN | All quiet in the town? |
| GORDON | The town is quiet. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I hear a boisterous music! and the [[Castle]]<br>Is lighted up. Who are the revellers? |
| GORDON | There is a banquet given at the [[Castle]]<br>To the Count Tertsky, and Field Marshal Illo. |
| WALLENSTEIN | In [[Honour\|honour]] of the victory. — This tribe<br>Can shew their joy in nothing else but feasting. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Rings. The Groom of the Chamber enters. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Unrobe me. I will lay me down to [[Sleep\|sleep]]. |
| *(didascalia)* | [WALLENSTEIN takes the keys from GORDON. |
| WALLENSTEIN | So we are guarded from all enemies,<br>And shut in with sure friends. |
| WALLENSTEIN | For all must cheat me, or a face like this |
| *(didascalia)* | [Fixing his eye on GORDON. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Was ne'er a hypocrite's mask. |
| *(didascalia)* | [The Groom of the Chamber takes off his mantle, collar<br>and scarf. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Take care — what is that? |
| GROOM OF THE CHAMBER | The golden chain is snapped in two. |
| WALLENSTEIN | [[Well]], it has lasted long enough. Here — give it. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He takes and looks at the chain. |
| WALLENSTEIN | 'Twas the first present of the Emperor.<br>He hung it round me in the war of Friule,<br>He being then Archduke; and I have worn it<br>Till now from habit ——<br>From superstition if you will. Belike,<br>It was to be a talisman to me,<br>And while I wore it on my neck in [[Faith\|faith]],<br>It was to chain to me all my life long<br>The volatile fortune whose first pledge it was.<br>[[Well]], be it so! Henceforward a new fortune<br>Must [[spring]] up for me; for the potency<br>Of this charm is dissolved. |
| *(didascalia)* | [Groom of the Chamber retires with the vestments.<br>WALLENSTEIN rises, takes a stride across the<br>room, and stands at last before GORDON in a<br>posture of meditation. |
| WALLENSTEIN | How the old [[Time\|time]] returns upon me! I<br>Behold myself once more at Burgau, where<br>We two were pages of the [[Court]] together.<br>We oftentimes disputed: thy intention<br>Was ever good; but thou wert wont to play<br>The moralist and preacher, and would'st rail at me<br>That I strove after things too high for me,<br>Giving my [[Faith\|faith]] to bold unlawful [[Dreams\|dreams]],<br>And still extol to me the golden mean.<br>— Thy wisdom hath been proved a thriftless friend<br>To thy own [[Self\|self]]. See, it has made thee early<br>A superannuated man, and (but<br>That my munificent [[Stars\|stars]] will intervene)<br>Would let thee in some miserable corner<br>Go out like an untended lamp. |
| GORDON | My Prince!<br>With light heart the poor fisher moors his boat,<br>And watches from the shore the lofty [[Ship\|ship]]<br>Stranded amid the [[Storm\|storm]]. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Art thou already<br>In harbour then, old man? [[Well]]! I am not.<br>The unconquered spirit drives me o'er life's billows;<br>My planks still firm, my canvas swelling proudly.<br>[[Hope]] is my goddess still, and youth my inmate;<br>And while we stand thus front to front almost,<br>I might presume to say, that the swift years<br>Have passed by powerless o'er my unblanched hair. |
| *(didascalia)* | [He moves with long strides across the saloon, and<br>remains on the opposite side over against<br>GORDON. |
| WALLENSTEIN | Who now persists in calling Fortune false?<br>To me she has proved faithful, with fond [[Love\|love]]<br>Took me from out the common ranks of men,<br>And like a mother goddess, with strong arm<br>Carried me swiftly up the steps of life.<br>Nothing is common in my destiny,<br>Nor in the furrows of my hand. Who dares<br>Interpret then my life for me as 'twere<br>One of the undistinguishable many?<br>True in this present moment I appear<br>Fallen low indeed; but I shall rise again.<br>The high flood will soon follow on this ebb;<br>The fountain of my fortune, which now stops<br>Repressed and bound by some malicious star,<br>Will soon in joy play forth from all its pipes. |
| GORDON | And yet remember I the good old proverb,<br>'Let the [[Night\|night]] come before we praise the day.'<br>I would be slow from long-continued fortune<br>To gather [[Hope\|hope]]: for [[Hope\|hope]] is the companion<br>Given to the unfortunate by pitying Heaven.<br>[[Fear]] hovers round the head of prosperous men,<br>For still unsteady are the scales of fate. |
| *(didascalia)* | (smiling) |
| WALLENSTEIN | I hear the very Gordon that of old<br>Was wont to preach to me, now once more preaching;<br>I know [[Well\|well]], that all sublunary things<br>Are still the vassals of vicissitude.<br>The unpropitious gods demand their tribute.<br>This long ago the ancient Pagans knew:<br>And therefore of their own accord they offered<br>To themselves injuries, so to atone<br>The [[Jealousy\|jealousy]] of their divinities:<br>And human sacrifices bled to Typhon. |
| *(didascalia)* | [After a pause, serious, and in a more subdued manner. |
| WALLENSTEIN | I too have sacrific'd to him — For me<br>There fell the dearest friend, and through my fault<br>He fell! No joy from favourable fortune<br>Can overweigh the anguish of this stroke.<br>The envy of my destiny is glutted:<br>Life pays for life. On his pure head the [[Lightning\|lightning]]<br>Was drawn off which would else have shattered me. |
