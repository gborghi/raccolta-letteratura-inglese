| *(didascalia)* | SCENE THE FIRST. — A hall of armory, with an altar in the part farthest from the stage. VELEZ, OSORIO, [[Maria|MARIA]]. |
| [[Maria]] | Lord Velez! you have ask’d my presence here,<br>And I submit; but (Heaven bear witness for me!)<br>My heart approves it not! ‘tis mockery! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Here ALBERT enters in a sorcerer’s robe.)* |
| [[Maria]] | *(to Albert.)* Stranger! I mourn and blush to see you here<br>On such employments! With far other thoughts<br>I left you. |
| OSORIO | *(aside.)* Ha! he has been tampering with her! |
| ALBERT | O high-[[Soul|soul]]’d maiden, and more dear to me<br>Than suits the stranger’s name, I swear to thee,<br>I will uncover all concealed things!<br>[[Doubt]], but decide not!<br>Stand from off the altar. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Here a strain of music is heard from behind the scenes, from an instrument of glass or steel — the harmonica or Celestina stop, or Clagget’s metallic organ.)* |
| ALBERT | With no irreverent voice or uncouth charm<br>I call up the departed. [[Soul]] of Albert!<br>Hear our soft suit, and heed my milder spells:<br>So may the gates of Paradise unbarr’d<br>Cease thy swift toils, since haply thou art one<br>Of that innumerable company,<br>Who in broad circle, lovelier than the rainbow,<br>Girdle this round earth in a dizzy motion,<br>With noise too vast and constant to be heard —<br>Fitliest unheard! For, O ye numberless<br>And rapid travellers! what ear unstun’d,<br>What sense unmadden’d, might bear up against<br>The rushing of your congregated wings?<br>Even now your living [[Wheel|wheel]] turns o’er my head!<br>Ye, as ye pass, toss high the desart sands,<br>That roar and whiten, like a burst of waters,<br>A sweet appearance, but a dread illusion,<br>To the parch’d caravan that roams by [[Night|night]].<br>And ye build up on the becalmed waves<br>That whirling pillar, which from earth to heaven<br>Stands vast, and moves in blackness. Ye too split<br>The ice-mount, and with fragments many and huge,<br>Tempest the new-thaw’d sea, whose sudden gulphs<br>Suck in, perchance, some Lapland wizard’s skiff.<br>Then round and round the whirlpool’s marge ye [[Dance|dance]],<br>Till from the blue-swoln corse the [[Soul|soul]] toils out,<br>And joins your mighty army.<br>[[Soul]] of Albert!<br>Hear the mild spell and tempt no blacker charm.<br>By sighs unquiet and the sickly pang<br>Of an half dead yet still undying [[Hope|hope]],<br>Pass visible before our mortal sense;<br>So shall the Church’s cleansing rites be thine,<br>Her knells and masses that redeem the dead. |
| *(didascalia)* | THE [[Song|SONG]] (Sung behind the scenes, accompanied by the same instrument as before.) |
| *(didascalia)* | Hear, sweet spirit! hear the spell<br>Lest a blacker charm compel!<br>So shall the midnight breezes swell<br>With thy deep long-lingering knell.<br>And at evening evermore<br>In a chapel on the shore<br>Shall the chanters sad and saintly,<br>Yellow tapers burning faintly,<br>Doleful masses chant for thee,<br>Miserere, Domine!<br>Hark! the cadence dies away<br>On the quiet moonlight sea,<br>The boatmen rest their oars, and say,<br>Miserere, Domine! *(A long pause.)* |
| OSORIO | This was too [[Melancholy|melancholy]], father! |
| VELEZ | Nay!<br>My Albert lov’d sad music from a [[Child|child]].<br>Once he was lost; and after weary search<br>We found him in an open place of the wood,<br>To which spot he had follow’d a blind boy<br>Who breathed into a pipe of sycamore<br>Some strangely-moving notes, and these, he said,<br>Were taught him in [[A Dream|a dream]]; him we first saw<br>Stretch’d on the broad top of a sunny heath-bank;<br>And, lower down, poor Albert fast asleep,<br>His head upon the blind boy’s dog — it pleased me<br>To mark, how he had fasten’d round the pipe<br>A silver toy, his grandmother had given him.<br>Methinks I see him now, as he then look’d.<br>His infant dress was grown too short for him,<br>Yet still he wore it. |
| ALBERT | *(aside.)* My [[Tears|tears]] must not flow —<br>I must not clasp his knees, and cry, my father! |
| OSORIO | The [[Innocent|innocent]] obey nor charm nor spell.<br>My brother is in heaven. Thou sainted spirit<br>Burst on our sight, a passing visitant!<br>Once more to hear thy voice, once more to see thee,<br>O ‘twere a joy to me. |
| ALBERT | *(abruptly.)* A joy to thee!<br>What if thou heard’st him now? What if his spirit<br>Re-enter’d its cold corse, and came upon thee,<br>With many a stab from many a murderer’s poniard?<br>What if, his steadfast eye still beaming pity<br>And brother’s [[Love|love]], he turn’d his head aside,<br>Lest he should look at thee, and with one look<br>Hurl thee beyond all power of penitence? |
| VELEZ | These are unholy fancies! |
| OSORIO | *(struggling with his feelings.)* Yes, my father!<br>He is in heaven! |
| ALBERT | *(still to Osorio.)* But what if this same brother<br>Had lived even so, that at his dying hour<br>The name of heaven would have convuls’d his face<br>More than the [[Death|death]]-pang? |
| [[Maria]] | Idly-prating man!<br>He was most virtuous. |
| ALBERT | *(still to Osorio.)* What if his very virtues<br>Had pamper’d his swoln heart, and made him proud?<br>And what if [[Pride|pride]] had duped him into [[Guilt|guilt]],<br>Yet still he stalk’d, a [[Self|self]]-created God,<br>Not very bold, but excellently cunning;<br>And one that at his mother’s looking-glass,<br>Would force his features to a frowning sternness?<br>Young lord! I tell thee, that there are such beings, —<br>Yea, and it gives fierce merriment to the damn’d,<br>To see these most proud men, that loathe mankind,<br>At every stir and buz of coward [[Conscience|conscience]],<br>Trick, cant, and lie, most whining hypocrites!<br>Away! away! Now let me hear more music. *(Music as before.)* |
| ALBERT | The spell is mutter’d — come, thou wandering shape,<br>Who own’st no master in an eye of flesh,<br>Whate’er be this man’s doom, fair be it or foul,<br>If he be dead, come quick, and bring with thee<br>That which he grasp’d in [[Death|death]]; and if he lives,<br>Some token of his obscure perilous life. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(The whole orchestra crashes into one [[Chorus|chorus]].)* |
| *(didascalia)* | Wandering demon! hear the spell<br>Lest a blacker charm compel! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(A thunder-clap. The incense on the altar takes [[Fire|fire]] suddenly.)* |
| [[Maria]] | This is some trick — I know, it is a trick.<br>Yet my weak fancy, and these bodily creepings,<br>Would fain give substance to the [[Shadow|shadow]]. |
| VELEZ | *(advancing to the altar.)* Hah!<br>A picture! |
| [[Maria]] | O God! my picture? |
| ALBERT | *(gazing at [[Maria]] with wild impatient distressfulness.)* Pale — pale — deadly pale! |
| [[Maria]] | He grasp’d it when he died. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(She swoons. ALBERT rushes to her and supports her.)* |
| ALBERT | My [[Love|love]]! my wife!<br>Pale — pale, and cold! My [[Love|love]]! my wife! [[Maria]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(VELEZ is at the altar. OSORIO remains near him in a state of stupor.)* |
| OSORIO | *(rousing himself.)* Where am I? ‘Twas a lazy chilliness. |
| VELEZ | *(takes and conceals the picture in his robe.)* This way, my son! She must not see this picture.<br>Go, call the attendants! Life will soon ebb back! |
| *(didascalia)* | *(VELEZ and OSORIO leave the stage.)* |
| ALBERT | Her pulse doth flutter. [[Maria]]! my [[Maria]]! |
| [[Maria]] | *(recovering — looks round.)* I heard a voice — but often in my [[Dreams|dreams]],<br>I hear that voice, and wake; and try, and try,<br>To hear it waking — but I never could!<br>And ‘tis so now — even so! [[Well]], he is dead,<br>Murder’d perhaps! and I am faint, and feel<br>As if it were no painful thing to die! |
| ALBERT | *(eagerly.)* Believe it not, sweet maid! believe it not,<br>Beloved woman! ‘Twas a low imposture<br>Framed by a guilty wretch. |
| [[Maria]] | Ha! who art thou? |
| ALBERT | *(exceedingly agitated.)* My heart bursts over thee! |
| [[Maria]] | Didst thou murder him?<br>And dost thou now repent? Poor troubled man!<br>I do forgive thee, and may Heaven forgive thee! |
| ALBERT | *(aside.)* Let me be gone. |
| [[Maria]] | If thou didst murder him,<br>His spirit ever, at the throne of God,<br>Asks [[Mercy|mercy]] for thee, prays for [[Mercy|mercy]] for thee,<br>With [[Tears|tears]] in heaven! |
| ALBERT | Albert was not murder’d.<br>Your foster-mother —— |
| [[Maria]] | And doth she know aught? |
| ALBERT | She knows not aught — but haste thou to her cottage<br>To-morrow early — bring Lord Velez with thee.<br>There ye must meet me — but your servants come. |
| [[Maria]] | *(wildly.)* Nay — nay — but tell me! *(A pause — then presses her forehead.)*<br>Ah! ‘tis lost again!<br>This dead confused [[Pain|pain]]! *(A pause — she gazes at ALBERT.)*<br>Mysterious man!<br>Methinks, I cannot [[Fear|fear]] thee — for thine eye<br>Doth swim with pity — I will lean on thee. |
| *(didascalia)* | *(Exeunt ALBERT and [[Maria|MARIA]]. Re-enter VELEZ and OSORIO.)* |
| VELEZ | *(sportively.)* You shall not see the picture, till you own it. |
| OSORIO | This mirth and raillery, sir! beseem your age.<br>I am content to be more serious. |
| VELEZ | Do you think I did not scent it from the first?<br>An excellent scheme, and excellently managed.<br>‘Twill blow away her doubts, and now she’ll wed you,<br>I’[[Faith|faith]], the likeness is most admirable.<br>I saw the trick — yet these old eyes grew dimmer<br>With very foolish [[Tears|tears]], it look’d so like him! |
| OSORIO | Where should I get her portrait? |
| VELEZ | Get her portrait?<br>Portrait? You mean the picture! At the painter’s —<br>No difficulty then — but that you lit upon<br>A fellow that could play the sorcerer,<br>With such a grace and terrible majesty,<br>It was most rare good fortune. And how deeply<br>He seem’d to suffer when [[Maria]] swoon’d,<br>And half made [[Love|love]] to her! I suppose you’ll ask me<br>Why did he so? |
| OSORIO | *(with deep tones of suppressed agitation.)* Ay, wherefore did he so? |
| VELEZ | Because you bade him — and an excellent thought!<br>A mighty man, and gentle as he is mighty.<br>He’ll [[Wind|wind]] into her confidence, and rout<br>A host of scruples — come, confess, Osorio! |
| OSORIO | You pierce through mysteries with a lynx’s eye,<br>In this, your merry mood! you see it all! |
| VELEZ | Why, no! — not all. I have not yet discover’d,<br>At least, not wholly, what his speeches meant.<br>[[Pride]] and hypocrisy, and [[Guilt|guilt]] and cunning —<br>Then when he fix’d his obstinate eye on you,<br>And you pretended to look strange and tremble.<br>Why — why — what ails you now? |
| OSORIO | *(with a stupid stare.)* Me? why? what ails me?<br>A pricking of the [[Blood|blood]] — it might have happen’d<br>At any other [[Time|time]]. Why scan you me? |
| VELEZ | *(clapping him on the shoulder.)* ‘Twon’t do—’twon’t do — I have lived too long in the world.<br>His speech about the corse and stabs and murderers,<br>Had reference to the assassins in the picture:<br>That I made out. |
| OSORIO | *(with a frantic eagerness.)* Assassins! what assassins! |
| VELEZ | [[Well]]-acted, on my life! Your curiosity<br>Runs open-mouth’d, ravenous as [[Winter|winter]] wolf.<br>I dare not stand in its way. *(He shows OSORIO the picture.)* |
| OSORIO | Dup’d — dup’d — dup’d!<br>That [[Villain|villain]] [[Ferdinand]]! *(aside.)* |
| VELEZ | Dup’d — dup’d — not I.<br>As he swept by me —— |
| OSORIO | Ha! what did he say? |
| VELEZ | He caught his garment up and hid his face.<br>It seem’d as he were struggling to suppress —— |
| OSORIO | A laugh! a laugh! O hell! he laughs at me! |
| VELEZ | It heaved his chest more like a violent sob. |
| OSORIO | A choking laugh! *(A pause — then very wildly.)*<br>I tell thee, my dear father!<br>I am most glad of this! |
| VELEZ | Glad! — aye — to be sure. |
| OSORIO | I was benumb’d, and stagger’d up and down<br>Thro’ darkness without light — dark — dark — dark —<br>And every inch of this my flesh did feel<br>As if a cold toad touch’d it! Now ‘tis sunshine,<br>And the [[Blood|blood]] dances freely thro’ its channels! *(He turns off — then (to himself) mimicking [[Ferdinand|FERDINAND]]’S manner.)*<br>‘A common trick of gratitude, my lord!<br>Old Gratitude! a dagger would dissect<br>His own full heart,’ ‘twere good to see its colour! |
| VELEZ | *(looking intently at the picture.)* Calm, yet commanding! how he bares his breast,<br>Yet still they stand with dim uncertain looks,<br>As penitence had run before their crime.<br>A crime too black for aught to follow it<br>Save blasphemous [[Despair|despair]]! See this man’s face —<br>With what a difficult toil he drags his [[Soul|soul]]<br>To do the deed. *(Then to OSORIO.)*<br>O this was delicate flattery<br>To poor [[Maria]], and I [[Love|love]] thee for it! |
| OSORIO | *(in a slow voice with a reasoning laugh.)* [[Love]] — [[Love|love]] — and then we hate — and what? and wherefore?<br>Hatred and [[Love|love]]. Strange things! both strange alike!<br>What if one reptile sting another reptile,<br>Where is the crime? The goodly face of [[Nature]]<br>Hath one trail less of slimy filth upon it.<br>Are we not all predestined rottenness<br>And cold dishonor? Grant it that this hand<br>Had given a morsel to the hungry worms<br>Somewhat too early. Where’s the [[Guilt|guilt]] of this?<br>That this must needs bring on the idiotcy<br>Of moist-eyed penitence—’tis like [[A Dream|a dream]]! |
| VELEZ | Wild talk, my [[Child|child]]! but thy excess of feeling *(Turns off from OSORIO.)*<br>Sometimes, I [[Fear|fear]], it will unhinge his brain! |
| OSORIO | I kill a man and lay him in the sun,<br>And in a month there swarm from his dead [[Body|body]]<br>A thousand — nay, ten thousand sentient beings<br>In place of that one man whom I had kill’d.<br>Now who shall tell me, that each one and all,<br>Of these ten thousand lives, is not as happy<br>As that one life, which being shov’d aside<br>Made room for these ten thousand? |
| VELEZ | Wild as [[Madness|madness]]! |
| OSORIO | Come, father! you have taught me to be merry,<br>And merrily we’ll pore upon this picture. |
| VELEZ | *(holding the picture before Osorio.)* That Moor, who points his [[Sword|sword]] at Albert’s breast —— |
| OSORIO | *(abruptly.)* A tender-hearted, scrupulous, grateful [[Villain|villain]],<br>Whom I will strangle! |
| VELEZ | And these other two —— |
| OSORIO | Dead — dead already! — what care I for the dead? |
| VELEZ | The heat of brain and your too strong affection<br>For Albert, fighting with your other passion,<br>Unsettle you, and give reality<br>To these your own contrivings. |
| OSORIO | Is it so?<br>You see through all things with your penetration.<br>Now I am calm. How fares it with [[Maria]]?<br>My heart doth ache to see her. |
| VELEZ | Nay — defer it!<br>Defer it, dear Osorio! I will go. *(Exit VELEZ.)* |
| OSORIO | A rim of the sun lies yet upon the sea —<br>And now ‘tis gone! all may be done this [[Night|night]]! |
| *(didascalia)* | Enter a Servant. |
| OSORIO | There is a man, once a Moresco chieftain,<br>One [[Ferdinand]]. |
| SERVANT | He lives in the Alpuxarras,<br>Beneath a slate rock. |
| OSORIO | Slate rock? |
| SERVANT | Yes, my lord!<br>If you had seen it, you must have remember’d<br>The flight of steps his children had worn up it<br>With often clambering. |
| OSORIO | [[Well]], it may be so. |
| SERVANT | Why, now I think on’t, at this [[Time|time]] of the year<br>‘Tis hid by vines. |
| OSORIO | *(in a muttering voice.)* The cavern — aye — the cavern.<br>He cannot fail to find it. *(To the Servant.)*<br>Where art going?<br>You must deliver to this [[Ferdinand]]<br>A letter. Stay till I have written it. *(Exit the Servant.)* |
| OSORIO | *([[Alone|alone]].)* The tongue can’t stir when the mouth is fill’d with mould.<br>A little earth stops up most eloquent mouths,<br>And a square stone with a few pious texts<br>Cut neatly on it, keeps the earth down tight. |
| *(didascalia)* | Scene changes to the space before the [[Castle|castle]]. |
